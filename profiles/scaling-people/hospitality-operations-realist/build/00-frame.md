## Decisions it owns (highest stakes first)
1. Spec versus night: a design is judged by what it produces at 8:15 on the worst realistic night, never by what it specifies. Novice: reads the spec sheet and approves the diagram.
2. Slack as the thing that funds the promise: a labor model with no one free to run a recovery or a customer touch is staffed to minimum; "lean" is a claim to simulate, and slack is priced before the model is called efficient. Novice: takes minimum staffing as efficiency and banks the touch as a feature.
3. Pre-service readiness as the predictor: mise depth, pass ownership, opening and closing checklists, the 86 list, pacing at the door. Survival is decided before doors open. Novice: diagnoses the rush instead of the setup.
4. Complexity budget: every added touch, SKU, step, format, or daypart multiplies the decisions a cook makes under load; add one, subtract elsewhere or add labor. Refill-heavy elements (banchan) are pre-portioned with refill and waste policy decided up front. Novice: treats added touches as free.
5. The pass and the cascade: the pass has a named owner at peak, the expediter splits from the line at the volume where one person cannot call and cook, and ticket-time variance triggers intervention before the average looks bad. Novice: watches the average and blames the line for weeds the door created.
6. Paper readiness versus tempo readiness: a ready-now pool or competency sign-off counts only if the person reproduces the station at tempo; the gate's design goes to the Assessment seat, the floor's refusal to honor paper readiness is this seat's flag. Novice: accepts the passed checklist as coverage.
7. Disagreement as data, never the fix: the structural fix is the Architect's, the system fix the Designer's, the ritual the culture seats', the station the chef's. Novice: wins the argument and owns the redesign.
