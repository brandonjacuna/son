# Models: Hospitality Operations Realist

Read when a tempo read has to argue why, not only what.

| id | model | use |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Slack is the funding source, not waste. Rigor on the controllable work ("manage your money like a crazy person 95 percent of the time so that, 5 percent of the time, you can spend foolishly") pays for recovery and hospitality. | Answer "lean" claims: the discretionary moment is affordable only because the controllable work is tight. |
| M2 | Recovery is a designed capability. It needs someone with slack to execute it and information to know it is needed. "A mistake is not a mistake until you fail to correct it." | Test any recovery promise for both halves: a free person and a signal. |
| M3 | Consistency across a rotating crew is a systems problem. Turnover is baseline, so any trained cook must reproduce the plate; the work is unvarying reproduction. | Judge readiness and station design by whether the next cook can reproduce it, not the best one. |
| M4 | Preparedness over heroics. Urgency is preparation, not speed; survival is decided before doors open (mise depth, station setup, pass ownership, opening and closing checklists, 86 list, door pacing). | Diagnose the setup before the rush. |
| M5 | The cascade is nonlinear. Variance precedes the average; once the caller "can no longer keep track of where each dish is," every ticket behind is already late. | Why R2 acts on variance. |
| M6 | Spec versus night. The single thing the rush takes out first is usually one of four: a light station, an unowned pass, a touch past capacity, or a setup cut for speed. | Start the simulation by checking those four. |

## Diagnostic sequence
Name the object, state its tempo, simulate the worst realistic night, find what breaks first, locate whose fix it is, check the seam, anchor to what Sŏn already holds (find the real gap, do not rebuild from scratch), mark the call.

## Translating sources
The sources say "guest" and "stage" and are profane. Carry the judgment, not the language: customer, paid practical.
