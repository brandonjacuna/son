# Models: people-systems-designer

Mental models and distinctions behind the core. Read when a design needs the reasoning, not on every call. Every model here transfers from knowledge work or retail; flag the transfer when it carries a recommendation.

## M1. A system is what it produces
A people system is the behavior it reliably produces on a floor, not what it declares. A problem universal across stations, dayparts, and people is a design problem, not a talent problem.

## M2. A people system is a loop, not a policy
The vicious cycle: labor treated as a cost, understaffing, operational failure, lower sales, less investment, back to understaffing. Canon 11 (labor is an asset) commits Sŏn to the other direction; a design that breaks one link and leaves the rest still runs the loop.

## M3. The good jobs system, not the good wage
Investment in people pays only alongside four operational choices, run as one system: focus and simplify; standardize and empower (every standard carries decision rights); cross-train (canon 14); operate with slack. Pay is necessary and not sufficient. This model says nothing about pay structure; pay built into the menu price stays out of this seat until Brandon opens that project.

## M4. Selection ranking
A work-sample test predicts better than a structured interview, which predicts better than an unstructured interview, reference checks, or years of experience. Use the order, never the coefficients. Most of an interviewer's read forms in the first minutes and the rest confirms it, so the system has to carry the read: a written rubric, documented questions, scores recorded before discussion.

## M5. The learning zone
Safety and standards are two axes. Low on both: apathy. High safety, low standards: comfort. High standards, low safety: anxiety, where output looks fine and problems hide. High on both: learning. Safety is engineered through three leader moves (set the stage, invite participation, respond productively). Silence is the default the system must beat.

## M6. Failure types
Basic failure (a known process not followed), complex failure (many factors meeting in a new way), intelligent failure (a thoughtful experiment in new territory). Few failures are truly blameworthy, yet most get treated as if they were. Sort first, then respond.

## M7. Progress and its blockers
The strongest daily driver of inner work life is progress in meaningful work. Catalysts and inhibitors act on the work; nourishers and toxins act on the person. Bad is stronger than good, so removing an inhibitor or a toxin outweighs adding a perk.

## M8. Nudge before mandate
Change the choice architecture (defaults, order, what is easy) to shift behavior before adding a rule or control.

## M9. The flat house raises the stakes of a hire
Sŏn's house compounds without headcount growth (target 4, partly confirmed), so development doubles as the career and retention system and a wrong hire costs more than in a tall structure.
