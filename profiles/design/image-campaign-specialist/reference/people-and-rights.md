# People and rights (reference)

Status: draft until counsel. Every row below is draft until counsel. Nothing here is a legal decision. Each row ends in a flag for the brief or a route to Brandon and counsel. Texas law for people in frame; federal law for ownership and work time. Counsel's release template does not exist yet, so every requirement in a brief stays unverified.

## People in frame (likeness)
- L1. A living person in frame (team member or customer) is the common law case; the Texas statute (Prop. Code ch. 26) covers the deceased. Draft until counsel.
- L2. The test to run on a frame: is the likeness used for its value, is the person identifiable, does Sŏn gain an advantage? All three yes: treat the use as unauthorized without consent. Draft until counsel.
- L3. Identifiable means more than a face: a distinctive tattoo, a uniform at a known location, or a name in the caption. Flag the frame; release before ship. Draft until counsel.
- L4. A site, menu, ad, or social set is use for value. Profit is not required, and "no money changed hands" does not remove the need for a release. Draft until counsel.
- L5. Consent covers what it names. Name every surface in the release request; a new surface later is a new flag. Draft until counsel.
- L6. An identifiable customer: flag the shot and hold it until a release exists. Whether a crop or blur removes identifiability is counsel's call. Draft until counsel.
- L7. Voice and signature are protected along with the face. A recorded voice in a story or reel is the same class of risk as a face. Draft until counsel.
- L8. A deceased person's image used as reference or concept (a historic portrait, for instance): the statute requires written consent for advertising goods or services. It reads as history, which is why it gets used as decor. Route to counsel. Draft until counsel.
- L9. A newsworthy or expressive exception: assume none applies, because Sŏn is advertising its own business. Route the question to counsel. Draft until counsel.

## Ownership (usage rights)
- L10. Work made for hire has two routes: an employee's work within the scope of employment, or a commission in one of nine listed categories plus a written agreement signed by both sides. An outside photographer's work usually fits neither, so assume Sŏn does not own the images unless a signed writing says so. Brandon decides assignment or license with counsel. Draft until counsel.
- L11. A work-for-hire clause must be signed by both parties. An email saying "use them" is not one. Draft until counsel.
- L12. A nonexclusive license is not a transfer of ownership; an assignment or exclusive license is. Read "license" as: Sŏn may not own or control the images. Draft until counsel.
- L13. A team member who shoots images: whether that is employee work within scope is a hard call, because a shoot sits outside the usual job. Payroll does not equal ownership. Counsel decides. Draft until counsel.

## Work time (team members on a shoot)
- L14. A team member in or helping on a shoot is on work time. Brandon decides pay treatment; the seat never sets it. Draft until counsel.
- L15. Waiting on set between setups can count as hours worked. Log call to wrap for Brandon; never trim it. Draft until counsel.
- L16. An off-site shoot, or one that runs past a shift: travel and the extended day may be paid time. Flag the times to Brandon. Draft until counsel.
- L17. Never call a shoot "voluntary" or unpaid. The meeting exception requires all four conditions, including "not job related," and a shoot for Sŏn is job related. Draft until counsel.
- L18. A shoot day can push a team member past 40 hours in the week. Flag the weekly total, because overtime is 1.5 times the regular rate. Draft until counsel.
