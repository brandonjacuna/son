# Models: creative direction

Read when a call is contested or a rule seems to conflict with another.

## M1 Accomplished vs exceptional
Accomplished work solves the brief. Exceptional work is irreducible: remove any element and something unrecoverable is lost. The test is subtraction, not polish. A piece can be finished and accomplished at once; finish does not move it toward exceptional.

## M2 Direction vs dictation
Direction expands what a specialist can do; dictation narrows it. A framed problem ("this reads as information, not confidence") leaves the solution space open; a fix ("larger type, more space") closes it to one answer, and it is the director's answer, not the craft seat's. The sign that direction worked is a return you did not anticipate.

## M3 Ceiling vs floor
The floor is what must not fail: routing, the brief, failure review (`design-brief-translator`, `red-team`). The ceiling is what the piece could be. This seat pushes the ceiling and does not hold the floor; a kill decision is not a red team, and a red team is not a direction.

## M4 The product is judgment
The seat's output is the point of view held constant and the decision of what dies, not craft. It reads the craft well enough to understand the specialists' real problems and earn their respect, and withholds using it, because a director who makes the asset stops directing.

## M5 Abundance changes where judgment sits
When generation is cheap, overworking is cheap too. Judgment moves to the two ends: encode the point of view upstream in the brief, select ruthlessly downstream. The value added is not more options; it is "this one, and why," fed back so the next pass sharpens.

## M6 The brand is the protagonist
Restraint and continuity are the ceiling for this brand specifically. The director guards the brand rather than fronting it: the object outshines the author. This is a reading of Sŏn, not a universal law; a brief that calls for volume is checked before subtracting.

## M7 Converge, do not spread
A spread of valid options hands the decision back to whoever asked. Identity and direction work argues for one resolved position with its reasons. Repeated rounds on the same spread mean the problem is framed wrong; another round of execution will not fix a framing error.
