# Reference extraction and register

Read when a request carries a reference image, a "like this brand", or a register adjective. The skill's R3 and R4 govern; this file is the method.

## Decode, do not describe
- X1. A reference is a revealed system. Descriptive observation ("muted, elegant, lots of white space") is where the novice stops; the brief is built from what each choice is for. Decode it.
- X2. "Like this brand / this competitor" is a surface-copy risk. The takeaway is the decision logic, plus a named list of what to leave behind.

## Layers that carry into a Sŏn brief
The old seven-layer protocol extracts more than a design-system maker can use: color, type, and material specifics collide with the closed system. Keep these, as proportions and logic only.

| id | layer | portable parameters |
|---|---|---|
| X3 | Layout | the governing ratio; margin-to-content ratio; grid declared or hidden; where and why it breaks |
| X4 | Density | the zone map (dense, transitional, open), what sits in each, the gradient direction |
| X5 | Accent discipline | which temperature leads; saturation ceiling; the accent's role and its coverage (for example, one accent under 10 percent of the field). The colors themselves come from the eight in `tokens/` |
| X6 | Hierarchy mechanism | what carries hierarchy (weight contrast, tracking, scale, space); type count and role; alignment convention. The faces come from `tokens/` |
| X7 | Register | the compound (X11) and the formal element that carries it |

## Layers that do not carry
- X8. The specific typeface, palette, photographic subject, and period color: pastiche, and the palette breaks the eight-color system. Era citation and materiality as full parameter sets are dropped: they are survey material with no brief decision here, and citing a tradition risks the Korean canon line (the ceramic traditions and baekja are reference only). If the era quote does work in the mechanism, say what it does in one clause; never name the tradition as brand.

## Close every extraction
- X9. Mechanism statement: one to two sentences on where the reference's power comes from, independent of any execution. Then "bring through" and "leave behind", each a short list.
- X10. Limits. A low-resolution or ambiguous reference: name the uncertainty rather than assert a parameter that cannot be read. Over-extraction is its own failure: stop at the parameters the function needs and leave the maker room to design. The test of the made thing: related but not imitative means the DNA came through; pastiche means the surface was copied.

## Register
- X11. Register is a compound: economic register (where each element sits on the accessible-to-exclusive axis) x emotional tone x relationship to the viewer, plus the primary load-bearer. "Mid-premium, quiet confidence, peer-to-peer" is a different brief from "luxury, authority, aspirational distance."
- X12. Adjective conversion. Each adjective in the request is replaced by the formal parameter that would produce it wherever one exists:

| request says | brief carries |
|---|---|
| premium, high-end | economic register and load-bearer (often space allocation and the absence of conspicuous branding) |
| clean, minimal | density zone map and margin-to-content ratio |
| modern | hierarchy mechanism and grid visibility |
| warm | which temperature leads among the eight, accent coverage |

- X13. The theme follows the register: name the daypart register in words and point the maker at the `data-theme` values in `company/brand/design-system/tokens/`. The system switches themes at the semantic tier; it never selects one.
