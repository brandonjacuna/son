# Bar Designer: models and figures

## Station dimensions (kb, Perlick training and Perlick equipment pages; `estimated` until a spec sheet is in `equipment/*.yaml`)
- M1. Hips at the speed rail to the drink rail: 10 to 11 in maximum. The reason double rails fail.
- M2. Aisle, front bar to back bar refrigeration: 36 to 40 in. Under 36 in, use 19 in narrow-swing or sliding doors.
- M3. Back bar refrigerator depth: 24 in (bar units), not the 31 in kitchen depth. About 1 in clearance each side so units can be pulled.
- M4. Trash: 12 in minimum per station, plus recycling where required. One dump sink per station, with strainer.
- M5. Task lighting: 6000K LED (Perlick training) versus 4000K (an Ellis mobile bar). Conflict open until the book's lighting chapter is ingested.
- M6. Perlick cocktail station PTE68-A: 68 in wide; bottle well, ice chest, liquor step, prep sink with foot-pedal faucet. Reach capacity about 27 US L, including a 12 L slanted speed rack at the bartender's right. Insulated bottle well holds nine 750 ml or six 1 L pourers.
- M7. Speed rails come in 24, 36, 42, 48 in straight lengths and 12 in curves. Ice chests come 24, 30, 36, 42 in wide with dividers, shallower depth, and raised work height.
- M8. Blank in the kb (`needs-book`, never fill): bar top height, work surface height, bar depth, scupper width, aisle for one or two bartenders, station width, length per seat, foot rail, knee space, back bar height, ADA section, reach envelope, floor sink placement, die wall (the structural wall under the bar top that carries it and hides plumbing).

## Accessibility (2012 Texas Accessibility Standards, TDLR text, last verified 2026-10-09; every item `unverified` for Sŏn's site)
- M9. A seated bar counter is a dining surface. An accessible seat gets a top 28 to 34 in above the floor, with forward-approach knee and toe clearance under it.
- M10. The service side (where the bartender hands over a drink or takes payment) is a service counter: parallel approach needs 36 in of counter no higher than 36 in; forward approach needs 30 in no higher than 36 in, with knee and toe space under it.
- M11. Toe clearance: 0 to 9 in above the floor, 17 in deep minimum, 25 in maximum, 30 in wide minimum.
- M12. Knee clearance: 9 to 27 in above the floor; 11 in deep minimum at 9 in, 8 in deep minimum at 27 in, tapering 1 in of depth per 6 in of height; 25 in maximum depth; 30 in wide minimum. No exceptions: an apron, footrail, or bar-front that enters this zone is a design problem.
- M13. In an alteration, the accessible counter portion may be 24 in long where a full section would cut existing stations.
- M14. Not read: how many accessible seats are required (scoping is TAS Ch. 2) and the 904.4 exception figure. A Registered Accessibility Specialist confirms.

## Food code (Austin Public Health page and 25 TAC Ch. 228; every item `unverified`, all go to `codes/register.yaml`)
- M15. APH plan review: hand sinks in all prep, dish, and service areas, with hot and cold water. Whether that means one per well is a question for APH.
- M16. APH: a three-compartment sink or a commercial dishwasher. Whether a bar glasswasher alone qualifies is a question for APH.
- M17. APH: drains are indirect (a 1 in air gap, meaning the waste pipe ends above the receiving drain so dirty water cannot siphon back), so a floor sink must be reachable from every drained unit. This fixes floor sink count before the pour.
- M18. APH: a mop sink with backflow prevention; it belongs on the bar-to-kitchen path.
- M19. Texas Food Establishment Rules (Ch. 228) adopt the FDA Food Code 2017; warewashing and indirect waste live in the Food Code, not Ch. 228. Ch. 228 asks for light finishes at the glass wash area or approval and extra lighting; flag a dark finish there.
- M20. From memory, confirm: Food Code 4-301.12 (manual warewashing needs three compartments); 5-402.11 (warewashing and prep sinks drain through an air gap or air break).

## Korean beverages and sake (WSET; one makgeolli study)
- M21. Sake: store below 12 C, ideally under 8 C, upright, away from bright light. Unpasteurized and fruity ginjo styles need refrigeration. Opened bottles last about two weeks refrigerated, so keep few by-the-glass labels.
- M22. Sake service bands (WSET's figures): chilled 5 to 15 C, room 15 to 18 C, warm 40 to 55 C (another WSET page says 40 to 50 C). Only warm service needs extra equipment, unnamed by the source. One temperature for every sake is wrong.
- M23. Makgeolli: unpasteurized changes faster at 4 C than pasteurized; short cold holds, fast rotation. Pasteurized lasts longer but heat can add off-flavor. The study gives no serving temperature or shelf life.
- M24. Soju and cheongju storage: no source read; mark `unknown`.

## Glass wash (FE&S guide)
- M25. Low-temperature machines sanitize with chemicals (cheaper to buy, chemicals add up); high-temperature machines run about 180 F, leave fewer deposits, dry faster, and need a booster heater (confirm sizing and load with the maker).
- M26. Note supply pressure at 25 psi or below and a reverse-osmosis option for spotting on the utility schedule. Carousel units run about 38 racks an hour; rack-style units exceed 1,400 glasses an hour.
- M27. Not supplied by the source: drain, venting, electrical, door handing, rack dimensions, booster sizing. Mark `unknown`.
