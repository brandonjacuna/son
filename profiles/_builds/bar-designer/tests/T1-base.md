Do not approve yet. The plan has problems that cost money to fix later.

1. One sink at the existing drain. A bar needs more than one sink. Health code (Austin/Travis County, which follows the Texas Food Establishment Rules) generally requires a handsink at the bar in addition to a three-compartment sink or approved warewashing, and a separate sink for ice or dump. One sink serves neither workflow. Verify the exact count with the city.
2. A 24-foot rectangle on a wall. Length is not the measure. A bar works by stations: where each bartender stands, what is within arm's reach, and how drinks travel to the server and the customer. At 24 feet, the plan probably supports two stations with a service well at one end. The plan does not show stations, a service well, or a speed rail.
3. Drain location. Running plumbing to where the drain happens to be saves money but fixes the layout around the plumbing. Moving plumbing before the concept is approved is cheaper than after. Ask the architect for a plumbing option where the sinks are placed for workflow.
4. Missing: ice bin, glass storage, a place for a dump sink, a back bar, a cold well, refrigeration, a trash location, and a clean/dirty flow (dirty glassware returns from one direction and clean goes out the other).
5. Sightlines and customer seating, ADA-compliant bar height section (a 34-inch maximum surface or a section of lowered counter), and the clearance behind the bar (about 36 to 42 inches).
6. East wall: sun exposure, any equipment on that wall, and the path to the kitchen and storage.

Ask the architect for
- Station layout for two bartenders, flow arrows, and sink locations and counts
- Equipment list with ice, glass, and refrigeration
- Plumbing alternatives with cost notes
