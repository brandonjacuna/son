# Learner Advocate: "reading a table" module (T2)

**Verdict:** Not yet built for the learner. Two senior leads liking it is evidence of quality, not evidence that a novice can follow it. Do not ship until a novice has attempted it.

**F1. Expert review is not novice proof (C8, C9).**
- Harm: the reviewers read tables fluently and the author did too. They share the blind spot: the foundational micro-steps have been automated for years and cannot be felt as missing.
- Evidence: the module has been cleared only by people who already read tables well. Nothing in the review came from a learner in the room.
- Requirement: a real novice attempts the module cold before ship. Every point of confusion is logged as a defect in the module, not as the novice's failure.
- Owner: instructional-designer designs the attempt. Brandon schedules it. frontline-advocate reads consent and paid hours. Before opening, the first hired trainees in paid pre-opening training are the novices.

**F2. Dense text, possibly read mid-service (C5).**
- Harm: a table is text-heavy. If it is read on the floor under time pressure, a second-language trainee will hide being lost rather than ask.
- Requirement: de-texting for the core skill, plus a non-confessional comprehension check that surfaces the gap without asking anyone to admit confusion and without asking about background (R8).
- Owner: instructional-designer builds the medium and check. materials-author-editor takes the prose if the words are the barrier.

**Open at ship:** none yet. F1 and F2 are the first flags on this module.

**Open questions:**
1. Does the module teach a table-reading step that a trainee performs mid-service, or only in a briefing?
2. Does any table carry Korean dish or ingredient names with no gloss? If so, that is C7 and needs a gloss.
3. Is there a private practice occasion before anyone reads a table in front of peers?
4. Which novice will attempt it, and when is paid time available for that attempt?
