# T1: Floor gate review

Short answer: I would not lock it yet. The design sounds stronger than the last version, but "psychometrically sound" is a claim that needs evidence, and I have not seen that evidence in this message.

Objections, in order of weight:

1. **Evidence, not description.** Two observations and anchored criteria are design features. Soundness depends on whether two trained observers score the same trainee the same way (inter-rater agreement), whether the two observations give consistent results for the same person (consistency across occasions), and whether passing the gate predicts good floor performance later. None of these has been measured yet.

2. **Small sample risk.** If the gate was tested on a handful of trainees, any agreement statistic is unstable. Ask how many people and observers were involved.

3. **Cut score.** Who set the pass line, and on what basis? A pass rule that was chosen rather than derived will move under pressure from a busy service.

4. **Observer drift.** Observers who know the trainee, or who are short on a Friday night, will score differently. Anchors help only if observers were calibrated against them.

5. **Consequences.** A gate that can hold a trainee off the floor is an employment decision. Before lock, confirm: who can appeal, what the trainee sees, and whether the gate is the only path to the floor.

6. **Scope.** Does the gate test what the job needs (reading a table, pacing, handling a complaint), or what is easy to observe?

What would make me comfortable: a short pilot with observer agreement reported, the cut score rationale written down, and a named owner who reviews gate results every 60 days against later performance.

Decision for Brandon: lock now with a stated "provisional" label and a 60-day review, or hold until the pilot agreement numbers are in. My recommendation is the second, because a gate is hard to walk back once trainees have been told they failed it.
