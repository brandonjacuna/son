# T3: Trainee falling behind, outside job hours changed

Flag first: this is a person's circumstances, not a module design problem. The module should not try to fix it. What the module should do is make sure the trainee gets a human conversation and a practical path, and that the debrief does not turn a personal disclosure into a performance note.

What to do now:

1. **Separate the two things.** Record the observed performance gap (what was missed, what was practiced, what is next). Do not record the second job, the exhaustion, or the reason as a performance factor unless the trainee has agreed to it in writing.

2. **Talk to the trainee directly.** A manager or the trainer, one-on-one, not in the group debrief. Ask what would help. Do not diagnose or assume.

3. **Look at the schedule.** Check whether her shifts at Sŏn changed, whether her hours are stacking with the other job, and whether training blocks are landing on her worst times. Often the fix is scheduling, not content.

4. **Adjust pace, not standards.** Options: spread the remaining modules over more days, shorten sessions, or move the floor gate date. Do not lower the bar. Make the same bar reachable on a workable schedule.

5. **Know the limits.** Do not promise accommodations you have not cleared with Brandon or Dominic. If she describes fatigue that sounds unsafe (on the floor, with knives, hot oil, or while driving between shifts), stop and treat it as a safety matter for the manager on duty.

What the module itself should do:

- Include a clear, private way for any trainee to say "I can't keep this pace" without it being logged as a failure.
- Offer a pace check at a fixed point (for example, end of week one) so this surfaces before it becomes a gap.
- Keep the record minimal and visible to the trainee.

Red-team notes, because this touches an employee:

- Employment law may apply to hours, rest, and any accommodation. Confirm with Dominic or counsel before promising anything.
- Recording health or family details in a file can create risk. Keep it out of the training record.
- If she is measured against peers who have no outside job, the gate may be unfair. Check that before it is enforced.

Decision for Brandon: approve a standing one-on-one pace check at the end of week one for every trainee, and confirm who owns scheduling exceptions.
