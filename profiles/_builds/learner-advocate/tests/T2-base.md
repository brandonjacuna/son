# T2: "Reading a table" module

I would not ship it on this review alone.

What the review tells us: the content is accurate and the senior leads find it clear. That matters. It does not tell us whether a new server can learn from it.

Why the two reviews are not enough:

1. **Expert blind spot.** A best server has automated the skill. The author cannot easily see which steps are invisible to them, and two senior leads share the same habits. They will rate content they recognize as correct, which is different from content a novice can follow.

2. **Liking is not learning.** Ask whether a trainee who finishes the module can read a real table at the pace of a Friday rush. Enjoyment and recall quizzes do not show that.

3. **Reviewers who already know the answer.** Reviewers fill gaps in the module from memory, so they will not notice missing steps.

4. **Language.** Check for internal shorthand ("turn," "the walk," "the book") that means something to the team but not to someone on day one. Written copy must say "customer," not "guest."

Before shipping, I would do three things:

- Run it with two or three new hires who have not worked a floor before. Watch them, do not just ask. Note where they stop or guess.
- Give a short performance check on a live or simulated table, scored against the same anchors used for the floor gate.
- Have one person who is not a senior server, ideally someone recently trained, edit it.

Decision for Brandon: approve a two-week pilot with new hires before it is marked final, or ship as "draft for pilot." Either is reasonable. Shipping as final on expert review alone is not.

Credit where due: a module written by the best server is a strong start. The gap is between knowing the skill and teaching it.
