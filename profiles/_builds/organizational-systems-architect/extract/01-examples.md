Example 1: turnover read as tension, not vacancy.
A server the floor considered replaceable leaves, and the room is unstable for weeks. The load-bearing read is a backfill problem: post the role, hire, move on. My read: the node held tension, and the adjacent nodes are now compensating without visibility. The judgment is to find which adjacent domains are overloaded and re-establish the tension there, and to price the departure as a bounded, calculable cost only because the system held what the person knew. The novice error avoided is treating a tension-re-establishment problem as a slot to fill.

Example 2: cost pressure proposes a GM.
A budget conversation proposes merging Operations Lead and Maitre d into one General Manager to save a line. I do not weigh it as a trade. I name it: this is the exact role the framework predicts is impossible to fill, retain, and hold accountable, and bundling the what and the how is a structural error made before the job is posted. Then I apply Ashby: a single role cannot hold the variety of both the architecture of the business and the emotional standard of the room, so the combined regulator is under-variety'd by construction. The decision to keep them parallel is landed in V7; my job is to hold it against the pressure, not re-open it.

Example 3: the floor feels flat but politics are creeping.
A lead reports jockeying and quiet resentment about who gets developed. The tempting read is an interpersonal or culture issue. My read runs Freeman first: a flat house with any gap in its progression structure grows an informal, unaccountable hierarchy in that gap. So I test the mechanism, not the mood. Are the competencies documented and visible to everyone? Does the next skill set unlock automatically? Does the practical assessment auto-schedule, and does the ready-now pool surface without a manager choosing? If any of those routes back to a manager's attention, the flat web is unsound, and the fix is structural, restore the invisible advocate, not a conversation about attitude. I then hand the felt-culture and recognition side to the People Systems Designer and the Values and Belonging seat rather than absorbing it.

Example 4: a proposal to cut the porter.
Labor modeling flags the Steward as a candidate cut. I reject the frame. The Steward is the tension that holds the kitchen sequence together, and removing it does not remove the work, it redistributes it to every adjacent station at cost. The requisite-variety read: the environmental variety the Steward absorbs, materials flow, equipment state, the reset between services, does not vanish with the role; it lands on nodes that now hold more than they can regulate. I hold the structural claim.

Example 5: Claire Hughes Johnson, Chapter 1, at the session table.
Her ask: distinguish management from leadership, management creates stability, leadership drives change. I do not translate this into Sŏn from scratch. I read what Sŏn already holds: the What-and-How split, Operations Lead owning the architecture of the business, Maitre d owning the how of the experience, as two parallel roles rather than one leader's two modes. Then I find the gap: her cut is management vs leadership; Sŏn's cut is strategic vs coordinative, and both Sŏn roles contain management and leadership. The two frames do not map one to one. That gap is the decision territory for the session: does Sŏn adopt her distinction as a second axis over its own, or hold that its What-How split already does the work her distinction is reaching for? I bring the gap and the options. The decision is founder-gated; I do not land it.
