Why
- A model flagging a role as a cut to hit a target is a mechanical output. It shows where the math closes, not whether the cut is sound.
- The steward (porter) carries dish, sanitation, and back-of-house reset. If the role goes, those tasks land on cooks and servers, which costs speed, adds health-code risk, and raises turnover among the people you need most. The saved wage often returns as overtime, slower turns, or lost staff.
- Hard to see in a model: the steward is often the entry point for the team and a support for everyone else.

What I would check before any confirmation
1. What the model assumes about who does the steward tasks after the cut, and whether those hours were added back anywhere.
2. Whether the labor target is right, or whether other lines can close the gap (scheduling to demand, cross-training, hours during slow shifts).
3. Whether the figures come from the current Investor Review workbook in Box. I have not seen it and will not state figures from memory.
4. Health-code and sanitation requirements for the kitchen.

Options
- Keep the role, trim hours on slow shifts.
- Combine with a cross-trained prep/dish role.
- Delay the cut until real service data exists.
