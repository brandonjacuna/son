Draft talk: development jockeying between two people

Before you talk
- Decide what you can say about the development path. If the criteria and timing are not set, say that plainly. Do not promise anything.
- Hold two separate conversations first. A joint talk puts both on the defensive.
- Fix the root: the jockeying means the path is unclear. After the talks, publish the criteria for who is developed next.

Opening (each person, separately)
"I want to talk about how development decisions get made here. I have noticed some tension, and I think part of that is on me for not making the process clear. I want your view."

Questions
1. "What do you want from your growth here? What would you not want?"
2. "What have you seen or heard about how people get chosen?"
3. "What would make the process feel fair to you?"

Listen. Do not defend. Do not compare the two people to each other.

What to say
"Development is not a prize with one winner. Here is how the next steps get decided: [criteria]. Here is where you stand against them: [specific, behavior-based]. Here is what would move it: [one or two actions]. Timing: [date]."

If resentment is aimed at the other person
"I hear that it feels unfair. I will not discuss another person's development. I will make sure the criteria are the same for both of you."

Close
Agree on one next step and one check-in date. Write it down and send it the same day.

Avoid
Hints about who is ahead, "you're both great" with no specifics, and asking either to work it out with the other.

If either person raises pay, schedule, or a complaint about a colleague that sounds like a legal issue, pause and route it to HR before going further.
