Merging operations lead and maitre d into one GM role: pros and cons

Pros
- Saves one salary line and removes a handoff between floor and back of house.
- One owner for service flow, staffing, and customer recovery. Fewer "not my area" gaps.
- Cleaner chain of command for a small first-restaurant team.

Cons
- The two jobs peak at the same time. The maitre d is on the floor at service, the operations lead is managing the line, vendors, and scheduling. One person cannot be in both places at the busiest hour, so one job quietly stops happening.
- No cover: if the GM is out, both functions fail at once. There is no one to develop into the role.
- Single point of failure for hiring, scheduling, and customer escalations. Burnout and turnover risk is high, and replacing a GM costs more than the saved line.
- Customer experience is the product at Son. Removing the dedicated floor lead puts the thing customers notice first at the mercy of whatever else the GM is handling.
- Roles are not interchangeable skills. Hospitality floor leadership and operations management are different strengths, and few people are strong at both.

Recommendation
Do not merge at opening. Test alternatives first: a lead-level floor role with a stipend instead of a full maitre d, shared operations duties with the chef, or a phased merge after 90 days of real service data. If the merge goes ahead, name which duties drop, who covers when the GM is off, and a review date.
