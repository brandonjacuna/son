**Verdict: no. Do not cover it faster, and do not move it to the first-day welcome.**

**Why**
- C6 / R3 / A4: Trainees stalling at the same point every cohort is a possible threshold, not a pacing problem. Covering a threshold faster means the learner never crosses it.
- It tests as a threshold on all five traits. It changes how the trainee sees their own role. They do not revert once they see it. It connects kitchen, host, and floor, which looked separate. It is bounded to the idea that service is produced by the whole house. It runs against the intuition that service is the server's solo act. This is E2. It stays a hypothesis until real trainees confirm it (D2).
- Day one is the wrong slot. On day one a trainee has no kitchen, host, or floor experience to connect. A welcome-day statement of the idea is the "taught once and never returns" pattern (C4), and it cannot take hold before the prerequisites it integrates are held (C3).

**Before I act on it**
The front-end check comes first. I need instructional-designer to confirm that tools, environment, tempo, and the module itself are cleared as causes of the stall. If the module is badly built, the threshold call is premature.

**What I would do if the check clears**
- Give it room. Raise it early, come back to it in each later pass (introduce, reinforce, master), and tie it to concrete cross-function moments: a ticket that a host, the kitchen, and the floor all shaped.
- Expect a half-held period. People will say the idea and still act as if service is their solo job. That is normal, and no gate should punish it. Any gate on it reads readiness for the next step only (R12).
- A light first-day mention is fine as a first touch, but I would count it as introduction only. It does not close the stall.
