Spec: peer recognition, with two changes I recommend and a reason for each.

As requested
- House-wide feed: anyone can post a shout-out to a colleague, with a tag for which value or behavior it recognizes.
- Notification to the recipient; reactions allowed.

Recommend changing
1. Monthly leaderboard of most-recognized: remove it. Rankings turn recognition into a popularity contest. They favor visible roles (front of house, extroverts, long-tenured staff) over quieter, essential ones (dish, prep, stewards), and people start to campaign or trade favors. Those left off feel measured and lose.
2. Bonus for the top three: remove it. Paying for the most mentions makes recognition transactional, invites collusion, and may create a pay equity or wage-and-hour question when discretionary bonuses are tied to a ranking. Recognition is worth more when it is not for sale. Any bonus should come from the compensation system the founders agree on, not from a feed.

What to build instead
- Every shout-out names a specific behavior and a value. Short template: who, what they did, what difference it made.
- Visibility for everyone: a weekly digest that features a range of people and roles, curated by a manager so nobody is left out.
- Private tally for managers only, to spot who is never recognized, and fix that by looking for what they do.
- Recognition from managers and customers in the same feed.
- Opt-out of public posts if someone prefers private thanks.
- Moderation: managers can remove a post; a clear rule about what is not posted.
- Language: "customer," not "guest."

Measure
Share of staff recognized in 30 days, spread across roles, and whether recognition predicts retention. No individual ranking.

If you still want a reward, make it a draw among all recognized staff or a shared team reward. Decision for Brandon and Dominic.
