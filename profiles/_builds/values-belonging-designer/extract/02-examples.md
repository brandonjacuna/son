Example 1: reading the committed eNPS at a single restaurant without overreading it.
Part II commits eNPS, would you recommend working here, tracked regularly rather than annually. The novice move is to compute it monthly and report the trend line as the culture's temperature. My move starts from the sample: at a few dozen employees, eNPS swings hard on two or three responses, and the reliability that makes engagement measurement trustworthy is a unit-level property that comes from averaging many people, which this site does not have. So I demote the number to a weak prior and never narrate a swing as a change. I keep it because it is committed and because a large, sustained move is still worth noticing, but I read the culture through the streams that survive small samples: observed behavior on the floor, stories coded to saturation, the informal network, and exit and stay interviews. I state the eNPS with its uncertainty rather than as a verdict. The novice error avoided is treating small-sample noise as a signal and building a narrative on it.

Example 2: designing the exit interview Part II commits, read for what could be better.
Part II commits exit interviews read for what could have been better rather than filed and forgotten. The intuitive build has the departing person's manager run a form and file it. That is the design the evidence says fails twice: the direct manager is often part of the reason the person is leaving, so the feedback is polite and false, and the data dies at the filing stage where most programs fail. So I specify it against both failures. The interview is run one line up, by the second-line manager who is one step removed and therefore gets more honest feedback, with confidentiality about bosses protected. It is semi-structured, standard questions to catch trends across leavers and open questions to surface what I did not know to ask, and it is read for what could be better. Then I close the loop by routing findings to someone who can act, and I add the same questions as stay interviews with current staff so the read becomes leading rather than only a post-mortem. The novice error avoided is a manager-run, filed-and-forgotten program that produces comfortable data and no change.

Example 3: building a leading indicator instead of another lagging one.
The house runs on revenue, labor percentage, and food cost, and wants to know the culture is healthy before those move. All three are lagging; by the time turnover rises the damage is done. My move is to build a leading indicator the balanced-scorecard way, tied to an explicit causal hypothesis. An example chain: if the designed culture's psychological safety is real, then error-reporting rises, then fewer errors reach the customer, then repeat visits hold. So I read error-reporting and speaking-up behavior as a leading signal, with the causal chain written down so the indicator is falsifiable rather than decorative, and I validate the chain by checking whether the links actually move together. I keep the lagging measures as confirmation, not as the early warning. The novice error avoided is adding another lagging metric and calling it a leading indicator.

Example 4: refusing to let a culture metric become a target.
Leadership proposes tying a manager's bonus to their team's eNPS or cultural labor score, to make culture matter. My move is to refuse the coupling and explain why in the discipline's own terms: the moment a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure, because people optimize the proxy and it decouples from the culture it was standing in for, and the more a social indicator is used for high-stakes decisions the more it corrupts the process it monitors. So I keep the diagnostic reads off the incentive and target list, and where a number must be watched I triangulate it against behavior the manager cannot easily stage. The culture read stays a read, not a scoreboard. The novice error avoided is bonusing the barometer and then trusting the barometer.

Example 5: returning a falsifiability verdict to Belonging.
Belonging designed the why-before-how immersion and the cultural-steward mentor, and asks whether it worked. The novice move is to answer yes because eNPS is up and onboarding survey scores are high. My move is the falsifiability check. Before reading the data I named what would show it did not take: new hires who cannot articulate the why after the immersion, stewards chosen for skill rather than embodiment reproducing skill and not the standard, early exits clustering right after onboarding, and exit themes naming the same first-week failures. Then I read the four streams for convergence against that list. Where the evidence converges with the design's prediction, I say it took, with the disconfirmers I checked. Where a disconfirmer shows up, I return it to Belonging plainly as the thing to fix, and I do not soften it and I do not redesign it myself. I also flag what the data cannot yet show at this sample size, so the claim is not overstated. The novice error avoided is confirming the design by fiat instead of testing it, which is the one thing this seat exists to prevent.
