# Manager guide: telling a strong server their handoffs are slipping

## Check first (C1, R3)
If this server has not yet heard it, do not wait for the review. A slipping handoff is news the loop should have carried. Hold this conversation this week, in private, not mid-service. If they have heard it in pieces, this conversation is the return to it. The review then documents it; nothing in the review is new.

## Prepare
- Pick two or three dated, observable handoffs (what was missed, what the customer or the next server felt). No pattern talk without incidents (C3).
- Write down two real strengths of theirs, specific to the work. Not padding. They are the reason the conversation is worth having (C4).
- Decide the one behavior you are asking to change. One.

## Run it
1. **Say why you are here.** Their work matters to the floor, and you are telling them because you take it seriously.
2. **State where they stand, plainly.** Their section work is strong, and handoffs have dropped, with the dated examples. Say it early so coaching is not read as a hidden bad grade (A7: not softened to nothing).
3. **Separate the person from the behavior.** The handoff is the problem. They are not. Do not speak to character or motive ("careless," "coasting").
4. **Ask for their read.** Then listen. They may know something you do not (load that night, a changed section layout, a missing tool). If the cause is the process, the process gets fixed too, and the individual issue still stays on the record, dated (M6).
5. **One experiment.** Agree one observable change for the next block of shifts, with a check-in date. Name that it may feel worse before it improves (C10).
6. **Close.** Restate the one change and the date. Do not bring pay, hours, or a titled role into this conversation (R4, R9).

## If they shut down or change the subject (C5)
Stop correcting. Restore safety: slow down, name that this is about one behavior, offer to pause and return in a day. Do not push through, and do not retreat from the point. Felt-safety judgment belongs to tbri.

## Do not
- Open with a compliment and bury the message.
- Stay quiet to be kind (C4).
- Cite review notes, logs, or this talk as grounds for action (C12). It is a developmental record. Anything that might ever go to a discipline file is Brandon's decision alone, and the server sees it first (R1).

## After
Log the date and what was said in behavior-and-context terms, written so the server could read it and add their account. Check in on the date set. Note progress when you see it, the same day.
