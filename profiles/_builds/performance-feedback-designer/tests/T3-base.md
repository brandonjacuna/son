# T3: Manager guide, telling our best server their handoffs are slipping

## Before the conversation
1. Gather facts. List 2 to 4 specific recent handoffs: date, shift, what was missing (open table notes, allergy, check status, side work), what it cost (a customer repeat, a delay, a comp). If you cannot name examples, you are not ready.
2. Check your side. Were the handoff expectations written and taught? Was the shift unusually heavy? Has anything changed for them (schedule, section, personal)? Hold your read loosely until you hear theirs.
3. Know the goal: handoffs back at standard, and the server still feeling valued. Both matter.
4. Pick the time: private, before a shift with time to talk, not mid-service and not at close when they are tired. Ask earlier that day: "Can we sit for 15 minutes before shift? I want to talk about handoffs."
   Never leave them guessing overnight.

## Opening (about 60 seconds)
Start with the standard and the real reason, then the respect.
"You are one of the strongest servers we have, and that is why I am raising this directly. Over the last two weeks a few handoffs have gone out missing information. I want to walk through them and hear what you see."

Do not use the compliment as a cushion followed by "but." Say the positive once and mean it, then stay on topic.

## Core (situation, behavior, impact)
- Situation: "Saturday the 3rd, table 12 handoff at 6:40."
- Behavior: "The seat 2 allergy was not passed on."
- Impact: "The incoming server found out when the customer told them."
Give one or two examples, not a list of twelve. Then stop and ask: "What was happening on your end?"

## Listening
- Expect defensiveness, tears, or silence. Slow down. Say "That makes sense, this is a hard thing to hear." Do not argue about the example if they dispute one detail; note it and return to the pattern.
- Do not diagnose motives ("you seem checked out"). Describe what happened.
- If they raise a system issue (no time, unclear handoff tool), take it seriously and log it.

## Agree
- One concrete change: e.g. use the handoff card for every table, flag allergies verbally.
- A check-in date within a week or two, then confirm it was fixed.
- Ask what support they want from you.

## Close
"Thank you for hearing this. I raise it because I want you at your best here." Follow up that shift with specific positive notice when they get it right (within 24 hours).

## Avoid
- "Everyone has noticed," or comparing to other servers.
- Feedback in front of the team.
- Surprise, or saving it for the quarterly review.
- Treating personal sensitivity as the problem. The handoff is the topic.

## After
Write a short note of what was discussed and agreed, share it with them, and follow up. If the slip continues after support, use the normal coaching steps, not a bigger conversation.
