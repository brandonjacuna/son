# T2: LEA module, upward feedback step

Assumption: "LEA module" is the learning module in which team members give feedback to the people who lead them. I have not seen a spec for it.

**Verdict: do not make the whole step anonymous.** Anonymity is decided per channel (R7, A3). Anonymous everything teaches that honesty needs a mask and invites input to be funneled back to the manager it concerns. The goal, people feeling safe, is right; the design below reaches it differently.

## Design: two channels, one loop
1. **Default: signed, two-way.** Each cycle (feeds the three-to-six-month review, people practices 8) the team member writes upward feedback to their lead, and the lead writes back. The lead replies in writing to each item: what changes, what does not, and why. A lead who gets feedback is not told to react in any scripted way.
2. **Protected route: for early warnings and feedback the least powerful need to raise without exposure.** It never runs through the manager concerned. Read by an owner outside that manager's chain (Brandon until a general manager exists; Dominic when the matter concerns Brandon). The honest limit goes in the module text: on a small team a detail can identify the writer, so the route promises no more confidentiality than it can keep.
3. **Not this module:** harassment, wage, and safety reports go to the complaint channel (hr-systems-designer designs; hr-implementer runs intake). The module shows that route on screen and hands any report across (C-seam, records-and-routes 5).

## Launch conditions (C7, R7). Do not launch until real.
- Written non-retaliation, visible to everyone.
- A closing loop: every item gets a dated response, routed to the issue owner, and the module syncs to the execution system (people practices 13). Show "you said, we did" within a set window.
- Check after launch: compare signers' ratings and any carries before and after. A carry soon after someone gave upward feedback goes to counsel first (R1).
- Upward feedback is never filed to the discipline record and never cited as grounds for action (C12).
