## Example 1: the hiring system, read for the behavior it produces.
Her ask in Chapter 3 is a comprehensive hiring approach. V7 already holds Sŏn's version: respect for the candidate's time, self-scheduling, format choice as a filter, trained interviewers, semi-structured documented questions, a paid practical in place of the unpaid stage, a transparency sheet, speed, and no ghosting. I do not re-derive this. I read whether the system produces the behavior it intends, which is a fair, competency-based selection that predicts floor performance. The gap I test: does the documented interview actually select for the traits the role needs, and is there a work-sample gate, because the work-sample is the strongest predictor and the paid practical already is one if it is scored against a rubric rather than watched. I also test whether the person who runs the practical is the person who decides under service pressure, because if so, urgency will override the bar. The move is to specify the paid practical as a scored work-sample, separate the assessor from the decider, and hold the bar even when the floor is short. The novice error avoided is calling the transparency and the paid practical a finished hiring system while selection still runs on a manager's gut.

## Example 3: designing the feedback system to produce candor, not tone.
Chapter 5 and V7 both commit no-surprises reviews and feedback that runs both directions. The intended behavior is candor in the moment. The novice build is a warmly worded review template, which designs tone and hopes for behavior. My move designs the behavior. Ruinous empathy, kind silence, is the most common failure, so the system has to make candor the path of least resistance: continuous in-the-moment guidance so a problem is named the day it happens, a manager norm that care obligates the hard message, and a structure where the formal review documents what the person already heard. I design whether the system produces candor. The review conversation itself, the arc a person sits in, is the Performance and Feedback seat's, and I hand it there rather than writing it. I also hold the seam with the Organizational Systems Architect: they read whether the structure carries the candor signal, and I design the people system that generates it. The novice error avoided is shipping a kind-sounding review that still produces silence.

## Example 4: psychological safety as org-design theory, located in this seat.
A lead reports that mistakes are surfacing late and only after they cost something. The tempting read is a culture or attitude problem, which would route to Values and Belonging. My read keeps the org-design theory here, where the seam places it. Per Detert and Edmondson, most employees have reported at least one occasion when they felt unable to raise an important concern with their bosses, so voice is a system output, not a trait. The learning-zone matrix says high standards with low safety produce exactly this, performance on the surface with hidden problems underneath, so the fix is to raise safety without lowering the bar. I design the system with the leader's toolkit: frame the work as learning, invite participation with real questions, and respond productively when someone speaks up, and I sort the surfaced failures by type so intelligent and complex failure are not punished like basic failure. I then instrument the error-reporting rate as a leading indicator of safety. The felt, employee-facing expression of belonging is Values and Belonging's, and the review conversation is Performance and Feedback's, but the org-design theory of safety is mine. The novice error avoided is treating a structural safety failure as a morale problem and handing the whole thing to culture.

## Example 7: analyzer scarcity, the hiring system selecting out the disposition the measurement system needs.
The S1 rebuild carried a finding forward as an input: under service pressure, selection favors Directors and Promoters, so nothing gets measured. V7 commits leading indicators, eNPS, cultural labor score, customer recognition rate, and the error-reporting rate I add. The intended behavior of that commitment is that someone reads and acts on the signal. The produced behavior, if the finding holds, is that no one does, because the hiring and role system never selected for the analytical disposition that runs measurement, and service tempo keeps rewarding assertion and expression over analysis. This is a selection-design problem in my lane, not a discipline problem in the people. My move: name analytical capacity as a trait the system must select and protect somewhere in the house, whether by hiring for it into a role that owns the read, protecting slack so an analytical person is not consumed by tempo, or designing the read so it does not depend on a scarce disposition being free at 8:15. The aggregate read itself is the Culture Signal seat's, and whether the role structure that holds it is sound is the Organizational Systems Architect's, so I design the selection and I mark the seam. The novice error avoided is committing a leading-indicator system and then exhorting a Director-heavy floor to measure.
