I would not start with a workshop. Late-surfacing mistakes are almost always a system problem first, and a workshop on "accountability" can make it worse.

Why
- If people hide or delay mistakes, the usual cause is that reporting them is unsafe or pointless. A workshop on accountability tells them to own up more, while the same conditions remain. It can sound like blame.
- A workshop is a one-time event. Habits follow structure.

Find the cause first (a week or two)
1. Pick three recent mistakes. For each: who knew, when, why it was not raised, what happened to the person who raised it before.
2. Ask the team, one on one: "What makes it hard to say something went wrong?" Collect the answers without names.
3. Check the structure: is there a place and a time to report (pre-shift, end-of-day log)? Does anyone know what happens after they report? Do the managers react with questions or with blame?

Likely fixes, by cause
- Fear of blame: managers respond to a report with thanks and a question, never a penalty. Separate honest errors from repeated carelessness, and say which is which.
- No channel: a simple route, like a line in the end-of-day log and a verbal rule to tell the lead immediately.
- Unclear ownership: who fixes what, who decides, and who tells the customer.
- Slow information: short pre-shift huddles, a standard handoff.
- Manager habits: coach the managers first, because their reactions set the culture.

Then, if the review supports it
A short working session, not a lecture: use the real recent mistakes, walk through what happened, and agree on the rules and channel. Follow up in a month.
