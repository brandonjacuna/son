2. Points by days worked reward presence, not skill. The development system cannot raise someone's pay through skill if pay depends on days worked. If development has no link to comp, people will see it as unrelated to pay and it loses force. If it does link, the point rules need a skill or role factor.

What the comp model does to development
- Whole team on one point system: it flattens seniority and role difference. A new hire working the same days as a senior cook earns the same, so progression has no financial reward.
- More days means more points: people may pick up shifts when tired, and it penalizes illness, caregivers, and students. It may cause burnout and favor those who can work most.
- Revenue share varies with the month. Growth in skill does not show up reliably in it.

How I would build development on top of it
- Make development about skills, responsibility, and trust: roles they can hold, stations they can run, people they can train.
- Link advancement to an added point weight or a role multiplier, or to non-pay rewards (title, schedule choice, ownership of a project, paid training). This needs a decision from the founders.
- Keep a clear ladder per position: what is expected, how it is assessed, how often, and who decides.
- Use mentorship and cross-training so people see a path.
