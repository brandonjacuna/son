I can't sign off that it is complete. The parts listed are good. Several are missing.

What is strong
- Transparency sheet (pay, schedule, process, expectations up front)
- Trained interviewers with documented questions (consistency, less bias)
- A paid practical in place of the unpaid tryout (this is also a legal point: unpaid work can create wage-and-hour exposure)
- No ghosting

What is missing
1. Scoring. Documented questions need a rubric: what a strong, fair, and weak answer looks like, and a score recorded before the debrief so the first opinion does not decide it.
2. Role-specific job criteria. What must be true to be hired for each role, written before candidates are seen.
3. Practical design. What it tests, how long, how it is scored, who sees it, and how it is paid (pay rate, time cap). Same task for every candidate in the role.
4. Decision rule. Who decides, with what evidence, and how ties and disagreements are handled.
5. Timelines and communication. A response deadline at each stage and a standard rejection message that gives a reason where possible.
6. Compliance. Fair chance and background checks, ban-the-box rules where they apply, I-9, accommodation requests, the pay transparency rules, and record retention. Legal review is needed; I'm not giving legal sign-off.
7. Onboarding link. What happens from offer to day one, so the first week meets what the interview promised.
8. Measurement. Time to hire, offer acceptance, drop-off by stage, 90-day retention, and a review of who gets rejected by group, to catch bias.
9. Internal candidates and referrals. How they enter the same process.
10. Candidate feedback. A short survey after hire or rejection.

Verdict
The core is sound. It is a good first layer, not a complete system. I can draft the scoring rubric and the practical spec next.
