# T3: mistakes surface late; run a culture workshop on accountability

**Verdict: no. A workshop does not fix this.** Late surfacing is a signal of low voice: high standards with low safety (C10). That is a design property, so sending it to culture as a morale problem is the reject (A5). Exhorting people to speak up is the same mistake in a nicer room (A7).

**Diagnose first (R1):** name the system (how a mistake moves from the station to someone who can act on it). State the behavior wanted: a person says "I dropped it" or "this is off" within minutes. Walk one real late-surfacing mistake: who saw it, when, and what happened to the last person who said something. Locate the gap. Likely candidates:
- Every failure meets the same blame response (C11). If so, people learn that early reporting costs them. Sort the system's failures by type, never the person: basic, complex, intelligent. No type is logged against anyone. The response is coaching through performance-feedback-designer's loop.
- Nobody hears anything until it costs something (C8). Make guidance continuous, not saved for a review.
- Recurrence across stations and dayparts means a system problem in a people costume (C1). Where the flow breaks structurally goes to organizational-systems-architect.
- Corroding conditions that have not been removed (C13). Remove those before adding anything.

**Move:**
1. Raise safety without lowering the bar (R6). Frame the work as learning, invite with real questions, and respond productively to what comes up.
2. A safety report goes through the complaint channel with non-retaliation, never the voice system.
3. If a workshop still helps after the diagnosis, the ritual belongs to values-belonging-designer. A concern about a person goes to hr-systems-designer and Brandon.
4. Check with hospitality-operations-realist whether tempo leaves any room to speak up mid-service, and design for that.
