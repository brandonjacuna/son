# T1: Does the pre-shift ritual survive the floor?

Short answer: probably not as written. Two parts hold up. One part will erode within weeks.

## What holds
- Reservations and dietaries. This is the content people actually need. It earns its time every shift.
- A fixed structure. Same order every day makes it easy to run and easy to notice when it is skipped.

## What breaks
1. **25 minutes is too long.** Pre-shift competes with side work, staff meal, and setup. Under pressure the first thing cut is the part that feels soft, which is the value. Lines that run 10 to 15 minutes survive. At 25, managers shorten it on busy days, and then it is unpredictable.
2. **The value read aloud by the manager is the weak point.** Reading a line off a card is the most common way a value turns into wallpaper. By week three the team hears it as noise. It is also one-way: the manager talks, nobody does anything.
3. **Opening with the value puts the least useful item first.** Late arrivals miss it, and the people who are present are waiting for the operational content.
4. **Dietaries at the end of a long meeting get lost.** Allergy information is safety-critical and should not sit behind a ritual.

## Fix
- Cap it at 10 to 12 minutes, standing, at the pass or floor.
- Run order: reservations and dietaries first (about 6 min), then the value (about 2 min), then one question to the room.
- Make the value concrete. Do not read it. The manager names one moment from the last shift where it showed up, or asks a server to. Specific beats recited.
- Rotate who gives it. Servers and cooks can carry it, not just the manager.
- Keep a one-line dietary readback so every server repeats the allergy tables they hold.

## Test before committing
Run it for two weeks. Track: start time, length, how many shifts it was skipped or cut, and ask three staff what the value was that week. If they cannot name it, the ritual is not working, whatever the attendance.

## Open questions
- Which shifts are busiest, and does the ritual run on them? That is the real test.
- Who runs it when the manager is pulled onto the floor?
