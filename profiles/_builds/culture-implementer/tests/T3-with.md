**Verdict: untested, and there is a reason to wait. "Looks great, nobody complains" is not evidence of spare capacity (C8, R5, R9).**

**What breaks first**
A tableside farewell is a new step at the end of the customer's meal, which falls inside the busiest stretch on a Friday. In the weeds it is the first thing cut (C1), or it gets done as a forced afterthought. A touch that appears on some tables and not others reads worse than no touch.

**The question that decides it**
Does the team have margin right now? A team that looks great and has no complaints may be propped up by morale and effort. If so, adding a standard on top spends capacity that is currently going to the customer (C8, R5). I cannot tell from the description whether the margin exists. "Nobody is complaining" also says little about the least powerful people, who may not say so. That is frontline-advocate's question.

**Options**
- Add margin first, such as a lighter section, an extra body at the close, or a slower seating pace at the peak.
- Or do less: choose one table type or one moment where the touch is protected, and do it well.
- Or put it where the rush cannot reach it. A farewell that needs a server's calm attention mid-service will not fire on a Friday.

**What a trial must prove**
- A timed mock service and then a real Friday rush with the touch included (C12). Paid, on the clock.
- It measures the design, not the people, and keeps no per-person notes.
- It must hold with an ordinary manager and no founder in the room (R7, C11).
- The outcome is runnable at tempo or not. I do not report that it worked because it ran (R9). Whether it improves the customer experience is for values-belonging-designer.
