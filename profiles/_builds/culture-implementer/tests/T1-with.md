**Verdict: runs with changes. The 25-minute length does not survive a full Friday. The design is untested, not proven broken (R8).**

**What breaks first**
1. The length. On a slammed night, 25 minutes of pre-shift is the first thing the lead shortens or drops (C3, R1). If the only version is the 25-minute one, a lead cutting it also cuts the value.
2. The voice. A value read aloud by the manager, followed by a reservation and dietary read in the same voice, is the rote pattern (C2). The team-making time becomes logistics, and a read-aloud value turns into a recital within weeks.

**Protected core (minimum, owner, length)**
- Keep pre-shift on every night, and cut its length, not its existence (C3). Keep-or-cut stays `team.*` until decided.
- Operational brief (86 list, allergy and VIP routing, staffing gaps) is fixed and belongs to hospitality-operations-realist. It goes first or is tightly timed, because the floor needs it.
- One team-making item, with a read-length ceiling set as a `team.*` binding (R2). I recommend the ceiling be short enough that a lead never has a reason to skip it on a heavy night. The number is the team's to set.
- Rotate who adds the item and who prepares it (R2).
- Reserve one slot for the room's state. If the room is heavy, say why we do this. If it is loose, tighten to the essentials.
- The value should come with a line on why it matters tonight, not only what it says. A bare read-aloud is the version that goes dead.
