# T2: Server keeps leaving the station unreset mid-service. Told twice. What do you do Friday?

Telling a third time will not work. Two verbal reminders that failed mean the problem is likely not awareness. Find out why before Friday, then change something.

## Before Friday (10 minutes, private, not during service)
Ask, do not accuse: "I have seen the station left unreset a few times mid-service. Walk me through what is happening when it does." Listen for the cause:
- **Too many tables / bad section.** They are underwater and the reset is the first thing dropped.
- **Unclear standard.** "Reset" means different things to different people. Does the server know exactly what a reset looks like and when it is due?
- **Missing tools or layout.** Supplies are far away, or the station is stocked badly.
- **Handoff gap.** Someone else is supposed to do it and nobody owns it.
- **Will.** They know, they can, and they choose not to.

## Friday
1. **Make the standard concrete.** Write down or show: what a reset includes, and at what point in service it happens. Have them say it back.
2. **Watch it yourself.** Be near the station during the first rush. Cue in the moment, quietly: "Station, before your next table." Real-time help beats a debrief.
3. **Remove obstacles** found in the conversation: adjust the section, restock, or assign a clear owner.
4. **Close the loop at end of shift.** One sentence on what went right, one on what to repeat tomorrow.

## Then
- If it improves, say so specifically.
- If the cause was will, and the standard is clear and the support is in place, the next step is a documented conversation with consequences stated. Keep it factual: what was expected, what happened, what happens next. Involve whoever owns HR process, since this touches an employee.

## What to avoid
- Correcting them in front of the team or customers.
- A third repeat of the same words.
- Fixing the symptom by resetting the station yourself every time without telling them. It hides the problem.
