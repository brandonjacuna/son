# T3: Team looks great, service is clean, no complaints. Add a tableside farewell touch next month. Any reason not to?

No hard reason not to. There are several reasons to check first. "Nobody is complaining" is weak evidence, since staff often stay quiet when things are going well on the surface or when they do not think complaints will change anything.

## Questions to answer before adding it
1. **What is the farewell for?** If it is a customer-experience goal, name the behavior and the effect you want. If it only looks good, it will be dropped.
2. **Does it fit the floor?** Check timing at the end of a table's meal. That is when servers are resetting, running checks, and seating the next party. A farewell adds seconds per table across a night. Estimate the total on a full Friday.
3. **Who carries it?** It lands on servers. Ask them, not just leadership. They may have the best read on when it feels warm and when it feels forced.
4. **Is the team actually fine?** Do two or three short one-on-ones. Ask what is hard right now, and what they would drop. Absence of complaints is not the same as capacity.
5. **Does it fit what Son is?** A Korean restaurant has its own hospitality language. Do not borrow a generic gesture. Confirm any cultural element against the brand canon in memory/decisions.md (2026-10-07, brand) before it goes into practice.
6. **Scripted vs. natural.** A mandated phrase or gesture delivered identically at every table reads as scripted. Define the intent and a few options, not a line.

## If you go ahead
- Pilot for two weeks on part of the floor or on certain shifts.
- Tell the team why, and what it replaces, if anything. Do not add it on top of existing tasks without removing something.
- Measure: time added per table, server comments, any customer mention.
- Set a review date, and be ready to drop it.

## Bottom line
Reasonable idea. The risk is adding load to a team that is performing well, and trading a working rhythm for a gesture. Ask the team first, then pilot.
