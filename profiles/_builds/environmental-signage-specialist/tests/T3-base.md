Not as written. Restroom signs are the most regulated signs in the building.

Problems
1. Pictogram alone is not enough. Accessibility rules (ADA and Texas Accessibility Standards) require permanent room signs to carry raised characters and Grade 2 Braille, in addition to the pictogram, on a non-glare background. A pictogram in a wordmark font fails this.
2. Wordmark font: a display or condensed brand font is not allowed for tactile text. Tactile characters need a sans serif or simple serif with regular proportions and stroke, not condensed, not decorative. Use the brand wordmark elsewhere, and a compliant font for the tactile text.
3. Mounting "where it looks balanced" is the wrong rule. Typical requirements: on the latch side of the door, with the baseline of the lowest tactile character about 48 inches minimum and the top of the highest about 60 inches maximum above the finished floor, with a clear floor space of 18 by 18 inches beyond the door swing. Verify against the current TAS and the City of Austin review.
4. Door-mounted signs are not allowed where a latch-side wall exists. Verify the exact condition for each door.
5. Gender: if the restrooms are all-gender or single-user, the Texas rules for sign wording and pictogram need checking. The accessible symbol is required on accessible restrooms.
6. Contrast and finish: matte, with strong contrast between characters and background.

What I would deliver
A restroom sign spec: pictogram, raised text, Braille, dimensions, finish, font, mounting height and location for each door. Brand treatment through color, material, and shape, not through the font.
