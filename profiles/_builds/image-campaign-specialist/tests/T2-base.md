# T2: Shoot brief, team plating on the line, for the website team page

## Purpose
Show who works at Sŏn, doing the work, so a visitor (customer, applicant, investor) sees real people and real craft. Not a staff directory photo.

## Pre-conditions
- **Consent and releases.** Written model release for every person photographed; employees must be able to decline without consequence. Confirm usage scope (website, social, print, duration).
- **Dishes.** Only dishes that are on the menu, plated to the standard, using real vessels. No faked plates.
- **Who is on the page.** List names and titles from Brandon and Dominic, spelled correctly; leave the final selection to them.

## Look
- Natural, honest: the line as it is, tidy but not staged. Warm, low-contrast light from the kitchen; add a soft off-camera fill if needed. Avoid flat overhead fluorescents and avoid heavy stylization.
- Candid hands-and-focus frames mixed with a few composed portraits.
- Consistent crop and color so the page reads as a set.

## Shot list
1. Wide: the line mid-service, 3 or more people working.
2. Plating detail: hands placing the final element (close, shallow depth).
3. Portrait per person at station: waist-up, looking at work and, in a second frame, at camera.
4. Pairs: two cooks passing a plate.
5. The pass: finished plate, tickets, hands.
6. Optional: pre-service prep.
Plus portrait and landscape crops for each hero frame and mobile.

## Logistics
- Shoot before service or at a service-like prep, 2 to 3 hours.
- Clean station, uniforms ironed, hair restraints and gloves per food safety rules. Do not break food-safety practice for a shot (no bare-hand plating that would not happen in service).
- Safety: photographer stays out of traffic paths; no flash into hot oil or open flame areas.
- Shot list on a card; a person from Sŏn directs the pace.

## Deliverables
30 to 40 edited selects, web sizes with alt text, raw files archived, a rights sheet with names and releases.

## Do not
Use stock, staged "chef pose" arms-crossed portraits, or props for Korean effect. No em dashes or "guest" in captions; say "customer."

## Questions
Date, budget, photographer, who appears.
