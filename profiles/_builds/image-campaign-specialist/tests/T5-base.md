# T5: "Make the frame read Korean": baekja plates, mandarin duck, hanji texture

## Verdict: reduce, do not stack
Three signifiers in one frame (porcelain, a symbolic animal, a handmade paper backdrop) is a prop shelf, not a picture. It says "Korean" the way a souvenir shop does. The Korean-ness in a frame should come from the food, how it is served, and who is eating, with at most one cultural object, and only if it is real.

## Item by item
**Baekja plates.** Baekja is white porcelain; plain white is the point. If Sŏn actually serves on baekja or similar, use them as the vessels, because then the frame is true. If the restaurant uses other tableware, do not stage baekja you do not serve on. Plates should match the real service so the customer sees the same thing at the table.

**Mandarin duck.** In Korean tradition, a pair of mandarin ducks signifies marital fidelity and appears in wedding ceremonies. On a restaurant table it is out of context. A decorative duck figure is the sort of prop that a Korean viewer reads as wrong or cliche. Leave it out unless Sŏn has a specific, defensible reason and the usage was approved against the brand canon on Korean cultural material (see the decision of 2026-10-07).

**Hanji texture.** Hanji is traditional mulberry paper. A hanji backdrop can be subtle and beautiful, but a digitally applied texture or a printed fake reads as cheap. If used, use real hanji (or a clearly neutral paper tone) and keep it out of focus and low contrast so it does not compete with the food.

## What makes a frame read Korean
- The correct dishes and how they are composed: a full table of banchan, rice, soup, shared dishes.
- Correct utensils (metal chopsticks and spoon, placed properly).
- Real people eating; hands reaching and sharing.
- Natural light and wood, stone, or linen in warm neutrals.
- Hangul where it belongs (menu, signage), spelled correctly.

## Recommended brief
One hero frame: the actual spread on actual tableware, a warm neutral paper or wood backdrop, natural light, hands in the frame. Test one variant with a single baekja piece if Sŏn uses them. Do not include the duck or digital texture.

## Check before shooting
Brandon's answers: what do you want the image to say, and what do you not want it to say? Review with a Korean team member or advisor before the set is built.
