# T1: Midjourney hero or stock Korean table, to skip the shoot

**Recommendation: do neither for the opening campaign hero. Shoot it.** The opening hero is the first impression and the one image every other asset borrows from. Both shortcuts carry specific problems.

## Why not Midjourney
- **It shows food that does not exist.** A hero implying a dish Sŏn does not serve is misleading advertising (FTC and Texas deceptive-trade rules apply to ads that misrepresent the product). Customers arrive expecting what they saw.
- **Korean food is where generators fail.** Plates, banchan, utensils, and table settings often come out wrong (chopsticks and spoon placement, impossible vessels, garbled Hangul). A Korean audience will notice immediately, and so will food press.
- **Rights are unsettled.** U.S. copyright does not protect purely AI-generated images, so Sŏn could not own or defend the hero; platform terms and training-data claims add risk.
- **No people, no place.** It cannot show the real room, kitchen, or team, which is what a new restaurant has to sell.
- Disclosure: if used at all, AI imagery should be labeled and never used to depict the food or space.

## Why not stock
- Generic "Korean table" stock is usually staged by non-Korean photographers: wrong dishes, wrong props, a pan-Asian look. It makes Sŏn look like every other restaurant and undercuts authenticity.
- License is non-exclusive; competitors can use the same frame.
- It is not Sŏn's food, so the same misrepresentation issue applies.

## What to do instead (lean options)
1. **Minimal shoot:** half a day, one photographer, 6 to 8 real dishes, hands plating, and the room. Brief it to a shot list (hero, detail, process, space). Cost is much lower than people fear and every campaign asset comes from it.
2. **If a shoot is not possible yet:** use a type-led or color-led hero with no food image, and hold the photographic hero until real dishes exist.
3. **Use the film if one exists** and pull stills.

## Rules for any image
Real food, real vessels, correctly set table; alt text; credits and usage rights in writing; model releases for any identifiable person; no Korean cultural props as decoration.
