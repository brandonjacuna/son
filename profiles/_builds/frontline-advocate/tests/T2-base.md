# T2: LEA module, development review, worker's side

"Our bench needs it" is the company's reason. A cook hears it differently, so the module has to work for the cook too, or it will produce polished answers that say nothing.

## What the cook will wonder, in order
1. **Is this safe to answer honestly?** "Where do you see yourself" can be a trap. A cook who says "running my own place" or "somewhere with better hours" may fear being cut from schedules or passed over. A cook who says "sous" may fear being marked as ambitious by a sous who feels threatened.
2. **Where does my answer go?** Who reads it, who keeps it, does it enter the performance file.
3. **Is the path real?** A mapped path to sous with no open sous role, no pay step, and no date is a promise that is not one. When it fails, trust in the whole review process goes with it.
4. **Is this for everyone or only the cooks you like?** "Each strong cook" means someone decides who is strong. Selection that is unwritten reads as favoritism, and in a kitchen it can become a discrimination issue.
5. **Is it on the clock?** A review and skill-building on days off, unpaid, shuts out cooks with second jobs or children.

## Fixes
- Ask two questions: what do you want, and what do you not want. Some cooks want to stay on a station and be paid well for mastery. That is a valid answer, not a gap to close.
- Offer more than one path: sous, a station specialist or trainer step with a raise, prep or pastry lead, or cross-training in front of house. Not every strong cook wants the sous seat.
- Write the criteria for sous: skills, tenure if any, what is assessed, by whom. Publish them to all cooks, not only those reviewed.
- State the pay at each step before the conversation. A path with no number is a hint.
- Say what the company commits to (training hours, paid, a date to revisit) and what it does not (a job). Do not imply a position that is not open.
- Keep answers confidential to the sous and chef plus the cook, and separate from discipline and performance scoring. Say so in writing.
- Let the cook decline without cost. The review is on paid time.
- Run it in the cook's strongest language, with an interpreter if needed.

## Risk to the bench
If three cooks are mapped to one sous opening, two are disappointed. Tell all three at the start, with the timeline.

## Decision for Brandon
Whether the module is opt-in, and what pay step exists below sous. Both are founder calls and touch employees (harsh red team).
