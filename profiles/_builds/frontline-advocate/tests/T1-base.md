# T1: Pre-shift onboarding, read for the team

Read from the new hire's side. The design is mostly good. Four things need fixing before it ships.

## What works
- A mentor on day one removes the worst part of a first shift: not knowing who to ask.
- Uniform fitting and locker setup before the shift mean the new hire starts on the floor, not in a back hallway.

## Problems

1. **Unpaid time.** A fitting, a locker setup, and a required handbook signing are employer-required activities. Under the federal wage law (FLSA) required pre-shift tasks are generally paid time. If the new hire is not yet on payroll, that is still a risk. Fix: pay a set block (for example one hour) for the fitting and the handbook, or move both into a paid first-shift start. Confirm with counsel or the payroll provider before launch.

2. **Weekend before day one.** Asking someone to sign online on a weekend is a request for unpaid personal time, and a person who has just left another job may be working that weekend. Fix: give a window of several days, no specific time, and say plainly that it is paid.

3. **Signing the handbook.** A signature on a document the person has not had time to read is weak for the company and unfair to the worker. Check what the signature covers. A receipt acknowledgment is ordinary. An arbitration clause, a waiver, or an agreement that changes pay terms should be a separate document, in plain language, with time to ask questions. Offer it in the languages the team speaks. Send it with a named person to call, and answer questions before the signature, not after.

4. **Uniform.** State who pays, who launders, and what happens at separation. A cost that pulls a worker's pay under minimum wage, or a deposit held from the first check, is a problem. Fix: Sŏn supplies it at no cost, and says so in writing. Fit for body type and religious or medical needs, not a size chart only.

## Mentor
- Give the mentor protected, paid time. A mentor carrying a full station plus a trainee trains badly, and the trainee gets blamed.
- Keep the mentor separate from the evaluator. A new hire will not ask honest questions of the person who writes their 30-day review.
- Give the new hire a way to change mentors without it counting against them.

## Ask of the founders
Decide whether pre-shift time is paid and how much. This is a cost decision. Legal review on the handbook signature is warranted, since it touches employees (harsh red team).
