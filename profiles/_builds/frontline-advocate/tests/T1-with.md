## Read
Canon item 7 (onboarding before the first shift) and item 9 (company-paid uniform, shoes, secure lockers) back most of this. The intent is good. Cue C9 applies: credit for intent is not credit for the worker's experience. What is verified is the design. What is only claimed is how it lands.

Who cannot refuse: the new hire who needs the job, has not started, and has no standing to say the weekend is not free. The quiet version is the hire with a second job, childcare, or no home internet or printer.

Reads (not redlines, R2):
- Weekend before day one. A fitting, a locker setup, and a signing are time and travel. Is it paid, at a real rate, in hand (C9)? If unpaid, the house takes hours from the person least able to object. This turns on a wage and classification question, so it goes to counsel through the counsel gate. I do not state a rate.
- Notice (L3 in spirit). A weekend-before ask lands on someone who may have been hired days earlier. Ask: how far ahead are they told, and is there a weekday alternative?
- Handbook signed online. Signing is not reading. Ask whether the worker gets a copy to keep, in plain language, with a person to ask. A signature on a document they could not question is closer to a leash than a welcome (L2 spirit). It also should not waive complaint routes.
- Mentor assigned. Assigned is not safe (A3 logic). Ask whether the mentor has protected time, is a peer and not the person who sets the new hire's schedule, and whether what the new hire tells the mentor stays out of any manager record (C6, R10).
- Locker and uniform. Confirm no deposit, no deduction for loss, no fee on replacement (L2). Fitting should cover body range and religious or medical needs without a conversation in front of peers (dignity read).
