# T3: Answering top servers on point-weighted distribution

No figures here. Any dollar number must come from the current Investor Review workbook in Box, not from this note. I have not seen the point table or the pool size, so the answers below are the structure of the argument, not the result.

## First, concede what is true
The top servers may be right about some of it. Under any pooled model, a high producer gives up some of what an individual tip would have paid, and others receive some of it. Saying "that is not true" loses the room. Saying "yes, and here is what you get in exchange" is the answer, if the exchange is real.

## What to check before answering
Run the numbers on the actual top servers. Compare their projected take under points with what comparable Austin servers earn on tips at similar volume. If they come out lower, say so and decide whether the point weights, a floor, or a premium for top performance should change. If they come out equal or higher, show it. An answer that rests on "trust us" is performing conviction.

## Answers that hold up
1. **Who produces the sales.** A server's check depends on the kitchen, the bar, the expo, the host, the dishwasher. Points that pay those roles distribute credit for results already shared.
2. **Menu price carries the wage.** Under the menu-price model, the customer pays for service in the price. A server's income stops depending on table assignment, section, shift, weather, and customer mood. Ask the top servers what share of their tip income today comes from luck of section and day.
3. **Points are the way to reward the top.** If the weights give servers more points for tenure, certification, volume, or reviews, then top servers are paid more by design, not by accident. Show the weights.
4. **Risk goes down.** Slow nights and winter pay the same pool. Top servers carry less downside.
5. **Retention.** Turnover in the kitchen and on the floor costs the whole team. Stable pay is part of how the top servers keep working with trained people.

## What not to say
- Do not call the top servers selfish.
- Do not promise they will earn more unless the workbook shows it.
- Do not present the model as final if it is under review.
- Do not claim legal compliance. Whether the distribution counts as wages, tips, or a service charge, and how it is reported, is a question for counsel and payroll.

## Offer them
A written points table, a back-test on past months, a review date, and a named person to bring complaints to. If the top servers can see the formula and test it, most of the argument is over.

## Decision for Brandon and Dominic
If the back-test shows top servers losing, decide whether to adjust weights or accept turnover among them. That is a founder call.
