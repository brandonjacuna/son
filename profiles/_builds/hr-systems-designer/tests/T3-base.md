# The Sŏn Way (enforceable version)

Your draft names three values. Values are hard to enforce as feelings, so each is rewritten as behavior a manager can observe, document, and coach. Warmth and jeong are not disciplined as attitudes. Conduct is.

## Respect
Standard: you treat every customer and teammate with courtesy.
Required behaviors:
- No slurs, insults, shouting, or demeaning jokes, including about race, sex, religion, national origin, age, disability, or sexual orientation.
- No harassment, threats, or physical aggression.
- You listen when a teammate gives work instructions and raise disagreement privately, not in front of customers.
- You do not discriminate in how you serve any customer.

Violations go through Corrective Action. Harassment, threats, and violence can lead to immediate termination.

## Warmth toward every customer
Standard: every customer is greeted and treated as welcome.
Required behaviors:
- Greet within [X seconds, DECIDE] of seating or arrival, with eye contact where possible.
- Use the customer's name when known. Say thank you and goodbye.
- Handle a complaint by listening, fixing or escalating it to a manager, and never arguing or blaming.
- Do not hurry, ignore, or treat a customer differently because of who they are or how much they might spend.

Warmth is coached through observation and shift feedback. A pattern of failing these behaviors, documented with dates, starts Corrective Action.

## Jeong between teammates
Jeong is the bond of care and loyalty that builds between people who work together. At Sŏn it appears in these behaviors:
- You help a teammate who is behind when your own station is clear.
- You share information needed to do the job, and you pass on tasks and handoffs completely.
- You tell a teammate directly and kindly when something is wrong, and raise it with a manager if that does not work.
- You cover for no one's misconduct, and you do not gossip about teammates.

You are not required to be friends, share personal information, or take part in social events outside work. Refusing to help, hoarding information, and undermining teammates are conduct issues and are handled like any other.
