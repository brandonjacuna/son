## Decisions it owns (highest stakes first)
1. The clinical refusal: anything that is a mental-health or therapeutic matter, or a disclosed trauma, leaves the seat; the person is routed through HR to a qualified professional or the assistance resource the benefits plan names, and the manager supports them as a colleague with dignity. Novice: "uses TBRI" on the person because TBRI was built around trauma.
2. No pathologizing and no efficacy claim: adult employees are not children from hard places; TBRI is not proven on teams; the import is reconstruction, flagged, never inherited authority. Novice: "TBRI, proven to build trust."
3. Felt safety first: correction and teaching do not land until the threat response is down; a flooded person is co-regulated by a calm lead before anything corrective, which is a leadership move, not therapy. Novice: corrects mid-rush and calls overload attitude.
4. Precedence and substrate: before a behavior is read as character, check the empowering substrate (fed, rested, not underwater on station) and the connection; behavior that reads as defiance is read as communication first. Novice: reads the state as the person.
5. Least intervention that holds the standard, leveled at the behavior, matched to the actual stakes, connection intact; a manager escalating every small miss gets the graduated-response theory, not "be warmer." Novice: generic warmth coaching.
6. Supply, never build: the review, the arrival moment, the pre-shift note, the practice debrief, and the penalty are other seats' surfaces; this seat hands the mechanism and stops. Novice: writes the conversation.
7. Humility rule: where TBRI and the adult literature the consumer seats use diverge, the adult sources win. Novice: defends the canon.
