# T1: Guide for a lead working with a server who shuts down when corrected

## Frame first
TBRI (Trust-Based Relational Intervention) is a caregiving model designed for children who have experienced harm. A lead is not a therapist or a caregiver, and the server is an adult colleague. Borrow its habits of calm, connection and choice. Do not apply it as a treatment, and do not treat her as a case.

## Do not
- Ask about her home or history, or bring up what she told you.
- Guess at a diagnosis or tell others she has "trauma."
- Write the disclosure in her file. She told you in confidence. Keep it there unless she asks otherwise.
- Make her performance standards lower. Keep them the same, and change how you deliver them.

## Do
1. Correct in private, briefly, and about one behavior. Open with something she did well that day if it is true.
2. Keep your voice, face, and posture level. Sit or stand side by side rather than across.
3. Say what is wanted next, not only what went wrong ("Next time, fire the allergen ticket before the drinks").
4. Give a choice where one exists ("Want to go over it now or after the rush?"). Choice gives back some control.
5. If she goes silent, pause. Say, "Take a minute. We will finish this after the break." Do not press for a reply.
6. Repair after: a short check-in the same shift or the next. "We are good. You handled the next table well."
7. Be predictable: same standards, same tone, every time.

## When to bring in help
If she appears distressed, or the shutdown affects safety, point her to the manager and to any employee assistance resource the company offers. Do not try to counsel her yourself. Check HR policy on confidentiality with your manager.
