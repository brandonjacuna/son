# T2: Relational layer for the 3-to-6-month review

## Scope note
TBRI was built for children with caregivers. For an adult employee review, use three of its habits (connect, empower, correct) as conversation design, not as a clinical lens. The reviewer does not assume or ask about anyone's history. Performance standards and ratings stay with Performance and Feedback; this layer covers how the conversation runs.

## Before the review
- Send the format ahead of time: purpose, length, what will be covered, no surprises.
- Ask the employee to bring two things: what is going well, and one thing they want help with.
- The reviewer checks their own state and does not do the review in the middle of a rush.

## During
1. Connect (first 5 minutes): ask about work, not life. "What has felt good lately?" Listen without fixing.
2. Evidence, not impressions: use specific moments from the last months, positive first and in proportion.
3. Empower: ask before telling. "How do you read that shift?" The employee proposes next steps before the reviewer does.
4. Correct: name the behavior and the standard, give the reason, and say what to do next time. Keep voice level. Allow a pause if the person goes quiet.
5. Choices: when options exist (schedule, training focus, station), offer them.
6. Close with the agreed next steps, who supports, and a date to check in.

## After
- Short follow-up within a week.
- Notes record behaviors and agreements only. No speculation about causes or personal circumstances.

## Reviewer guardrails
Same structure for everyone. No questions about home or history. Escalate welfare concerns to the manager rather than handling them in the review.
