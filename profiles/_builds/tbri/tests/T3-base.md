# T3: Manager handbook line on TBRI

I would not publish the line as written. Three problems.

1. "Proven." TBRI has research support from work with children in foster and adoptive care, much of it small studies and some by the program's own developers. It has not been tested on employee correction in restaurants. "Proven to build trust" overstates the evidence and, in a handbook, can become a promise managers are held to.
2. "Trauma-informed" in an employer context. It implies leads should assume that employees carry trauma. Leads are not trained or licensed to identify it, and the assumption can lead to treating people differently on a guess. It also touches employee privacy and potentially disability law. Have counsel review, since this is an employee-facing policy.
3. "Every correction with that lens" is a large, unmeasurable duty. Leads will not be able to meet it, and it could be read as a standard in a dispute.

## Replacement line
> Our correction approach borrows principles from Trust-Based Relational Intervention (TBRI), a model developed for working with children: stay calm, correct in private, be specific about the behavior and what to do next, offer choices where they exist, and repair the relationship afterward. Leads apply these to every employee in the same way. Leads do not ask about or assume anything about an employee's personal history.
