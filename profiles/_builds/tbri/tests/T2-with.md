**Verdict: supply the mechanism. The review arc, template, and wording are yours (C11).**

**Relational layer for the 3-to-6-month review**
1. Felt safety before the hard message (R2). A person braced for a verdict cannot take in a correction. Open by restoring settledness: a private room, no surprises, the content already shared in the ongoing loop. Being safe is not feeling safe (C2).
2. Connect, then correct (A2). Start with what the person did well and what they asked for, then the gap. A hard message with no relationship under it lands as attack.
3. Level every point at observable behavior, never the person (R4, C8). "Three tickets this week went out unchecked," not "careless."
4. Read behavior as communication before character (R3, C5). Before rating a miss, check substrate: breaks, meals, station load, chaotic handoffs (C4). Part of a gap may belong to the system, and the review should say so.
5. Calm delivery, so the person can stay in the conversation. If they withdraw or defend, pause and reconnect, then continue (C3).
6. Least intervention that holds the standard (R5). Most gaps get a coaching step, not a formal one. Heavier steps are support, not punishment.

**Limits**
- The review is developmental. It creates no discipline record and does not route to that file (R10). The log notes that the conversation happened and the agreed next step, never its content, and never health, family, leave, or trauma facts.
- Do not ask about the person's past or use clinical language (R9).
- Manager-facing text carries these practices in Sŏn's words, with no framework name and no "proven" claim (R7, R8).
- A safety, harassment, or legal-duty matter is stated at once, not held for rapport.
- A review following a complaint, leave, injury, or safety report goes to counsel first.

**Handoffs**
- hr-systems-designer: any consequence attached to a rating.
- assessment-competency-designer: validity of a not-yet call.
- learner-advocate or frontline-advocate: final read on the receiving end.
