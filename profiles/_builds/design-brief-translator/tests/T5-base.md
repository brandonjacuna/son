# T5: "Make the whole site feel more Korean"

## Problem with the request
"More Korean" is not a brief. It can be satisfied by decoration (patterns, motifs, brush fonts), which tends to read as costume and can be disrespectful to the culture the restaurant is built on. Before briefing, define what "Korean" means for Sŏn and what the site should do differently.

## Questions before design (Brandon)
1. What do you want: what on the site feels not Korean to you today?
2. What do you not want: motifs stuck on, a theme-park look, stereotype, or Korean text used as decoration?
3. Is there a brand canon line on Korean cultural material? Check the project decisions (brand decision, 2026-10-07) and follow it.
4. Who is the reader: neighbors, food press, investors? Each reads "Korean" differently.

## Where Korean-ness can actually live (ranked by authenticity)
1. **Content:** correct dish names (Hangul plus romanization used consistently), the stories behind dishes, ingredients, and techniques, named producers. Highest value, lowest risk.
2. **Voice:** specific and plain; explain terms once.
3. **Photography:** real food, real vessels, real people from the restaurant.
4. **Material cues, used sparingly:** color drawn from real objects (earthenware, ceramics), and only as tokens in the design system.
5. **Typography:** proper Hangul type pairing with the Latin face, tested for weight and line height.
6. **Motifs and patterns:** last resort. Decorative use needs a reason, or leave it out.

## Brief (draft)
**Outcome:** The site communicates Sŏn's Korean identity through content, language, and image first, with design cues restrained.
**Deliverables:** an audit of every page (what reads Korean now, what does not), a content plan, a Hangul typography spec, and a short list of optional visual cues for approval.
**Not in scope:** new logo, pattern wallpapers, stock "Asian" imagery.
**Accessibility:** language attributes on Korean text, readable sizes.
**Next step:** answers to questions 1 to 3, then the page audit.
