## Why
- The pages are one veteran's self-description (C7, A1). Experts describe gestures and leave out the diagnosis and anticipation that make the read work. A novice who learns her gestures learns the motions without the read behind them.
- One voice offered as the standard for every judgment (C8). Whose read sets the Sŏn standard is Brandon's call, and her read has to be sourced judgment by judgment, with other voices kept visible.
- No incident-based elicitation stands behind any cue (R1). The elicitation folder does not exist yet; there are no transcripts.
- Reading a table is perceptual. Text never makes a learner competent at perception (R2, C13). Even after elicitation, the module is the smallest part: the transmission is trailing with the expert speaking aloud, plus classification practice.

## What the pages are good for
They are a source of probes. Each sign she names becomes a question in her elicitation round: what it looks like on the floor, a time it meant something else, when she would have done otherwise (skill Stage C). Mark every row "probe" until a transcript backs it.
