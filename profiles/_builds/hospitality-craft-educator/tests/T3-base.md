# T3: Warmth module

**Recommendation on the two ideas:**
- **Required greeting lines:** I would not script them word for word. Scripts tend to sound recited, which reads as colder, not warmer. Better: give two or three example openings and one rule (greet in your own words, within the first minute, eyes up).
- **"Smile more":** Leave it out of written training. It targets appearance instead of behavior, it lands badly with staff (especially women), and it can create an HR and fairness problem when it gets used in reviews. Train behaviors you can observe and coach instead.

Before building, confirm the diagnosis: who says servers come across cold (customer reviews, managers, the founders), and in which moments. Sŏn has not opened yet, so this may be a prior team. Cold can come from understaffing, rushed sections, or not knowing the menu, and none of those is fixed by a warmth module.

## Module outline (about 40 minutes plus floor practice)
**Outcome:** A server makes each customer feel noticed in the first minute and at every touch.

1. **What warmth looks like here (5 min).** Defined as behaviors: eye contact on approach, using the table's cues, unhurried body, remembering one thing they said.
2. **Watch two versions (10 min).** Same greeting done flat and done warm. Trainees name the differences. Usually it is pace, eye contact, and stance, not words.
3. **Your own opening (10 min).** Each server drafts an opening that sounds like them. Practice in pairs, partner gives one note.
4. **Small moves (10 min).** Return a name if given, follow up on something they mentioned, explain a banchan or dish with genuine interest, a real goodbye.
5. **Floor practice (one shift).** Trainer watches three greetings and gives one specific note each.
6. **Check.** Observed sign-off on a short behavior checklist, not a rating of personality.

## Coaching language for managers
Specific and behavioral: "You were looking at the POS when they sat down; look up first." Not: "Be warmer" or "Smile."
