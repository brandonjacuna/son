# T2: Operations review of the SVC module (four-touch sequence plus tableside banchan refill)

I have not seen the module text. This review is of the design as described. Gaps are marked as questions.

## What works

- A fixed touch sequence gives new servers a clear frame and gives managers something to observe.
- Banchan refill is core to Korean table service and customers expect it.

## Operational concerns

1. **Labor math.** Four touches plus refills at every table, in a 120-seat room, is a set number of server minutes per table. Has the module been timed against the real section size on a full Friday? If a server has five or six tables, the sequence may not be physically deliverable at peak. Training a standard the floor cannot staff produces failure the server gets blamed for.
2. **Fixed sequence versus customer state.** A rigid four-touch script gets delivered to a table mid-conversation or in a rush. The module needs the rule for when to skip, delay, or compress a touch, and who decides.
3. **Banchan refill ownership.** Who owns refills: server, runner, or busser? Tableside refill by the server costs the most expensive labor on the cheapest task. Kitchen has to produce banchan volume on top of the line. Is there a banchan station, a par, and a waste budget? Refill is a cost-of-goods and labor decision, not only a service one.
4. **Allergen and safety.** Refilling shared dishes tableside needs a rule on allergens, clean-plate handling, and not returning served banchan to the kitchen.
5. **Coverage.** What happens to the sequence when the floor is short a person? The module should teach the degraded version, in priority order.
6. **Assessment.** What is the competency check? Observed on the floor with a checklist, signed off by whom, with what pass standard? A completed module is not a competent server.
7. **Language.** Use "customer," not "guest," throughout the module text.

## Recommendation

Do not release until: the sequence is timed on a real section; the degraded-staffing version is written; refill ownership, par, and waste are set; and a floor-observed sign-off is defined. Then pilot with one shift and revise from what servers actually could not do.
