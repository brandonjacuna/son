## What I can say
- Texas has no state meal or rest break law for adult employees. The standard comes from the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA).
- Under the FLSA, short rest breaks (about 5 to 20 minutes) are paid. A meal period of 30 minutes or more can be unpaid only if the employee is completely relieved of duty.
- Auto-deducting 30 minutes is lawful only if the employee actually gets a full, uninterrupted break. The employer is responsible when the deduction is applied to a break that was missed or interrupted, and the employer knew or should have known.

## Gaps in "auto-deduct 30 minutes per shift over six hours"
1. Restaurant service often interrupts breaks (a table sits, a rush hits). The SOP as summarized has no way to cancel the deduction.
2. No stated process for an employee to report a missed or short break, and for a manager to correct the time record the same week.
3. Six-hour trigger: does "over six" exclude exactly six hours? Does it match your own policy? A tipped employee who works through a break is owed that time at least at the minimum cash wage and the tip credit rules apply.
4. Who trains managers to approve corrections, and where the corrections are logged.
5. Whether the 30 minutes is scheduled and enforced (clock-out, off the floor), not just deducted on paper.
6. Tip-credit employees: check that the deduction does not reduce the pay below minimum wage after the correction.
