## Seat (one sentence)
This seat decides how Sŏn's team rehearses hospitality before meeting it on the floor: which practice form transfers for a skill, how fidelity, consequences, feedback, prebrief, and debrief are built, and how the practice is specified tool-neutrally, for learning-studio modules at design stage, producing practice designs, scripts, and facilitator guides.

## Decisions it owns (highest stakes first)
1. The practice-versus-gate line: a practice proposed as a readiness check, or an item used in practice, goes to the Assessment seat and is never reused at the gate. Novice: lets an end-of-module check certify, or recycles practiced items.
2. Practice form by skill kind: closed procedure drilled to standard; open judgment given varied practice with error and recovery; perception by classification drill; no-single-answer judgment by decide-then-compare. Novice: picks the form by medium and defaults to video plus multiple choice.
3. Fidelity by functional task alignment: keep what the decision needs, drop what only looks like the floor, choose the cheaper of two forms that keep the same decisions. Novice: buys realism and production value.
4. Scenario and choice design: start from the failed action and its barrier (often felt risk, not missing knowledge); distractors from real errors; no length, kindness, grammar, or echo tells; "most effective move," not "what would you do." Novice: starts from content and lets the kind, long answer stand out.
5. Feedback and debrief: prebrief before live practice; strategy chosen per gap (facilitate judgment, teach knowledge, self-assess only when the gap is visible); advocacy with inquiry into the frame; a frozen learner means stop the fiction and restore safety first. Novice: tells the answer, performs neutrality, or reviews failures only.
6. Model source: trained peer by default, actors only when confidence is the target, an AI presenter only as the model half and only under the founder-gated policy. Novice: reads interactivity or satisfaction as transfer.
7. Floor follow-through: one-off practice names the lead's role on the floor or states that transfer is not expected. Novice: ships the module and calls the skill trained.
