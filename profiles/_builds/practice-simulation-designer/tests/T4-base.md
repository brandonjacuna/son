# Debrief: role-play where the new server froze

For: the lead running the debrief. Use within 24 hours, one-on-one, in private. About 15 minutes.

## What I know and do not know
I know the server froze and went silent. I do not know which scenario ran, how long the silence lasted, who played the customer, or who else watched. Fill those in before you start. Do not guess them in the room.

## Before you talk
- Check the scenario. If it was a hard one (angry customer, allergy, wrong order), freezing is a normal response and says little about the person.
- Check the setup. Was the server watched by peers? Was the role-play cold, with no walkthrough first?
- Decide your one goal: the server leaves knowing what to do when they blank, and knowing the freeze did not cost them their place.

## Open (2 minutes)
Say it plainly: "Last night's role-play stopped partway. That happens to most people at some point. I want to go through it with you, and nothing here is a mark against you."

## Ask first, then listen (5 minutes)
Ask, one at a time, and wait through silence:
1. "What was going through your head when it stopped?"
2. "What would have helped in that moment?"
3. "What did you want to say that you could not find?"

Listen for the cause. Each needs a different fix:
- Did not know the content (menu, allergens, service steps): training gap.
- Knew it but panicked in front of people: pressure, not knowledge.
- Did not know what to do with a customer problem: no fallback phrase.

## Give one thing to do (5 minutes)
Teach a stall line the server can say when blank: "Let me check on that and come right back to you." This works with a real customer. Practice it out loud together, twice.

Then replay only the moment before the freeze, at low stakes. Just the two of you, same scene, shorter. Stop as soon as they get through it once.

## Close (2 minutes)
- Name one thing they did well in the scene, specific and true.
- Agree the next step and a date: a repeat role-play, a shadow shift, or more menu study, depending on the cause.
- Ask: "What do you want from me before the next one?"

## Avoid
- Calling it a failure, or comparing them to other new hires.
- Debriefing in front of the group.
- Listing everything that went wrong. One fix is enough.
- Filling the silence for them when you ask a question.

## After
Note the cause and the agreed next step in the training record. If the scene design caused the freeze (too hard, too early, too public), change the scene, not the person.
