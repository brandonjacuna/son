## What "right" means
A server gets it right when the customer with an allergy gets an accurate answer, the kitchen gets an accurate ticket, and the dish that arrives is the dish that was confirmed. Guessing is wrong even when the guess is correct.

Pass standard for every scenario: server never states an ingredient fact they have not checked, flags the ticket as an allergy, and uses the handoff to the kitchen lead.

## Part 1. Knowledge check (5 min, scored, 90% to pass)
1. Name the nine major allergens: milk, egg, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soy, sesame.
2. Which of these commonly hide in Korean cooking? Match each to a source: soy (doenjang, ganjang, gochujang), sesame (oil, seeds), wheat (soy sauce, gochujang, batter), fish (anchovy stock, fish sauce), shellfish (saeujeot, kimchi paste), egg (batter, gyeran). [MENU: confirm against the matrix.]
3. Allergy or preference? "I don't eat shellfish" gets handled as an allergy until the customer says otherwise.
4. True or false: if a dish arrives and you are unsure it is the safe one, you serve it and mention it. (False. Hold it.)

## Part 2. Scenario drills (role play in pairs, 3 rounds each)
Trainer plays customer. Server uses the script: Ask, Check, Confirm, Flag, Deliver.

- S1 Simple ask. Customer: "Is there sesame in the [MENU] rice bowl?" Correct: "Let me check with the kitchen," then check the matrix or lead. Wrong: answering from memory.
- S2 Severe allergy. Customer: "I have a peanut allergy, it's serious." Correct: ask what happens, flag the ticket as allergy, notify the kitchen lead, tell the customer who is handling it.
- S3 Hidden source. Customer avoids soy. Server must see that soy sauce, doenjang, and gochujang all count, and that most marinades are affected.
- S4 Shared surfaces. Customer: "Can you make it safe if you just leave it off?" Correct: ask the kitchen about cross-contact; do not promise. If the kitchen cannot make it safe, say so plainly and offer alternatives.
- S5 Pressure. Table is busy, customer says "it's fine, I'll risk it." Correct: still flag and confirm. Never waive the process for the customer.
- S6 Wrong dish at the pass. Runner brings a dish without the allergy marker. Correct: stop, hold the plate, return to the kitchen lead. Do not serve.
- S7 Table mixed order. Allergy customer shares plates with the table. Correct: tell the table which shared dishes are affected before ordering finishes.
- S8 Changed recipe. A special has a new ingredient not on the matrix. Correct: treat as unverified until the chef confirms.

## Part 3. Live assessment (observed, pass/fail)
Trainer runs two unscripted scenarios drawn from S2, S4, S6, S8. Server must hit every item:
- Asks what the allergen is and how severe.
- Checks a source, not memory.
- Flags the ticket and names the kitchen lead.
- Confirms the plan with the customer before the food is fired.
- Verifies the plate at delivery.
Any single "guessed an answer" or "served an unverified plate" is an automatic fail and a repeat of the drill.

## Part 4. Keeping it right
- Spot check on the floor once in the first 30 days: trainer observes one real allergy order.
- Re-run S6 and S8 at each menu change.
- Log near misses without blame; review monthly.
