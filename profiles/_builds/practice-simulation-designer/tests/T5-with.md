# Recovery practice: hire actors? (practice spec)

**Verdict (C12, R13, R5).** No, not by default. Run recovery practice with trained peers playing the customer, working from a short brief on the customer's frame. Hire actors only if the named target is confidence in front of an unfamiliar, upset stranger. Today the target is the recovery decision, and peers demand the same decisions at no cost. Actors are not ruled out on their face; they need a named reason.

**Skill kind (R2).** Open judgment, not a closed procedure. Varied scenes where errors can happen, with instruction on handling them.

**Decisions demanded.** (1) Read what kind of failure this is. (2) Act within the recovery the house authorizes. (3) Recognize when escalation is due. A peer customer forces all three. Actor skill adds look and feel, not a decision (A5, A1).

**Fidelity kept / dropped (R5).** Kept: the customer's frame and pressure, the table's pace, a consequence that continues after a weak move (R8). Dropped: professional range, costume, rehearsed lines (C3).

**Choices and scenes.** Wrong moves come from real errors elicited from Sŏn practitioners, as composites with no names, each with its pressure in the stem (C6, R7). Scripts set the situation and the customer's side only. The server's words and feeling stay their own (A2). If a move is wrong, play out the consequence, then make the learner recover forward (R8). Instruction asks for the most effective move, not "what would you do" (C7).

**Prebrief (R10).** What is practiced and why; the fiction agreement; anyone may stop or pass, and a pass costs nothing; nothing recorded or noted per person; a stated commitment to respect.

**Feedback and debrief (R11, R18).** End the fiction, ask what they saw, use advocacy with inquiry into why they read the failure as they did, close on one thing that went right. Judgment gaps get inquiry, not the answer (A6). Any trained member may run it, so the facilitator guide carries the debrief strategies and when each fits. A lead does not play the customer to a direct report. Freeze: stop the fiction, end their round, learner decides on debrief (R12; how safety is restored comes from tbri).

**Floor follow-through (R14).** Name the lead's role: a short after-action review within the shift week, plus a near-term chance to use the skill, as paid time. Follow-up is announced support, never an unannounced check.

**Not a gate (R1, A3, A4).** Practice scenes stay out of any readiness check and are never recorded per person. If someone wants a check, hand to assessment-competency-designer with a separate bank. Evidence is aggregate performance, not satisfaction (C13).

**Gates and bindings.**
- Recovery authority: `brand.*` or `team.*` binding, unset in canon. Brand Guidelines p.08 is reference only (R16). Founder-gated.
- Floor cues and wrong moves: elicitation-gated until hospitality-craft-educator runs (R15, A7).
- Voice review of scripts: materials-author-editor. Feedback landing: tbri.
- Live practice is not recorded by default; anyone on camera waits on HR consent.

2. Who sets what a server may offer when a failure happens?

**Trace (R19), one decision point.** Cue: elicitation-gated. Decision: read failure kind and choose a recovery within authority, with authority founder-gated. Choices: tell-free, composite errors, elicitation-gated. Feedback and debrief: landed. Gate separation: landed.
