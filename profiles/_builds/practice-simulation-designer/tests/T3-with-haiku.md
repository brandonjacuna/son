# Scope: table-mood practice (request: high-production AI video sim)

**Verdict.** Do not build the high-production AI video sim. A cheaper live form trains the same decisions: a short peer role-play from a one-page scene card, with the table state stated and the choice made in the room. Drop the video, the presenter, and the AI mood reader. (A1, R5)

**Skill kind.** Open judgment with a perception part. Reading the table is a seeing skill (R3 classification drill, C10). Choosing the move is open judgment (R2: varied practice, errors allowed).

**Decisions demanded.** (1) Read the table's state. (2) Choose the move. (3) Choose timing: act now or hold.

**Fidelity kept.** Stated table cues; the pressure behind each option (felt risk: elicitation-gated); the consequence, shown by continuing the story (R8).
**Fidelity dropped.** Video, faces, likeness, AI mood detection. Low fidelity beats no practice (M1, M3).

**Choices.** Three per item (R7), built from elicited real errors, equal in length and tone (C5). Stem asks for the most effective move, not "what would you do" (C7).

**Feedback and debrief.** Continue the story to the consequence; no mid-story verdict (A6, C8). Judgment gap, so advocacy with inquiry into the learner's frame (R11). Close on one thing that went right.

**Prebrief (R10).** What is practiced and why; the fiction agreement; anyone may stop or pass; nothing recorded or noted per person; a stated commitment to respect.

**Freeze (R12).** End the learner's round; learner chooses whether to debrief; no note. Check the prebrief ran. Safety: tbri.

**Model source.** Trained peers (R13, C12). Poor models are composites (C11).

**Floor follow-through.** Lead runs the after-action review (M6), then a near-term shift to use the move. Paid time: [to set].
