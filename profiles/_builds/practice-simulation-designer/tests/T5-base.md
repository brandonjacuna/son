# T5 (base, no seat): Hire actors to play difficult customers?

## Answer
Not yet. Start with staff and leads playing the customer, and add paid actors only if a specific gap shows up that this cannot close.

## Why
- Recovery skill is mostly a short sequence: listen without defending, name the problem, fix or offer a fix, check it landed. That sequence can be practiced with a scripted colleague. It does not need acting craft.
- Actors cost money before the first service (rate, rehearsal, scheduling, a director or lead to brief them) and their realism is only useful if the scenarios are right. Bad scenarios with good actors teach the wrong thing.
- Hired actors do not know Sŏn's menu, pricing, comp limits, or Korean dining context. A staff member playing the customer who knows these makes the exercise feel like the floor.
- Pressure from a stranger can shut down a new hire. Trainees need a safe first round before a realistic one.
- Trainee psychological safety and consent matter. Abusive scripted behavior toward staff is a risk, and the lead should set limits (no slurs, no touching, a stop word).

## What to do instead
1. Write 6 to 8 short scenarios from real incidents (slow ticket, wrong dish, allergy scare, a complaint about price, a loud table). One page each: customer's goal, what they say first, what a good recovery does, what it must not do.
2. Run them in pairs with a lead observing and a 4-item checklist. Rotate roles so everyone plays the customer once, which also builds empathy.
3. Record two or three runs on a phone, with consent, for debrief.
4. Review after the first cohort: where did trainees still freeze or default to excuses?

## When actors would be worth it
- Leads and managers handling escalations (refunds, health complaints, a customer who will not leave), where staff role-play is too gentle.
- A pre-opening dress rehearsal with outside people as customers, which is a different goal (testing service flow) and cheaper through friends and family.
- Only after scenarios exist and one internal round has shown the gap.

## Questions for the lead
- Which incident prompted this? If real incidents are not yet logged, start there.
- Budget and who briefs the actors?
- Is the aim confidence for new hires or escalation skill for leads? The answer changes the recommendation.
