# Practice spec: EX-001 Taking an allergy at the table

**Verdict.** Branching scenario for the judgment moment, peer role-play for the spoken routine. Cheapest forms that demand the decisions (R5). No video or AI practice; video is the model half only (R13).

**Skill kind.** Two. The three questions, repeat-back, flag, and kitchen confirm: closed procedure, drill to a standard (R2). Not minimizing under social pressure: open judgment, vary cases, teach recovery (R2, R8).

**Decisions demanded.** D1: ask all three questions when an allergy is minimized. D2: route before the order fires, whatever the severity. D3: on a miss, stop the dish, tell the escalation contact, return to the table before food lands.

**Fidelity.** Kept: the minimizing line, the friend's pressure, the busy pace, the three questions. Dropped: POS screens, real menu items, the Trainual interface.

**Choices (S1 fixes).**
- D1 option B is longest, warmest, most complete: the tell (C5). Equalize length and tone; drop the scripted phrase "not difficult at all" (A2).
- D2 option B ("watch the table") is a strawman (C6). Replace with an elicited real error (R15).
- 2B ends in failure with no path. Add a redemption path (R8).

**Feedback and debrief.** Decide-then-compare on D1. Advocacy with inquiry: ask what made "a little" sound safe. Close on the correct action (flag, confirm, no fire until both) and one thing that went right (R11).

**Prebrief (R10).** What and why; fiction agreement; anyone may stop or pass; nothing recorded per person; commitment to respect. Freeze (R12): stop the fiction, end that round, no health questions, no note.

**Facilitator (R18).** Any trained team member. A lead never plays the customer to a direct report.

**Floor follow-through (R14).** Lead asks the three questions at pre-shift; learner repeats back every allergy that shift. Paid time: founder-gated.
