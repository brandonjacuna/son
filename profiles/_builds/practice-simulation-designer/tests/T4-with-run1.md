# Debrief guide for the lead: the server who froze (R12, R10, R11, R18, R19)

## What this is
A guide for you, the lead, after a role-play where a new server froze and went silent. The fiction already ended last night. Do not rerun it, grade it, or make it a test (A3).

## Before you do anything
1. The server chooses whether and when to debrief (R12). Offer once, privately, with no deadline: "Last night's round is closed. If you want to talk it through, tell me when. If not, that is fine." Silence or no costs nothing.
2. Do not ask about health or what was wrong. Make no note about this person: not in a file, a review, a chat, or a handoff (R12, R10).
3. How you restore felt safety (tone, timing, what to say if they are shaken) belongs to tbri. Open item: tbri has not been run on this. Mark it **binding, not landed**.
4. You were not the customer in the round. Never play the customer to a direct report (R18). If they want to try again, a trained peer plays the customer and the server may pass on any round.

## If they say yes: the order (R11, Output)
1. **End the fiction.** "That was practice. You are not in the scene anymore."
2. **Ask what they saw.** "What do you remember noticing right before it stopped?" Listen. Do not fill the gap.
3. **Advocacy with inquiry.** Say what you noticed, then ask: "I saw you stop after the customer said X. I am curious what was going through your mind." Treat it as a judgment gap: find their frame, do not hand them the answer (A6).
   - If the gap is knowledge (they did not know what to say or who to get), teach it plainly, one item.
   - If a safety or legal item was in play (allergen, alcohol), state the correct action before you close (R11).
4. **Close on one thing that went right.** Name something specific and true from the round before the freeze (a greeting, a read of the table, asking for help). Do not invent one.

## What to ask yourself afterward (aggregate only, A3)
- Was the scenario asking for a decision the floor demands, or only for performance under watching? If the barrier was the audience, say so to the facilitator; that is a design fault, not the server's.
- Floor cues in that scenario: **elicitation-gated** (R15) until hospitality-craft-educator has run Sŏn's practitioners. If the scenario used an invented cue, flag it (A7).
- Follow-up the server knows about (R14): paid time for a short one-on-one practice with a peer, offered, not required, and a near-term chance to use the skill. Not an unannounced check.

## Gates
This round and the items in it stay out of any readiness gate (R1, A4). Freezing is not evidence of readiness in either direction.

## Open
Recovery authority (what a server may do when stuck mid-service) is **founder-gated** until Brandon sets a `brand.*` or `team.*` binding (R16). Do not tell the server a rule on this until it exists.
