# T3: HighScope claim for recruiting page and training intro

I won't write the line as asked. It makes three claims that I can't support as stated, and the recruiting page is public.

## Problems
- "Proven": plan-do-review is a HighScope classroom routine built for young children. The evidence behind HighScope is the Perry Preschool study and related work, which followed a small group (about 120 children) from the 1960s. It supports early-childhood programs. It does not test plan-do-review used with adult restaurant trainees.
- "Sixteen dollars back per dollar": that figure comes from cost-benefit estimates of Perry Preschool, which were driven by outcomes such as reduced crime and higher earnings. Published estimates differ by study and method (roughly single digits to high teens), and independent reanalyses give lower rates of return. The figure belongs to a preschool program, not to our school. Citing it on a recruiting page implies a return for a trainee or for Sŏn that nobody has measured.
- "That's why": the causal link is not honest. We use plan-do-review because it is a sound practice routine (set an intention, do the work, reflect), not because Perry's ROI transfers.

## Safe versions
Training intro:
> Our training uses a plan, do, review cycle. Before a task you say what you will do, you do it, then you look at what happened with your trainer. The routine comes from HighScope's early-childhood work, and we adapted it for the floor and the kitchen.

Recruiting page:
> You learn by doing, with a trainer who checks your work after every task.

If we want a number later, measure our own: time to floor gate, error rates, and retention for trainees.
