**Verdict: do not use the standard new-hire station module as is. It is pitched at a novice in everything. This learner is expert and novice at once (R1, C2, E1). Branch it inside one person.**

Design by element
- Opening: reason first, in terms of what garde manger changes for someone who already runs the floor (R11). No orientation to service, tickets, or pace.
- Stage branch:
  - Skip (held): service pace, standards, ticket flow. A gate or observed performance shows this; that read is assessment-competency-designer's. Re-teaching it wastes their time and risks expertise reversal.
  - Full support (new): knife work, station schema, mise en place, plating and cold-line specifics. Worked examples, heavy scaffolding, elements isolated before integrating (R3, C3).
- Unlearn step: a server's habits (timing from the pass side, how they read a ticket) may conflict with how the line reads it. Add an explicit contrast step (C9).
- Medium: expert demo narrated live to the hands, no talking head; expert judgment spoken aloud comes from hospitality-craft-educator. I invent no floor cues.
- Practice wrap: full plan-do-review on the new station work (R8 trigger: cross-training). Plan aloud: what, order, where the risk is. Do with real product at the real station, learner choosing. Review by reconstruction. Review is protected first (A7). Practice move itself goes to practice-simulation-designer.
- Fade: critical feature marked for two attempts, one prompt, then nothing. Job aid off after two clean unaided runs, tuned to the floor (R2).
- Retrieval and spacing: recall a shift later, return a week on, spread across shifts, knife and plating interleaved (R4, C5). Retrieval items are never gate items. Added paid shifts go to frontline-advocate.
- Measure: unaided station performance on the floor, with the best and worst cases read (C15).
- Bindings and owners: knife specs, portions, and station schema owned by the kitchen lead; I state none (R13).
