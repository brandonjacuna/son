# Review: two-page Sŏn story, neighborhood association handout

Basis: the description only. The file was not provided, so I could not check the copy, the photo, or the type.

## Overall
The layout is tidy and consistent, but nothing leads. A reader at a meeting gets the page for a few seconds, and right now the page does not tell them where to look first. Three problems drive that.

## Main issues
1. **Headline and lead photo have the same weight.** They compete. Choose one to lead. If the photo is strong, make it larger and let the headline be smaller and quieter. If the headline carries the story, shrink or crop the photo. A neighbor should see one entry point in about two seconds.
2. **Everything is centered.** Centered text is fine for a short headline, but body copy centered over two pages is tiring to read. Left-align the body. Keep center for the headline only, if at all.
3. **Even spacing everywhere.** With the same gap between every element, nothing is grouped and nothing gets emphasis. Put more space around the headline and photo, less between a caption and its image, and group related paragraphs.
4. **Two equal columns at equal density.** The page feels flat, and the reader has no sense of progress. Make one column or section the main one. Use a pull quote, a larger first paragraph, or a short sidebar (hours, address, contact) to vary the rhythm. Let page 2 be lighter than page 1 and end with a clear next step.

## For this audience
Neighbors at an association meeting want to know what Sŏn is, what it will be like next door, and who to call. Put answers to the practical questions (hours, noise, deliveries, parking, a contact person) in an easy-to-find place. Keep the tone plain and warm. Write "customer," not "guest," and avoid em dashes and claims of conviction like "we believe."

## Quick fixes, in priority order
1. Pick the lead (headline or photo) and scale the other down.
2. Left-align the body text.
3. Vary the spacing so related items group.
4. Make the columns unequal, or add a sidebar.
5. Make page 2 lighter, with one clear closing line and contact details.

## Checks I could not make
- Photo quality and crop (does the subject face into the page?).
- Line length and gaps in justified text.
- Font choice against Sŏn's brand type.
- Print: size, margins, paper, and color. Print a proof on the real paper before running copies, and have Brandon approve it.

## Questions
- What is the one thing you want a neighbor to remember?
- Is the lead photo a real photo of the space or team?
- How many copies, and where will they be printed?
