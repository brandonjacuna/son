# T2 review: two-page Sŏn story, neighborhood association handout

Basis: the description only (everything centered, even spacing, two equal columns at equal density, headline and lead photo at the same weight). I have not seen the file, so I name what to check rather than assert it.

## Verdict
Not ready to hand out. The content may be fine. The layout gives a reader no entry point, no order, and no pause, so a neighbor holding it at a meeting will not know where to start.

## Problems, in order of cost

1. **No hierarchy.** Headline and lead photo compete at equal weight. The eye has no first stop. Pick one to lead. For this audience (neighbors deciding how they feel about a new restaurant on E St. Elmo), the lead should be the headline, or a photo of the building and street if one is strong. The other drops to clearly secondary.

2. **Everything centered.** Centered body text has a ragged left edge, so every line start is hard to find. Centering suits a headline, a short pull quote, or a sign-off. Body copy, lists, and the practical details should be left-aligned.

3. **Even spacing.** Equal gaps between every element make the page read as one block. Spacing should group: tight between a subhead and its paragraph, looser between sections. Right now nothing says what belongs together.

4. **Two equal columns at equal density.** Two full columns of same-size text look like a report, not a story. Vary the density: one wider column for the story, one narrower for facts (address, timeline, hours, contact, what we are asking of neighbors). Give the page a place to rest, such as white space, a pull quote, or a photo that breaks the grid.

5. **Two pages, one rhythm.** If page 2 repeats page 1's structure, the reader sees no progression. Page 1 should be the story and the pitch. Page 2 should be what neighbors need: operating hours, noise, parking, deliveries, trash, a named contact.

## What to do
- Set a three-level type scale: headline, subheads, body. Make the headline clearly the largest thing on the page.
- Left-align body. Keep centering to the headline only, if at all.
- Use a 2/3 and 1/3 column split. Story left, facts right.
- One lead image, large. Remaining images small and captioned.
- Add one pull quote or one number-led fact as the pause.
- Put a contact name, email, and phone at the bottom of page 2. Neighbors will act on this if it is easy to find.

## Copy checks for this audience
- Use "customer," never "guest." No em dashes. Declarative sentences, no "we believe" or "our goal is."
- Lead with what the neighbors get and what Sŏn commits to, not the brand story.
- Do not reference anything outside Sŏn.
- Any financial figure must come from the current Investor Review workbook. If a number appears, confirm its source before printing.

## Open questions for Brandon

- Is there a photo of the actual building or street? That is the strongest lead image for this room.
- Print only, or also a PDF? Black-and-white copies change how much the photo can carry.
