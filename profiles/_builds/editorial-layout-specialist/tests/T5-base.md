# T5: Porch menu board draft, check the layout

The draft is not attached, so I give the check I will run and the questions that decide it. A board is read standing, at a distance, often outdoors, so the standard is different from a table menu.

## Readability on a porch
- **Distance.** Item names readable at 6 to 8 feet at least; a rule of thumb is about 1 inch of letter height per 10 feet of reading distance. Prices smaller than names but never tiny.
- **Light.** Outdoors means glare by day and dim light at night. Test in both. If the board is lit, check for glare on glossy materials and shadows from fixtures.
- **Contrast.** Dark on light or light on dark with strong contrast; avoid thin weights and low-contrast greys. Chalk or matte surfaces read better than gloss.
- **Weather.** Material is outdoor-rated; ink does not fade; sun and rain tested.

## Layout
- One column or two at most; items scannable top to bottom.
- Three levels: section, item, description. Descriptions short; cut anything that needs reading twice.
- Prices aligned in one place, without dot leaders or currency clutter.
- Spacing groups related items; do not fill the board edge to edge. Margins at least 5 percent.
- Allergen and dietary marks consistent, with a legend; check what Texas rules or the health department require for any posted claims.
- Hangul and Latin set with matched sizes and weights; dish names spelled consistently with the main menu.

## Content
- Matches the current dinner menu exactly: names, prices, and availability. A board that shows an item that is not available is a customer-service problem. Prices only from the approved menu source.
- Use "customer," never "guest," in any copy. No em dashes. Declarative descriptions.
- Include hours, address, and how to order if it is a walk-up.

## Process questions
1. Board size and material? Printed, painted, or chalk? Who changes it, and how often?
2. Is it a fixed board or a template that must update by hand? If it changes often, design for edit-ability (modular strips).
3. Is the porch a public-facing sign? Check signage rules for the property and the city before fabrication.

## Output
A pass or numbered fixes, ordered by readability first.
