Example 1: the quiz that guards the floor.
A peer-authored "server readiness" module ends in a fifteen-question written quiz and a line for a lead to initial after watching one shift. Two faults at once. The quiz certifies a does-level competence, running a section, with knows-level evidence, which is construct underrepresentation, and the initial-after-one-shift is ad hoc entrustment, not a summative decision. The judgment: the quiz is fine as the ID's low-stakes learning check and worthless as the gate. Rebuild the gate as observed performance on a real section across at least two occasions, scored against behavioral anchors, with the sign-off framed as an entrustment level, not a pass mark. The novice error avoided is trusting a quiz because it is objective and easy to score, when objectivity about the wrong thing is not validity.

Example 2: two leads, two bars.
The same servers are rated "ready" by one lead and "not yet" by another, and the difference tracks the lead, not the server. This looks like a training problem. Diagnosed properly it is an absolute-decision reliability failure: the classification is not consistent across raters, and the honest reliability question is decision consistency, not the alpha of any score. The judgment: the first moves are structural, anchor the scale with observable floor behaviors and set the standard with contrasting groups so both leads are reading against the same references, and only then does frame-of-reference calibration help at the margin. The novice error avoided is reaching first for "train them to be objective," which the evidence says does little for rating accuracy.

Example 3: setting the bar for "runs a section unsupervised."
Someone asks what the passing standard should be for solo section work. There is no true number to look up. The judgment: treat it as a policy decision made defensible. Use contrasting groups, take servers the leads already agree are clearly ready and clearly not yet, rate both cohorts on the anchored dimensions, and locate the cut where the distributions separate, then document it and frame it as the entrustment level at which unsupervised practice is allowed. Set it on what competent, safe service requires, not on whatever is hardest. The novice error avoided is inventing a round number, 80 percent, and treating it as discovered fact.

Example 4: the failure that was a barrier, not a gap.
A cluster of otherwise-strong staff keep failing the allergen gate, which is a timed written scenario, and they share a first language other than English. Two things are true at once: the gate has construct-irrelevant difficulty, reading and language load moving a result that should be about allergen handling, and that same mechanism is producing a fairness problem. The judgment: run the four-fifths screen, and because the failing rate falls below it, stop treating the failures as unreadiness. Move the check to a demonstrated does-level task in the real setting, strip the reading load, and re-read the group rates. Adverse impact by itself is not proof the people are not ready; it is a flag to find what in the gate is not allergen competence. The novice error avoided is concluding the people are not ready when the assessment, not the person, was the problem.

Example 5: the gate that passed people who could not perform.
Recently signed-off servers generate a run of complaints about pacing and table reading. The instinct is to blame the individuals. Traced as a validity argument, the weakest link has broken: the gate relied on a shows-how role-play with a lead rather than does-level performance on a live floor, so it generalized within the role-play and failed to extrapolate to real service. Because this is the gate that feeds advancement, every misread compounds upward into the succession engine. The judgment: rebuild the evidence base at the does level and across enough occasions to saturate the call, and add a downstream consequence signal so an extrapolation failure surfaces in weeks, not quarters. The novice error avoided is patching individuals when the gate is manufacturing the errors.
