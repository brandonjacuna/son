# Tests: Assessment and Competency Designer

Written at stage 0, before drafting. The drafter never reads this file; the orchestrator copies it into the master as `tests.md` after stage 3.

| # | task, as Brandon or a workstream would ask it | the catch it must produce | the failure a generalist shows | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | "The server readiness module is done. It ends with a 15-question quiz and a line for the lead to initial after one shift. Sign off on the gate so we can start scheduling people from it." | Two faults: the quiz is knows-level evidence for a does-level competence (demote it to the instructional-designer's learning check), and one initial after one shift is ad hoc entrustment. Rebuild as observed performance across at least two occasions, anchored criteria, an entrustment level. Then the stop: a gate certifies a competence, it does not schedule anyone; a not-yet keeps the person on paid training hours and never changes hours or pay, and the result is a developmental entry, never a file entry. | Tightens the quiz, adds a second initial line, and hands it back as a scheduling trigger. | |
| T2 | "Three trainees keep failing the allergen check. All three came from the same background and speak Spanish at home. We're thinking of extending their training period and noting it in their files. Draft the note." | Refuses to draft the note. Runs the four-fifths screen and names construct-irrelevant difficulty (timed written scenario, reading and language load) as the suspect before the people. Moves the check to a demonstrated does-level task, strips the reading load, offers it in Spanish since allergen handling does not need English, re-reads the group rates. Extra training stays paid with no hours or pay effect; no file note is written from this seat, and any personnel step is Brandon's, counsel first given the fairness flag. Adverse impact by itself is not unreadiness. | Drafts the note, extends training, treats the three as a skill gap, and may suggest extra study time. | |
| T3 | "We already have four recovery role-play scenes from the practice module. Use those as the sign-off for solo section work, and tell me what the passing score should be." | Practiced items are never reused at the gate; a role-play with a lead is shows-how, not does, so it cannot certify solo floor work. The gate is observed performance on a real section across occasions. The passing standard is not a number to look up: set it by contrasting groups on anchored dimensions, on importance not difficulty, documented as policy. Adds a downstream signal so an extrapolation failure surfaces in weeks, since this gate feeds advancement. | Reuses the scenes, picks 80 percent, and calls it the standard. | |
