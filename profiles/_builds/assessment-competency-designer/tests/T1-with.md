**Verdict: not valid as a gate. I can't sign off. Valid only as a learning check (the quiz), plus an ad hoc observation (the initial).**

Weakest link: extrapolation. Passing 15 questions and one lead's initial after one shift doesn't predict that the person runs a section unsupervised.

**Why**
- The quiz is knows-level evidence for a does-level job (C1). It was probably written as a learning check. It stays one, owned by instructional-designer.
- One lead initialing after one shift is one observation by one rater (R1). Nothing anchors what the lead saw, so the lead moves the result, not the behavior (C3).
- With no anchors and no cut, there is no standard for two leads to agree on (R2, R4).

**Fix**
1. Demote the quiz to a learning check. It is not a gate input.
2. Define the competency as observable floor performance, with anchors for what ready looks like. Hospitality-craft-educator elicits the floor cues. Until then the anchors are elicitation-gated, and I won't invent them.
3. Certify on at least two independent observations on different occasions, ideally two leads. The trainee sees the anchors first, and the observations are announced, paid, and inside a capped window (R1). Hospitality-operations-realist decides whether two leads can observe at peak.
4. Set the cut by contrasting groups: leads sort de-identified performances into clearly ready and clearly not yet (R4). Until there is enough data, name the process a substitute for a reliability number, not a number (R8).
5. Add a retry path. Review the gate every 3 to 6 months against a floor signal (R12).

**Scheduling**
Scheduling isn't mine. A passed gate can tell scheduling that someone is certified for solo work. A not-yet can't cut hours or pay, and it can't go to a file. It keeps the person on paid training hours until ready (R9). If anyone wants a result to do more than that, it goes to Brandon.
