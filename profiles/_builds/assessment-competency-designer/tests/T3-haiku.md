# Recovery role-play scenes as sign-off for solo section work

**Verdict: not valid as a sign-off.** The four scenes are valid only as a learning check inside practice. I will not set a passing score for them.

## Why
- C13: practiced scenes proposed as the sign-off. Exposure inflates the result, and scenes show how a trainee handles a recovery, not that they do it on a live section. Solo section work is a floor gate (R11): who runs a section unsupervised.
- The four scenes are now practiced items. They leave the gate bank and go on the retired-item ledger, reported by practice-simulation-designer. No future gate item can be drawn from them.
- No passing score can come from four practice scenes. A cut is set by contrasting groups on de-identified live performances rated against anchors (R4). A number chosen now would be a guess presented as a cut. Until data exist, the method is a procedural substitute, named as one (R8).

## What the gate should be
1. Live section performance, observed by two independent leads on different occasions, inside paid hours, announced, with anchors shown first (R1). Whether two leads can observe at peak is hospitality-operations-realist's call.
2. Anchors for recovery moves come from hospitality-craft-educator. Until elicited, mark them elicitation-gated.
3. Interim rule, before any cut exists: two independent live observations, each rated against the anchors. The call rests on the anchors, not a score.
4. Once enough performances exist, set the cut with contrasting groups. Leads sort de-identified performances into clearly ready and clearly not yet. The cut goes where the distributions separate. The name link is dropped once the cut is set, and the sort is not kept as a record of people (R4).
5. Practice stays in practice. Practice-simulation-designer rehearses the moves. This seat holds the live gate items.
6. A not-yet keeps the trainee on paid training hours, with a retry. The result is a developmental entry at most (R9).
