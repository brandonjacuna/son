**Verdict: not valid as the sign-off for solo section work. I won't set a passing score for it.**

Weakest link: extrapolation. The scenes are practiced, so exposure inflates the result. And a role-play shows how, not does, which is the wrong level of evidence for a floor skill (C13).

**Why no passing score**
- A score only means something once a cut is set (R4). A cut is set on the anchored criteria, using contrasting groups, on performances the trainees haven't rehearsed. The scenes can't produce that. People who rehearsed them will score high whether or not they can recover a real service moment.
- Any score I named now would be a number with nothing behind it. Per R8, until real data exists the defensible substitute is process: several observers, anchors, aggregation, and a retry path. I'll name that a substitute, not a number.

**Fix**
1. Keep the four scenes as practice. They go back to practice-simulation-designer. Every scene that was practiced is reported to this seat and retired from the gate bank (retired-item ledger).
2. Gate solo section work on live floor performance of recovery moments, with items kept out of practice. This premise is inferred.
3. Two independent observations on different occasions, ideally two leads (R1). The anchors are behaviors observed on the floor. Hospitality-craft-educator elicits them from Sŏn practitioners, and until then they are elicitation-gated.
4. Set the cut by contrasting groups: leads sort de-identified live performances into clearly ready and clearly not yet (R4). The first hired trainees are the novice reference group.
5. Where a live recovery moment doesn't come up in an observation window, a fresh, unpracticed scene is a supplement, never the whole sign-off. I'd treat that as a design question for the gate spec.

**Boundaries**
A result certifies competence only. A not-yet keeps the person on paid training hours and leads to a retry (R9). It doesn't set schedules, pay, or files.

- Is the section back of house? If so, a chef signs off first.
