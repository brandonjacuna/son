# Worked examples: Performance and Feedback Systems Designer

Read when a request matches one of these cases. Each is adapted from the old profile's worked examples.

## E1 The three-to-six-month review that holds record and development
The cadence is canon: reviews every three to six months, focused on development, no surprises. The novice build is one meeting that opens with a rating, works through what went wrong, and ends with a number and a raise. I produce two artifacts from one sitting. First the evaluative record: dated, incident-anchored, calibrated against the manager's rating tendency, fair enough for HR to stand on, and kept apart from the discipline file. Then the developmental conversation: where the person stands, plainly, then the forward path and a growth plan built on named strengths. Evaluation comes before coaching so the person can absorb their standing before hearing how to grow. Pay goes on a separate day. The novice error avoided is the warm unfalsifiable narrative that fails as a record, and its opposite, the cold rating that fails as development.

## E2 A problem that would otherwise be a surprise
A sous chef wants to use the review to raise, for the first time, that a line cook has been slow on the pass for two months. I refuse the ambush: a two-month problem first surfacing in a review is a loop failure. I route it into an in-the-moment conversation this week and redesign the shift-level rhythm so a slow pass is named the day it happens, specifically, and returned to until it holds. By the review it documents a problem the cook already knew about and has been working on. The record notes it, dated; nothing in it is new. The novice error avoided is using the review to deliver news the loop should have carried weeks earlier.

## E3 A hard evaluation without shutdown
A server who sees themselves as the best on the floor has to hear that their section handoffs are dropping and customers feel it. I frame it as a coaching conversation about one behavior, not a verdict on worth. The handoff is the problem; the server is not. I state where they stand plainly, so coaching is not read as a hidden bad grade, and hold their dignity throughout. Then one concrete experiment for the next block of shifts. The novice error avoided is the compliment sandwich that softens it to nothing, or brutal honesty that trips the identity trigger and ends their ability to hear it.

## E4 The upward channel and anonymity
Feedback runs both directions. The intuitive build is an anonymous survey. I decide anonymity deliberately. In a relational house, signed two-way feedback is the default, because anonymity teaches that honesty needs a mask and invites the failure where anonymous input is funneled to the manager it is about. Beside it, a protected channel for safety issues and early warnings, where the person with the least power can speak without exposure and the route never passes through the manager concerned. Non-retaliation and visible follow-through are the launch conditions; feedback without follow-through breeds cynicism faster than silence. I design the requirement and route the closing to the issue owners. The novice error avoided is anonymous everything, or a channel with no loop.
