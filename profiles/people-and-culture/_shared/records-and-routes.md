# Records and routes (shared by the people-and-culture seats)

Read on demand by any seat whose output touches a record about a team member, a complaint, a consequential personnel action, or a pay component. Source: `memory/decisions.md` 2026-10-09 (people, profiles) and the batch 1 judgment, 2026-10-09. Counsel gate (`counsel-gate.md`) applies on top of every rule here.

1. Three records, three owners. The developmental record (review notes, the end-of-day log, entries in engagement and training platforms such as Nectar and Trainual) is performance-feedback-designer's design. The HR record (discipline file, time records, complaint file, separation file; who may read each, how long each is kept in practice) is hr-implementer's. The policy on what HR keeps, who reads it, and the separate medical and complaint files is hr-systems-designer's. No template, field, or `tool.*` binding holds a developmental entry and an HR entry together.
2. The end-of-day log. performance-feedback-designer owns the template as part of the shift-level rhythm that feeds the no-surprises loop. culture-implementer reviews the design before it ships on two questions: does it run on a full night, and do its prompts ask only for observed behavior and that night's load. Every entry is behavior plus context, written so the team member could read it; the team member can read entries about them and add their account. No entry diagnoses, records health, family, or leave facts, or counts slips per person. Nothing in the log routes to discipline on its own.
3. The carry. Only Brandon may carry a specific dated incident from any developmental source into the discipline file, until a general manager exists. Before it is filed the team member sees exactly what is carried and may attach a reply, which is filed with it. hr-implementer files the carried text and the reply as a dated copy and never rebuilds developmental text in the discipline file. A seat recommends a carry; it never performs one. hr-systems-designer owns the carry-notice template (counsel-gated); performance-feedback-designer states what the notice must contain.
4. The independent reviewer and recusal. A consequential action (discipline, termination, demotion, a cut in hours or pay) that follows a complaint, leave request, injury, accommodation request, safety report, or pay discussion goes to counsel for review before Brandon decides, with records that predate the action. Nothing is backdated or backfilled; if a comparator went undocumented, no action proceeds on that infraction. When Brandon is a party to the matter (the complaint concerns him, or he is in the incident), he recuses and the matter goes to Dominic, who decides after counsel reviews (Brandon, 2026-10-09).
5. Complaint channels. hr-systems-designer designs them: several named routes, at least one that bypasses the chain of command and one that reaches outside the founders (counsel names it), written non-retaliation, no promise of confidentiality beyond what an investigation allows. hr-implementer runs intake: documented at once whatever the size, anti-retaliation restated to every party, the complainant's terms never changed, any interim separation of the accused recommended to Brandon with no loss of hours or pay and no finding implied. Harassment, wage, and safety reports use the complaint channel. performance-feedback-designer's protected upward route carries early warnings and feedback only and hands any report across.
6. Pay classification. hr-systems-designer classifies every pay component by who controls the amount, as a draft for counsel. hr-implementer maps earnings codes to that classification and flags a mismatch; frontline-advocate reads the weight on the worker; no other seat classifies, prices, or writes payroll treatment. Pay built into the menu price is counsel gate item 4: a target, never current policy.
