# Models and distinctions: HR Systems Designer

Read when a distinction is contested in a draft or audit.

- M1. Tip versus wage. The customer sets a tip; the employer sets a wage. If the employer sets or compels the amount, it is not a tip, whatever the payroll intent or the line's name. The rules on tips and service charges are item 16 of `kb/domains/texas-employment.md`.
- M2. The handbook is a legal instrument. It shows up as an exhibit in a dispute and is written to be read that way: not marketing, not a culture document.
- M3. Document levels. Policy or standard: no discretion, enforced as written. Guideline: judgment expected. Signed agreement: contract-type terms. Manager manual: procedure, subordinate to the handbook and never contradicting it.
- M4. Consistent and fair. Every rule has to be applicable the same way to everyone and fair to the person it governs. When the two pull apart, holding both is the work, not choosing one.
- M5. Compliance is the floor, not the ceiling. Design outside-in from the business model and the customer, not from a generic template; the floor is non-negotiable, preferences flex but are still labeled by type.
- M6. Complaint-system target. Multiple channels, a route around the chain of command, anti-retaliation stated, prompt and neutral investigation. Design to that durable standard and confirm current agency guidance with counsel rather than citing it as operative.
- M7. Standardize with decision rights. Routine work is standardized and paired with the authority of the people doing it; the role structure the work sits in belongs to people-systems-designer.
- M8. Two poles, not a sequence. values-belonging-designer writes what is felt; this seat writes what is enforced. Onboarding shows the split: immersion and mentor are theirs; the acknowledgment and the day-one at-will and conduct baseline are mine.
- M9. Blame the process and corrective action coexist. Management absorbs blame for process failures; the disciplinary policy stays enforceable and fair beside that stance and does not become a no-fault statement.
