# Models: Values and Belonging Designer

Read when a cue or rule needs its underlying distinction, or to run either procedure end to end.

## Design half
- M1. Values vs virtues. A value is what the house says; a virtue is what it does. A value not expressed as action is not yet culture.
- M2. Four value types. Core (upheld at a cost), aspirational (not yet true), permission-to-play (the baseline any room owes), accidental (arose on its own). Mixing them confuses people about what is non-negotiable.
- M3. Taught vs posted. A value earns its place only when it is woven into hiring, the first days, recognition, and correction.
- M4. Recognition vs appreciation. Recognition is conditional and about the past: what you did. Appreciation is about who you are and lands even on a night the work did not. A culture needs both, kept separate.
- M5. For vs to. Service is the technical act; hospitality is how it makes a person feel. Culture is present when a thing happens for a person and absent when it is done to them. This applies to the team as much as to the customer.
- M6. Belonging is built outside-in by many small repeated signals (you are safe here, you are connected, there is a shared future). Easy to destroy, slow to build.
- M7. Ritual vs habit. The same act; meaning separates them. Habit automates, ritual animates.
- M8. Constant gentle pressure. Return the saltshaker every time: gently enough never to deny dignity, firmly enough the standard is never in doubt. The gentleness is the dignity; the constancy is the standard.
- M9. Two poles. This seat is relational, lived, felt; HR is precise, fair, documented. They are opposite poles of people and culture, not steps in a sequence.
- M10. Design order. Name the material (value, ritual, recognition, mentorship, recovery) -> sort the value -> check the seam (whose is this) -> translate to behavior -> design the felt experience -> floor test (does it survive a Friday rush; does it invite the self or overwrite it) -> hold it (gentle pressure; belonging re-signaled on a cadence).

## Read half
- M11. Latent construct vs proxy. Culture has no direct measure; every metric is a proxy that can be biased or gamed.
- M12. Three levels: artifacts, espoused values, underlying assumptions. A survey cannot reach the assumption layer; behavior can.
- M13. Four evidence streams: self-report, behavior, story, network. A claim is only as good as their convergence.
- M14. Exit vs stay. An exit interview is lagging by nature; the same questions asked of current staff are leading.
- M15. Leading indicator with a causal chain. Pair each lagging measure with a leading one carrying an explicit if-then hypothesis (for example: if safety is real, error reporting rises, then fewer errors reach the customer), and check that the links move together.
- M16. A measure that becomes a target stops measuring: people optimize the proxy and it decouples from what it stood for.
- M17. Read order. Name the construct and the design claim -> pre-commit the disconfirmers -> place sensors on the floor and in the network, not only on leadership -> gather the four streams -> read for convergence -> strip incentive-tied and tiny-n signals -> verdict, with what the data cannot yet show.
- M18. Retention and internal promotion are corroboration that development commitments are real; read as evidence, never as targets.
- M19. If Sŏn becomes multi-unit, survey numbers gain the sample they lack; re-weight toward them then, not before.
