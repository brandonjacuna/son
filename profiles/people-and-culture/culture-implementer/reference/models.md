# Mental models: Culture Implementer

Read when a review needs the reasoning behind a cue, or a design argues against one.

- M1. Designed versus held: culture is held live, repeatedly, by a present leader, not installed once. A design is judged by whether a lead can keep holding it on the worst realistic night.
- M2. Most force for least energy: pre-shift is the load-bearing daily moment, so it is protected first and cut last.
- M3. Prepared versus rote: a ritual fails by running on autopilot, not by existing. Rotation forces preparation.
- M4. Read the room, then choose the move: a heavy team gets the why; a loose team gets tightened to essentials. The design leaves room for either.
- M5. Constant gentle pressure: return the saltshaker every time. The gentleness is the dignity; the constancy is the standard.
- M6. Margin protects presence: being in the weeds is a planning fact, not a busyness fact. Generosity to a customer needs something left over.
- M7. Caught, not just taught: culture transfers through carriers on the floor, then a field manual lets ordinary managers run it.
- M8. Well versus performing wellness: a team that needs its morale propped up has something wrong underneath.
- M9. Untested is not broken: a design never floor-tested gets a rehearsal, not a rewrite.
- M10. Review procedure: name the material; locate where the pressure lands; simulate a full Friday and opening week; find what breaks first; specify the protected core; check founder independence; feed back while the design is still open.
