# Models: TBRI (reference)

Read when explaining why a rule holds, or answering where the theory comes from.

## The adult use (stated once)
The framework was built and studied for children and their caregivers. Sŏn applies its relational mechanisms to an adult professional workforce: safety then connection holds for adults under stress. That application is Sŏn's adaptation, not the framework's own research. It is the seat's working position, never claimed as proven, and there is no clinical use. Outputs do not repeat this statement.

## Mental models
- M1. Being safe vs feeling safe. A room can be safe while a person still reads it as unsafe. This is the master distinction; every other model serves it.
- M2. Precedence. Empower (physical and environmental needs), connect (the relationship), correct (the behavior). The order is the point: correction succeeds only on a foundation of the first two.
- M3. Behavior is communication. A lapse points at a state, a system, or an unmet need before it points at character. This is Sŏn's blame-the-process stance in relational form.
- M4. Graduated response. Most moments are handled at the lightest level. Heavier responses are support for regulation, not punishment.
- M5. Co-regulation precedes self-regulation. In a rush or after a mistake, a calm leader briefly carries the regulation the person cannot, then hands it back. It is a leadership move, not a clinical act.
- M6. Felt safety vs psychological safety. Felt safety is a person-level mechanism inside one interaction (this seat). Psychological safety is team and org design (people-systems-designer). They are companions; never collapse one into the other.

## Sŏn mappings (adaptation)
- S1. The cooling system is the empowering substrate plus co-regulation: burnout is a system failure, not a character flaw.
- S2. Dignity as a precondition for presence is felt safety stated operationally.
- S3. Reviews every 3 to 6 months with no surprises (`memory/decisions.md`, 2026-10-09) are what let correction sit inside connection: the cadence is performance-feedback-designer's; the relational reason is this seat's.
- S4. The mentor is a proven cultural steward, not the top performer: connection, not skill, carries belonging.

## Sequence check (when a moment is handed to me)
1. Is this treatment? If yes, R1 and stop.
2. Is the person settled enough to hear anything?
3. Substrate and connection in place?
4. What is the behavior communicating?
5. What is the lightest intervention that holds the standard?
6. Who co-regulates, and when is it handed back?
7. Which neighbor owns the surface?
