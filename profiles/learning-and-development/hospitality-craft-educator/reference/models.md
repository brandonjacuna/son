# Models: Hospitality Craft Educator

Read when a task needs the distinction behind a rule, not on every call.

## M1. Two layers
The service layer is house-set and checkable: a monologue the house writes. The hospitality layer is what the customer feels: a dialogue, read one table at a time. New hires default to service-process thinking, so the distinction itself has to be taught. What separates an expert from a non-expert is the interaction (read, advise, accompany), not the gestures; schools that teach gestures and sequence leave the interaction to a short simulation.

## M2. Kinds of knowledge
Tellable knowledge can be written. Bodily knowledge (carrying, pouring) is drilled. Collective knowledge (the house's sense of fit) is acquired by working inside the team and is named as such, never shipped. Perceptual knowledge moves only through trailing with the expert speaking aloud, plus classification practice. Classify before choosing any medium.

## M3. The recognition model
An expert sees cues, forms expectancies, holds a goal, takes the typical move, and checks it. Absence is a cue. A cue is trustworthy only where it predicts and the floor gives fast feedback; otherwise the learner needs a check. Experts describe their work as manual and do not name diagnosis or anticipation, which is why "describe what you do" yields fiction and incidents are probed instead.

## M4. Attention, not feeling
Surface acting (displaying a required feeling) costs well-being and does not raise performance; deep acting and natural attention do better, and autonomy buffers the strain. Teach the aim (where attention goes, how the moment is appraised) and leave the expression to the person.

## M5. Recovery levers
Three levers: what is given, how fast and by what process, and how the person is treated. Severity rises from service lapse to product to food; food is the most severe and words will not carry it. The sequence: awareness, acknowledgment, apology, action at once, extra generosity where judgment says so, no alibis, then close the loop on the outcome. Do not lean on a regular's goodwill.

## M6. Saturation in elicitation
Code saturation: the range of issues is known ("heard it all"). Meaning saturation: the issues are understood ("understand it all"). The first comes well before the second. In a homogeneous sample most themes surface early and the core cross-case structure is visible sooner still, but that was one large non-restaurant study; the right stop depends on purpose, heterogeneity, sample, and complexity together, not one number. Sŏn's small pre-opening crew is outside every study's range.

## M7. Availability displays (bar-counter evidence)
At a bar, service opens when a customer displays availability with the body (torso and gaze on the server, close to the counter, payment in hand); words come second. A turned back or a gaze into the distance is not a request; eyes on the menu means not ready. A party orders through one lead speaker. The customer's own readiness is part of the server's speed. All of this is counter service; on a seated floor it is reasoned extension, and a fine-dining server reads before any display.

## M8. Calibration and liking
Panel tasting skill narrows fastest with immediate feedback against reference standards, not with more tasting. Expert quality and consumer liking are separate axes: in one cheongju study, what consumers liked most was not what sommeliers rated highest, and both age groups leaned sweet. Both summaries are vendor-written.
