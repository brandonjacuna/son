# Models and evidence: Practice and Simulation Designer

Read when weighing evidence for a fidelity, model-source, error-practice, or transfer call. None of this evidence comes from restaurant service.

## Fidelity
- M1. Both high- and low-fidelity simulation beat no practice.
- M2. Across 24 medical studies (auscultation, surgical technique, complex management such as resuscitation), high fidelity showed almost no advantage over low fidelity on performance. Borrowed evidence: state it as borrowed when applied to Sŏn service. The source gives no mechanism in what was read; state none.
- M3. Role-play and full simulators have been found equally effective; role-play is a valid low-fidelity form.

## Error practice and transfer
- M4. Error-management practice pays most on novel tasks and on later performance, not within-training scores. Use it where the floor will not repeat the scene exactly.
- M5. Open versus closed skill changes how strongly transfer predictors act. This is the basis for splitting form by skill kind; it does not prove any single form.
- M6. Lead (supervisor) support predicts transfer more strongly than peer support, on small samples. Do not treat peer follow-through as a substitute for the lead's role.

## AI and modeled practice
- M7. In a small three-arm pilot (didactic, agent role-play, passive video), arms differed on satisfaction, not competence; the authors attribute the satisfaction gap to novelty and usability. Video of worked examples matched agent role-play.
- M8. Trainees described agent practice as safe and non-judgmental. That may lower a felt-risk barrier for a first rehearsal, with a human debrief after. A position for the founder-gated policy, not a verdict.
- M9. In one pilot, total scores can hide a weak sub-skill (all arms fell short on one ratio). Check sub-skills, not only totals.
- M10. Virtual-human practice draws attention to the agent while it speaks; view switching may undermine natural interaction. Attention was measured, not transfer.
- M11. Agent personas are perceived on four factors (credible, facilitating learning, engaging, human-like). A lens for reviewing an AI model, nothing more.

## Diagnosis
- M12. Diagnose a practice by its absent expectancies: elicited cues, a form matched to the skill, kept fidelity, tell-free choices, recoverable errors, feedback, prebrief, debrief, follow-through. Each absence is the defect. Walk a new server through it as if onto a real Friday; each snag is a defect located.
