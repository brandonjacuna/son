# Models: Assessment and Competency Designer

Read when a gate audit needs the distinction behind a rule, or someone challenges a call.

- M1. Validity belongs to the inference and its use, not the instrument. There is no "valid test," only a valid reading of readiness for a stated decision.
- M2. Two threats cover most floor-gate failures: construct underrepresentation (the gate samples too little of the competence, as a quiz for a floor skill) and construct-irrelevant variance (something other than the competence moves the result: reading load, English, timing, nerves, rapport with the lead).
- M3. The criterion is not the cutscore. Defining what competent performance looks like and deciding where "ready" begins are separate acts, done in that order.
- M4. Entrustment is prospective: the call is how much oversight a person needs going forward, not a grade on what they did. Ad hoc entrustment (one lead, one shift) differs from summative entrustment (independent of context and rater, built on enough observations).
- M5. A pass or not-yet is an absolute decision, so rater leniency and severity count as error. The fitting reliability read is dependability and decision consistency, not a norm-referenced coefficient such as alpha.
- M6. The rater is a biased instrument, but some rater variance is true signal about integrated performance. Structure the environment around the rater; do not demand objectivity or zero disagreement.
- M7. Utility is multiplicative: a highly reliable quiz with near-zero validity for the floor has near-zero utility.
- M8. The inference as an argument: observation (was the right thing seen), score (was it scored on anchors), generalization (would other occasions and raters agree), extrapolation (does it predict the floor), decision (is the cut consistent). The chain fails at its weakest link.
- M9. A flat house with cross-training and internal advancement compounds a misread gate upward, which is why gate validity is a separate seat.
- M10. Recognize, then check expectancies: name what the gate rests on (a proxy, a single ad hoc look, an impression, or aggregated does-level evidence), then check for the expected safeguards; each absence is the diagnosis. Then mentally run a real borderline person through it: would two leads, or the same lead on a bad night, classify the same, and does anything but the competence move the outcome.
