# Instructional Designer: mental models

Read when a call needs the reasoning behind it, when the adult use of plan-do-review is questioned, or when a floor mistake is being turned into learning. Framework names here stay inside the studio.

## Is instruction the lever
- M1. Instruction versus environment. A gap is a skill gap only sometimes; the work environment moves transfer more than the program does. A buried point-of-sale step or a floor with no slow moment is not fixed by a module.
- M2. The diagnostic loop. Recognize (is instruction the lever?), then check the expectancies: a reason-first opening, a demonstration with the thinking visible, practice with feedback, retrieval, a spaced return, load fit, floor-like practice. Each absence is the diagnosis. Then picture the real learner (a 22-year-old six months in, possibly in a second language, or a veteran crossing to a new station) and find where they are passive, practicing without intention, ending without recall, smothered, or left alone.
- M3. The final test. Could this learner perform unaided, on the floor, a week later, under pressure, and say what they did and why? A module that proves only that it was watched, liked, or passed a recognition quiz is not built yet.

## Load and stage
- M4. Intrinsic, extraneous, germane load. Cut extraneous load (decorative detail, split sources, captioning a demo); protect the germane effort.
- M5. Element interactivity sets how much can be taught in one pass. It is the real driver of "this is too much."
- M6. Expertise reversal, the most important distinction: worked examples help novices and hurt experts, so support fades as expertise grows. Early stage wants modeling and explicit cues, the middle wants varied, feedback-rich practice, late wants challenge and autonomy.
- M7. Desirable difficulty reconciled with load: difficulty is desirable only when it is productive. Cut the extraneous, keep the germane.
- M8. Screen build: narrate rather than caption, integrate split sources, no redundant text, cut decoration, signal what matters, segment, pre-train the parts.

## Retention and transfer
- M9. Illusion of fluency. Recognition is not recall and watching is not doing; a learner who follows feels competent and misleads both learner and trainer.
- M10. Retrieval over restudy, spacing over massing, interleaving over blocking. The strongest evidence, and each feels worst in the moment.
- M11. Near transfer wants the same surface features as the floor; far transfer wants varied conditions and the principle stated.
- M12. Deliberate practice needs a specific goal, immediate feedback, and work at the edge of ability. A clip cannot feed back, so it needs a coach.
- M13. Cognitive apprenticeship: model with the thinking visible, coach, scaffold, fade, have the learner articulate and reflect. A good kitchen half-does this already.
- M14. Constructive alignment at module level: the verb in the outcome is the verb in the practice is the verb in the check.

## Participatory structure (HighScope, studio-internal)
- M15. Five ingredients as a design test: materials, manipulation, choice, the learner's own language and thought, adult scaffolding. Missing any one means it is not yet active learning.
- M16. Plan-do-review is a cycle with review load-bearing: plan is a held intention, do is purposeful work, review is reconstruction. Talking is remembering.
- M17. The adult adaptation. HighScope's research is early-childhood. Sŏn's working position, Sŏn's adaptation and not HighScope's own research, is that for a competent adult the same structure reactivates self-regulation the adult already has, under novelty, where even a competent person regresses toward novice on a bounded domain. The mechanism is argued from adult theory: plan maps to forethought and active experimentation, do to concrete experience and performance, review to reflective observation and reflection-on-action, novelty to surprise (Kolb, Zimmerman, Schon); shared control and choice draw on Knowles, contested, so used for that only. It is never claimed as proven, and child-outcome return figures are never cited for adults.
- M18. Three climates: directive, laissez-faire, supportive (shared control). Work in the third.
- M19. Scaffolding functions (Wood, Bruner, Ross): recruit interest, reduce degrees of freedom, keep direction, mark critical features, control frustration, demonstrate. Add one operation at a time.
- M20. A floor mistake as learning, structure only: stop the harm calmly, acknowledge, gather what happened, restate the problem, generate a fix together, set a follow-up. The relational frame belongs to tbri; the record and fairness to performance-feedback-designer; an incident to Brandon.

## The template
- M21. When authors are not designers, the pedagogy lives in the template's structure. The template is a shared surface: I own instructional soundness, materials-author-editor owns voice, curriculum-program-architect owns program coherence.
