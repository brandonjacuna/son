# Models: Curriculum and Program Architect

Read when testing a threshold, choosing a sequencing logic, diagnosing an existing program, or writing a transfer goal or coherence finding.

## M1. Threshold versus hard topic
A threshold concept is transformative (changes how the learner sees the role), probably irreversible, integrative (connects parts that looked separate), bounded, and troublesome (runs against intuition). It needs a different architecture: room, a spiral, and an expected half-held period. A hard topic is just hard; it needs pacing.

## M2. Epitome, then elaboration
Open with one simple but complete authentic whole (a full run of a shift end to end), then elaborate each part in later passes. The learner holds the whole from the start and hangs detail on it.

## M3. Alignment
Constructive alignment: the outcome verb, the practice verb, and the assessment verb match. Vertical alignment runs across time (what each stage builds on); horizontal alignment runs across concurrent tracks (front and back of house learning the same service at once).

## M4. Three sequencing logics
Prerequisite, simple-to-complex, and spiral. Choose deliberately and often combine them. The macro order across the program is this seat's; the micro order inside a module is the instructional designer's.

## M5. Diagnostic for an existing program
1. Recognize what currently sequences it: content, discipline, expert path, or readiness.
2. Check expectancies: a one-sentence goal, a traceable chain, named thresholds, an I/R/M map, matching verbs. Absences are the diagnosis.
3. Mentally walk a novice through it and note where they would stall.
4. Test: trace one terminal competency back to entry; every link must be held before the next.

## M6. Output exemplars
- Transfer goal done right: "On their own, a person can run a station through a full service, read where it is failing, and recover it, without being told." Not "understands station operations." The first is a performance in a new setting; the second is coverage language.
- Coherence finding done right: "Modules 4 and 7 both teach pacing, module 4 as a floor skill and module 7 as a kitchen constraint, and the map shows no connection between them. A learner will hold two unrelated ideas of the same thing. Connect them or co-locate."

## M7. Program, not manual
The unit of design is a program of study built like an institution, mostly digital, with the platform downstream. In a flat house, the school likely doubles as the career-development and retention system; cross-training is the dependency chain made visible as a path toward internal succession. Both are positions to test, not settled facts.
