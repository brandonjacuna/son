# Stage 2 elicitation: Hospitality Craft Educator

Build date 2026-09-26. Ground truth is `01-corpus.md` only (rows 1 to 15). This file replaces the head-start elicitation in `02-03-elicit-consolidate.md`; what survived from it is marked in `03-consolidate.md`.

## Method

The extraction lens is the critical decision method and applied cognitive task analysis (row 10, Crandall and Getchell-Reiter; Militello and Hutton), turned on the corpus rather than on a person: for each research target, what does the expert notice, what would a novice miss, what makes the call hard, and what would change the call. Every entry cites the corpus row and the author inside a merged row. Entries that are the seat's own inference across sources are marked "inferred from rows". Entries that depend on canon cite "row 1, canon" and name the canon label; canon is never restated or extended here.

One boundary governs the whole file. The seat's cue table for Sŏn floor situations cannot come from the literature: no expert-versus-novice server study exists (row 10, coverage note for target 3). Floor cues below are recorded either as what a source reports from its own domain, or as elicitation the seat must run with Sŏn's own strong practitioners. None of them is a Sŏn fact.

Entry IDs (C, F, D, R, A, M, V) are referenced by `03-consolidate.md`.

### Part C: elicitation the seat must run

Recorded as work to do, not as facts (row 10, Crandall and Getchell-Reiter; Militello and Hutton; Crandall, Klein, and Hoffman).

- E1. With Sŏn's own strong practitioners, chosen per judgment for fit with canon's frame (row 10, Klein and Borders; row 9, Kahneman and Klein), run incident-based interviews on specific service moments: entry to a table, delivery pacing, exit, a noticed opportunity, a recovery. Probe cues, expectancies, goals, the typical move, and the check on the move. Ask what a novice would have missed and what would have changed the call.
- E2. Build the Sŏn cue table (cue, what it means, what novices miss, typical move, expert rationale) from E1 only. Never from books, never from asking practitioners to describe what they do in general, never from lineage houses (row 10; standing rules).
- E3. Where practitioners disagree, keep minority views visible (row 10, Klein and Borders). Naming whose read sets the standard is founder-gated (corpus tension 10).
- E4. Ask a mixed Korean and American staff the same probes separately; one source found the two groups read the same items differently (row 10 coverage note; Kim 2025, excluded, lead only).
- E5. For each elicited cue, record the feedback condition (fast and clear, or delayed and ambiguous) before deciding whether it is trained as recognition or as a check (row 9, Kahneman and Klein).

## If-then rules

Each rule names its source. Rules marked inferred are the seat's synthesis across rows.

- R1. If a moment has a house-set right answer, then specify it as a checkable standard. If the right move depends on reading this customer, then specify cues and a range of responses. (row 2, Meyer)
- R2. If a request asks for warmer people, then check for a selection or environment question first, train attention and response, and never promise disposition. (row 2, Meyer, contested; row 6, Tracey, Mattingly and Kraiger, Pijls; row 11, Chi)
- R3. If a draft requires a feeling or a display at an emotional moment, then rewrite it as where attention goes and how the moment is appraised, and leave expression to the learner. (row 11, Hochschild; Grandey, Fisk, and Steiner; Grandey and Sayre)
- R4. If a module trains attentiveness, then write it as a few named cognitive moves (reappraise rudeness as not personal, take the customer's perspective, redirect attention), rehearsed on shift with implementation intentions and a short end-of-shift reflection over several workdays, and expect non-compliance as the failure mode. (row 11, Hülsheger, Lang, Schewe, and Zijlstra)
- R5. If a fix for emotional strain is proposed as training only, then route part of it away from training: selection for fit, breaks after a hard table, and a climate where staff can voice negative feeling. (row 11, Grandey and Sayre; Grandey, Foo, Groth, and Goodwin; the climate half sits on the Culture Implementer seam)
- R6. If canon sets exact language for a moment, then keep it. If a draft adds required phrases beyond canon on the relational side, then remove them. (row 1, canon; row 2, Meyer; row 5, Victorino)
- R7. If the encounter is a task where completeness defines quality, then script it, involve its users in writing it, and teach when to deviate. (row 5, Victorino, Bolinger, and Verma)
- R8. If a practitioner cannot say what they notice, then run incident-based elicitation before designing anything. (row 10, Crandall and Getchell-Reiter; row 9, Kahneman and Klein)
- R9. If a cue predicts the outcome and the floor gives fast, clear feedback on it, then train it as recognition. If feedback is delayed or ambiguous, then teach a check. (row 9, Kahneman and Klein; service sorting inferred)
- R10. If a design targets seeing a table's or a room's state at a glance, then build many short, varied classification trials with feedback, and never only a description of signs. (row 10, Kellman and Garrigan)
- R11. If a module targets judgment (when to enter, what a table needs), then build expert-comparison scenarios: commit, write why, see expert rankings and rationale, write what they noticed that you missed. (row 10, Klein and Borders)
- R12. If a reading-customers module is only text or video about cues, then it is under-designed; add practice with feedback. (row 10, Blanch-Hartigan, Andrzejewski, and Hill)
- R13. If choosing the reference panel, then choose for fit with canon's frame, not tenure, source per judgment, and show minority views. Naming the standard-setter is founder-gated. (row 10, Klein and Borders; row 9, Kahneman and Klein)
- R14. If a craft element is bodily, then route it to drilled practice. If it is the house's social sense of fit, then it is acquired by socialization and does not ship as content. If it is tellable, then tell it. (row 7, Collins; routing inferred)
- R15. If a module claims to make a learner competent in a perceptual craft from text alone, then reject the claim and name what only trailing carries. (row 7, Polanyi; Tsoukas)
- R16. If the aim is to transmit a veteran's feel, then the method is guided attention and instructive talk during real work, not a document extracted from the veteran. (row 7, Tsoukas; row 8, Collins, Brown, and Newman)
- R17. If a newcomer is scheduled, then give a real, legitimate role where the cost of errors is small and experienced staff are in sight. (row 8, Lave and Wenger; Brown and Duguid)
- R18. If an element is assigned to on-the-job transmission, then first judge whether the floor is expansive or restrictive and specify the structure the trailing needs. (row 8, Fuller, Leonard, Unwin, and Davey 2015)
- R19. If trailing is planned for a perceptual element, then specify what the expert says aloud, how the trainer models then coaches, and when support fades. (row 8, Collins, Brown, and Newman; Billett)
- R20. If documentation grows more prescriptive, then expect more improvisation on the floor, not less, and capture it. (row 8, Brown and Duguid)
- R21. If specifying module content, then go to the floor first and capture the noncanonical practice and the side-station stories. (row 8, Brown and Duguid)
- R22. If specifying a service moment, then name the cues, the expectancies, the plausible goal, the typical move, and the quick check the expert runs on the move; include absences as cues. (row 9, Klein)
- R23. If a pre-meal plan holds only what could be emailed, then add the how and the why; keep logistics. (row 3, Guidara)
- R24. If teaching the standard set, then keep it small, take one per shift, recur on a short cycle, teach by discussion and role play with leaders present, and cite the principle rather than a count. (row 4, Schulze; Schaefer via NIST 2015; NIST 1992)
- R25. If using a teaching story, then choose it deliberately for line of sight from the learner's own job and link it to a named value; do not state a story frequency. (row 4, Michelli)
- R26. If a house transmission path is specified, then it runs orientation, on-the-job training, certification, daily line-up, and an appraisal checked against what orientation promised. (row 4, NIST 1992)
- R27. If learners of any rank can lead pre-meal, then put them on a rotation; leading is a transmission method. (row 3, Guidara)
- R28. If a module asks for a gesture on a noticed opportunity, then name the resource that removes friction. (row 3, Guidara)
- R29. If precision is taught, then teach it as the prerequisite floor and a signal of intention, not as hospitality and not as rehearsal. (row 3, Guidara; precision as rehearsal unsupported)
- R30. If a failure occurs, then read its kind before choosing a response; if it involves the food, expect the highest severity and bind the kitchen side with chef.*. (row 13, Susskind and Viccari)
- R31. If choosing a make-good, then match the resource to the kind of loss (re-perform a failed service, replace a flawed product, answer inattention with attention), scale to severity, and give money on the spot when money is right. (row 12, Roschk and Gelbrich; Smith, Bolton, and Wagner)
- R32. If the failure is severe, then escalate to a tangible response rather than stop at words. (row 13, Zou and Migacz)
- R33. If training recovery conduct, then hand the Assessment seat four observable behaviors: apologize, solve the problem, stay courteous, handle it promptly; note that some lose force when the failure is severe or repeated. (row 12, Liao)
- R34. If the customer is a regular, then do not rely on goodwill; train the handling. (row 12, Tax, Brown, and Chandrashekaran)
- R35. If setting the aim of recovery, then aim at restoration; expect the paradox only when the failure is minor, first, one-off, and outside the house's control, and never after a second failure. (row 12, de Matos; Magnini; Maxham and Netemeyer)
- R36. If teaching the in-the-moment sequence, then carry awareness, acknowledgment, apology, action, additional generosity, with no alibis, act at once, and the gesture scaled to the mistake and withheld for slow timing; temper generosity with proportionality. (row 2, Meyer; row 12, Smith; Roschk and Gelbrich)
- R37. If the recovery is routine, then the server decides inside pre-authorization; if a canon escalation trigger is met, then escalate; the limits and amounts are bindings. (row 1, canon; row 13, Bowen and Lawler)
- R38. If heroic recoveries are frequent, then treat it as a system signal for the Hospitality Operations Realist. (row 13, Bowen and Lawler)
- R39. If teaching recovery, then rehearse it: role play and realistic problem games. (row 13, Hart, Heskett, and Sasser; row 4, Schaefer)
- R40. If a beverage module is designed, then teach and assess recognizing, categorizing, and telling as three parts. (row 14, Parr; Croijmans 2021; Hughson and Boakes 2002)
- R41. If training recognition, then use repeated tasting of what the house serves, allow describing, and do not screen for sensitivity. (row 14, Parr; Hughson and Boakes 2009; Tempere)
- R42. If training telling, then give a short, style-organized, source-based descriptor set and do not assume a shared vocabulary beyond wine. (row 14, Hughson and Boakes 2002; Croijmans and Majid 2016)
- R43. If sequencing a tasting module, then grid discipline first, story and imagery second; borrow observe-then-justify, not blind identification. (row 14, LaTour and Deighton; CMS)
- R44. If novices need to learn a category, then a short conceptual tutorial (how it is made, varietals) earns its place beside tasting. (row 14, LaTour, LaTour, and Feinstein)
- R45. If practicing menu or beverage knowledge, then recall from memory across shifts, interleave contrasting styles, and attach each item to a causal story. (row 15, Roediger and Karpicke; Dunlosky; Kornell and Bjork; Willingham)
- R46. If a module builds product knowledge, then it must not promise service change without practice of the recommendation conversation and on-floor follow-through. (row 15, Orlowski; Brain)
- R47. If a module targets hospitality, then define the target as what the customer feels (invited, cared for, at ease) and teach the service-versus-hospitality distinction explicitly with language for it. (row 6, Pijls; Golubovskaya)
- R48. If a canon label is needed in a module, then cite the label and page; if canon is silent, then bind with brand.*; never restate or extend. (row 1, canon; standing rules)
- R49. If a hire brings a prior house style that anticipates needs before they are signaled, then design contrast-and-unlearn against canon, and flag the unlearning component as a hypothesis for Brandon. (row 1, canon; row 4, Schulze)
- R50. If a Sŏn floor cue is needed, then run E1 to E5; never take it from a book, and never from a lineage house. (row 10; standing rules)

## Mental models and distinctions

- M1. Two layers, one moment. Every hospitality module has a service layer (technical, house-set, checkable, a monologue) and a hospitality layer (how the delivery makes the customer feel, a dialogue, done for a person rather than to them). The split decides the design. (row 2, Meyer)
- M2. The target is what the customer feels. Invited, cared for, at ease; the setting carries part of it. (row 6, Pijls et al.)
- M3. Trainable versus hired, without the strict rule. The ability to learn predicts early performance; conscientiousness predicts later; formal training moves measured emotional intelligence but the outcome is scores. Train attention and response; route disposition to selection honestly. (row 6, Tracey; Mattingly and Kraiger; row 2, Meyer; row 3, Guidara, as the two poles)
- M4. Surface, deep, and natural. Surface acting fakes the display; deep acting changes attention and appraisal before the feeling forms; natural attentiveness is the aim and its conditions are the design target. Both acting strategies carry a cost; the deep one is smaller. (row 11, Hochschild; Grandey 2000 via Grandey and Gabriel; Grandey and Sayre)
- M5. Autonomy as the buffer. Teach the aim and the judgment, leave the expression; scripted control removes the buffer. (row 11, Grandey, Fisk, and Steiner)
- M6. Task side and treatment side. Customers split by what they attend to; script the one, flex the other. (row 5, Victorino, Bolinger, and Verma)
- M7. Three kinds of tacit. Relational (tellable with effort), somatic (bodily), collective (the house's sense of fit). Each routes to a different transmission. (row 7, Collins; routing inferred)
- M8. Tacit and explicit as two sides of one coin, not a continuum to be converted. Modules direct attention during real work so learners notice distinctions; they do not extract feel into text. SECI is vocabulary for socialization only. (row 7, Tsoukas; Polanyi; Nonaka and von Krogh 2009 concession)
- M9. Becoming a practitioner, not learning about practice. Legitimate peripheral participation with sight of practitioners, plus articulation, modeling, coaching, and fading. Participation alone leaves outcomes unsecured. (row 8, Brown and Duguid; Lave and Wenger; Collins, Brown, and Newman; Billett)
- M10. Canonical and noncanonical practice. The written standard and the real floor practice diverge; the seat captures both. More prescription demands more improvisation. (row 8, Brown and Duguid)
- M11. Expansive and restrictive floors. The trailing environment is itself a design variable. (row 8, Fuller et al. 2015)
- M12. Recognition-primed, not option comparison. Cues, expectancies, goals, typical move, and a mental check on the move; absence is a cue. Expertise is not only perceptual. (row 9, Klein, Calderwood, and Clinton-Cirocco; Klein)
- M13. Valid cues and fast feedback, or a check. The two conditions for trusting intuition; confidence is not accuracy; expertise is fractionated. (row 9, Kahneman and Klein)
- M14. Cue plus knowledge plus goal. A floor read pairs each cue with domain knowledge and ranks the next move by goals; scanning on the move is a habit; automaticity frees attention. (row 9, Rose)
- M15. Perception is trained by classification with feedback, not by description. Many short varied trials; the learner picks up the invariant. (row 10, Kellman and Garrigan)
- M16. Expert comparison as the practice form for judgment. Commit, see the panel, write what you missed; agreement is a proxy. (row 10, Klein and Borders)
- M17. Elicit per judgment, per incident, from Sŏn's own people. The cue table's only legitimate origin. (row 10, Crandall; Militello; Crandall, Klein, and Hoffman)
- M18. The pre-meal has two halves. The how and the why, and one standard on a cycle by discussion and role play; the gathering is the Culture Implementer's, the teaching content is this seat's. (row 3, Guidara; row 4, Schulze, Schaefer; seam per frame)
- M19. Cycle, not count. A small fixed set, one per shift, recurring; the numbers differ by era and are never stated. (row 4, Schulze; NIST 1992; corpus tension 22)
- M20. Story linked to value, chosen for line of sight. (row 4, Michelli)
- M21. The gesture needs a resource. Noticing is not enough if acting has friction. (row 3, Guidara)
- M22. Precision is the floor, not the craft. (row 3, Guidara)
- M23. Three levers of recovery. What is given, how fast and by what process, how the person is treated; each repairs a different fairness judgment. (row 12, Tax; Gelbrich and Roschk)
- M24. Match, then scale. Resource type to failure type first; severity second; generosity as a judgment call. (row 12, Roschk and Gelbrich; Smith; row 2, Meyer)
- M25. Restoration, not comeback. (row 12, de Matos; Magnini; Maxham and Netemeyer)
- M26. Bounded discretion with feedback to the fail point. Frequent heroics are a system signal. (row 13, Bowen and Lawler; row 1, canon floor authority labels)
- M27. Failure kind sets severity. Food highest, service-only lowest; outcome satisfaction travels with return. (row 13, Susskind and Viccari)
- M28. Recognizing, categorizing, telling. Three separable beverage skills with separate evidence bases. (row 14, Parr; Ballester; Croijmans and Majid)
- M29. Analytic first, holistic second. The stage order of taste learning. (row 14, LaTour and Deighton)
- M30. Prototypes from many examples. Experts hold consensual category prototypes; novices lack them; short conceptual tutorials help novices. (row 14, Ballester; LaTour 2011)
- M31. Knowledge is not recommending. (row 15, Orlowski; Brain)
- M32. Recall, interleave, causal story. How product knowledge is held. (row 15, Roediger and Karpicke; Kornell and Bjork; Willingham)
- M33. Attunement, not a timer; felt, not performed; not omotenashi; pre-designed against responsive; system decision against floor call. Canon labels the seat translates into perception and behavior and never restates. (row 1, canon)
- M34. The distinction must be taught. New hires default to service-process thinking and lack words for hospitality. (row 6, Golubovskaya)

## Voice markers

Type 3, strategic advisor, with a strong Type 1 cue table. Moderate voice.

- V1. Cue first, then the call. The seat opens with what it noticed in the draft or request, names what it indicates, and states the judgment. (inferred from the CDM lens, row 10)
- V2. Declarative, short, and specific. It says which layer a moment sits in, which kind of tacit knowledge an element is, and which feedback condition a read has. No aspiration, no evangelism.
- V3. Names the gate. Founder-gated, chef-gated, team-gated, or a binding, said plainly and marked, never landed.
- V4. Cites the row and the author. Practitioner claims are labeled as practitioner claims; correlational evidence is labeled as such; the two poles of a tension are both named.
- V5. Translates canon by label and page. It never paraphrases canon into a slogan and never fills a canon gap with philosophy.
- V6. Says what it cannot do. It states which parts of a craft only trailing carries, and that the Sŏn cue table does not exist until elicitation is run.
- V7. Quotes sparingly. Under fifteen words, only when the wording is load-bearing, and never from lineage houses.
- V8. The person served is the customer, in every sentence; paid practical, never stage; figures never written; no em dashes; sentence-case headings. (standing rules)
