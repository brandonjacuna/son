## Unresolved tensions

1. **Number of options.** Row 15 says three good choices usually suffice. Row 6's decision points carried three to six options. **Hold both:** three for recognition items, more when ranking is the task, as in expert comparison.
2. **A facilitator or not.** Row 6 gains came with no facilitator. Rows 12 and 13 put the learning in a skilled facilitator's debrief. **Hold both:** expert comparison can run self-serve; role-play cannot.
3. **Anyone can facilitate, but debriefing is a skill.** Brandon's answer is that any trained member can run live practice. Rows 12 and 13 make facilitation the hard part, and row 12's authors report it is hard to teach. **Implication:** "trained" has to include debrief training. The spec for that training is a design question for this seat and the Instructional Designer. Who is trained, and when, is the Operations Realist's and the Architect's.
4. **The AI presenter.** Row 11 shows a felt-safety benefit (non-judgmental) but no transfer advantage. Brandon has not ruled AI presenters out, and the policy is founder-gated (`founder.ai_presenter_policy`). **Hold:** a narrow position, marked.
5. **Interleaving ownership.** The Instructional Designer's profile claims interleaving across shifts. Row 8 grounds interleaving inside a drill. **Resolved as a seam:** the module schedule is theirs, and the drill's contrast set is mine.
6. **The upstream craft content is unaudited.** The Hospitality Craft Educator profile skipped Stage 6. **Hold:** consume its cues as elicited Sŏn content only. Its other claims are theirs to answer for.
7. **The frozen learner.** No source addresses it. **Hold as an inference** leaning on row 12 (safety), row 13 (strategy), and the TBRI seat's felt-safety precedence.
8. **Canon names a tool.** Page 08 names the reservation system and specific devices. **Resolve:** every tool reference in a scenario is a binding, never written as fact.
