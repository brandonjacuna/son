# Taking an allergy at the table

## Activate

A customer who tells you about an allergy is trusting the whole house with their safety. They cannot see the kitchen. You are the only link between what they said and what arrives on the plate. Most allergy incidents do not come from a cook ignoring a ticket. They come from something said at the table that never reached the ticket, or reached it in a softer form than the customer meant.

### Experienced

You have taken allergies before. The difference here is one rule that may be new: you never judge severity. "A little allergic" gets the same routing as "anaphylactic." Your experience is exactly what tempts you to make that call. Do not.

## Demonstrate

Watch the modeled version first ({{bind:tool.video.allergy_model_link}}). Here is what the expert is doing, and why.

1. **Hears it in any form.** An allergy arrives as "I can't do shellfish," "no nuts for her," "is there dairy in that," or a glance at a menu item followed by a question. The expert treats every one of these as an allergy until the customer says otherwise.
2. **Asks the three confirming questions.** What exactly are you avoiding? Is it an allergy or a preference? Does it include traces, shared equipment, or cooking oil? The expert asks all three even when the answer seems obvious, because the third one is where the surprises live.
3. **Repeats it back.** "So no shellfish at all, including anything cooked alongside it. I'm going to flag that with the kitchen." The repeat-back catches mishearing and tells the customer they were heard.
4. **Never judges severity.** If the customer says "it's fine if it's just a bit," the expert says, "I'll still flag it so the kitchen can tell you exactly what's safe." Severity is the customer's to know and the kitchen's to handle. Not ours to downgrade.
5. **Routes it before anything fires.** Flag on the ticket ({{bind:tool.pos.allergen_flag}}), then confirm with the kitchen ({{bind:workflow.kitchen_allergy_confirm}}). The order does not fire until both happen.
6. **Knows where allergens hide.** Fermented, pickled, and sauced items can carry allergens that are not visible on the plate or obvious from the menu name. Which of ours do is in the allergen matrix ({{bind:chef.allergen_matrix}}). When in doubt, the answer is "let me confirm with the kitchen," never a guess.

## Practice

1. Run the branching scenario in `scenarios.md` (in Trainual, the uploaded scenario package). Take the path you would really take first, then go back and try the others.
2. Role-play with a partner using the rubric in `assessment.md`. Your partner plays three customers: one clear, one vague, one who minimizes it. Switch roles.

## Retrieve

- Next shift: without looking, say the three confirming questions to your lead at pre-shift.
- One week later: the short test in Trainual (randomized, so it will not be the same questions twice).
- One month later: a single "what would you do" at pre-shift.

## Integrate

On your next shift, repeat back every allergy or restriction you hear, including the ones that sound small. After service, note one time you did it and how the customer responded. If an allergy was ever mentioned and not flagged, you stop the order, tell {{bind:people.allergy_escalation_contact}}, and go back to the table before any food lands. That is a recovery, and it is the right move every time.
