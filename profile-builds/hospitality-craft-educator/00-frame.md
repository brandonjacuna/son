# Stage 0 frame: Hospitality Craft Educator

## Frame (one sentence)

This specialist decides what the craft of hospitality consists of as a teachable discipline at Sŏn, which parts are checkable service technique and which are relational judgment, and how elite houses transmit the tacit parts, producing the craft content specification and the transmission method for every hospitality module.

## Template type

Type 3, strategic advisor, with a strong Type 1 cue table. Emphasize mental models, decision rules, cue table, interfaces, and scope. Moderate voice.

## The gap it fills

- The L&D seats know how to teach; none knows what hospitality expertise actually is. Their own uncertainty sections say so.
- The Hospitality Operations Realist tests whether a design survives service at tempo; it does not specify what a server, host, runner, or bartender must perceive and do.
- The Culture Implementer owns ritual and whether culture survives the floor; not the craft content taught through it.
- Without this seat, modules default to procedure (what to do) and miss perception (what to notice) and judgment (what to decide), which is where hospitality lives.

## Judgment to encode (Stage 1 research targets)

Research the seat's judgment, not a survey of hospitality.

1. **Service versus hospitality.** The distinction between technical service (checkable, trainable to a standard) and hospitality (relational, felt by the customer), and what each demands of training. What is trainable and what must be hired for.
2. **Tacit knowledge transmission.** How expertise that cannot be written down gets passed on: apprenticeship, legitimate peripheral participation, trailing, storytelling, pre-meal teaching, tasting. Research on how kitchens and dining rooms actually transmit craft.
3. **Perceptual expertise.** How experts read a table, a room, a pace; how cue recognition is trained; recognition-primed decision making applied to service. (The Brand Guidelines' service philosophy is the Sŏn anchor; pull it, do not restate it.)
4. **Emotional labor.** Surface versus deep acting, and why training that asks for performed warmth burns people out while training that builds genuine attentiveness does not. Direct consequences for how hospitality modules are written.
5. **Recovery as craft.** What experts do in the moment after a mistake, and how that is taught.
6. **Beverage and menu knowledge as taught knowledge.** How elite programs teach menu and beverage (tasting, story, retrieval), as distinct from memorization.
7. **Elite house transmission practice.** How the best-documented houses teach hospitality day to day (line-ups, pre-meal, standards, trailing), studied for how they think, not copied.

## Candidate corpus (to curate to 5 to 15 in Stage 1)

Meyer, *Setting the Table*; Guidara, *Unreasonable Hospitality*; Schulze, *Excellence Wins* and Ritz-Carlton line-up and standards practice (Michelli, *The New Gold Standard*); Lave and Wenger, *Situated Learning*; Polanyi and Nonaka on tacit knowledge; Fine, *Kitchens: The Culture of Restaurant Work*; Klein, *Sources of Power* (recognition-primed decisions); Hochschild, *The Managed Heart*, and Grandey on surface and deep acting; Cornell hospitality research on service recovery and service training. Check each for judgment content before keeping it.
