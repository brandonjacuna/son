## Unresolved tensions

Carried from `01-corpus.md` where they bear on the seat's judgment, plus new ones from Stage 2. Not smoothed over. Each ends with what the seat does in the meantime.

1. Can caring be taught? Meyer, Lutz via NIST, and Schulze route it to selection; Guidara says everything can be taught; Mattingly and Kraiger show training moves measured emotional intelligence, but the outcome is scores (rows 2, 3, 4, 6). Meanwhile: train attention, reading, and response; route disposition honestly; never promise warmth (T2).
2. What to hire for. Meyer's 51-49 weighting and managers' preference for agreeableness against Tracey's finding that the ability to learn predicts early performance (rows 2, 6). Meanwhile: keep hiring out of the module's promise; design early modules assuming learning speed varies.
3. Where hospitality lives. Meyer puts it in disposition and dialogue; Pijls finds the setting carries part of it; Golubovskaya points to organizational practices (rows 2, 6). Meanwhile: not every hospitality gap is a training gap; check the environment before writing a module.
4. Standard language against rote. Ritz-Carlton common language by daily repetition; Meyer's rote critique; the Saveur pull-back; Victorino's task-treatment split is customer-perception research and recommends training deep acting, which row 11 prices (rows 2, 3, 4, 5, 11). Meanwhile: T4, and canon-set language stays.
5. Conversion of tacit knowledge. Nonaka against Polanyi and Tsoukas; Collins reconciles in part (row 7). Meanwhile: classify each element before choosing a medium (T5).
6. Participation against designed teaching. Lave and Wenger against Collins, Billett, and Fuller (row 8). Meanwhile: trailing necessary, not sufficient; specify its structure (T6).
7. Documented standards against actual practice. Brown and Duguid against a standards-led house (rows 4, 8). Meanwhile: write standards and capture the noncanonical practice and stories.
8. Trusting a read against checking it. Recognition-primed decisions against the validity and feedback conditions; customers mask feeling (row 9; Puccinelli excluded). Meanwhile: sort reads by feedback condition (T7); canon's attunement model is the anchor, not a new rule.
9. Fast off-floor gains against slow floor-built skill. ShadowBox and perceptual learning modules show quick proxy gains; Rose and Klein say the skill takes volume and varied cases (rows 9, 10). Meanwhile: off-floor practice speeds trailing and does not replace it.
10. Who counts as the expert. Expertise is fractionated; ShadowBox dropped experts for frame fit and showed minority views (rows 9, 10). Meanwhile: source per judgment, show minority views; naming whose read sets the Sŏn standard is founder-gated; lineage houses cannot serve as the reference without Brandon.
11. Is deep acting costly? Humphrey et al. (excluded) say no; Grandey and Sayre and Hochschild say yes, small (row 11). Meanwhile: aim at natural attentiveness and autonomy; never sell deep acting as free.
12. Should display rules exist at all? Grandey, Rupp, and Brice against Humphrey et al. and the hospitality meta-analyses (row 11; Xu et al. excluded). Meanwhile: the narrower rule, no scripted feeling, discretion over expression, canon-set language stays.
13. Is surface acting always harmful to performance? Small negative link overall; pays for extraverts; penalized mainly when detected (row 11; Groth excluded). Meanwhile: "fake it" is a fit matter, not a training one.
14. Generosity against proportionality. Meyer's err-toward-generosity against Smith's proportionality and Roschk and Gelbrich's matching (rows 2, 12). Meanwhile: match first, scale second, generosity as a judgment call.
15. Comeback against restore. Meyer's great last chapter against the paradox evidence (rows 2, 12). Meanwhile: aim at restoration.
16. Break the rules against bound the discretion. Hart against Bowen and Lawler (row 13). Meanwhile: bounded discretion; the limits are bindings; canon's escalation triggers govern.
17. No alibis against explanation. Meyer rules out excuses; explanation research (Bradley and Sparks, unverified) suggests a credible explanation can help (row 2). Meanwhile: carry no alibis; leave explanation open.
18. Perceptual against cognitive basis of wine expertise. Parr against Ballester and Hughson and Boakes; Croijmans 2021 against Croijmans and Majid 2016 (row 14). Meanwhile: different tasks; train and assess all three parts.
19. Verbal overshadowing. Parr proposes it; Hughson and Boakes 2009 found describing helped (row 14). Meanwhile: allow describing.
20. Generalization of sensory training. Tempere's compound-specific gains against Wang's small pilot (row 14). Meanwhile: train on what the house serves.
21. Training and sales. Correlational positives (excluded) against quasi-experimental nulls (row 15). Meanwhile: knowledge alone does not move behavior; require practice of the recommendation.
22. Ritz-Carlton specifics. Story frequency and standard count differ by source and era (row 4). Meanwhile: cite the principle, not a number.
23. Pre-meal purpose. Guidara's how-and-why against Ritz-Carlton's one standard by repetition and role play (rows 3, 4). Meanwhile: two halves of one slot (T11).
24. Precision as rehearsal. The sibling Operations Realist profile carries it; this build could not confirm it (row 3). Meanwhile: teach precision as prerequisite; flagged for Brandon, not edited.
25. Early learning speed against long-run fit. Tracey (ability to learn early) against Chi and Humphrey (person-job fit decides whether emotional labor helps or harms) (rows 6, 11). Meanwhile: both may hold; they answer who learns fast and who lasts.
26. New. Which Sŏn floor cues are valid. No expert-versus-novice server study exists; every floor cue in Stage 2 is from another domain or a single account (rows 2, 9, 10). Meanwhile: E1 to E5; no cue is content until elicited and its feedback condition is recorded.
