## Test 1: a lead asks for a module that makes servers warmer

Frame candidate 2. Tests: trainable versus hired; routes disposition, trains attention.

### Output

"Before building this, I read the request. 'Warmer' is a disposition word, and a module cannot install disposition. Training moves measured emotional intelligence, not felt care on a floor, so I will not promise warmth.

Three checks come first:

1. **Selection.** If the gap is who was hired, route it to the People Systems Designer. No module fixes a hiring bar.
2. **The room.** The setting carries part of what the customer feels. If staff are in the weeds with no slack, warmth has no resource behind it. Route that to the Hospitality Operations Realist.
3. **The distinction.** New hires default to service-process thinking. Do they have words for hospitality at all? If not, teach the service-versus-hospitality split explicitly.

What is left is trainable: attention and response. I specify that as a few named cognitive moves:

- reappraise rudeness as not personal
- take the customer's perspective
- redirect attention to the table

These are rehearsed on shift, with implementation intentions and a short end-of-shift reflection over several workdays, not one classroom session. The expected failure mode is non-compliance.

No required smiles or phrases beyond what canon page 08 sets. Canon's label is attention felt, not performed.

The cues a server reads to know attention is needed come from incident-based elicitation with Sŏn's own practitioners. They do not come from me, and they are elicitation-gated.

- To the Assessment seat: observable attention behaviors.
- To the Practice and Simulation Designer: the rehearsal."

## Test 2: review a draft pre-shift greeting script with required phrases

Frame candidate 3. Tests: felt versus performed; keeps canon-set language, removes scripted feeling.

The draft used: (a) "Welcome to Sŏn, we're so happy you're here!" said with a smile, required; (b) confirm party name, count, and any allergy; (c) "Is this a special occasion?" required at every table; (d) "Enjoy your evening!" at seating; (e) "Servers should feel excited to greet each table."

### Output

"I split each line by layer.

- **(b) is task side.** Completeness defines quality here, so the script stays. The staff who use it should help write it, and the module teaches when to deviate. The allergen capture path is a tool binding.
- **(a) and (d) sit at arrival.** Canon page 08 sets greeting and arrival language. Wherever these lines match canon, they stay verbatim. Wherever they go beyond it, they come out. The required smile comes out: authenticity, not smile extent, moved customers. I cannot confirm the canon wording from here, so each line is marked 'check against page 08' and I do not paraphrase canon.
- **(c) is treatment side, scripted.** One fixed question at every table reads as going through the motions. Replace it with the cue to read: what signals an occasion. Give a range of responses in the server's own words. The cue itself is elicitation-gated.
- **(e) is a feeling rule.** A feeling cannot be assigned. Rewrite it as where attention goes at approach, and leave the expression to the server."
