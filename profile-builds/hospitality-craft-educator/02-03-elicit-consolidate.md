# Stages 2 and 3: elicitation and consolidation, Hospitality Craft Educator

## Decision requirements table

| Difficult judgment | Why hard | Cues | Strategy | Novice error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is this skill service or hospitality? | Most tasks carry both; the split decides the whole design | Is there a right answer set by us (monologue) or does the right move depend on reading this customer (dialogue)? | Split the module: standard parts get checkable objectives; responsive parts get perception and judgment practice | Writing hospitality as a procedure, or leaving a checkable standard vague |
| Is this trainable, or a hiring question? | Practitioners hold that emotional skills are mostly hired; some training still moves attention and appraisal | The request asks a module to make people care, be warm, or want to | Train what is trainable (what to notice, why it matters, the range of good responses); route disposition questions to hiring seats | Building a module to install warmth, which produces performed warmth |
| Will this module produce felt or performed attention? | Scripts are the easiest thing to train and check, and they drive surface acting | Required phrases, required smiles, fixed lines for emotional moments | Teach intent and cues; allow the learner's own words; keep fixed lines only where canon sets exact language | Scripting the emotional register, which canon forbids and research links to burnout |
| Can this be learned from a screen? | Tacit knowledge resists description | The expert cannot say what they notice; learners pass the module and still miss it on the floor | Make the module the preparation and debrief around a trail; externalize the expert's cues first | Shipping tacit craft as e-learning and calling it trained |
| What does the expert actually notice? | Experts are unaware of their own cues | Answers like "you just know" or "you feel it" | Elicit with critical-incident questions and expert comparison; name the cues | Teaching the expert's actions without the cues that trigger them |
| How does canon become teachable behavior? | Canon states philosophy and decisions; the floor needs perception and action | Canon uses words like attunement, felt, earned, pause | Translate each canon decision into cues, decisions, and actions; never restate or extend canon | Paraphrasing canon into slogans, or inventing canon to fill a gap |

| Cue | Indicates | Source | Judgment |
|---|---|---|---|

## Consolidated themes

1. **Two crafts, two designs.** Service and hospitality are taught differently. Every module declares which parts are which.
2. **Attention, not performance.** Canon's "felt not performed" and the emotional labor evidence point the same way: teach what to notice and why, never scripted feeling.
3. **Perception is the skill.** Hospitality expertise is recognition of cues. The seat's core deliverable is the cue set for each skill.
4. **Tacit craft moves through people.** Modules prepare, name, and debrief what the trail transmits. They do not replace it.
5. **Daily reinforcement beats one-time training.** The line-up is the proven vehicle. One item, told through a real story.
6. **Canon is the source, translation is the job.** The seat turns canon into teachable perception and action and flags gaps, never filling them.
7. **Trainable versus hired.** The seat protects modules from being asked to do selection's job.

Unresolved tension kept for Stage 6: whether emotional skills are trainable. Meyer's practitioner stance says mostly not; deep-acting research suggests appraisal and attention can be shifted. The seat holds both: do not try to install disposition, do train attention and appraisal.
