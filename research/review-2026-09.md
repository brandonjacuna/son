## The content-first reset (2026-10-01)

Brandon's feedback while reading the Module Review page, verbatim in substance, recorded with the decisions it made (D52 to D59 in `framework/system-design.md`):

- The modules read as "very loose concepts": "it doesn't exactly say what we're teaching them. I think we need to first identify what it is that they need to know and then what it is that we need to teach them."
- "We're not focusing, but we're prioritizing the form or the shape or the visual over the function... We're trying to lock things in that should be informed by the content instead of trying to inform the content based off the form and the structure."
- "Let's get rid of all the jargon, the titles, the trunk, anything that's visual, format, form, and shape... I just want to focus on the content and make sure that when we're identifying modules, they can be populated with real work and real teachable things."
- "I don't want to be esoteric for the sake of being esoteric... but I do want to be beautifully respectful of the art form... elegant and hyper-professional." "Anytime we can build and reinforce the self-perception that what we're doing is elegant, matters, and professional, the more likely we are to produce professional and self-respecting employees."
- Front servers at a Sommelier 1 level of knowledge; stacked wine knowledge by position (food runner, back server, front server) that can gate promotion; research the cost, time and efficacy of the traditional course against an internal program. Brandon's view: the certification culture builds exclusionary language and rewards flexing knowledge over communicating it to the customer; he names this as his view, held without having taken the course.
- "We're never presenting compliance as culture or culture as compliance."
- Follow-up: "wine should be within beverage"; other beverage specialties work the same way without gating promotion as hard (coffee at a very high level, likely through Barista Hustle rather than Q Grader, someone who could own a coffee program; beer and spirit certifications). "the 2-3 window isn't the overall time constraint... that is for the training that we deem the absolute must go through before reaching the standard"; ongoing education extends well beyond it. New front servers would need deep wine knowledge already, or front servers are not hired externally, "which isn't unreasonable for our model." Test-outs "considered, designed, and implemented wherever feasible." Knowledge tracks also improve "employees ability to advocate for their customers... which means they can be trusted to give feedback on products." "I don't want you to just throw away all the modules you identified, but they are empty modules rn. so i want to know where their place is in the journey and overall system, and what needs to be/what would be taught." Training week: "lets say a full 40 hours of training, but some of that can be remote."

Effect on the review: the Module Review page is paused. Part E (confirming the 274 modules as written) is replaced by the content-first rebuild.
