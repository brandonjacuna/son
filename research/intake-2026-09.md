## Group 1: scope

Questions asked: which roles and areas are in; whether pre-opening training and ongoing hiring are one program or two; anything explicitly out.

> Now, on the first question, roles and scope, we should do all of them, and I'll list all the positions for you. The org chart is mostly finalized. There are some reporting structures that I can't lock in right now, and with our chef, as I'll get into, things will change a little bit, probably. You have me as the CEO. You have Dominic as the Chief Financial and Technology Officer. He has no direct reports, as everything else funnels up to me. We have, on the front line, building back up:
> - Our hosts, and above that, we'll probably have maybe one lead host.
> - Our service team with baristas and bartenders. Externally, we're going to hire for baristas and bartenders. I really want our beverage team to be, at some point, kind of homogenous. Bartender or barista is just Italian for bartender anyway, and culturally, I don't want there to be a real divide between those types of positions.
> - Barista, bartender, and barback.
> - On the floor service team, we have front server, front waiter, back waiter, and food runner.
>
> Now, for now, I'm going to call food runner and back waiter their own positions, but that's really going to be dictated by the volume of the restaurant. The higher the volume, the more likely we can make food runners their own talent pool. What will most likely be happening is that we'll have stations for the back waiters. Some of them will be stationed as food runners, and the others will be stationed on the service floor. Their responsibilities will just be hybridized a little bit. Above that, we're not going to start with lead positions or position leads, but I want those to be promotable positions. That's like:
>
> - lead bartender
> - lead server
> - lead food runner
> - lead host
>
> These are people that are exactly as they sound. They are just leaders. We're paying them for being a leader in their position.
>
> Things that we're developing, I have very specific ideas of how I want people taught and trained. That seems to be something that nobody ever really scales inside this industry that we need a plan for. And then above that, we'll have the operations manager, and then we'll have the manager. Where they sit in the org chart, I'm not exactly sure.
>
> Really, what I'm doing is I'm taking big restaurant structures from New York, although they have a service director and a major D. In my mind, they have a lot of overlap, and they undersell both positions. I'm taking that structure and putting it into a smaller-volume business, and then just combining the service director and major D into one position, which is what I think they should be anyway.
>
> Major D is going to be everything that's not operations. Operations is more people-focused, less ops-focused, in as much of a traditional sense of the term as possible. I imagine that we'll have a head of beverage as well somewhere, who will have a dotted-line reporting structure over the bar team. And then, back-of-house structure is going to be likely very similar to a brigade system. As I mentioned, I don't have an executive chef hired right now, so their opinions are going to be prioritized above what I'm saying right now. They're likely going to be in a very similar system to what I'm describing.
>
> We don't call dishwashers dishwashers; we call them porters. We'll have line cooks. I like having:
> - a chef de cuisine, which is prep chefs
> - chef de partie, which is the line cooks
> - chef de tourner, which is someone that kind of goes in and out of each station
>
> I don't really care about the terminology if we have a CDC, sous chef, senior sous chefs, or anything. What I care about is that the executive chef has two assistants: one that is ops-focused and one that is production-focused, or one that's ops and production-minded and the other one that's service- and R&D-minded. They have a left-hand and a right-hand mentality. Seeing as the major D and the operations manager, I want them to have the same split.
>
> It'd be really hard for me to wrap my head around pre-opening training right now. I think what we need to do is just build these programs out as the ongoing training. When we get closer to pre-opening and I have a full headcount, timeline, and budget, we'll jump back in and develop the pre-opening timeline and training program. As far as anything external, yes, legal certifications, things that we know we have to do. I don't need us to write them because we're going to use other modules and other training that we can then link to Trainual. People will take their food handlers inside of Trainual. It's just not going to be our content that we're writing because there are other programs and things we're paying for that.
>
> On the same note, Trainual has a huge catalog of compliance modules. Anything that we can use there and should be using for training should be considered, and those will be the things that we wouldn't want to write our own versions of.
