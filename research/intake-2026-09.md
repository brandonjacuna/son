## Group 2: program shape

Questions asked: shared core plus role tracks, or separate paths; what "degree paths, not handoff docs" means in practice; moving between roles; whether the training system runs the invisible advocate.

> Shared tracks and specialty when specialty focus  is needed.
>
> When I think about degree paths, not handoff docs, what I imagine is when you log into a AAA video game, let's say Horizon or something. There are these multiple stacks of skill trees that lean into different types of playstyles. You don't have to be a master of all of them. You could be a master of all of them, and then you just be a complete, overpowered character. Or you can lean into different playstyles and different tendencies.
>
> That's kind of how I want the training in the curriculum to be built, but also presented as much as possible within the constraints that we have in front of us (which will be the platform that we're doing the training through). Does it mean that we can't present them with another visual, another way of talking about it? That's what I want. I want them to be able to self-learn and self-progress, and as things interest them, they unlock a different progression. That progression informs the business, and it employs leaders where we should be supporting them. It doesn't have to be that once they get to a certain level, there should be other electives and things unlocked. Once you become so much of a master in one role or one area, absolutely, I would want cross-training to be unblocked.
>
> If someone's interested in learning about beverage and they're on the service team, or if someone's a bartender or barista and they want to learn about wine, I want them to. The things that could block it are really just conversations. It's a conversation that could be automatically scheduled onto a leader's calendar for 10, 15 minutes, just to have a quick stand-up about it and unblock it.
>
> That's what I want the practicals and things to be like: really proven conversations. Prove to us that you have mastery of this, and that we're not just building this training for someone to click through it and check a box off. We want to encourage people to be masters and to be self-motivated, and encourage them to follow their self-motivation that they have.
>
> I think, moving across roles or teams, there are just some gates: conversations and approvals. There's an employee that wants to start learning. We want to know: is it for learning, or is it because you're interested in moving and changing roles? Both are fine. We just need to have clear expectations so we know how to support them and can be honest about supporting them.
>
> They might want to move into another pool, but we don't have space for them, so they're going to go into Cornever Reserve, etc. We want people to be cross-trained as much as possible. We see that as being a part of the solutions that allow us to respond to the dynamics of the restaurant industry, where we need people to step in and out of their trained roles. We want it to be a planned design system, not something that we're just falling back on and gritting our teeth through. So yes, while there is some progression, right? We have:
>
> - barback in a bartender
> - ideally, it would be food runner in the back and a bartender in the front
> - same with host into lead host or host into service team, which would go into the bar team or food runner, back waiter, etc. I want our training system to run on self-scheduling and notifications. I don't want them to have to say, "Hey, can we schedule my practical?" No. What we'll do is we'll have managers always have 4 hours a week across 2 different days, or 2 hours across 2 different days, blocked out on their calendars for talent tasks, which will be these types of tasks, as well as hiring interviews. They can get self-scheduled onto their calendars on these blocks, and these types of things can also get self-scheduled onto these blocks.
>
> I want practicals, any conversations, anything to just appear. You're right: that's the invisible advocate. Technology democratizes opportunity, and reducing that friction of administrative time and time to action is the invisible advocacy that I'm talking about.

## Group 3: module size and where learners take them

Questions asked: how long a module should take; where and when people learn (on or off the clock, own device or house device, protected paid time); how much learning happens on the floor versus off it; whether pre-shift is a standing teaching slot.

> I don't know how long the simo module should take. I have a tendency to overbuild and make things too long and too wordy. I think part of this process should be surfacing what is most ideal for retention, for interest, for enjoyment. I want these modules to be highly successful and ethical, so efficacy should drive that decision, not how much content I want to deliver at them. If I want to deliver a bunch of content, then we'll do multiple modules, etc., within a certain concept or lesson. I think that having stuff that's super necessary and on the critical path should probably be a little bit leaner and fully focused on efficacy. Other modules that might just be me or another partner talking about something we're interested in could be longer and do not have to live in those barriers.
>
> There are also ideas that I have that I want to use, like a trainable internal masterclass. Myself and my other business partners have so many great connections. There are times where we end up having a really amazing conversation with somebody that would have been amazing if we were able to share that with our team.
>
> Part of our kit that we're keeping with us is that we're always going to have microphones on us, or we'll just record things from our phone. We'll record those conversations and upload them to our trainable for people to listen to, almost like spontaneous, almost unfiltered podcasts. On where and when, yes, I want people only learning on the clock. Maybe there's stuff that could be done off the clock, but I can't really confidently answer that because I can't think about what those modules will be at the moment.
>
> They will ideally be doing as much training on company devices as possible, but there's likely a scenario where our 8 hours of remote training is done for every hire. They can do that remotely on their own computer or device, or they can do it on site on our devices. They can choose one or the other, but I always want people paid for their learning.
>
> Like I said, when we hire somebody, because we're doing so much digital, there's probably not going to be a need for us to have them on the property just sitting and looking at a screen. We'll know how much time it takes to complete that initial round of remote training, and then we'll pay them for it. They just have to go complete it remotely. On the floor versus off of it, it's a great question. There's education, and there's, I think, training. Coming from a musical background, I always try to split the muscle memory away from what you're thinking about in the moment. That's a real thing that I try and do with the physical training that I put employees through: I want them to learn macro movements. I want them to be focused on the muscle movements that they need to stop thinking about so they can actually think about the decisions they need to be making.
>
> I think what I'm saying is I want them to be split. I want to split the artistic decisions, the creative decisions, the autonomous decisions, and the physical macro movements. I used to just practice for 5 minutes at a time, just changing, moving one finger up and down, and changing notes over and over again until it was completely smooth at a bunch of different tempos. When I would go and practice music, practicing that pattern would just make me better whenever that pattern came up inside the music. That's kind of the same way that I like to approach physical versus material training.
>
> The material training should be focused on the material things, and the physical thing should be focused on just the physical, bare-essential macro movements, whatever we deem that to be through this process. They merge together in a way where they don't have to think about things as hard when you combine them. Pre-shift is definitely a standing teaching slot. Every single day, there is some kind of teaching and improvement that is going on through those conversations. I don't want to lock ourselves into any kind of format (how long it'll take or when pre-shift is happening). I can't say for certain yet. Those are decisions I make with the team that I hire: Maturity and that operations team, lead host, etc. No matter how many pre-shifts we have throughout the day or when they happen, there's always going to be a component of improvement. Usually, that focus of improvement is through the lens of some kind of education or learning.
