## 1. Training, development, hiring and people commitments

### Hiring (WP p.17 to 18, "Talent acquisition")
- **Candidates choose how they interview.** They self-schedule and pick the interview language and the format (phone, video or in person). They are told what to expect at each step. High-volume roles (line cook, porter) get a short phone screen first.
- **Interviewers are trained.** Interviews are semi-structured, use documented questions, and assess competencies, cultural alignment and growth potential.
- **Paid practical second interview.** For roles with a physical skill, the second interview is a paid practical in a real environment. It replaces the unpaid tryout shift.
- **Transparency sheet at the first interview.** It covers the org chart, the direct lead, compensation and benefits, communication expectations, the training plan with pay dates, the compensation mechanics and the advancement path.
  - This sheet is a direct input to Brandon's standing request for job descriptions.
- **Fast decisions and no ghosting.** Leadership and salaried roles have a final-round dinner in the room.

### Onboarding (WP p.18, "Onboarding")
- **Before the first shift:** information is delivered, expectations are set, the peer mentor is connected, and the locker, uniform, tools and shoes are ready.
- **"Why comes before how."** Orientation to mission, values and the behavioral standard comes before operational training. This is the source of catalog ORI-001.
- **Mentor pairing.** Every new person gets a mentor who is "a proven cultural steward rather than simply the strongest performer".
- **Documented progression from the start:** milestones, skill assessments and feedback.
- **A fixed-length early plan** with check-ins. Its length is a figure; see the register.

### Readiness gate (WP p.19, "Training infrastructure")
- "No one touches a table until they are actually ready." This is the source of catalog SVC-001. The WP does not say what "ready" consists of.

### Learning platform, peer authoring and paid teaching (WP p.18 to 19)
- The platform is "engaging and personalized and constantly expanding". The WP does not name it.
- "Any qualified team member can create training modules and get paid for teaching everyone else." This is the source of LEA-001.
- "The more modules completed, the more earned." It is ambiguous whether this means authors earn per module written or learners earn per module completed. See open questions.

### Advancement and the ladder (WP p.11, p.13 to 14, p.19)
- **Documented competencies.** Every position has competency requirements visible to anyone. "Advancement requires preparation, not permission." (p.11, p.14)
- **Invisible advocate** (p.11). The mechanism is described in section 3 below.
- **Performance management** (p.19). When ready, the employee "schedule[s] a competency conversation with the appropriate lead and [is] signed off". Advancement is tied to demonstrated mastery.
- **Internal succession** (p.19). Over time, leadership is filled from within, "to the point where the building rarely hires a manager from outside".
- **Progression is "connections, not altitude"** (p.11). This is the inverted web: the employee sits at the center of their own knowledge map.
- **Cross-strand fluency is "the real development marker"** and is "recognized in compensation as meaningful" (p.11). How it is paid is an open item (p.15).

### Cross-training (WP p.19)
- Cross-training is framed as "career development, not only operational insurance". The WP's own example is "a server who learns the kitchen", which is a chef-gated domain for this studio.

### Pre-shift and the shift rhythm
- WP p.14: "The shift brief is not happening" is named as a symptom of the "sequence corruption" failure mode.
- WP p.26: the rhythm is prepare at the start of the shift, serve during it, reflect and strengthen the relationship at the end. The end-of-shift work is a thank-you note from every server to every customer. An overnight cleaning crew replaces server closing duties.
- BG-md 06, "Daypart Lighting Transitions": a pre-service lighting scene is person-triggered and visible to staff.

### Measurement
- **Leading indicators** (WP p.12, p.22 to 23): employee NPS, cultural labor score, customer recognition rate.
- **Cultural impact** (WP p.23):
  - Quantitative: retention, internal promotion rate, engagement (psychological safety, values alignment), participation in development.
  - Qualitative: stories, exit interviews, customer feedback about the team, growth beyond the building.
- **Reviews** (WP p.19) happen on a stated cadence (a figure). They are development-focused under a "no surprises" rule, and feedback runs both ways.
- **Structured feedback channel** (WP p.22) syncs to the execution system; "the loop gets closed".

### How the team is treated, in principle
- **BG 01, "Company non-negotiables":** three lines that do not move, plus the employee-first protection order. Cite the label; do not restate it.
- **BG 03, "Playground Philosophy":** "conditions for agency" for customers, founders and employees. BG-md 01 adds two clauses:
  - It prohibits "training systems that measure procedure execution without building toward judgment".
  - It requires "training architecture that progresses from procedure mastery to autonomous judgment".
- **WP p.16 to 17:**
  - "Labor as asset, not cost."
  - "Employees are the users": systems are designed for the 22-year-old server, with feedback loops.
  - "Blame the process, fix the process": management absorbs blame, and post-mortems are private and focused on process.
- **WP p.19 to 20, "We pay for what matters":** company-paid full uniforms including shoes, smart lockers, a real employee area and break space ("basic dignity").
- **WP p.20, "No hierarchy of importance".**

### Pay in principle (WP p.20, "How the team is paid"; WP p.22, "Open decisions")
- Team compensation is built into the menu price on the backend. It is not a service charge and not a tip.
- It is distributed to the whole team, the culinary team included, on a point system weighted by days worked. It is calculated daily and paid on "a reasonable rhythm".
- The percentage is still being finalized. It is a figure, lives in Airtable, and is never carried here.
- Benefits are planned from the start. The WP names Rippling; for modules, treat it as a `tool.*` binding.
- BG 15, "Arrival choreography", adds: no tipping screen.

## 9. Open questions for the module system

1. **People ownership (C1).** Does the Maître d' own training, development and the invisible advocate, as the WP says, or does the operations role, as the intake says? Who owns the module system, talent blocks and sign-off rosters?
2. **Management titles (C5).** Operations Lead or operations manager? Is the BG "floor manager" the Maître d' or a third role? The WP holds role naming for a separate session. Until then, bind every title as `people.*`.
3. **Unlock rule (C2).** Time in role, demonstrated mastery, or time as the minimum plus mastery as the gate? Which unlocks need a conversation and which are fully automatic?
4. **Pool name and function.** Confirm that "on deck reserve" replaces "ready-now pool". Does it also rank people by how far along they are, for succession when someone leaves (WP p.14)?
5. **Strands or ladder (C3).** Is runner to back waiter to front waiter a promotion path, or parallel strands of equal standing where cross-strand fluency is the marker? The skill-tree presentation depends on this.
6. **Leads (C4).** Paid, promotable positions, or per-shift designations? This also bears on the intake's compliance note about hourly leads running practicals.
7. **Pay for learning and teaching (C8, C9).** What does "the more modules completed, the more earned" mean? Pay for authors, pay for teachers, or a completion bonus for learners? How does it fit the anti-checkbox measure and the hourly pay for all learning?
8. **Mentor (C18).** Is the WP's cultural-steward mentor the intake's angel-shift trainer, the peer practical partner, or a separate role? What are the criteria for "cultural steward"?
9. **Critique rituals (C14).** How do private, process-focused post-mortems sit beside an open whole-team critique shift? Route this to the TBRI and Culture seats.
10. **Recovery limits (C11).** For SVC-002: is the generosity range bounded ("known limits", escalation triggers) or open ("whatever it takes")? Any threshold is a `fact.*` binding.
11. **Door ownership (C6).** Does the Maître d' or the Lead Host receive customers at the threshold? This changes the host track.
12. **Daypart scope (C12).** Which dayparts must the ongoing program cover at opening? Is there a lunch or daytime register and uniform to train for?
13. **AI presenters (C7).** Does the BG Tier 4 imagery prohibition cover internal training video? This is founder-gated. Brandon's intake answer says yes to AI presenters, but the brand canon needs an explicit carve-out.
14. **Teaching Korean terms.** BG 09 "Rule 2 · No translation" forbids glossing on brand surfaces. Can internal training explain Jeong, Nunchi, Mahk and the persona labels, which learners must understand, and in what form?
15. **Measures (C15).** Should "participation in development" and employee NPS be kept, reframed or dropped, given Brandon's distrust of participation signals and his deferral of holistic feedback?
16. **Uniform and version drift (C13).** Which BG version is current? A module that references uniforms, the nine-beat sequence or escalation needs the living version confirmed. It should cite labels only and bind the specifics.
