## 2. Teaching approach

**Format** (verified-primary unless noted).
- Written lessons as the spine, with videos and gifs for movement. Each chapter opens with "What's in this chapter?", runs short lessons, and closes with a recap and glossary, then a comprehension test. A final assessment gates the certificate.
- New courses are released a chapter at a time over weeks, drip-fed to subscribers.
- Courses include "practical exercises and experiments for bringing learning into your environment" and at-home experiments counted in study time.
- Online content is deliberately "always accessible and non-interactive": no live tutoring. Hands-on teaching is pushed to the coach network.

**Science first, cited, myth-busting.** Lessons cite primary literature inline. The free microfoam lesson says the horizontal position and angle of the steam wand make no difference, only its depth does, citing a food-science paper; the latte art flow lesson measures a champion's actual flow and builds a flow profile for each design (verified-primary, previews of LA 1.01 and LA 2.05). A writer-researcher and an editor check every course (verified-primary).

**But the entry course teaches how, not why.** Barista One's note to trainers says it "does not teach why; it teaches how": it sets the baseline for earning a wage, drills learners in following a recipe and what to do when things go wrong, and trains the physical side and injury avoidance. Designing recipes and the underlying science wait for the Advanced tier (verified-primary, B1 0.01 preview). This is the opposite order from Sŏn's "why before how." It is a deliberate choice for speed to the bar, and worth weighing, not copying.

**Breaking skills down** (verified-primary, previews).
- **A named vocabulary of movement.** BH built a Latte Art Lexicon: named terms and icons for the major milk pitcher manoeuvres that most designs are built from. Two, placing and cutting, are called fundamental and are to be mastered before the others (B1 5.03, LA 5.01, glossary).
- **Motor memory is taught as a concept.** LA 2.08 explains encoding and consolidation, says difficulty sets how long a pattern takes to consolidate, and compares it to a musician building scales slowly over months: learn simplified versions first (a tulip with few petals before many). The course order itself (heart and tulip before rosetta) is justified by consolidation.
- **Isolate one element and drill it.** LA 4.04 teaches "squiggles": pour only zigzags to rehearse the fishtailing motion on its own, repeat in quick succession across practice pours, and do not add cutting or shaking while the movement is being encoded.
- **Specs for the body.** Pouring height, flow rate and line width are given as specifications, and learners are told how to measure their own flow rate by pouring into a measuring vessel for a fixed time (LA 2.02, LA 2.05).
- **Specs from the customer's side.** Pattern directions are defined from the customer's view, and cup handles are oriented toward the customer's dominant hand (LA 1.02). Craft is framed around what the customer sees.

**Assessment.**
- Online: chapter comprehension tests plus a final exam with a published pass mark; unlimited resits (verified-primary). This proves knowing, not doing.
- Practical: the Baseline scoresheets (free PDFs). On the Steep-and-Release sheet the coach scores recipe statement, dosing, tare, rinse, pouring accuracy, agitation technique, consistency across three brews, time management (use of spare time during drawdown), and total preparation time against a time limit (verified-primary). The barista first states their recipe and intended approach to the coach (verified-primary, IM 7.01 preview). Coaches may retest weak areas at the end of the session, "a kind of built-in re-sit," then enter scores into a BH web app that issues a verifiable certificate (verified-primary, Become a BH Coach).
- BH also offers the scoresheets "as staff training aids" for self-practice before certifying (verified-primary, Baseline Tests newsletter).

**Progression.** Self-directed and self-paced: any course, any order, any device, in the learner's own time (verified-primary). Tiers suggest a path (Zero, then One, then Advanced). Blended flow for coached learners: online content and a quiz first, so the in-person session covers only weaknesses; BH's stated aim is to save trainers "from having to say the same thing, every day" (verified-primary, paraphrased to remove an em dash).

**Match with Sŏn's style.**

| Sŏn principle | BH | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Isolate physical patterns and drill them | Lexicon of manoeuvres; fundamentals first; squiggles; simplified patterns first | Strong |
| Why before how | Science-first in Advanced; Barista One explicitly how-first | Partial; order is reversed at entry |

## 3. Latte art and milk: drills for pour control

- **Closest match to Brandon's water drill:** BH has learners pour liquid into a measuring vessel for a set time to learn their own flow rate, and teaches flow rate, stream width and pouring height as the three controlled variables, with a slow fill and a faster paint phase (verified-primary, LA 2.02 and LA 2.05 previews). Brandon's slow, fast, slow-to-fast and height-change sequence isolates exactly those variables. BH supplies the reason and a way to measure them; Brandon's drill supplies the reps.
- **Squiggles** isolate one wrist motion (fishtailing) with nothing else added (verified-primary, LA 4.04).
- **Placing and cutting first**, then the other manoeuvres (verified-primary, B1 5.03).
- **Pin-drop pour:** fill from height in an unbroken stream; too slow breaks the stream, too fast discolors the surface (verified-primary, LA 2.02).
- **Grip and orientation** are taught as their own lesson before any pattern (verified-primary, LA 1.02 and LA 1.03 titles).
- **Not found on BH:** a water-only or soapy-water practice drill. BH says latte art practice "may prove difficult without access to a machine" (verified-primary). Water and dish-soap practice is common in independent tips (lead-only; Home-Barista, Chowhound, Tasting Table search results). Brandon's drill is Sŏn's own and fills that gap.

## 4. How a business uses it

- **Team plans exist** in more than one seat size, monthly or annual; a money-back window applies (verified-primary). No prices are given here.
- **Adding staff:** a team manager invites by email or a shareable joining link, can regenerate the link, remove and swap members, and set other members as managers (verified-primary, Help page).
- **Recording completion:** the team page shows a "User Progress" view per member. Barista One's note to trainers says managers see which knowledge areas need work from each assessment (verified-primary). Removed members keep their progress and certificates and can re-enroll elsewhere (verified-primary). That is portable for the employee and not owned by Sŏn.
- **Assigning courses: no.** A team member gets access to all courses; BH says it cannot currently limit a member to one course (verified-primary). Assignment and sequencing would have to live in Sŏn's own system.
- **Coach and wholesale options:** BH Coaching lets a roastery or chain run an in-house coach who delivers Baseline practicals and issues certificates; a Wholesale Training Team bundle enrolls customers or baristas in Barista One, Percolation and Immersion before in-person training, with practical scoresheets (verified-primary). Coaches apply and must show ability as baristas, teachers, mentors and managers (verified-primary).
- **LMS and SCORM:** no SCORM package, LMS integration, API or single sign-on was found (unverified; ask BH). What does exist: public course URLs and a shareable, verifiable certificate link per learner (verified-primary). Per `adapters/trainual.md`, a Trainual page can link out and a native test or checklist can record an attestation; the BH certificate link, uploaded and checked by an Admin, fits D17 option (b) in `framework/system-design.md` (Sŏn inference, unverified with BH).
- **Licensing limits** (verified-primary, Terms and course FAQ): no reproducing, duplicating, copying, selling or exploiting any portion of the service without express written permission; each user must hold their own access; BH may limit IP addresses per account. So Sŏn may link to BH and require a BH certificate, but may not paste, screenshot, re-record or re-host BH lessons, videos or quizzes in Trainual. The free scoresheets are offered as staff training aids, but copying them into Sŏn material still needs written permission; link to the PDF instead.

Do not link Hospitality and Service Culture into the core: Sŏn's hospitality is defined by its own canon and Brand Guidelines, and an outside course risks teaching a different house standard. At most an elective library link (Sŏn inference).

**Build in BH's spirit, Sŏn's own content:**
- **BEV-002 Pour control.** Brandon's water drill plus BH's move of making the variables measurable: time a pour into a measuring vessel, film it, compare against a reference clip. One variable at a time, simplified versions first, no added complexity while a motion is new.
- **A Sŏn movement lexicon** for the whole beverage team: short names for each isolated physical pattern (pitcher manoeuvres, shake, stir, strain, pour to line, tray carry for BEV-003), mastered fundamentals first. BH's lexicon is the model; the names and content are Sŏn's.
- **Baseline-style practical scoresheets** for Sŏn gates: state your recipe and plan aloud first, then score technique, consistency across repeated makes, time management and total time. This fits the peer-led practical and the "concepts at a screen, mastery in conversation" split. Criteria are `people.head_of_beverage` and `workflow.*` bindings; time limits are `fact.*` or team-gated.
- **Blended hand-off:** screen content and a quiz first, then the in-person session spends time only on the weaknesses the quiz and practice clip show. Built into Sŏn's booking-link flow (D10).
- **Measurement next to taste:** Advanced Coffee Making ties sensory impressions to measured extraction and strength. Sŏn can do the same for dilution and temperature in cocktails and tea.

**Beyond coffee:**
- **Cocktails.** The same variables govern a jigger pour or a long pour (flow, height, line), stirring and shaking (a repeatable motion, dilution as a measured outcome), and the station (mise en place, efficiency structures, cleaning down, as in Barista One's closing chapter). Perger's coffee-and-spirits title is a small signal that the crossover is natural. BH itself teaches no cocktail, tea or wine content (verified-primary, catalog).
- **Tea and matcha.** Brew variables (dose, water, temperature, time) and water chemistry map directly from Percolation, Immersion and the Water Course; the cezve and steep-and-release scoresheets show how to score a manual brew. Brandon's matcha method stays founder- and chef-gated.
- **Barbacks.** Baseline-style scoring of time management and total time suits restock and station resets once BEV-007's workflow is set.
- **One homogenous team.** BH's shared tiers and open enrollment support Brandon's intent: the same foundations for barista, bartender and barback, with depth by interest.

**Where not to copy BH:**
- Its online certificate proves recognition, not performance. Sŏn's gates stay practical.
- Barista One's how-first order conflicts with Sŏn's why-first rule; Sŏn can put a short why ahead of each drill without the full science.
- BH has no sequencing or assignment. Sŏn's trunk-and-branches map and unlock rules stay in Sŏn's system.
- Progress data lives in BH and follows the employee out. Sŏn records its own gates.
