### Cultural Aesthetic Frameworks

Three Korean aesthetic frameworks govern every decision in this brand system. They are not themes or references. They are operational constraints with veto power over any downstream decision. When a decision violates a framework, it is wrong regardless of how well it reads, how well it photographs, or what it costs.

**Jaeyeonmi (자연미) — Beauty of the Natural**

Nothing in this space should conceal what it is, where it came from, or how long it has been here. Materials are chosen for their actual nature, not their appearance. Evidence of time is a design feature, not a maintenance problem. The room will look more itself in ten years than it does on opening day.

Prohibits: materials chosen for appearance rather than nature / veneers performing as solid wood / chemically applied patina or artificially aged finishes / surfaces maintained to look identical in year five as on opening day / decorative objects placed for visual composition rather than arrived at through use, meaning, or provenance / trend-driven deployments of natural aesthetics / construction that conceals itself when it could be expressed / copy that describes materials with adjectives that perform what the material should communicate on its own / photography with artificial patina, texture enhancement, or aging added in post.

Requires: materials selected with aging behavior documented as part of the design brief / a maintenance philosophy that distinguishes care from correction / objects in the space that have a reason to be there beyond arrangement / service that speaks to what something is and where it came from.

Applies to: every material, every surface, every object, every copy surface, every photograph.

---

**Ma / Yubaek-ui-mi (여백의 미) — Beauty of Meaningful Negative Space**

Empty space is not absence. It is an active element holding as much intentional weight as what surrounds it. The empty space in this room is never accidental — it is held. Every surface has a documented intention. Empty is a specification, not a default, and it is protected against the constant pressure to fill it.

Prohibits: filling empty space because it feels unfinished / decorative objects added without a specific account of what the empty space was doing before them / seasonal refreshes that default to addition rather than subtraction / visual loudness competing across surfaces / shelving, ledges, or horizontal surfaces that function as an invitation to accumulate / copy that rushes to fill silence / photography that crops out negative space / digital interfaces that fill empty states with promotional language / service staff who manufacture activity because standing still feels wrong.

Requires: a written surface-and-zone baseline document for every zone on the property / any addition cleared against that baseline by principle, not by taste / staff understanding that the empty wall is a decision, not a gap / copy that ends before it explains itself / composition that holds space as deliberately as it holds subject.

The Surface-and-Zone Baseline Document for 207 E St. Elmo Rd is complete. It lives in ClickUp doc 2ky45bmy-15773.

**Mahk (맛) — Taste as Memory**

Taste is never just sensory — it is relational. A dish carries the memory of who made it, where you were, and who you were with. The job of a kitchen operating from Mahk is not to produce a perfect flavor profile but to produce a flavor with enough specificity and integrity to become a memory. Generic experiences cannot do this. An experience calibrated to please everyone produces Mahk for no one.

Prohibits: dishes designed by committee or calibrated for broad inoffensiveness / menu language that describes flavor without evoking the thing itself — "savory," "rich," "complex" are placeholders, not Mahk / culinary decisions driven by trend rather than integrity / inconsistency that breaks the memory / copy calibrated to offend no one / photography that shoots for appetite rather than memory / service delivered by script rather than felt knowledge.

Requires: the kitchen holds a point of view / consistency treated as a cultural commitment / the Executive Chef can articulate what each dish is reaching for, not just what it tastes like / menu language names things specifically / service speaks to a dish with felt knowledge, not recited description / the room is slow enough that a customer is actually present to the experience.

**Bent Spoon Principle — extension of Mahk**

"Experiential platitude" is a named forbidden category. Working definition: a thing the customer already knows how to receive, delivered without intention, producing no pause and therefore no memory.

The inverse — the bent spoon — is the design target: objects, moments, service behaviors, and copy specific enough to require the customer to stop, consider, and be present. This applies across all disciplines.

### Playground Philosophy

The brand's operational obligation across every concept, every daypart, and for customers, founders, and employees, is to build conditions for agency. A playground is a structured space with legible rules and open-ended results. Clear enough to feel safe; open enough to stay.

Test: does this build or foreclose conditions for agency? If it forecloses agency without a documented reason, it violates the philosophy.

Prohibits: service protocols built entirely on scripted delivery / training systems that measure procedure execution without building toward judgment / spatial decisions that route customers through a single prescribed sequence / menu and brand language that resolves ambiguity on the customer's behalf / customer data systems that require customers to reconstruct their own history at each visit / digital interfaces that route customers rather than invite them.

Requires: training architecture that progresses from procedure mastery to autonomous judgment / service authority distributed to the closest-to-customer position / spatial design that holds possibility in reserve / customer history infrastructure that serves the customer / brand language that names and invites.

### Service Philosophy

**Jeong (정):** Deep affectionate bond developed through shared meaningful time. Accumulated relationship, not performed welcome. Cannot be scripted into the opening of a service interaction. Requires time, repetition, and memory.

**Nunchi (눈치):** Intuitive reading of what someone needs before they ask. Not diagnostic inquiry. Not a checklist. Developed through mastery, exercised through judgment. The human expression of the Playground Philosophy at the floor level.

This is not omotenashi. The distinctions are substantive.

### Persona Profiles

**Mia** — she carries Korean food as personal history. Korean heritage, grew up close enough to remember the food before it was adjusted. Arrives with an internal standard she has never been asked to describe. Cannot receive unsolicited instruction. Explanation of what she already knows is an insult.
Dayparts: Good Energy as first test, Sŏn dinner as sustained relationship. Failed experience: technically correct, emotionally inert. Nunchi requirement: read that she is not a visitor to this food.

**David** — he wants to be brought inside something real. No Korean cultural fluency but arrives hungry for a kitchen with an argument. High-engagement advocacy multiplier. Cannot receive the signal that his enthusiasm is unwelcome. Kitchen access, small treats, and chef introduction are system decisions — not floor calls. Must be pre-authorized in SevenRooms and communicated to the kitchen before service.
Dayparts: Sŏn dinner as entry, Good Energy as secondary proximity. Failed experience: enthusiasm met with protection.

**Clara** — she built something here without deciding to. Good Energy as daily infrastructure. Cannot receive a dinner that treats her as a first-time customer. The entire dinner depends on the digital system carrying her daypart visit history to the reservation record before she is seated. If that data does not travel, the conversion fails structurally.
Dayparts: Good Energy habitual, Sŏn dinner as conversion event. Failed experience: dinner correct and impersonal, morning stop becomes quieter.

**Marcus** — one dinner made him a believer. High social stakes, occasion-driven, acute perceptual attention. Cannot receive being treated as a standard customer. The occasion must be legible to the room without being announced.
Dayparts: Sŏn dinner as defining event. Failed experience: occasion goes unread.

## 03 — Visual Identity

### Five Visual Principles

1. Restraint over decoration. Silence is more luxurious than noise.
2. Warmth over flash. Gold lives as paper and stone, never as metallic glint.
3. Signature over trope. One green, one clay, one paper-gold.
4. Memory over marketing. Photography captures how the room felt.
5. One system, four moods.

### Wordmark

**Typeface:** GT Sectra Fine, Book weight. Grilli Type. Huber, Kappeler, Leu. 2013. Calligraphic and blackletter construction. Warm editorial register. Not on Google Fonts — passes the AI typeface filter.

**What this face asks of the brand:** the calligraphic construction pulls from European medieval tradition. Texas warmth lives in the service, space, and voice — not the letterforms.

**Subfamily and weight:** Fine subfamily only. Book weight only. Display prohibited. Standard prohibited. Light prohibited. Medium and above prohibited. No exceptions.

**Diacritical treatment:** Breve retained in all primary wordmark applications. Optically corrected at wordmark scale — not redrawn with calligraphic weight variation. Fallback rendering: "Son" — never "Sohn," never "Seon."

**선 Glyph:** Centered-below the Latin wordmark with a specified gap. Not integrated into letterforms. Not right-adjacent. The glyph is drawn at wordmark scale using Sandoll Myeongjo as stroke authority, then optically corrected — choseong/jongseong weight distribution and counter proportions adjusted so 선 reads as equivalent visual density to "Sŏn" in GT Sectra Fine Book. Tested in actual lock-up at actual production scale.

선 is the sole mandatory structural constant — it appears on every daypart surface at a constant scale ratio. Cannot vary. Cannot be exempted.

**Responsive architecture:** full vertical lock-up (primary) / Latin wordmark alone (secondary) / 선 alone (icon and minimum-scale state).

**Governing principle:** The mark is specific enough to be unforgettable and warm enough that it doesn't ask permission.

Prohibits: GT Sectra at scales where calligraphic construction collapses / pairing with cold modernist sans / all-caps at any scale / italic used decoratively / any weight outside Fine Book for wordmark use / 선 positioned anywhere other than centered-below / the gap left unspecified / sub-concept marks without 선.

### Pattern System

Role: present, edge and accent scale only — not dominant surface, not ground fill, not textile.

Cultural tradition: Joseon-era baekja (백자) — geometric, process-honest, non-decorative. Cultural precedent; does not supply the geometry.

Governing logic: pattern derives from the structural geometry of the Sŏn wordmark — proportional relationships, curve radii, and structural logic extracted from the letterforms. The pattern is the mark in a different register.

Applies to: interior packaging surfaces, edge register details on print collateral, secondary emboss/deboss applications, interior zones of folded print pieces.

Does not apply to: primary menu covers, exterior and dining room walls, primary digital surfaces, photography backgrounds, any surface where Ma/Yubaek-ui-mi specifies absence.

Prohibits: letterforms as pattern units / 선 as pattern element / wordmark tiled at any scale / borrowed motifs or stock libraries / pattern as dominant surface / symmetry as structural default / any execution where wordmark origin is untraceable.

### Texture Principle

Texture is a material selection decision, not a design decision applied after material selection. The texture of a surface is the surface reporting what it is and how it was made.

Prohibits: digital grain overlays / faux letterpress, faux deboss, faux foil / artificial aging or applied patina / surfaces chosen for photogenic texture / decorative emboss/deboss / any treatment concealing the material's actual nature.

### Iconography System

Role: present, reserved — enters only where the wordmark system cannot do the work: wax seals, bag tags, embossed closures, small-format stamps, merchandise below wordmark legibility threshold.

Drawing approach: silhouette-first, single closed path, filled silhouette primary state. Outlined secondary state for embossing and debossing only. The cultural referent for every mark must be semantic — chosen for what it means, not for how it looks.

**Bird mark referent confirmed:** 원앙 (mandarin duck). Devoted partnership, mate for life. Directly connected to Jeong. Visual execution deferred.

Governing rules for all future marks: legible at 18mm (wax seal test) / single closed path / documented Korean cultural referent with articulable semantic weight / built on wordmark structural grid / always subordinate to the wordmark / primary state is filled silhouette.

Prohibits: illustrative marks requiring internal detail / minhwa, woodblock, dancheong, folk painting traditions / decorative cultural referents / multiple line weights / marks competing with wordmark / 선 as an iconographic mark / stock icon libraries.

## 04 — Typography

### Typeface System

**Display / Wordmark: GT Sectra Fine Book** — Grilli Type, 2013. Calligraphic and blackletter construction. Warm editorial register. Book weight only. Authored, not borrowed.
Prohibits: use where calligraphic construction collapses / pairing with cold modernist sans / all-caps / italic used decoratively.

**Body Text: GT Alpina Fine Standard** — Grilli Type, 2020. Same foundry, different register.
System relationship: Sectra leads and is felt. Alpina follows and is read.

**Korean Companion: Sandoll Myeongjo (산돌명조)** — Sandoll foundry, Seoul, est. 1984. Brush-calligraphic stroke modulation, correct construction affinity with GT Sectra Fine Book. Both faces are drawn, not engineered. One Korean typeface, all Hangul deployments, all contexts, no exceptions.
Prohibits: Noto Serif KR in any public-facing context / mixing Sandoll Myeongjo with any other Korean typeface on the same surface / Korean text set in a Latin typeface with partial Hangul coverage.

**Superseded:** Adobe Jenson Pro (display) and Cronos Pro (body) — do not appear in this system.

**Fallback stacks:** GT Sectra → Cormorant Garamond → Georgia. GT Alpina → Source Serif 4 → Georgia.

### 선 Glyph Rendering

The Brand Designer draws the 선 glyph at wordmark scale using Sandoll Myeongjo as stroke authority, then corrects: choseong/jongseong weight distribution, counter proportions, jungseong vertical position. Visual weight criterion: 선 reads as equivalent visual density to "Sŏn" in GT Sectra Fine Book when tested in actual lock-up at actual production scale.

### Typographic Hierarchy

| Headline | GT Sectra | Display | Regular | 48–72pt | 60–96px |
| Section header | GT Sectra | Display | Medium | 28–36pt | 36–48px |
| Subhead | GT Sectra | Standard | Regular | 18–24pt | 22–30px |

OpenType: GT Sectra — SMCP for initial letter contexts only / LNUM for all pricing and tabular / SS01 compact descenders in tight-leading. GT Alpina — ONUM default for body text / LNUM for pricing only / SMCP for initial letter contexts / SS03 alternate ampersand evaluated per daypart.

Sentence case always. UPPERCASE reserved for single-line eyebrow text only.

### Hangul Deployment Rules

Rule 1: Hangul appears when Korean script is the primary cultural signal. Romanization appears when pronunciation guidance serves the customer. Translation never appears — if a term requires translation to be used, it is not yet ready to be used publicly.

Rule 2: Korean terminology stands without explanation in all brand contexts from day one. No parenthetical translations, no footnotes, no italicized definitions.

Rule 3: Sandoll Myeongjo governs all Hangul deployments — one Korean typeface, all contexts. The 선 glyph is a drawn logotype element, not a typeface deployment.

Environmental scale: Sandoll Myeongjo runs 10–15% larger than paired GT Sectra for optical equivalence at viewing distance.

### Token Architecture

**Three-tier system:**

Primitive tier: raw values, identity names. Categories: color (9 values), font family/size/weight, spacing (8pt grid, px-value identifiers), border (1px hairline), border-radius (slots reserved), shadow (slot reserved), motion (slots reserved). No intent, role, or context at this tier.

Semantic tier: maps primitives to named roles. Five theme contexts: `son.theme.morning` / `son.theme.dosi` / `son.theme.dinner` / `son.theme.luxe` / `son.theme.base`. Token names identical across all themes; primitive resolution changes. Accessibility enforcement lives here. Gold Foil structural enforcement here — `son.color.accent.secondary` exists only in `son.theme.luxe`; its absence in other themes is the enforcement mechanism.

Component tier: exception layer only. Structure: `son.{component}.{category}.{role}.{state}`. Press-production values (Pantone 871C, Kurz Luxor 220) exclusively in `son.menu` component tokens.

**Naming convention formula:** `son.{position-2}.{position-3}[.{position-4}][.{position-5}]`
Position 2 as category name = primitive or semantic. Position 2 as component name = component tier. Position 3 as identity name = primitive; role name = semantic. Rules: son. first / general to specific / kebab-case / two abbreviations only: bg and fg / state names always last and explicit (default is written).

**Applied examples:** `son.color.pale-jade` (Primitive, #C8D4BE) / `son.font.family.korean` (Primitive, Sandoll Myeongjo) / `son.color.surface.primary` (Semantic) / `son.button.color.bg.default` (Component) / `son.menu.color.foil.physical` (Component, Pantone 871C).

**Theme switch:** at the semantic tier. `data-theme` attribute activates CSS custom property block. No JavaScript required. Three levels of nesting resolved by cascade.

**Type weight and leading as theme variables:** within the type scale, weight and leading are theme-aware variants — morning lighter weight and more generous leading; dinner deliberate weight and tighter leading. Token-level variations, not structural changes. Specific values require Typographer and Multiscript Specialist coordination.

**Governance:** Primitive — Brandon and Design Systems Architect only. Semantic — three-party sign-off (Design Systems Architect + Brand Designer/Art Director + Dominic Thomas). Component — consuming team proposes, Design Systems Architect approves. Versioning: SemVer with full deprecation cycle.

### Accessibility Constraints

WCAG 2.2 AA minimum. AAA target for type-heavy surfaces.

**Peacock on Pale Jade resolution:** The morning palette is a spatial and decorative system, not a text-on-background system. Peacock on Pale Jade approved for large display type above 18pt/14pt bold and non-text elements. Prohibited for body text, interactive labels, running text in `son.theme.morning`. In `son.theme.morning`: `son.color.text.primary` resolves to Plum Ink #120916. Peacock available as `son.color.border.default` and `son.color.icon.default` only. Enforced architecturally — the token that would expose Peacock as text color in morning does not exist.

**Additional restricted combinations (enforced at token semantic layer):**
- Gold Foil on Parchment: prohibited for text at any size
- Jade on Bone: prohibited for text; approved for decorative and non-text use
- Onggi on Parchment: restricted to display type above 18pt or 14pt bold only

## 06 — Spatial and Environmental Design

### Property

207 E St. Elmo Rd, Austin, Texas 78745. Converted residential bungalow. 2,310 SF interior. 2,868 SF patio. Existing facade not altered.

### Cross-Zone Governing Principles

- We walk, we never point — absolute, all staff, all zones, all dayparts
- Net positive, net neutral, never net negative — every surface including back-of-house
- Aged and accumulated, not installed — the space looks more itself over time, not less
- No spectacle during dinner service — the room recedes so the experience comes forward
- Sound is removed at the source — equipment and placement decisions first, treatment second
- The space changes with the daypart — the room must hold the full register range

### Spatial Sequence

Nine beats. Fixed arrival sequence. Open interior paths.

Beat 1 — Rideshare drop-off: Designated south parking zone. Landscape and path geometry orient the customer. No signage.

Beat 2 — Exterior approach: Along the building facade. Landscaping density is the primary design lever. Facade not altered. No commercial hospitality interventions on the exterior.

Beat 3 — Entry threshold: Porch steps provide the natural 2–4 step buffer. Maître d' at the top of the steps before the customer crosses. Porch is a held space — covered, climatized, distinct floor material from both approach and interior.

Beat 4 — Dining room reveal: Dining room not visible from the threshold. Revealed in motion. First sightline lands on the fireplace — the room's gravitational center. Three paths simultaneously legible: bar / dining / patio. No path prescribed. Playground Philosophy held by existing floor plan geometry.

Beat 5 — Bar: Counter-level. Bartender always in proximity. Slightly brighter than the dining field. Fixed constraint: every dining table holds bar sightline. The bar is the hinge of the dinner-to-late-night transition.

Beat 6 — Patio sequence: Covered patio (1,186 RSF) primary dining patio. Trellised zone (861 RSF) most reserved — probable pyeong-sang position. Ground-level garden most permeable. Pyeong-sang gated by reservation.

Beat 7 — Pyeong-sang: Probable trellised zone (not confirmed — see pre-CD items). Vine structure provides privacy from above. One-directional sightline. Slowest pace, darkest zone. Preset tasting menu.

Beat 8 — Service threshold: Invisible. Kitchen door recessed. Buffer from existing building layout. Kitchen sounds do not cross the threshold.

Beat 9 — Departure: Maître d' threshold in reverse.

Daypart variation: Morning — exterior only, walk-up window, no customer entry to building. Dinner — full nine-beat sequence. Late night — atmospheric transformation from the bar; room becomes something rather than ends something.

Prescribed: rideshare drop-off → porch steps → dining room reveal → pyeong-sang (gated).
Held open: three paths from entry, multiple patio configurations, bar accessible at any point.

### Materiality Matrix

All material selections are governed by Jaeyeonmi. Every material has a documented aging intention.

**Interior dining room**

Ceiling: existing vaulted white painted tongue-and-groove with clerestory ridge. Form not altered. Finish: warm white in Bone or Parchment token register. Acoustic treatment in ceiling cavity. Aging: patina with lamp heat — acceptable, no repainting schedule.

Floor: white oak smoked or fumed, 7–10 inch wide plank, wire-brushed, matte UV-cured finish, glue-down over slab. Aging: traffic patterns by year three — this is the design intention. Maintenance: no re-coating with gloss finish. Ever.

East wall: existing floor-to-ceiling stone. No alteration. Flanking plaster: smooth, warm white, no texture.

West wall / bar face: stone veneer echoing fireplace. Bar top: honed limestone or dense honed Calacatta, 1.5 inch, eased edge. Aging: patina at elbow positions — not polished out. Bar back: low-level reflectivity required for late-night Jade accent — must be confirmed against this requirement before specification closes.

Entry: double glass door, dark oxidized actual steel frame — not paint, not powder coat. Aging: oxidation deepens. This is the design intention.

Service threshold door: oxidized steel family, heavier gauge, solid core. Acoustic insulation in door assembly. Frame continuous with wall.

**Covered patio — 1,186 RSF**
Overhead: dark fumed or ebonized oak beam pergola. Aging: beams weather to silver undertones over years two through four.
Floor: large-format honed limestone pavers, warm buff. Aging: surface variation with weather and foot traffic — correct behavior.

**Trellised zone — 861 RSF, probable pyeong-sang**
Overhead: vine structure — grown, not designed. Two to three years to canopy density. Substrate: dark oxidized steel rod, thin — vine does the visual work.
Floor: decomposed granite with stabilizer, compacted and sealed. Reads as garden ground.
Pyeong-sang platform: white oak, natural oil finish — warmer and more tactile than interior floor. Re-oiled on maintenance schedule, not re-finished.

Rejected: string lights — off the specification entirely.

### Daypart Lighting Transitions

System: Lutron HomeWorks QS + Ketra tunable white. 2700K standard, 3000K max. CRI 95+, R9 80+.

Morning: minimal ambient — maintenance state. Bar at task level 30–50fc for coffee prep. Exterior: warm, directional, sconce at entry porch — not commercial downlights.

Pre-service: 30–45 minute programmatic scene before first seating. Person-triggered — not clock-automated. Visible to staff; imperceptible to customers.

Dinner state by zone:
- Interior dining room: 5–8fc ambient, 2700K. Fireplace accent at 4:1 ratio — brightest surface in the room.
- Bar: task layer 30–50fc — slightly brighter and more active than dining field.
- Covered patio: pendant fixtures at table height, 2700K, no string lights.
- Pyeong-sang: 3–4fc ambient. No direct overhead fixture. Light arrives laterally.
- Entry threshold: single warm source. Dark exterior → lit threshold → warm dining room.
- Clerestory: dark at dinner.

Dinner-to-late-night: person-triggered, not clock-automated. Same trigger as service and sonic shift. Bar shifts first — Jade accent enters bar back. Dining room drops: 5–8fc toward 4–6fc. Color temperature holds 2700K throughout. Last turn table completes last. Total curve: 15–20 minutes, imperceptible in real time.

Late night: 4–6fc ambient, 2700K. Jade accent at bar back. Bar is the warmest, most active zone.

### Signage and Wayfinding

One sign type: restroom identifier. Recessed flush into the plaster wall. Icon-only primary. ADA text descriptor "RESTROOM" in raised characters. Braille integral to sign face material. All other destinations delivered by spatial geometry, staff, or direct sightlines.

The pyeong-sang is not findable — it is granted.

Exterior: the building does not announce itself as a restaurant.

## 08 — Service Model

### Step-Back Architecture

Three-phase approach: entry (attunement-triggered, not timer-triggered) / delivery (the action) / exit (clean, no manufactured closing exchange). Server holds a coverage position permitting observation without proximity. Trigger for the next approach is attunement, not a timer.

Floor authority: server owns the table — attunement calls, approach timing, whether a dish gets a spoken note or arrives in silence. Floor manager owns the room — coverage, pattern recognition, backs up without intercepting. Routine recovery pre-authorized. Escalation triggers: financial recovery above threshold / kitchen failure affecting multiple sections / genuine emergency.

Daypart coherence: behavioral logic constant across all dayparts. Expression changes; logic does not. Same foundation, four registers.

### Arrival Choreography

Host stand removed. Credenza-scale storage at threshold — functional, not institutional. SevenRooms via foldable phone, pocket-carried — no visible screen in the dining room. No tipping screen at any point.

Maître d' at top of porch steps. Language: "We have you" — not "Do you have a reservation." Single exchange, clean.

Table walk: below street pace, no narration.

Table arrival: no preset table. Centerpiece only — scaled for active dish placement, either removed as a deliberate choreographic beat or designed to coexist without shuffling. Maître d' seats and exits. Server's first contact: spoken only — water preference. Water returns. Service begins.

Daypart variation: late night — same sequence, looser register.

### Key Protocols

David protocol: system decision, never floor call. SevenRooms flagging, pre-service kitchen communication, pre-authorized. Never improvised.

Dinner-to-late-night transition: same trigger as lighting and sonic — same person, same moment. Not announced. Register loosens. Last turn table held — transition moves toward them. Nothing cleared prematurely.

Pyeong-sang: pre-designed, not responsive. Server is keeper-of-the-arc. Silence is the product. The walk functions as decompression. Dining room service is responsive; pyeong-sang service is pre-designed.

### Uniform System

Five roles governed by functional task profile:

| Role | Uniform | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Floor manager | Business suiting, Korean fashion sensibility | Most formal |
| Hosts | Blazer + button-up shirt | Most considered register |
| Servers | Fluid structured jacket + structured shirt + wide-leg pant + chunky loafer | Physical freedom required |
| Bar | Elevated button-up shirt | Standing at height, full arm range |
| Daytime | Chore coat + cargo pant + tee + hat + designated shoe | Michelin register, no exceptions |

No pyeong-sang dedicated role — server system covers all zones. Wide-leg pant must accommodate floor-level crouch without restriction. Role-distinct garments, not base-with-layers.

Material standard: Michelin register without exception including daytime. Synthetic fabrics reading cheap under 2700K high-CRI ruled out. Wear test is the governing production gate — no garment goes to production without a full service shift test under actual conditions. Acceptable aging: evidence of honest use. Not acceptable: visible neglect, loss of structure.

What does not change through the transition: Michelin register / fabrication standard / color arc continuity / step-back behavioral foundation.

## 09 — Verbal Identity

### Voice Rules

**Rule 1: Copy never names its own effect.**
If a sentence announces what it intends to produce in the reader, it has already failed.
Disqualifies: "A dish that surprises you." "A moment of stillness." "The kind of meal that stays with you." Every sentence in this category.

**Rule 2: Every noun carries its own weight or it doesn't appear.**
Test: replace the noun with another from the same category. If the sentence survives unchanged, the noun is wrong.
Disqualifies: "The finest ingredients." "Memorable experiences." "Thoughtful details."

**Rule 3: The sentence ends at its last load-bearing word.**
Qualifiers, softeners, and echo words extend sentences past where they are done.
Disqualifies: any sentence ending on a softener ("gently," "carefully," "with intention").

**Rule 4: Implication is the customer's work.**
A sentence that makes the implication explicit answers a question the customer was about to ask and eliminates the inquiry.
Disqualifies: any sentence beginning with "because" or "which is why" that closes a loop the previous sentence had correctly left open.

**Rule 5: Specificity is precision, not density.**
A sentence is specific because it names the one thing no other sentence could name.
Disqualifies: any descriptor that applies equally to ten other dishes at comparable restaurants.

### Six Binary Tests

Applied in sequence. A sentence fails at the first test it doesn't pass.

1. Does this sentence name its own effect? → Out.
2. Can the primary noun be replaced by a category sibling without changing what the sentence does? → Out.
3. Does the sentence continue past its last load-bearing word? → Cut from there.
4. Does this sentence answer a question the reader hasn't yet asked? → Out.
5. Could this sentence appear unchanged in a competitor's copy? → Out.
6. Does sentence two explain sentence one? → One of them is wrong. Fix that one.

### Structural Patterns

- Sentence length is a byproduct of precision, not a target. A sentence stops when it's done.
- Secondary clauses must introduce contrast or friction — elaboration is wrong.
- Default clause structure: subject + verb. No nominalization. Passive only where the absent actor is itself the statement.
- Sentences in sequence do not explain each other — they accumulate.
- The statement ends before the implication. The brand fills the honesty. The customer fills the meaning.

### Register Matrix by Surface

**Menu / Sŏn dinner:** maximum precision, minimum surface area. Dish name carries identity. Descriptor names what identity doesn't contain. Execution assumed. No adjectives performing quality. No sequential or spatial language.
Example: *Galbi / Aged thirty days. Scored before the fire.*

**Menu / Dosi:** short enough to read at decision speed. Descriptor closes the decision. No dinner register. No explanation of Korean food.
Example: *Dosirak / Braised short rib, multigrain rice, four banchan. Eat in or out.*

**Short-form digital:** one statement. All binary tests at compression. No setup sentences. No emojis. No exclamation points.
Example: *Scored before the fire. Table available tonight.*

**Longer-form digital:** statements accumulate without explaining each other. Brand position named directly. No "We believe," no "journey," no founder hero narrative.
Example: *Most restaurants are pointed at the plate. We are pointed at the relationship. The plate is how we earn the right to have one.*

**In-space copy:** present when found, not announcing itself. Korean terminology stands without explanation. No instructional copy.
Example: *선 / 207 E St. Elmo Rd*

**Correspondence:** names what it knows specifically. Confirms without reselling. No hospitality-speak.
Example: *Your table is confirmed for Saturday at 7. Outdoor seating, as requested. We'll see you then.*

### Preferred Terms

Korean culinary and cultural terminology — stands without translation: Jeong, Nunchi, Jaeyeonmi, Ma, Mahk, Galbi, Dosirak, Banchan, Buncheong, Baekja, Pyeong-sang, Ganjang, Doenjang, Gochujang, 선.

Preparation as decision, stated factually: scored, aged, braised, pressed, set, rendered, reduced.

Specific over categorical: "thirty days" not "long-aged" / "white oak" not "hardwood" / "onggi clay" not "traditional vessels."

Customer relationship: "return" not "loyalty" / "table" as primary reservation term / "record" for what the brand holds about a customer.

### Forbidden Lexicon

**Forbidden in all contexts — no exceptions:**
Elevated / experiential / innovative / disruptive / authentic / delicious / mouthwatering / vibrant / seasonal / chef-driven / hand-crafted / house-made / community-driven / passion / journey / curated / curated experience / farm-to-table / artisanal / crafted / must-try / amazing / incredible / thoughtful / intentional / memorable / unforgettable / beautiful / stunning / barbecue as genre descriptor / any emoji / exclamation points in any written brand voice context.

**Forbidden in specific contexts:**
"Inspired by" — forbidden in menu copy and in-space copy.
Quality-signal adjectives (crispy, tender, rich, bright, bold, complex) — forbidden when functioning as reassurance; test: does this adjective appear in competitor copy at comparable caliber? If yes, cut.
"Welcome," "enjoy" — forbidden in all written copy; acceptable as in-person service language only.
Duration and quantity descriptors (extensive, wide selection, long-standing) — forbidden in all contexts.

### Menu Copy Governing Principles

**Governing principle:** a menu entry names what is present, how it is present, and the role of its presence. The descriptor is a statement of the decisions that produced the dish — not a description of the finished dish.

**Name to descriptor relationship:** name carries identity; descriptor names what identity doesn't contain. They do not overlap. If the descriptor restates the name in other words, one of them is wrong.

**Sŏn dinner entry structure:** Korean dish name, no translation. One to two declarative sentences naming decisions not outcomes. No adjectives performing quality. No sequential or spatial language. Execution assumed.

**Dosi entry structure:** English primary; Korean where it is the correct and only term. More complete than the dinner entry — customer is deciding at speed. What's in it. How it's assembled. Format options.

**The test:** can this descriptor apply to a different dish at a comparable restaurant without modification? If yes, the entry is wrong. Secondary test: does the descriptor answer a question the customer was about to ask? If yes, stop at the statement.

## 10 — Photography

### Philosophy

Photographs as memories, not documents. People always present. Light found, not lit. Composition decentered. Frame imperfect. Closer than comfortable. Film grain accepted. AI-generated imagery prohibited on all public-facing surfaces — absolute, no exceptions. AI-assisted minor edits permitted under the Harvard SEAS disclosure tag convention.

### Three Photography Contexts

Context A — The Room: space, atmosphere, time-of-day. People as animating evidence that the room is inhabited — not as performers.

Context B — The Table: food in the context of consumption or service. Vessel and surrounding hands always present. Food alone is prohibited. Exception: food in preparation where the cook constitutes the human context.

Context C — The People: portraits and relationships. Customers, kitchen, service team. No role photography. No subject is backdrop.

Non-negotiable across all three: no image in which the subject has adjusted posture, expression, or gaze in response to the camera. Awareness is permitted. Alteration is not.

Two production modes: Produced (scheduled, crew-supported, Contexts A and B primarily) and Ambient (photographer during service/preparation, minimal footprint, Context C primarily).

Temporal arc governs capture conditions — not post-production: Morning shot in morning light. Dinner shot at dinner. A single-day shoot cannot cover all dayparts.

### Light Direction

Found light: window light, candlelight, installed 2700K CRI 95+ architectural lighting, firelight, ambient outdoor glow, reflected light from warm materials.

Rules out: strobe, flash, ring lights, softboxes, beauty dishes, reflector panels, any crew-introduced light source.

Footprint rule: no production lighting in the dining room for ambient or Context A/C shoots. For Context B produced shoots: negative fill only. No light addition.

Daypart variation: morning — window light at low angle, shadows not filled. Dinner — installed warm white lighting plus candlelight, no flash. Late night — high-ISO required; grain at ISO 3200–6400 is the honest record, not a defect.

The camera adapts to the room. The room does not adapt to the camera.

### Subject and Framing

People: present in every frame. In Contexts A and B, never looking at camera. In Context C, direct gaze permitted but not posed.

Food in context: only in transactional context. Vessel and hands always present. Food alone prohibited.

Composition governing rules:
- Decentered: subject's primary element at or outside inner thirds intersection; center may be empty.
- Imperfect: horizon not level, visible motion blur in at least one element; no tripod with plumb line.
- Closer than comfortable: move forward until something important is cropped — hand at wrist, face at jaw, dish at rim.

Never in the frame: brand signage as primary subject / introduced props not belonging to space or service / visible production equipment / tables styled for camera / compositions communicating transaction over exchange.

### Post-Production Parameters

Color: grade toward confirmed palette. Skin warms to Onggi. Shadows deepen to Aubergine — not cool blue-black. Highlights hold in Parchment/Bone. Outdoor greens hold in Jade/Peacock. Prohibited: cooling, blue shadows, muted-neutrals register.

Grain: preserved not removed. ISO 800–3200 consistent grain as specification. Late-night ISO 3200–6400 grain is correct. Artificial grain added in post is a Jaeyeonmi violation.

Retouching: Permitted — exposure, color balance, dust removal, incidental equipment removal. Prohibited — skin retouching beyond blemish, food texture correction, architectural surface correction for actual imperfections.

AI-assisted editing: Permitted under disclosure tag — noise reduction, lens correction, distortion/perspective correction within actual capture geometry. Prohibited outright — any generative AI creating content not in original capture (sky replacement, object generation, background extension, skin synthesis, generative content-aware fill). The prohibition is absolute.

### Photographer Selection Criteria

Must demonstrate: inhabited spaces with unaltered people / available light and high-ISO comfort with grain / decentered composition as deliberate choice / portraiture without performance.

Immediate disqualifiers: food-only portfolio / heavy post-production visible / overhead tabletop dominant / strobe as primary light source / technically proficient commercial restaurant photography aesthetic / standard AI-processing rights contract without modification.

Cultural fluency evaluation: briefed on Jaeyeonmi, Ma, and Mahk before confirmation. A photographer who asks how to make food look more appetizing after reading the Mahk standard has failed the evaluation.

Photographer reference library (sensibility anchors — not style targets):
Daniel Krieger, Heami Lee, Lukas Wassmann, Adrianna Glaviano, Wolfgang Tillmans, Nan Goldin, Ryan McGinley, Joshua Gordon, Quil Lemons, Heather Glazzard, Grant Cornett.

## 12 — Digital Identity

### Component Library

**Atomic level:** the token layer made visible. No new values. Everything references the token system. Governed exclusively by the Design Systems Architect. A request requiring a value outside the token system is an RFC, not a design decision.

**Composed level:** functional units from atoms. Three simultaneous requirements that all must be met: (1) token compliance, (2) state completeness — all six states (default, hover, focus, active, disabled, error) across all four daypart themes, (3) brand exclusion list. All three required.

**Template level:** composed components assembled into full-screen layouts. Governed by the digital touchpoint hierarchy — not by convention.

**New component criterion:** warranted only when no existing component can produce the required semantic function without modifying structure — not token values, structure. Extension is the default. "We need more flexibility" is not a structural justification.

**Daypart theme governance:**
Invariant across all four themes: 8pt grid / type scale hierarchy / component structure / interaction behavior / iconography construction.
Variable across themes: all semantic color token resolutions / type weight and leading within the type scale (theme-aware variants, not different sizes).

**Must never contain:**
- Components routing customers through single prescribed sequences
- Hardcoded values outside the token system
- Decorative dividers, colored left-border accents, 2px borders, double rules
- AI-generated imagery on any public-facing surface
- Loading states performing urgency or apologizing for wait time
- Components pre-selecting defaults encoding customer assumptions
- Components animating to signal importance rather than state change
- Social proof widgets
- Components announcing what they intend to do to the customer

### Digital Touchpoint Hierarchy

**Reservation flow:** profile lookup at initiation (not post-submission). SevenRooms profile match triggers merge before data collection. Clara conversion dependency addressed structurally — daypart visit history informs confirmation and pre-visit register. The customer does not feel processed. No tipping screens. No upsell. No forced account creation.

**Post-reservation:** Confirmation — functional, not congratulatory; "We can't wait to see you" is prohibited; present without performing. Pre-visit — profile-aware; warmer register for returning Good Energy customers. Post-visit — does not solicit a review; does not present a rating scale.

**Good Energy digital presence:** `son.theme.morning` token resolution. Prohibits: any visual marker of the specialty coffee category / chalkboard aesthetics / hand-drawn elements / countdown timers / urgency signals / social proof widgets / neighborhood café signifiers.

### Interaction Principles

Motion: marks state change, not importance. Motion as emphasis is prohibited — an element that pulses or blinks because it wants attention is a brand register violation.

Duration tokens: fast 100ms / standard 200ms / slow 400ms.
Easing tokens: standard cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) / enter cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.2, 1) / exit cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1).
`prefers-reduced-motion`: one token override disables all non-essential animation.

Feedback: communicates confirmation, process state, and specific errors. Does not communicate enthusiasm, urgency, social proof, or commentary. A successful reservation is confirmed — not celebrated. No confetti. "Something went wrong. Please try again." is prohibited.

Error and empty states: Ma/Yubaek-ui-mi governs. Empty is not absence to apologize for. The action is present and accessible. The interface does not direct. "No reservations yet — book your first visit!" is prohibited.

Prohibited interaction patterns: forced sequences without documented data dependency / pre-selected defaults / tooltips explaining how to use an interface element (redesign the element) / confirmation dialogs on non-destructive actions / progress indicators not reflecting actual progress / any pattern announcing what it intends to do to the customer.

Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA minimum; AAA target for type-heavy surfaces. Full accessibility constraints including Peacock on Pale Jade resolution, restricted color combinations, and token enforcement are documented in Section 05.

## 14 — Deck Design Governing Principles

**Internal production standard — not a public-facing brand guideline.**

This section governs all presentation decks generated using the Sŏn brand system — investor decks, brand decks, operational decks, vendor presentations. Decks are an editorial format, not a brand expression format. They use the brand's identity system without mimicking its environmental or spatial register. A deck is not the restaurant. It is a document about the restaurant.

The governing standard: the most editorial expression of the brand. Think high-end architecture monographs, Kinfolk, Cereal, or Apartamento — not brand presentations for a hospitality company. The restraint that makes the physical environment feel luxurious must operate with the same authority on the page.

Decks are not public-facing. They do not appear to customers. They will be used actively during the pre-opening funding and partnership phase, then recede. They do not require a separate brand identity — they require the brand's identity applied with editorial discipline.

---

### Background Hierarchy

**Primary slide background: Bone #EDE7D8 and Parchment #DBC9B0.**

The overwhelming majority of slides — content slides, evidence slides, diagram slides, photography slides — use Bone or Parchment as the background. These are not safe or neutral choices. They are the warm, considered, editorial register of the brand applied to a page.

| Background | Use |
|---|---|
| Bone #EDE7D8 | Default content slide background |
| Parchment #DBC9B0 | Secondary content background; warm variation |
| Plum Ink #120916 | Section title slides only; maximum one per section |
| Aubergine #2E1F31 | Major statement callout slides; maximum two or three per deck |
| All other colors | Accent use only — never as slide background |

**Prohibits:** Aubergine or Plum Ink as the background for content slides / gradient fills / any color background that competes with the content for attention / running more than two consecutive dark-background slides without returning to a neutral.

The deep colors are not prohibited in decks. They are controlled. A section title slide in Plum Ink followed by four slides in Bone is correct use. Twelve consecutive slides in Aubergine is not.

---

### Layout and Negative Space

**Negative space is a specification, not unused space.**

The Ma / Yubaek-ui-mi principle applies to the deck page as it applies to the physical environment. An empty slide margin is held intentionally. A slide that looks underbuilt is not underbuilt — it is disciplined.

Governing rules:

- **One primary idea per slide.** A slide that presents two competing ideas presents neither well. If two ideas need to appear together, one is subordinate to the other — not equal.
- **Generous margins.** Slide content does not extend to the edge of the frame. The deck is not a container to be filled. Minimum margin: 10% of slide dimension on each side.
- **Content does not center by default.** Centered layouts read as symmetrical and decorative. Left-aligned or asymmetric layouts read as editorial and considered. Centering is a deliberate choice, not a default.
- **Asymmetry over symmetry.** A single column of text on a slide with significant empty space to the right or left is correct. A perfectly balanced two-column grid is a template, not a decision.

**Prohibits:** centered text as a default layout / content extending to slide edges / multiple competing visual elements at equal weight on a single slide / decorative frames or borders around content / persistent header or footer chrome that adds visual noise without structural function.

---

### Typography in Decks

The typeface system is identical to the brand system. The application rules adjust for the presentation format.

**Headlines and section titles:** GT Sectra Fine Book. Large. Generous leading. The headline does not compete with body copy — it stands alone on the slide or dominates the upper register clearly.

**Body and supporting text:** GT Alpina Fine Standard, Regular or Light weight. Smaller. More generous leading than print — decks are read at distance. Tracking slightly open.

**Minimum sizes:** Headlines — minimum 48pt equivalent. Body text — minimum 18pt equivalent. Anything smaller is illegible in presentation conditions and should not appear in the deck.

**Sentence case always.** UPPERCASE reserved for single-line eyebrow labels only — category markers, section identifiers.

**No bullet point forests.** A slide is not a listicle. If content requires a list, the list has a maximum of four items, each is a complete thought, and the list does not occupy the entire slide. If the content requires more than four list items, it requires a different approach to the slide.

**Prose over fragments.** A single complete sentence in 72pt GT Sectra Fine Book on a Bone background is more powerful than eight bulleted fragments in 18pt body text. The deck should make arguments, not transmit data.

**No em dashes.** Same rule as all brand copy.

---

### Color Use on the Slide

**The temporal arc does not govern deck color.** The temporal arc — Jade/Peacock opening the day, deepening to Plum Ink/Aubergine, Jade re-entering at late night — is a sensory environment principle for the restaurant. It does not apply to a presentation deck.

**What does govern color in the deck:**

Gold Foil: not applicable in digital deck format. The brand's foil is a physical production specification. In deck contexts, Gold Foil is referenced typographically or in material specification slides — it is not simulated as a flat fill or gradient. If a deck slide references Gold Foil, the text names it as a physical material.

Color accents in decks: Jade, Peacock, and Onggi may appear as accent colors — a colored rule, a category label, an icon. They are never a dominant element.

Type on dark backgrounds: when a Plum Ink or Aubergine slide is used, type is in Bone or Parchment only. No Jade or Peacock type on dark backgrounds.

**Prohibits:** Gold Foil simulated as flat digital fill / full-slide color backgrounds on consecutive content slides / more than one accent color in use on a single slide / gradient fills of any kind.

---

### Image Use in Decks

Photography in decks follows all Section 10 governing principles. The following rules are specific to the deck format.

**Full bleed or large format.** A photograph in a deck occupies at least 50% of the slide, or it is full bleed. Thumbnail-sized photography next to a block of text is a PowerPoint default, not a design decision.

**One photograph per slide.** Two photographs on the same slide compete for attention. One photograph is a statement.

**No captions unless functionally necessary.** A photograph of the property does not require a caption explaining it is a photograph of the property. If a caption is required, it is minimal — a single short line in GT Alpina Fine Standard Light, small, recessive.

**Prohibits:** stock imagery / AI-generated imagery / photographs with watermarks / photographs with production equipment visible / photographs that violate any Section 10 governing principle / photograph thumbnails arranged in grids as slide filler.

---

### Deck Architecture and Sequence

**Slide count is not a measure of effort or thoroughness.** A 30-slide deck that makes its case with precision is stronger than an 80-slide deck that repeats itself. When evaluating deck length, the question is not "what else can we add?" but "what can we remove without losing the argument?"

**Section dividers:** Plum Ink or Aubergine background, single line of GT Sectra Fine Book in Bone or Parchment, generous negative space. No decorative elements. No icons. The section title is the entire content of the slide.

**Opening slide:** the brand mark and the single most important thing the deck is for. Nothing else.

**Closing slide:** direct and complete. Either a clear call to action or a closing statement. Not a "Thank you" slide with contact information that could have appeared on any deck from any company.

**Slide templates:** the deck should not read as templated. Slides that vary in layout — a full-bleed photograph followed by a single-statement text slide followed by a spare diagram — read as editorial. Slides that use the same layout repeated sixteen times read as a template output.

---

### What This Standard Prohibits Outright

- Full-slide gradient fills
- Drop shadows on text or images
- Beveled or embossed elements
- Decorative frames or borders
- Clip art or icon library illustrations
- Charts or graphs with heavy grid lines, unnecessary data labels, or default application styling — if a chart appears in the deck, it is styled to the brand's typographic and color system
- Transitions or animations beyond the simplest fade
- Multiple fonts beyond the two-typeface system (GT Sectra, GT Alpina)
- Any slide that requires the presenter to apologize for or explain — if a slide is confusing without narration, redesign the slide

---

### The Governing Test for Any Deck Slide

Could this slide appear in a premium editorial publication — a monograph, a brand book, a high-end print journal — without embarrassment?

If yes, it is ready.

If no, identify what makes it wrong and remove or redesign that element until the answer is yes.
