Since its founding in 2007, the Spirited Awards have become one of the industry’s most revered awards, recognizing beverage professionals, products, establishments, journalists, and media across every facet of the spirits and cocktail industry on a global scale. The award categories spotlight a range of talent, ensuring each aspect of the industry is well represented and recognized for its contributions.
The Spirited Awards are comprised of industry accolades, both U.S. and International, and a few highly coveted, overall awards that transcend regionality, including World’s Best Bar, World’s Best Cocktail Menu, and Tales Visionary Award.

What is an unsanctioned event and how can it affect my Spirited Award nomination eligibility?
Toggle What is an unsanctioned event and how can it affect my Spirited Award nomination eligibility?
+ -->
An unsanctioned event is an event taking place during Tales of the Cocktail that has not been officially registered with the conference organizers. Unsanctioned Events put at risk the Foundation’s partnership with the City of New Orleans and these events are not in compliance with the Foundation’s Code of Conduct requirements.
If an establishment, person or brand who has been nominated for a Spirited Award hosts an unsanctioned event, they can be determined to be disqualified and ineligible for any further award consideration. Ineligibility can also extend to the staff of an establishment or brand nominated for a Spirited Award.

What are the requirements to be a Spirited Awards Judge?

+ -->
Below are the requirements to be considered and serve as a judge
Must currently work in the industry as a bartender, bar owner, brand ambassador, educator or writer.
A minimum of 5 years of experience in the cocktail/bar industry.
Regularly visits bars locally and within their region.
Has regularly visited bars locally, nationally and internationally within the last 5 years.
Within the last 5 years has regularly traveled to other markets (outside of their region) for a broader perspective of the industry.
Required to submit at least 5 nominations in their region (these can be in any of the Spirited Awards ® categories).
In order to protect the integrity of the voting process, judges are required to complete ALL of their voting assignments across all rounds of voting (barring recusals).
More information on the judging process can be found here .
How do the judges score nominations?
Toggle How do the judges score nominations?
+ -->
Upon receiving all of the nominees and information for each category, each judge will vote online using a ranking system of up to 5 top choices in each category. Judges are required to rank 5 in each category, otherwise, judges must choose to abstain from voting in that category.
Choice 1 will be weighted with 5 points
Choice 2 will be weighted with 4 points
Choice 3 will be weighted with 3 points
Choice 4 will be weighted with 2 points
Choice 5 will be weighted with 1 point
You can read more on the process here .
Additional information to keep in mind:
Toggle Additional information to keep in mind:
+ -->
Any person or establishment that has previously won a Spirited Award is not eligible for nomination in the category for which it won.
If a bar has previously won “ World’s Best Bar ,” they are no longer eligible to win another Spirited Award, except for “Timeless” (which is selected through a different set of criteria).
If an individual has previously won “ Tales Catalyst ” they are no longer eligible to win another “person” Spirited Award, except for “Helen David Lifetime Achievement” (which is selected through a different set of criteria).
If a bar is determined to be a part of a “franchise bar” and one of its locations has previously won a specific award, other locations are no longer eligible for consideration for that award.
However, they are eligible for “Best (International/U.S.) Bar Team”
If a bar has previously won “ Best (International/U.S.) High Volume Bar ” (now retired), they are NOT eligible to be considered for another “establishment” award: “Best Cocktail Bar,” “Best Hotel Bar,” and “Best Restaurant Bar” as they have already been recognized in the establishment category.
“Best Cocktail & Spirits Publication” & “Best Broadcast, Podcast, or Online Video Series”: Winners are limited to no more than one (1) award every five (5) years.
“Best Cocktail & Spirits Writing”: The writer of the winning piece is limited to no more than one (1) award every five (5) years.
This does not impact future nominations with the same publisher.
“Best New Cocktail or Bartending Book” & “Best New Book on Drinks Culture, History, or Spirits”: An author may not win the same category two (2) years in a row and is limited to no more than two (2) awards in the same category within five (5) years.
What is considered a “franchise bar”?
Toggle What is considered a “franchise bar”?
+ -->
The Spirited Awards Committee defines a “franchise bar” as a bar concept that already exists in another geographical location, and has the same name, the same or similar cocktail menus, and the same or undeniably similar other key elements to the guest experience.
If a bar is replicated by the same owners in another location using the same name, the same or similar menu, and the same or similar key elements of the guest experience then that bar is considered a “franchise bar.”
If a former Spirited Award-winning bar is later replicated by the same owners in another location using the same name, the same or similar key elements of the guest experience then that new (or those new) bars will not be eligible for nomination in an establishment category as the bar concept has already been celebrated for its excellence. Those subsequent bars will be eligible for the “Bar Team,” “World’s Best Cocktail Menu,” and “World’s Best Spirits Selection” awards.

Best New Book on Drinks Culture, History, or Spirits
Toggle Best New Book on Drinks Culture, History, or Spirits
+ -->
This award recognizes the best long-form nonfiction published in the designated date range, including works on drinks culture, cocktail history, drinks-related personal memoir, spirits, and distillation, or other aspects of the drinks world as it relates to spirits and cocktails.
The award for Best New Book on Drinks Culture, History, or Spirits goes to a book that may include recipes, but is otherwise primarily concerned with the history and material and non-material culture surrounding cocktails.
Considerations include:
Content must be related in some way to spirits, cocktail history, bar culture, or similar themes.
Including: memoirs, narratives, and explorations of specific spirits or spirit categories
Well-researched, innovative, and compelling writing
For the purposes of the Spirited Awards ,
Material culture covers all the tangible objects, artifacts, and technologies associated with drinks culture (tiki mugs, punch bowls, written records, books, or bar tools, for example).
Non-material culture includes ideas, thoughts, and beliefs — changing perceptions of gin over the centuries, a philosophy of cocktails, cocktail slang, etc.
History books primarily discuss, analyze, and present the people, events, trends that have affected cocktail culture and spirits-drinking and -making through an examination of the historical record. Such histories often rely upon both material and non-material drinks culture and may include, for example, oral histories of bartenders, examination of distilling traditions, spirits advertising, and any other topic that relies upon presenting, analyzing, and interpreting historical records.
This category also includes books that focus on one or more spirits — Irish whiskey, for example, genever, rum, or other spirits from a specific region or culture. It does not include books on fermented drinks such as wine, beer, cider, and mead unless such books make specific and sustained connections to cocktails. A book on this history of sparkling wine, for instance, would not qualify, but one on the history of sparkling wine as it has been used in cocktails would. Likewise, a history of oatmeal stout is not eligible; one on vermouth is because of its long and integral connection to cocktails.

Spirited Awards Committee
Tales of the Cocktail Foundation’s Spirited Awards® Judges , including Co-Chairs, Regional and Writing & Media Judges, are composed of respected bartenders, bar owners, educators, and writers from across the globe. Drawing on their years of experience and their knowledge of the current work being done locally, nationally, and internationally, together the Co-Chairs and Judges can evaluate nominees from far and wide to ensure that the Spirited Awards are inclusive and represent the breadth and diversity of the global drinks industry.
This Committee is dedicated to valuing the inclusion of the communities we serve by enlisting a judging panel that reflects their incredible diversity in terms of race, gender, ethnicity, and sexuality.
