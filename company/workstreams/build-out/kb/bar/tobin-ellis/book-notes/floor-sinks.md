# Floor Sinks

## The idea in Brandon's words
(Paraphrase from the book pages Brandon sent; no copied passages.)

Once the slab is poured and floor sinks are set, equipment can barely move, so the bar ends up designed around the drains, which is backwards. Floor sinks are designed around the bar engine, never the reverse, and that matters most in renovations.

Floor sinks are code-required: plumbing drains into them through an air gap so water cannot back up. They also keep floors maintainable. They are large, and many underbar units stand on legs at close spacing, so a leg cannot land in a floor sink and a cooler or glasswasher cannot sit on one; the plumbing needs clearance and the health department wants floor sinks visible or reachable for inspection and cleaning. The usual answer is to put them under the ice bins, the widest underbar pieces. Set too far forward, they become "ankle-breakers" where the bartender stands. Every sink, drainboard, scupper, and drip pan drains to a floor sink with pitch, so floor sink locations and the plumbing schematic wait until the equipment design is final.

Best location on the front bar: fully under each station's ice bin. It forgives installation variance, sits closest to the items that drain, keeps grates out of the bartender's footing, and is easy to repeat from plan to plan.

If the floors are already poured and the sinks cannot move, his order of fixes: explain why designing around MEP is backwards; show long-term lost revenue against the one-time cost of sawing the slab; present designs with and without relocating; suggest modular die walls that remove equipment legs and hide MEP; design around the sinks and document the compromise; ask the manufacturer or fabricator to move or remove offending legs; or decline the project.

## Rules and numbers
- Floor sink footprint about 12 by 12 in, a hole with a partial grate (p. 101).
- 1 in air gap on plumbing directed into floor sinks, a standard requirement (p. 101; code status for Sŏn unverified).
- Much underbar equipment comes in 12 in widths on legs about every 8 in (p. 101).
- Ice bins usually at least 24 in wide (p. 101).
- Set floor sinks back at least 8 to 10 in from the equipment front edge (p. 102).
- Drain lines pitched about 1/4 in per foot (p. 102).
- Some floor sinks are raised above grade for backflow compliance, which makes them harder to work around (p. 101).

## What Ellis says to avoid
- Placing equipment to fit existing drains.
- Floor sinks under the front edge of a speed rail or ice bin (ankle-breakers).
- Designing MEP or floor sink locations before the equipment engine is final.

## Sŏn application
- Applies before any slab or rough-in work once a site is under lease: the bar engine is designed first, then floor sinks go under each station's ice bin, set back from the front edge.
