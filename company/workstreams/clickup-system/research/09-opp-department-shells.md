# Opportunity Sweep: Department Shells (Track: dept-shells)

Prepared 2026-09-16. Scope: for each of the 11 department spaces, research how independent restaurants structure pre-opening → opening work, then propose the shell's purpose, a folder/list/doc skeleton, what it holds now vs. at construction vs. at opening, and activation signals. Read-only against ClickUp; grounded in `/audit/*.md` for current state. Capture lists are staying (decided in DECISIONS.md) — nothing here proposes removing them.

Confidence key: **CONFIRMED** = stated directly by a cited source. **UNCERTAIN** = inferred, generic-template-sourced, or thin/conflicting evidence. Community sources are marked `[community]`.

## 1. Cross-department findings (apply to every shell)

**CONFIRMED — pre-opening runs on a backward-planned critical path, not an open-ended backlog.** Standard framing: 8 weeks out = hiring/management start; 6 weeks out = tech systems stood up; 4 weeks out = marketing launch begins; 1–2 weeks out = soft opening; 350+ tasks is a typical granular critical-path checklist size for full pre-opening. Sources: [Toast — 2026 opening checklist](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/what-do-you-need-to-open-a-restaurant), [SOP for Hotel — Restaurant Pre-Opening Critical Path/Checklist, 350+ tasks](https://sopforhotel.com/document/restaurant-pre-opening-critical-path-checklist/), [GoFoodService — 10-step opening day checklist](https://www.gofoodservice.com/guides/opening-a-restaurant-prepping-day-1).

**CONFIRMED — procurement (FF&E/OS&E) starts far earlier than most owners expect.** "Start procurement 4 months before opening, not 4 weeks." Custom-branded items (logo amenities, printed tableware, embroidered uniforms) need 8–16 weeks lead time; standard items need 4–8 weeks plus shipping. Order ~20% over par for opening because breakage/loss is higher in training and the first 30 days. Source: [Ron Group — Hotel OS&E Procurement Guide](https://www.rongroupglobal.com/opening-a-restaurant/blog-hotel-ose-procurement-guide.html) (hospitality-general, applies directly to restaurant OS&E).

**CONFIRMED — permits/licensing is the longest and most bottleneck-prone track.** Realistic timeline from first application to all permits in hand: 3–12 months. Health permit plan review: 4–16 weeks. Liquor license: 30–120 days, and can involve public notice, hearings, and caps that create queues. A typical full-service opening needs 8–12 separate approvals from 4–5 agencies (city, county, state, federal). Sources: [MarketMan — Licenses & Permits](https://www.marketman.com/page/licenses-and-permits-needed-to-open-a-new-restaurant), [Tableview — Restaurant Licenses & Permits Checklist](https://www.tableview.com/blog/restaurant-licenses-and-permits/), [RestaurantLaunchpad — 2026 Permits Guide](https://restaurantlaunchpad.io/how-to-get-restaurant-permits-and-licenses/).

**CONFIRMED — training has a specific minimum shape, not just "train staff."** Recommended minimum: 6 days of training (3 classroom + 3 practice-meal/dress-rehearsal days), covering menu knowledge/allergens, timed mock service, POS operations, and ending in a friends-and-family soft opening. Soft opening itself: 1–2 weeks before grand opening (marketing sources say 7–10 days), staffed with a staggered A-team/B-team rotation rather than the full crew at once. Sources: [7shifts — Restaurant Soft Opening](https://www.7shifts.com/blog/restaurant-soft-opening/), [Goodwin Recruiting — When to Staff Up](https://www.goodwinrecruiting.com/when-to-staff-up-for-your-new-restaurant-opening), [MAJC — Flawless Soft Opening](https://majc.ai/post/restaurant-soft-opening/).

**CONFIRMED — marketing/PR starts 2–6 months out, well before Promotion currently has anything.** PR work: start 6 months before opening. Broader marketing: 60–90 days before, building a landing page/email waitlist. Cadence: 3 months out = establish digital presence; 2 months out = ramp posting + menu content; 1 month out = announce date, open reservations; 2 weeks out = soft-launch event for media/influencers. Reviews and food photography matter early: 94% of diners read reviews before choosing, and professional dish photography can lift item sales up to 30%. Sources: [Sprouthouse — PR + Marketing Timeline](https://sprouthouseagency.com/public-relations/restaurant-opening-timeline-from-a-pr-marketing-perspective), [Bolt PR — Grand Opening Marketing](https://www.boltpr.com/article/restaurant-launch-marketing-tips), [Futura — Pre-Launch Marketing Strategy](https://futurarestaurantmarketing.com/pre-launch-marketing-strategy-every-restaurateur-adopt).

**CONFIRMED — tech stack should be integrated and tested before staff training starts, not bolted on piecemeal.** POS, online ordering, payroll, scheduling, KDS, and reporting should be set up and tested together ahead of the 6-weeks-out mark. Source: [Owner.com — Restaurant Tech Stack Guide](https://www.owner.com/blog/restaurant-tech-stack), [Restolabs — Tech Stack Guide](https://www.restolabs.com/blog/restaurant-tech-stack-guide).

**CONFIRMED — construction/buildout runs its own phase sequence, largely independent of the other departments' timelines until late.** Phases: concept development → preconstruction planning → design coordination (health dept./building official/fire marshal contact before drawings are final) → permitting/approvals → procurement → construction execution → commissioning. Phased permitting (submit foundation/shell permits before full interior plans) can shorten the overall timeline. Design-build (one contract for design + construction) is a common way independents compress the schedule. Sources: [EB3 Construction — Restaurant Planning & Construction](https://blog.eb3construction.com/construction/hospitality/restaurant-planning-construction/), [Bowser Construction Group — 10 Essential Steps](https://bowserconstructiongroup.com/blog/restaurant-construction-guide/).

**CONFIRMED — vendor/purveyor onboarding needs a structured intake, not ad hoc calls.** Practice: document every vendor in a central registry (contact info, credit terms, delivery windows, account numbers, categories, pricing notes) before first order. Every restaurant needs at least one mainline (broadline) distributor plus local/specialty purveyors for perishables (produce, dairy — often 12–24 hr order-to-delivery). Source: [The Restaurant HQ — Food Suppliers](https://www.therestauranthq.com/startups/restaurant-food-suppliers/), [Supy — Supplier Onboarding Checklist](https://supy.io/blog/learn-supplier-onboarding-checklist-restaurants).

**CONFIRMED — SOPs have a standard 5-part shape and a recommended authoring process.** Title, purpose, scope, procedure steps, responsibilities. Recommended process: identify key operational areas for full coverage, involve staff who'll execute the SOP in drafting it, customize rather than copy a generic template wholesale. Some states (e.g., Michigan) require SOPs to be submitted/approved as part of licensing — worth checking Sŏn's jurisdiction. Sources: [GetMaintainX — Restaurant SOPs](https://www.getmaintainx.com/blog/the-5-stars-of-restaurant-standard-operating-procedures), [GoAudits — Implementing Restaurant SOPs](https://goaudits.com/blog/restaurant-standard-operating-procedures/).

**CONFIRMED — menu R&D has a costing discipline that should run in parallel with recipe development, not after.** Every dish considered for the menu should be costed as part of development, before finalization — not adjusted post-launch. Typical pricing target: 25–35% food cost (i.e., menu price at ~3–4x plate cost). Ideation/development commonly runs ~3 months before launch, with tastings in the following weeks. Source: [The Restaurant HQ — Menu Development](https://www.therestauranthq.com/menus/menu-development-planning-and-design/), [Restaurant Business Online — How to Succeed at Menu R&D](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/food/how-succeed-menu-rd).

**CONFIRMED — brand identity work has to be finished before signage/print/menu production, which have their own separate lead times.** A cohesive visual identity (logo, color, type, voice) needs to work across signage, menus, delivery-platform listings, social avatars, packaging, and uniforms — meaning Design & Visuals' output gates Promotion, Product (menu print), Property (signage), and People (uniforms). One community-adjacent tactic: soft-launch the new logo on social 2 weeks before it hits signage/menus, to get one more round of public feedback. Source: [FoodShot AI — Restaurant Branding Guide](https://foodshot.ai/blog/restaurant-branding-guide), [ION Hospitality — Branding Guide](https://www.ionhospitality.com/2026/04/05/step-by-step-restaurant-branding-guide-lasting-identity/).

**UNCERTAIN [community] — Reddit-specific "what I wish I knew" threads did not surface directly** despite multiple targeted searches (`r/restaurateur`, `r/KitchenConfidential`, `site:reddit.com` construction-delay and KPI-dashboard queries). What did surface repeatedly and consistently across independent trade/operator sources (non-Reddit but operator-written, e.g. Chef's PSA podcast guide, MAJC operator-tested template library) echoes the same two warnings worth flagging to Brandon as the closest available community signal: (1) construction timelines that "used to take 4–5 months" are commonly running 9+ months now due to trade-labor and materials shortages — pad the Property timeline accordingly [Axios, non-Reddit but reporting on the pattern: https://www.axios.com/local/raleigh/2022/06/16/restaurant-openings-supply-chains-labor-shortage]; (2) operators repeatedly cite prime cost (COGS + labor) as the one number to track from day one, which argues for Business Intelligence's first dashboard being prime-cost-and-labor, not a general BI platform. Treat both as UNCERTAIN/directional, not confirmed community consensus, given the sourcing gap.

**CONFIRMED — a ClickUp-native "Restaurant Project Plan" template family already exists** (Project Plan, Project Charter, Scope of Work, Work Breakdown Structure, Gantt Chart, SOP, CRM — all under `clickup.com/templates/.../restaurant*`). These are generic templates, not evaluated in depth here, but worth a follow-up look before hand-building list schemas from scratch, since ClickUp's own WBS/Gantt/SOP templates may shortcut some of the skeleton work below. Source: [ClickUp Restaurant Project Plan Template](https://clickup.com/templates/project-plan/restaurant), [ClickUp Restaurants SOP Template](https://clickup.com/templates/sop/restaurants).

## 2. Per-department proposals

Each entry: purpose · skeleton (folders/lists/docs to pre-build) · now / at construction / at opening · activation signal. "Now" state respects the audit — nothing here proposes touching Scaling People, the Carryover Register, or removing any Capture list.

### Business Intelligence
*Audit state: empty shell — 1 list, 0 tasks, unused "revist"-typo status.*

- **Purpose:** the restaurant's numbers layer once it's open — prime cost, labor %, covers, average check, menu-item performance. Pre-opening, it has almost nothing to do; its only real job before doors-open is deciding *what* will be measured and *where the data will come from* (which is really a Technology/SaaS-Map decision).
- **Skeleton:** keep BI Capture as-is (per decision). Pre-build one Doc — "BI — Metrics & Dashboard Plan" — with pages for (a) the metric list this restaurant will track (prime cost, labor-to-sales, COGS%, covers/ADT, comps/voids), (b) source-of-truth mapping (which SaaS tool in the Function → SaaS Map feeds each metric), (c) an "AI opportunity" page since ClickUp AI/Brain could eventually summarize these — but flag credit cost, matching the "AI on rollover credits" constraint.
- **Now:** Capture list only; the metrics-plan Doc is the one thing worth pre-building, since it's cheap and doesn't depend on construction.
- **At construction:** still mostly dormant. Could start pulling early numbers from Finance (funding pipeline, cap table).
- **At opening:** becomes real once POS/scheduling data exists — first dashboard should be prime cost + labor (the one number both community and vendor sources agree matters most in year one).
- **Activation signal:** POS and scheduling systems are live and producing data (i.e., after Technology's stack is chosen and Hospitality is taking real covers).

### Design & Visuals
*Audit state: Design Capture has 19 tasks, two unlabeled clusters (print/signage vs. digital photo/video workflow); Design Documents folder is empty.*

- **Purpose:** owns brand identity (logo, color, type, voice) and everything downstream — signage, menus, uniforms, social templates, photography/video system.
- **Skeleton:** split the existing flat Design Capture conceptually (via the existing Category field, not a new list, per the "don't remove Capture" decision) into the two clusters already visible in the audit: *Brand & Print* (signage, business cards, menus, matchbooks, cards) and *Content System* (Lightroom/LUT/photo-video pipeline). Pre-build a Design Documents doc — "Brand Guidelines" (logo usage, palette, type, voice) — as the deliverable everything else points back to. Sequence the existing "Content System Build Out" subtasks as a real dependency chain (Adobe account → Lightroom setup → LUTs → presets), confirmed as a gap in the audit.
- **Now:** brand identity ideation + the content-system prerequisite chain — both can run before there's a physical space.
- **At construction:** signage specs need final dimensions from the buildout (Property), so signage tasks gate on Property's drawings; menu layout can proceed once Product's menu is drafted.
- **At opening:** photography of the finished space and finished dishes (professional food photography, cited as up to a 30% lift on item sales) becomes a hard pre-launch dependency for Promotion.
- **Activation signal:** brand identity (logo/palette/type) is locked — that single event unblocks Promotion's social templates, Product's menu print, and Property's signage order simultaneously, so it's the one shell worth watching most closely as a cross-department trigger.

### Events
*Audit state: 20 tasks, almost all nested 3 levels under "Design Pop Up Concepts" → "Sponsors" → 13 vendor names, all "revist," no fields used.*

- **Purpose:** pop-ups, collabs, and post-opening programming — plus, per the audit, an accidental vendor/sponsor lead list that overlaps Product's beverage/culinary purveyors.
- **Skeleton:** keep Events Capture; the audit's own recommendation (flatten the 13 sponsor sub-subtasks into their own tracked items with real status, rather than 3-deep nesting) is the highest-value fix here and doesn't require new structure — just using the existing "Department Crossover" field, which is defined but unused. Pre-build nothing new; this space is genuinely idea-stage pre-opening.
- **Now:** idea capture only — appropriate for pre-opening.
- **At construction:** dormant; events can't be scheduled without a finished space.
- **At opening:** activates for post-launch programming (pop-ups, collabs) once the grand opening has happened and covers are stable.
- **Activation signal:** grand opening date is set — Events work (beyond idea capture) should not start until after the soft-opening period, per standard sequencing (soft opening is for fixing core service, not adding events on top).

### Finance
*Audit state: 0 tasks; one strong Doc, "The Finance and Technology Seat: Operating Manual," combining both functions.*

- **Purpose:** funding, cap table, budget-to-actual, cost structure, and (per its own Doc) the operating cadence once open.
- **Skeleton:** the existing Operating Manual already has the right page structure (pre-opening funding-to-doors-open, operating cadence, cost benchmarks) — nothing to add there. Pre-build a lightweight Finance Capture triage: use the existing Category/Notes fields to separate "Fundraising" items from "Operating cadence" items, since the Founding Punch List's Investors & Fundraising view will need a Finance-tagged supply of tasks (per the Cross-Department field decision).
- **Now:** the Operating Manual doc is the real asset; task tracking is legitimately empty because fundraising work lives in the Founding Punch List by design (all active tasks there until past funding+lease).
- **At construction:** budget-to-actual tracking against the buildout budget becomes real — this is where Finance and Property intersect hardest (construction cost overruns are the most commonly cited independent-restaurant financial risk in the sources above).
- **At opening:** the "operating cadence: daily, weekly, period" page in the existing doc becomes the operating manual for actual bookkeeping/reporting.
- **Activation signal:** lease signed / construction budget finalized — that's when Finance needs its own tracked tasks (budget-to-actual) rather than living entirely inside the Punch List and the Operating Manual doc.

### Hospitality
*Audit state: 4 tasks (Toilet Paper, Soaps, Customer Amenities, First timer gift), no structure; one task has stray fields bled in from another list.*

- **Purpose:** the guest-facing service model — amenities, service standards, reservations/waitlist policy, service-recovery playbook, first-timer gestures.
- **Skeleton:** consolidate the 3 sibling amenity tasks under "Customer Amenities" (audit's own suggestion) using subtasks, not a new list. Pre-build one Doc — "Service Standards & SOPs" — with pages mirroring the standard SOP shape (title/purpose/scope/procedure/responsibilities) sourced above: greeting/seating, service recovery, reservation/waitlist policy, first-timer gestures. This is squarely where the confirmed "SOP development" research applies — Hospitality is the department most SOP-heavy of the eleven.
- **Now:** amenity ideas + a service-standards doc skeleton — both writable before the space exists.
- **At construction:** floor plan and covers count (from Property/buildout) determine seating/service-flow SOPs, so this waits on Property's drawings to finalize.
- **At opening:** service standards get tested in the mandated dress-rehearsal / friends-and-family soft-opening days — this is the department whose SOPs get validated hardest in that window.
- **Activation signal:** floor plan finalized (seat count, service stations known) — that's when service-flow SOPs can move from draft to final.

- **Purpose:** per DECISIONS.md, Operations already has a defined, protected purpose (the Scaling People translation program) that this sweep must not touch or reinterpret. The *shell* opportunity here is narrower: the general "how independent restaurants run day-to-day ops" research applies to whatever Operations becomes **after** the Carryover Register's canon is finished, not to the register itself.

- **Now:** entirely occupied by the protected canon-building work; no pre-opening operational-logistics content (opening/closing checklists, manager daily walk, shift-change procedures) exists yet, and per standard sources that's normal — those checklists are typically built once a physical space and staff exist to test them against.
- **At construction:** still dominated by canon-building; day-to-day ops checklists (opening/closing, daily walk-throughs, cash handling) don't have anything to attach to yet.
- **At opening:** this is where standard "restaurant operations" content (opening/closing checklists, manager systems, daily/weekly/period cadence) would eventually live — but that's future scope, flagged here only as an observation, not a proposal, since it would sit alongside (not replace) the protected Scaling People content.

### People
*Audit state: 3 capture tasks; two Docs folders hold real content (one finished training module, interview notes) that isn't represented as tasks at all.*

- **Purpose:** hiring, training, culture/onboarding, scheduling policy — overlaps operationally with the protected Scaling People content but is legally/organizationally distinct (per DECISIONS.md, Scaling People informs but doesn't own People's task tracking).
- **Skeleton:** the audit's clearest gap — one finished training module (FDN.01 "Before the Restaurant," with Synthesia script, quiz, adjustment log) has zero corresponding tasks (build/review/publish). Pre-build a "Training Modules" list (separate from People Capture) with one task per planned module, mirroring FDN.01's structure, so future modules get tracked as work rather than appearing only as finished Docs. - **Now:** the Docs-first pattern (real training content, no task tracking) is fine pre-hiring — nothing to hire or train yet.
- **At construction:** management hiring should begin here per the 8-weeks-out benchmark, timed backward from the target opening date once construction has a completion estimate.
- **At opening:** full staff hiring/training executes against the 6-day minimum training structure, culminating in the mandated soft-opening dress rehearsal.
- **Activation signal:** a firm opening-date estimate exists (construction substantially complete) — that's the trigger to count backward 8 weeks and start management hiring tasks in the Founding Punch List (all active tasks live there until past funding+lease, per the hard constraint).

### Promotion

- **Skeleton:** keep Promotion Capture as the idea inbox. Pre-build one Doc — "Launch Marketing Plan" — with pages matching the confirmed cadence: PR (6 months out), digital presence (3 months out), content ramp (2 months out), date announcement/reservations open (1 month out), soft-launch media/influencer event (2 weeks out). *Audit state: 1 task ("ClubHouse Ceramics for Coffee"); Beverage and Culinary folders are empty shells; menu development has no visible presence.*

- **At construction:** kitchen equipment specs (from Property/FF&E) constrain what the menu can actually execute — menu finalization should follow, not precede, equipment selection, per standard sequencing (smallwares/equipment should be "coordinated with the actual menu," which runs both directions: equipment gates menu feasibility too).

- **Activation signal:** kitchen equipment/layout is finalized (from Property) — that's the point recipe development can move from "concept" to "costed and testable," since equipment constrains technique.

### Property
