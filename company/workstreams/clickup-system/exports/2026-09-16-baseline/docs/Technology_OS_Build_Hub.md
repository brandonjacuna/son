## Toast

Toast is a really important place to start. A point of sale is in hospitality and restaurants. It's both the crux of a lot of our technology integration and data flow issues, as well as maybe the most important hub that we have. Toast is one of the very few software and hardware companies that we're working with. We're dependent on them for software and hardware. They are credit card processors, and that's really where they make their money.

We are not completely beholden to Toast. We are also considering Point of Sale, Spot On. For the time being, for outward investor-facing, we're going to say Toast or Spot On. That decision has to come later. I really want to work with Spot On. I just don't know if they're up to par with the tech data in our integration use right now, so we're going to call it Toast until I meet with Spot On. I'm confident that we'll ultimately end up moving with Spot On, but they don't have as much market recognition. Toast has a host of better integration software and native integrations, which is beneficial to us. That's why we're moving forward with Toast in this conversation.

Outside of being a credit card processor, this is where all of our transaction data goes. All of our transactions and all of our sales go through here. The point of sale category has done a very strong disservice to the minds and strategies of modern day operators. The point of sale is often the most expensive choice that you make. Toast has succeeded because they've wrapped everything all in one. It's no longer getting credit card processing through WorldPay and then getting a point of sale that integrates with the WorldPay processing unit. It's all in one. SpotOn and Square and Revel all try to build their own software so you can stay natively within their platform and lower operational costs and friction for operators who wouldn't be doing things the way we're doing them. These platforms are very good for them. However, they are incredibly expensive, and the software and features they offer are never very good.

Before point-of-sale companies started providing their own sales dashboards and reporting functions, you had to just extract a CSV from your raw sales data and then build it yourself in Excel or another spreadsheet platform. A lot of operators assumed, "Well, these are the reports that are being generated, so these are the things that should be important to us." The reports, metrics, and KPIs that were created by point-of-sale companies were informed by what operators were looking at before this type of reporting existed. The KPIs were built on a manual process, manual-era KPIs, manual-era metrics, and then we've just been using those KPIs and reports in building our entire businesses around them.

There are a few examples of things that lead people astray. The first and foremost is PPA, the per-person average. This is probably the most talked-about and highest-level surface-level KPI that most people in the industry use. They talk about general sales, gross sales, net sales, and their PPA: how many covers are you doing? That's their one plus two equals how successful your business is. But PPA is a fully homogenized KPI. At Coqodaq, you could come in and get our preset menu and walk out before tip paying $52, but we also had people come in and spend thousands, sometimes $10,000. At that point, a PPA is completely useless, and people are building their pro formas around it, funded around it. People are getting more money because they can show what a PPA looks like at their current restaurant, and it's a completely useless metric in almost every single restaurant concept.

The metric that should be important is modal customer spend. I want to know what range of dollar amounts are frequently purchased. What's the most frequently purchased dollar range or an interactive dollar range people are spending? I want to see it segmented. If a customer can come into my restaurant and spend $1,000 or spend $50, I want to know what most of my customers are spending, not what a fully homogenized KPI is. If you take a thousand people, I don't know what any of them are spending. Who am I building the restaurant for? If 5% of my customers are spending $10,000 and skewing that PPA, that doesn't tell me who I should be building the restaurant for. It doesn't tell me what my customer base is.

Another metric KPI is the PMIX report, product mix report. Another fully homogenized report that doesn't tell you the whole story. It just tells you what everyone that day or that period you're pulling it up, a blended purchase. It doesn't tell you by customer segment. Doesn't take a demographic. Doesn't tell you by spend amount, which doesn't inform your menu decision-making accurately.

We're only using Toast for the point of sale. We're not using any of their other software because it's not good enough, it's not strong enough. We are not the customer they are buying these companies for and developing these products for. It's too watered down, and it doesn't offer us the opportunity to build our business correctly or understand our business well enough. Toast is a way for us to store our items and process our transactions. It's the core basic functions that we're running it off of.

## ResQ

ResQ is a facility management platform. This company will come out and put QR codes on every single piece of hardware equipment, anything that we ask them to. We will pre-select tradespeople and vendors who will be responsible for repairing any of those items if and when they need repair or any kind of issue.

We can build this to where a certain type of equipment or a certain spend threshold can be automatically put in by any employee or certain levels of employees. So it makes work order creation really fast and super easy with QR codes.

It also helps us with our asset management. But it also saves cost because it automatically will surface troubleshooting tips, giving full work context and automating preventative maintenance.

Because it automatically surfaces troubleshooting tips, the person that actually scans it can run through troubleshooting tips before they just push it out to get fixed. This allows us to reduce return visits and shoddy repairs with on-site sign-off, and reduces our time to have to go and find a vendor. They're pre-vetted. We don't have to collect COIs. They're already collected for us on behalf of ResQ and not spent on file. We get visibility into all the R&M spend across all store funds, so we can always see what's happening with repair and maintenance long term. It can also sync to R365, so the invoices that come into ResQ can sync with R365, which could be a nice feature.

## Winnow

Winnow is a food waste system built around AI. AI has a camera that sits over a trash can, which can also sit on a scale. AI recognizes the food items that are discarded, automatically logging waste without scanning. It eliminates manual data entry, so kitchen teams don't change how they work. It produces accurate, actionable waste data instantly.

This is something that every restaurant that I've ever worked at purely just does not do. Even the best restaurants like Emmer and Rye, who focus a lot on zero waste, don't track waste effectively the way that we could or should. This is a zero-friction, zero-bandwidth tool. There's no manual input. It recognizes tons of food items, and we can add in things that are easy to train. It's ideal for multi-site operations, which will help us as we scale, and it cuts food purchasing costs by probably 5-12%.

For us, we're also going to have a dedicated data specialist that guides us to accelerate the impact at startup and throughout our time utilizing the service and product. And we can use this in different areas. We can use this as general scrap waste. What are the things we're throwing away that never get made into something for the customer? And then what about plate waste? What about portions? Sometimes we don't know if customers unilaterally or on a trend are not eating a certain type of food or certain ingredient or certain thing.

Normally, a server will come over into the dish area and just scrape the plate into a bin, and we never know what goes in there, what wasn't eaten. Now we'll be able to track that too. It's an incredible amount of data that we will have that our peers just cannot have. Which will inform us about running a very effective and lean business operation.

**2\. We capture data our peers cannot.** Every Angle reads the demographic composition of our customer base at the door, not facial recognition, a real demographic read.
