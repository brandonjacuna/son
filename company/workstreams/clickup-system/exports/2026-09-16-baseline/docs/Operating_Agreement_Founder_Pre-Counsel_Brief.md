## Two legal facts that govern this entire document

1. **Texas locks 50/50 members in by default.** Under Texas Business Organizations Code §101.107, a member cannot be expelled unless the company agreement says otherwise. Every removal, buyout, and cure provision in this brief only exists if it is explicitly drafted.
2. **The company agreement amends by unanimous consent by default** (TBOC §101.053). These provisions must be set while both founders are aligned, not after a dispute begins.

# 01 — Founder Values Charter (Dominic Review Required)

## Founder Values Charter

Brandon's original articulation, revised with Dominic's position on culture language.

### The seven values

1. **Mutual respect is the floor.** Not a goal, a precondition. Non-negotiable and non-performative.
2. **Designed not to harm.** A refusal to reproduce the industry's known harms, built into systems rather than left to intent.
3. **Time is the one resource no one gets more of.** Every person's time is humanly equal even when it is not monetarily equal. The business earns the time people give it.
4. **Trust is earned daily.** Employment is a daily choice. The business holds up its end every shift or forfeits that trust.
5. **Confident, capable, confirmed.** What every person who works here feels, every day.
6. **Transparency by design.** People can see what the business is building, where things stand, and what is next. No black boxes.
7. **Strategic execution.** Strategy precedes system design. The business out-executes because it out-thinks first. Repeatable, executable systems are how values become real.

### Cultural language decisions

**Family language is permitted, but bounded.** Sŏn may cultivate the warmth, loyalty, care, and belonging often associated with family culture. Family language may never be used to blur employment boundaries, excuse poor compensation, avoid accountability, pressure employees into unpaid sacrifice, or substitute emotion for clear expectations.

**Expected behaviors remain the enforceable standard.** Culture can be warm, loyal, and deeply human, but accountability must stay observable, specific, and consistent. The operating standard is not whether someone feels like family. The operating standard is whether the person is honoring the stated behaviors, responsibilities, and commitments of the company.

**Just Culture is the accountability model.** Three categories of conduct, each with its own response: human error gets consoled and the system gets fixed, at-risk behavior gets coached, reckless behavior gets disciplined. The model's core insight is that drift, not malice, is the largest contributor to harm, which fits a business built to prevent drift through system design. Just Culture governs enforcement, training, and the conduct framework. A dedicated design session follows.

# 03 — Non-Negotiable Conduct Standards

## Non-Negotiable Conduct Standards

Hard stops for both founders, under the brand, under any circumstance. These form the basis of the morals clause in Section 9. Where a hard stop involves harm to a person, it maps to the "reckless" tier of Just Culture and routes to the harm track, not the operational ladder.

1. **Domain creep.** Making decisions outside your defined role (operational, financial, or programming) without authorization.
2. **Raising your voice at an employee.** Zero tolerance, no exceptions. Volume is the last resort of the inarticulate.
3. **Physical harm, sexual harassment, or sexual misconduct** with any employee or customer.
4. **Intentional deception.** Lying to a partner, vendor, or investor. Manipulative behavior outside of legitimate strategic context and out of line with the values.
5. **Financial misconduct.** Theft, embezzlement, or misappropriation.
6. **Unilateral major decisions.** Choices that change the direction of the business made without partner consent.
7. **Political statements or reposting harmful content** in any way that reflects on the brand. See Section 8 for the full covenant.

### How these are enforced

*   Items 1, 4 (lesser instances), 6, and 7 are generally **operational track** (Section 9): notice, partner meeting, cure.
*   Items 2, 3, 5, and any criminal conduct are **harm track** (Section 9): immediate removal, external investigation, for-cause buyout on confirmed findings.

## Management Structure

Authority is organized by domain. Each domain has an owner who acts unilaterally within it unless the decision is listed as a reserved matter or materially crosses into another founder's domain.

### Domain ownership

| Domain | Owner | Authority |
| ---| ---| --- |
| Operations, hospitality, brand execution, programming, people, property, guest experience | Brandon | Unilateral within domain, subject to budget and reserved matters |
| Finance, technology, systems, data, software/AI tooling, automation, reporting infrastructure, cybersecurity, vendor technology, investor-model infrastructure | Dominic | Unilateral within domain, subject to budget and reserved matters |
| Culinary | Future Chef Partner | Authority only after admission or contract, within the scope approved by both founders |
| Beverage | Chef Partner and Brandon | Collaborative within approved operating and budget limits |
| Core business strategy, investor narrative, equity, capital structure, member admission, company sale/change-of-control | Brandon and Dominic | Shared; no unilateral tiebreaker |

### Technology boundary

Dominic owns technology architecture, systems selection, data structure, automation, financial tooling, software implementation, and AI tooling. Technology decisions that materially affect hospitality workflow, guest experience, brand presentation, or operating procedures require Brandon collaboration. In a pure technology, systems, data, software, or financial-infrastructure deadlock, the decision defaults to Dominic.

### Gridlock rule

A deadlock defaults to the owner of the domain the decision falls under.

*   Financial and technology deadlocks default to Dominic.
*   Operations, hospitality, brand execution, programming, people, property, and guest-experience deadlocks default to Brandon.
*   Core business strategy, equity, capital structure, member admission, debt, sale/change-of-control, and other reserved matters require unanimous founder approval and do not have a unilateral tiebreaker.

Genuine strategic impasses route through the Dispute-Resolution Ladder in Section 10.

### Hiring

*   **Frontline employees:** the domain owner hires within their own authority and approved budget, no partner approval needed.
*   **Non-frontline, management, leadership, equity-bearing, or public-facing key hires:** requires both founder approval.

### Expense thresholds

Annual budgets require both founder approval.

Within an approved budget:

*   A domain owner may approve expenses up to $1,500 per transaction within that domain.
*   A domain owner may approve up to $5,000 aggregate per month within that domain.

Unbudgeted expenses:

*   Up to $500: domain owner may approve.
*   $500-$2,500: domain owner plus Dominic approval.
*   Over $2,500: both founders approve.

### Reserved matters

The following always require both founder approval regardless of domain or dollar amount: debt, loans, guarantees, leases, equity issuance, investor terms, member admission, dilution, tax elections, distributions, capital calls, member loans, founder compensation, bank accounts, payment processors, payroll systems, accounting systems, insurance, major vendor contracts, contracts over 12 months, contracts over $10,000 total value, legal claims, settlements, regulatory matters, sale or license of company IP, sale of the company, merger, dissolution, bankruptcy, or material change in business purpose.

Dominic may pause or decline spend that lacks documentation, falls outside budget authority, or would create financial or compliance exposure until the founders review it.

> **Founder position:** Dominic is CFO/CTO, not CFO only. Finance and technology are Dominic-owned domains. Brandon owns operations, hospitality, brand execution, programming, people, property, and guest experience.

Work product created specifically for Sŏn in a founder's company role belongs to Sŏn. Contractor, employee, chef partner, advisor, or vendor work must be covered by written work-for-hire or IP-assignment language before any equity, payment, or public launch reliance.

# 08 — Political-Neutrality and Non-Disparagement Covenant (Attorney Review Required)

## Political-Neutrality and Non-Disparagement Covenant

### Founders' position

The business does not take public political stances. The brand stays publicly neutral. Privately, the founders hold and may share their own views. The covenant binds members, applies in both partisan directions, and is built to protect the workforce and community without making Sŏn a partisan brand.

### Drafted covenant language

No member may publicly interact with any political individual, act, or message, including posting, sharing, commenting, endorsing, liking, attending, or being photographed in association, where that individual, act, or message has taken or promotes a documented position that is harmful, hateful, incendiary, or prejudiced toward any customer, employee, or partner of the company on the basis of race, ethnicity, national origin, immigration status, gender, sexual orientation, religion, disability, or economic class.

This covenant applies regardless of party affiliation. It is evaluated on the nature of the position or content, not on the political identity of its source.

It applies to all public contexts, including personal accounts and personal appearances, where a reasonable person could associate the member with the company.

### Carve-outs

*   Legally required disclosures
*   Whistleblower-protected speech
*   Activity protected under NLRA Section 7 (discussion of wages and working conditions)
*   Private conduct with no reasonable public association to the company

### Why it is built this way

A covenant keyed to party would be a partisan document and would read that way to investors. Keying it to the nature of the conduct (harmful, hateful, prejudiced toward the protected groups) does the intended work and holds consistently in both directions.

> **Flag (Attorney Review):** "Documented position" and the "reasonable person could associate" standard must be drafted precisely or the clause is vulnerable to a vagueness challenge. Confirm the protected-class list is complete and that the carve-outs are exhaustive under Texas law. Note: Texas private employers have broad latitude here (at-will, no state political-speech protection for private employees), which raises the retention and ethics stakes even where legal risk is low.

# 09 — Morals Covenant and Buyout Trigger

## Morals Covenant and Buyout Trigger

### Purpose

This section establishes the conduct standards, enforcement process, investigation process, and buyout rights that apply when a founder materially violates the operating agreement or causes harm to the company, its employees, customers, partners, investors, or reputation.

The agreement is intended to protect the company without turning every disagreement into a removal event. Ordinary business disagreements are handled through the dispute-resolution ladder. Misconduct is handled under this section.

### Two-track enforcement structure

Founder misconduct is divided into two enforcement tracks:

1. Curable operational breach.
2. Non-curable harm event.

The applicable track depends on the conduct, the harm caused, whether the conduct can reasonably be corrected, and whether the founder can remain involved without creating risk to the company.

### Curable operational breach

Curable operational breaches include conduct that violates the operating agreement but can reasonably be corrected without immediate removal. Examples include:

*   Domain creep.
*   Repeated unilateral decisions outside a founder's authority.
*   Failure to follow approved budgets or expense thresholds.
*   Failure to follow company reporting, documentation, or approval procedures.
*   Repeated conduct that creates internal dysfunction but does not involve fraud, violence, theft, harassment, discrimination, or criminal conduct.

### Curable breach process

Curable operational breaches follow this process:

1. **First breach:** Written notice, mandatory founder meeting within 48 hours, and written path to cure.
2. **Second breach:** Written notice, founder meeting, and 30-day cure plan with specific milestones.
3. **Third breach:** Written notice, founder meeting, and final cure period.
4. **Fourth breach:** Final decision period. The breaching founder must either recommit to the operating agreement and cease the conduct or trigger the company's for-cause buyout rights.

For purposes of this section, repeated conduct means either three material breaches within any 12-month period or four material breaches within any 24-month period.

### Non-curable harm event

A non-curable harm event is conduct that creates immediate legal, financial, operational, reputational, or human risk to the company. Non-curable harm events include:

*   Theft, embezzlement, fraud, or misappropriation.
*   Intentional falsification of company records or investor materials.
*   Sexual harassment, sexual misconduct, assault, violence, or threats of violence.
*   Discrimination or retaliation involving an employee, contractor, customer, vendor, investor, or partner.
*   Criminal conduct that materially affects the company or the founder's ability to serve the company.
*   Material breach of fiduciary duty, confidentiality, IP ownership, or company security obligations.
*   Unapproved debt, guarantee, equity issuance, investor commitment, lease, or other reserved matter.
*   Conduct that materially damages the company, its brand, its capital raise, its employees, or its ability to operate.

### Immediate protective measures

Upon a credible allegation of a non-curable harm event, the non-accused founder may take temporary protective measures reasonably necessary to preserve the company. Protective measures may include:

*   Temporary removal of the accused founder from company premises and employee-facing operations.
*   Temporary suspension of management authority.
*   Temporary suspension of access to company bank accounts, payment systems, payroll systems, accounting systems, investor records, employee records, source files, passwords, software systems, and confidential information.
*   Preservation of documents, communications, system logs, financial records, and other relevant evidence.
*   Notice to necessary advisors, insurers, investigators, financial institutions, or vendors, limited to persons with a need to know.

Protective measures are not a final determination of wrongdoing. They are temporary risk-control measures pending review or investigation.

### Investigation and determination

If the alleged conduct can be verified by company records, admissions, written communications, financial records, system logs, or other reliable evidence, the non-accused founder may make a written determination based on that evidence.

If the facts are disputed or the conduct involves employee harm, harassment, discrimination, assault, fraud, theft, or criminal allegations, the company should retain an independent investigator, employment professional, forensic accountant, or other qualified neutral appropriate to the issue.

A finding is confirmed when the evidence reasonably supports that the conduct occurred and that the conduct constitutes a non-curable harm event or repeated curable operational breach under this section.

### Compensation during investigation

During an investigation or protective-measure period, ordinary founder compensation, draws, or distributions may be held pending determination if the alleged conduct could support a for-cause buyout, damages offset, repayment obligation, or forfeiture under this agreement.

If the allegation is not confirmed, held amounts are released. If the allegation is confirmed, held amounts may be offset against damages, repayment obligations, investigation costs, legal costs, or the purchase price for the breaching founder's interest, to the extent permitted by this agreement.

### For-cause buyout trigger

The company and the non-breaching founder have the right to trigger a for-cause buyout if either of the following occurs:

1. A founder commits a confirmed non-curable harm event.
2. A founder commits repeated curable operational breaches and fails to cure after the required process.

Upon a for-cause buyout trigger, the breaching founder loses management authority and voting rights on matters related to the breach, investigation, buyout, company protection, and enforcement of this section.

### Buyout valuation

The purchase price for a for-cause buyout is equal to the fair market value of the breaching founder's membership interest, reduced by a 33% for-cause discount.

Fair market value should be determined as of the date the for-cause buyout is triggered. The valuation should account for the company's actual stage, assets, liabilities, revenue, signed commitments, investor commitments, intellectual property, brand value, and going-concern value, if any.

No fixed dollar value applies unless both founders later approve that value in writing.

### Offsets

The company may offset the following amounts against the buyout price:

*   Stolen, misappropriated, or improperly used company funds.
*   Damages caused by the breaching founder.
*   Investigation costs.
*   Legal, accounting, forensic, or professional costs.
*   Unpaid obligations owed by the breaching founder to the company.
*   Costs required to restore systems, records, accounts, property, customer trust, employee trust, investor trust, or operational continuity.

Offsets are applied after the 33% for-cause discount unless both founders approve a different written treatment.

### Payment terms

The company may pay the for-cause buyout over time rather than in a lump sum if immediate payment would materially harm company operations, payroll, vendor obligations, debt service, tax obligations, or the company's ability to continue as a going concern.

The default payment period is 36 months. The company may prepay without penalty.

### No waiver of other remedies

A for-cause buyout does not waive claims for fraud, theft, breach of fiduciary duty, breach of confidentiality, IP misuse, conversion, or other harm caused by the breaching founder.

The company may pursue damages, injunctive relief, repayment, return of property, return of records, system access restoration, and any other available remedy in addition to the buyout rights in this section.

### Founder position

The intended standard is firm but not reckless. Correctable operational drift receives notice and a cure path. Serious harm receives immediate protection, investigation where appropriate, loss of control, and a for-cause buyout at fair market value less a 33% discount.

# 10 — Dispute-Resolution Ladder

## Dispute-Resolution Ladder

For genuine strategic impasses where neither founder is in the wrong and no single domain owns the decision. The founders named a real risk: early on, their altruism and youth as operators may make them too kind to each other, sitting in unproductive impasse too long. The ladder removes that discretion. The escalation clock is mandatory at every step. Neither partner can pause it unilaterally.

1. **Good-faith partner meeting.** Mandatory within 48 hours of an identified impasse. Documented in writing.
2. **Business founder counselor.** Mandatory referral if unresolved within 7 days. In year one this is a standing relationship the founders are already committed to. From year two onward, retained on-call.
3. **Professional mediator.** Mandatory if unresolved within 14 days of the counselor referral. Neutral, mutually agreed in advance.
4. **Domain default.** If mediation fails, the decision defaults to the domain owner. Finance, technology, systems, data, software, automation, reporting infrastructure, and investor-model infrastructure default to Dominic. Operations, hospitality, brand execution, programming, people, property, and guest experience default to Brandon.
5. **Reserved matters.** Equity, member admission, investor terms, debt, leases, sale/change-of-control, dissolution, founder compensation, distributions, tax elections, and material changes to the business require unanimous founder approval and do not have a unilateral tiebreaker.
6. **Buy-sell clause.** Last resort. Triggered only if the impasse threatens the business and no resolution is reached within 30 days of entering mediation.

### Note

The standing first-year founder counselor is a deliberate design choice to support the founders through the period when their instinct toward mutual kindness is most likely to harm the business.

> **Founder position:** Day-to-day deadlocks should resolve by domain. Company-changing decisions should not resolve by unilateral tiebreaker.

# 11 — Crisis-Response Authority (Attorney Review Required)

## Crisis-Response Authority

Pre-authorizes Brandon, as primary spokesperson, to act during a fast-moving crisis without convening a full member vote. The purpose is to prevent paralysis during events that move faster than a partner meeting can.

Within this authority, Brandon may:

*   Issue public statements within the spokesperson protocol (Section 2).
*   Engage PR counsel and legal counsel.
*   Take protective operational steps.

### Scope limits

The authority is bounded by:

*   A defined spending limit.
*   A defined duration.
*   Notice and ratification requirements, so partners are informed promptly and the action is reviewed after the fact.

This sits alongside the crisis rule in Section 2: Brandon speaks, or all partners issue a joint statement. The authority here is about operational and engagement decisions in the moment, not a license to set brand position unilaterally.

> **Flag (Attorney Review):** Scope this carefully. Emergency authority must not become a backdoor to unilateral control. Define the dollar limit, the time limit, and the ratification mechanism precisely.
