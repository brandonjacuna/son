## 3. What was dropped and why

Three kinds of material left the working manual. First, everything that rested on the brand guidelines: four of the eight decision-rights instances (sonic governance and its tiers, the David protocol and its flag, the design token system, token naming); the "owner of the room" conflict between canon's floor manager and the white paper's Maitre d, which Brandon has since ruled on; the three-periods-or-four disagreement, which exists only against canon; the further canon drift list; canon's six binary tests as a test for goals and canon's voice rules as the internal style guide; the persona-based counterweights and the persona-based conversion item; the three canon-only trade-offs (no host stand, no signage, no review solicitation); and the deck's "the order is the decision," whose substance survives on WP pp. 12 and 17 through 2.1.4. Second, program machinery: the four marks and the marks register; readiness-row numbers and session cross-references in the old items' comments; the document-methodology gate on every instrument; the S11 communication architecture's inventory of speech acts and the S15 practices that extended the escalation rules, which go beyond what the book asks of this section and are noted only where a later chunk will meet them. Third, the private vocabulary: "the record" for the white paper, "daypart charter," "canon," "the layer" as a proper noun, and "the partners' page," each replaced with a plain phrase. Nothing that survives without brand support was removed: the decision-rights form and its operating rules, the cross-domain and lead-to-lead rule, the trade-offs list, the horizons sequenced by proof, bandwidth as the scarce resource, the requisite-variety test and its eight classes, the goal form and the baseline rule, the leading-indicator metric layer and its owners, the split-at-the-seam rule, the three added mechanisms, the alert-actor rule, the transparency line, the three channels, the team home as distinct from the OS surface, and the readiness test all remain, restated as considerations, options, and tasks.
