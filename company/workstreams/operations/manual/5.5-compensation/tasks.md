# 5.5 Compensation: the work

The book's point (pp. 413 to 418): a company needs at minimum a compensation philosophy, a decision about what is fixed and what is variable, job levels to price against, market data refreshed on a stated schedule, and managers who can explain all of it, because the pay conversation is the most fraught one a manager has. The white paper has already settled Sŏn's form: compensation built into the menu price, never a service charge, distributed to the whole team including the kitchen on a point system weighted by days worked, calculated daily and paid on a reasonable rhythm, with the percentage itself still open (WP pp. 20, 22). This chunk builds the mechanism around that form. No figure appears here or in any file this chunk produces; every value is set in Dominic's domain, with counsel where wage law applies, and financials are not a source for this work.

### 5.5.2 Decide the compensation philosophy in plain words
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 413 to 414 (a company needs a core philosophy; her example is market-competitive pay with higher reward for higher performance, "pay for performance")
- Default assumption (WP pp. 16, 19, 20): labor is an asset, not a cost; "if it matters to the experience, Sŏn pays for it" and costs are not shifted to employees; the culinary team shares in "the nights they help fill"; cross-strand fluency is "recognized in compensation as meaningful" (WP p. 11); teaching is paid (WP p. 19). The old work's option: a philosophy a team member can say in their own words, in which pay is legible (a person can compute their own from records they can read), defensible (the same inputs always produce the same pay), and disconnected from any judgment, with "performance" carried by assessed competency, demonstrated versatility, and days worked rather than by anyone's rating
