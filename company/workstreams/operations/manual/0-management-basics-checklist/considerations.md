# 0 Management basics checklist: considerations

What is worth knowing before doing the work in `tasks.md`. "WP p. N" is the white paper (`sources/extraction/s01/record.md`). Nothing here is a decision; where the old synthesis argued a position it appears as an option with its reasoning and what it commits Sŏn to.

## 1. Her premise, and where Sŏn's differs

### What the white paper holds

The book was written for companies growing from a couple of hundred people to thousands. Sŏn opens near its full size and stays there: "roughly eight leadership lines running the full span of the day" (WP p. 22), and the web "never acquires altitude, only reach" (WP p. 09). What compounds is what the system knows, the customer relationship, and the brand (WP p. 06), not headcount. Progression "develops connections, not altitude" (WP p. 11). The two strands are "not a promotion sequence" (WP p. 11).

### Considerations

**Her "scaling to the call" does not fire; a different call does.** A line cook in year three is a line cook in the same house. The role does not grow because the company does not. Anything in the book that presupposes headcount growth is suspect by default. What grows at Sŏn is a person's threads: depth in the domain, fluency across strands, the ability to hold complexity (WP p. 11). Career development cannot be sold as promotion; retention is the whole game because a departure removes accumulated customer knowledge that cannot be rehired (WP pp. 09, 14); the service periods are the main lateral surface. This frame was the old program's first standing carryover and it applies to every chunk after this one.

**The checklist applies twice at Sŏn, and once oddly.** It is written for a manager with reports and a manager above. At Sŏn it applies to the two leads as the management layer for the floor, where it fits with translation, and to the founders as a partnership with no one above them, where its "You" section has no manager to point at.

**It is a gap list, not a scorecard.** Several rows assume structures the white paper replaces on purpose: a manager above the founders; ladders and levels; a desk 1:1; a periodic feedback event. Treating their absence as failure would re-import the model the white paper argues against. Running the list once, to find gaps and route them, is useful. Re-running it as a readiness test is not; chunks 2.2 and 2.3 produce Sŏn's own definition of "the operating system is running."

## 3. Options on what to do with the checklist

**Option A: run it once as a gap list and retire it.** The table above is the run; each gap is handed to its chunk; the checklist is not used again. Commits Sŏn to producing its own readiness definition in 2.2 and 2.3 and to not scoring itself against a form built for a different kind of company. The old synthesis argued this.

**Option B: keep it as a recurring check for the leads.** The rows that fit a lead with reports (1:1s, agendas, goals, feedback, team meetings, on time) become part of how a lead is reviewed. Commits Sŏn to translating each row into its shift-native form first, and to 5.9 (managing managers) carrying it.

**Option C: both.** Retire it for the founders; keep the translated rows for the leads.

## 4. The partners as each other's manager

### What the white paper holds

Nothing on how the two founders hold each other to goals or meet on a rhythm. The founders are "equal principal[s]" (WP p. 05). The one joint decision it states is the lunch decision (WP p. 36). The content engine has a daily pulse, a weekly read, and a monthly review (WP p. 31), which is the only founder-level rhythm the white paper describes.

### Considerations

The checklist's "You" section (goals agreed with your manager, 1:1s with your manager, context from above) is the part most likely to be skipped at Sŏn, because there is no one to require it. A partnership that agrees goals together, meets on a rhythm that is rarely rescheduled, and shares what each founder knows about priorities is holding the same basics for each other that a manager would. The alternative is that the founders coordinate by proximity during the build and lose it the day the building opens and one of them is on the floor at dinner while the other is in the model.

**Options.** (a) The partners stand in as each other's manager for the checklist's basics: goals agreed together each quarter, a standing partner conversation, priorities shared in writing. Commits them to a rhythm 2.3 must design and to keeping it when the house is open. (b) The founding document and the cadence (2.1, 2.3) carry this without naming the partners as managers of each other. Commits Sŏn to the same outcome by a different route, with the risk that no one owns the partner conversation. (c) Neither; the partners coordinate as they do now. Commits nothing, and leaves the row open.

## 5. Where Sŏn may diverge from the book

She treats the checklist as prerequisites. At Sŏn several of them are outputs of the work this manual organizes (the seat descriptions, the goals layer, the review instrument), so they cannot be prerequisites. The honest reading is that Sŏn starts the book before the checklist is complete, and finishes the checklist by doing the book.
