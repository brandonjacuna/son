## 3. What was dropped and why

Three kinds of material left the working manual. First, everything that rested on the brand guidelines: the claim that canon governs every decision and the charter's standing clause against it; the six-tier hierarchy conflict between two brand artifacts and the three rulings offered to resolve it; canon reconciliation and the versioning authority over canon; the "company non-negotiables" (the three refusals exist only in the brand material, though the protection order beneath them survives on WP pp. 12 and 16 to 17); the brand's voice rules and six binary tests as a drafting constraint on the principles; the brand's candidate mission sentence; the partner-versus-hire reading of the chef seat, which existed only because a brand document said "executive chef hire"; the persona-based "first customer" field; and the decision-rights examples drawn from the sonic system, the floor protocol, the design system, and naming sign-off. Second, program machinery: the recommended, founder-gated, chef-gated, and team-filled marks; the held-and-deferred mark system (the open-items list itself survives); carryover routings, ledger bindings, readiness rows, session cross-references, and the document-methodology gate on instruments; and the "who supplies what" rule, whose substance is now the manual's way of working. Third, the private vocabulary: "the operating charter," "daypart charter," "canon," "the record," and "the layer" as a proper noun are replaced with plain words, and Brandon names the founding document himself. Nothing that survives without brand support was removed: the coherence argument for founding documents, the operating agreement as the upstream instrument, the OS surface seam, the two-partner interim, the service-period charter as a form for the lunch decision, the three registers of principle, and the compensation commitments split all remain, restated as considerations and options.
