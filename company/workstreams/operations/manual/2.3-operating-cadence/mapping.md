## 3. What was dropped and why

Three kinds of material left the working manual. First, everything that rested on the brand guidelines: the "person-triggered, no timers" rule and the claim that a cadence "triggers nothing in the room" (the Realist's plainer rule, that reviews sit in the pre-service window or after the close, survives on its own); the playground test, its six questions, and the ten conditions for agency as a layer on the calendar; the care-versus-correction framing of the reset (the split between continuous fixes through the close and a periodic reset survives on WP p. 13 and the book); the chef seat's fixed onboarding sequence; the "versioned decision, never drift" rule as a canon citation (versioning survives on its own merits in 2.1.18 and the partners' page); the claim that the day is "one arc" with no period a reset (the white paper's "one restaurant met at different times" carries what is needed); and the canon-derived findings in section 14. Second, program machinery: the marks; readiness row numbers and "S5 P37"-style references; carryover routings and ledger bindings; the document-methodology gate on every instrument; the seventeen specifications as a separate register. Third, the old vocabulary: "daypart," "the record," "canon," "the stack" as a proper noun, "the three channels" as a term (spoken at the brief, written on the internal home, carried by the lead is stated in plain words where it is needed), "team home" (now "the team's internal home"), and "OKR" as a word to refuse (that fight is 2.2's). Nothing that survives without brand support was removed: the two-rhythm shape, the seam rules, the brief's and close's spines, the check-in in full, the leads' review and its agenda, the closed day, the partners' review and page, the house review as her QBR translated, the gathering as a decision, the gate review, the charter calendar, the reset, the calendar as one table, and the bandwidth ledger all remain, restated as decisions, options, and deliverables.
