# 5.7 The steady middle: what the book says

Claire Hughes Johnson, *Scaling People*, Chapter 5, p. 431. This file is a quick reference; nothing here is about Sŏn. Full text: `sources/extraction/build/s14/book.md`.

## Medium performers are a real category, not a phase

One school of thought treats "medium" performers as people who simply haven't yet shown their true ability, high or low. That can be true early on, when a company is growing fast and many people are new. But given enough time, she believes every company will show the full normal curve of performance: high, medium, and low. A company can skew toward higher performance, but she doubts any company, especially a large one, is made up entirely of the upper tail.

## Why they matter

Medium performers are easy to neglect, which is unfortunate because they are often sources of stability and frequently the culture carriers of a team. Every manager should treat their team as a portfolio of talent, and each person in it should be assessed and either supported, coached toward better performance, or moved to a more suitable role, team, or company.

## Match the person to the kind of team

Consider your team's context: is it fast-paced with an ever-changing scope tied to top company priorities, or does it need to deliver similar work consistently well on a predictable cycle year after year? The latter kind of team is often better suited to medium performers. Those performers should still have development plans and be recognized for their contributions.

## Terminal levels are acceptable

It should be acceptable for someone to reach a "terminal" level, meaning they may not be promoted past a given level. Provided they're contributing well, allow that stasis, seek their feedback on how to improve the team, and take time to celebrate their consistent performance. Recognition here may look less like compensation and more like verbal recognition and special assignments.
