## 1\. Her ask against the record, and how much is already decided

### 1.1 What she asks

Her formal review process asks for five things a company must have once it has a few dozen people: a talent strategy, which "could be as simple as 'pay for performance'"; rubrics tied to job levels and ladders; agreement on how to assess results against them; "a program to capture performance feedback and promotion nominations via written and numeric assessments"; a calibration process "to equalize treatment of people at the same level"; and "a compensation philosophy and a means for the performance and promotion processes to feed into any compensation outcomes" (Book pp. 399 to 400). The review itself is a written document the manager drafts after reading three to five peers' feedback and the person's self-assessment, carrying a promotion nomination and a performance designation on a five-point or three-point scale, submitted into calibration, adjusted there, formalized in an HR tool, and delivered in a conversation that spends little time on the past and most on the next half (Book pp. 402 to 407). Calibration is a roll-up of managers by level, facilitated by a division leader, checking outliers, distributions, and bias, and ending in a spreadsheet of changed designations and promotions checked against compensation budgets (Book pp. 407 to 413). Compensation is a philosophy, a fixed and variable split, job levels, market data from an outside source, salary bands and equity targets, and an annual refresh; the compensation conversation is a manager explaining the system, sharing a raise or a bonus or the absence of one, handling comparisons and disappointment, and pointing a disappointed person at the job ladder (Book pp. 413 to 419). Her closing sentence is the one this page answers: employees are "assessed and rewarded fairly, meaning appropriately for their contributions at their level, within the system you've built" (Book p.419, context line).

The workbook adds two instruments: a Performance Review Template (name, current level, proposed designation, proposed promotion, why not higher, why not lower, the one-sentence takeaway, three impact areas with metrics, two strengths and two development areas each with an approximate designation, and a promotion proposal with its timing) and a Compensation Conversations Preparation and Guide (the timeline and resources, preparation at the individual level including the size of the last increase, owning the message, and an outline that reminds the person of their current pay, shares base salary and target bonus for the coming year, and opens for discussion).

Every piece assumes a salaried knowledge worker, a manager who assigns, reviews, and rates from above, an annual or semiannual cycle with a review season, a calibration committee, levels and a ladder, salary bands and an equity refresh, a raise or a bonus as the reward the review produces, and a written narrative about the person in the manager's voice. None of that is Sŏn, and the session task says so. The task's own premise is tested in 1.5.

### 1.5 The session task's premise, tested, and the task text's figure

The task says almost none of her compensation machinery transfers, and that what transfers is the principle: compensation must be legible, defensible, and connected to a review people trust. Tested piece by piece, the premise is right about the form and wrong about one of its three principles.

What transfers as function once the form is removed, and is built here: a published account of how pay works that every person can explain in their own words (her philosophy, section 13); a stated mechanism from which a person can compute their own pay (her bands and market data have no equivalent, but her "instill trust in your systems" does: the pay page, section 13); a calendar (her review cycle becomes the person's own clock, section 2); a conversation guide (her compensation conversation becomes the offer conversation, the mechanics module, and the destinations of a pay question, section 14); and a rule for what a lead may and may not say about pay (section 14). What the record already decided and this page does not re-derive: built into menu price, never a service charge and never presented as one, no tips, the whole team including the culinary team, a point system weighted by days, calculated daily, paid on a reasonable rhythm, benefits from the start.

The third principle does not transfer. At Sŏn compensation is not "connected to a review process people trust." It is disconnected from the review by design, and the disconnection is what makes the review trustworthy and the pay legible (section 12). Her pay is connected to her review because her review produces a rating and her rating produces a raise; the record's review produces "development, growth, and the path forward rather than a verdict," the check-in refuses pay, and no feedback act touches pay (`17tn048qcjg`). What the task's principle is reaching for, that a person trusts the system that pays them, is carried at Sŏn by a system with no judgment in it: the seat, the horizon, the markers, and the days, each on a record the person can read.

The task text also carries a specific percentage and names a data source that is on the program's exclusion list. The record itself says the built-in compensation is "set at a defined percentage that is still being finalized" (WP p.20) and that "the remaining open piece is the final percentage" (WP p.22). The task text and the record therefore disagree about whether the figure exists, and the team did not use the figure, reason from it, or let it shape a sentence; financials are not a context source for this program. That is a finding about the task text, not a position, and it is stated once.

**P1 (Recommended).** Her review and compensation material lands on a record that settled the pool's principle and form and the review's focus, interval, and governing rule, and on eleven sessions that built everything a review would read and fenced everything it may not. What this page adds is the review as a designed mechanism (when, where, who, on what, writing what, refusing what) and the compensation architecture as a mechanism with no figure in it. Her forms are refused wherever they assume a rating, a level, a manager's narrative, or a raise; her functions survive as the pay page, the mechanics page, the person's own clock, and the rules for what a lead may say.

**P2 (Recommended).** The session task's premise is answered in two parts: her compensation machinery does not transfer as form, and two of its three underlying principles transfer as function (legible, defensible), while the third (connected to a review) is refused by design, because at Sŏn the review's trustworthiness and the pay's legibility both depend on their separation. The task text's figure and source were not used, and the record's own statement that the percentage is unfinalized is the one this page follows.

## 2\. The review, placed: interval, calendar, clock, room, holder, language

### 2.1 The interval, inside "three to six months"

The record states a range and no owner (S4 finding, line 41). Session 4 argued the check-in to monthly as "the longest interval at which the review every three to six months can be true to its own rule" (S4 4.5). This page picks the review's interval inside the range from the other side: the longest interval at which the review still does work the check-in does not, and the shortest the Maitre d's windows can carry beside every floor check-in.

Six months. Three arguments. First, the check-in already carries the running conversation monthly, so a review at three months is the fourth check-in wearing a longer form: it would re-read three lines the lead has already read and the path line the career conversation just wrote. At six months the review reads five check-in lines, a quarter's captures twice over, at least one unlock window on most seats, and a fluency window's currency, which is enough record for the dimensions to say something the last check-in did not. Second, the Realist's arithmetic (S4 4.5) governs: the Maitre d holds every floor check-in monthly, and a review is a longer block on the same windows; at six months the review adds one block per person twice a year, at three months it adds four, and Session 12 has already added placed and extended check-ins to what the seat spends (S12 P37). Third, the record's word is "every three to six months," and the six-month end is the one that keeps the review formal: a review that runs as often as the check-in stops being the moment the record wrote it as. The interval is a reset parameter with a floor: the reset may shorten it toward three months and never below, on one read only, whether reviews changed a path line or a supplied line the check-ins had not touched, which would mean the check-ins are not carrying the path. Reviews that contained something new are not a reason to shorten the interval; they are a candor failure on the lead's entry (section 4).

### 2.2 The calendar: the person's own clock, never a season

Her cycle is a company calendar: everyone reviewed in the same weeks, calibration after, delivery in a two-week window (Book pp. 408, 411). At Sŏn the review runs on the person's own clock, placed by the stack: the first review six months after the person's first paid hour, and every review six months after the last. There is no review season, because a season is a load spike on one seat's windows in one month, because it makes the review a house event rather than a person's, and because the only reason her season exists, calibration across everyone at once, is refused (section 6.4). The clock is the same clock the check-in runs on (S4 P15), and the stack places both.

Against the other rhythms. The ninety-day plan runs six check-ins every two weeks and closes as a state (S7 P25 to P29); the review never falls inside it. The career conversation is held once, "in the month after the plan closes and before the first review" (S9 P43); at six months from the first paid hour the first review falls about two months after it, which keeps the record's order and gives the path line time to be re-read at a check-in. The leads' review is weekly and the house review is quarterly, and neither reads a person (S4 P22, P26; S3 P10.4); the review's block is placed so that it never displaces the brief's slot or the readiness block, which are the two things a pre-service window already owes (S10 P20; S12 P37). The mechanism reset and dinner's first-quarter freeze: inside the freeze the room's cadence mechanisms do not change (S4 2.7; S9 P21), and the review is a person mechanism the stack places on the person's clock, so the freeze does not stop a review from being held; in practice none is held in dinner's first quarter, because the cohort is inside its plans and the leads' first reviews wait on `86akhb2jg`. The reset reads the review's parameters (the interval, the block's length, the load) like every other mechanism's.

### 2.3 On the clock, in a block, and where

The review is a scheduled block, paid as shift, placed by the stack as the career conversation is placed (S9 15.2): in a pre-service window with slack or at a closed hour, never mid-service, never at the close, never off the clock, ending before the brief. Its length is thirty minutes to start, a reset parameter, the same as the career conversation's and twice the check-in's, because it reads a period whole and carries an upward half longer than the fourth question. The lead may extend it when the window has the slack, at the lead's own read, and may move it to the person's next shift the lead also works when the window is short-crewed, by the check-in's own range (S4 P20); a moved review keeps its place and is never cancelled. "Nothing is required of a person before their first paid hour" and nothing is owed off the clock (S7 P15): a self-review written at home is refused, and the person's own half of the review is written in the block (section 5).

Where: not on the floor, not in a room the roster is in, and never with a third person present except an interpreter designation under S11 P20. Canon's rule that the environment names no person in a room (S10 P14) and the record's "post-mortems are private" (WP p.17) hold; the review is between two people and its content reaches no one beyond them (S11 P32, P33).

### 2.4 Who holds it

The lead who holds the person's check-in: the Maitre d for every floor seat on both strands (S8 P21, P24), the Head of Beverage for beverage seats, and, for the kitchen, whoever the chef partner rules holds the kitchen's check-in (S4 P16, chef-gated). The holder does not change for the review, because the review opens on the check-in's lines and a second reader would be reading a record written in a relationship they were not in. A lead's own review waits on `86akhb2jg`, with the team's reading that its holder is whoever holds the lead's check-in (section 8.5). The mentor is never in the room and never consulted (S7 P42; S10 P32). A founder holds no review except by the default Session 12 named for the first leads (`17tn048qcjz`, to Session 16).

### 2.5 In which language

The person's chosen language, as the check-in (S4 4.3; S11 P17), with the review record written in it and canon's terms standing untranslated (S11 P19). Where the lead and the person share no language the interpreter designation's rule for a check-in applies unchanged and is founder-gated with it (S11 P20; S12 I9).

**P3 (Recommended).** The review's interval is six months, placed on the person's own clock by the stack: the first review six months after the first paid hour, each review six months after the last, with no review season. The interval is a reset parameter with the record's three-month floor, shortened only if reviews change a path line or a supplied line the check-ins had not touched.

**P4 (Recommended).** The review is a scheduled thirty-minute block, a reset parameter, paid as shift, in a pre-service window with slack or at a closed hour, never mid-service, at the close, or off the clock, movable and extendable by the check-in's range and never cancelled; it is held by the lead who holds the person's check-in, between the two of them, in the person's chosen language, and never in a room the roster is in.

### 3.2 The four dimensions at review, read as states and never as marks

Session 6 fixed the dimensions and the terms, and said "the marks Session 13 uses at review are Session 13's to set, and the record constrains them already: a review is 'development, growth, and the path forward rather than a verdict' (WP p.19), so the review's marks carry no verdict either" (S6 6.2). This page sets them as states read from records, in the record's own vocabulary, with no scale.

**Dimension 1, competency for the seat.** Read from the progression state: the horizon the person holds on the seat sheet's field 6 and the date of the next scheduled unlock. There is nothing to mark. A person in a seat holds its entry horizon by construction, because the readiness form read ready before the first solo (S7 P23), and holds each later horizon because an assessor read the form ready and the lead recorded it (S8 P37). A lead who "rated" competency at review would be judging an unlock, which no lead may do. So the review's dimension 1 is a sentence the stack writes: the horizon held, since when, and what is scheduled next. Her "does not meet" has no place here, because a person who did not hold a horizon would be a plan not held (Session 14's, S7 P29) or a readiness read that reads not yet (S12 P21), and neither is a review.

**Dimension 2, self-directed mastery.** Read from the invisible advocate's own record, "not from a lead's impression, because 'No one's advancement waits on a manager's attention or judgment'" (S6 6.2): unlocks opened and taken, modules completed, modules authored and approved, connections added, and the fluency markers' currency, as a list with dates. ## 4\. "No surprises" as a mechanism

### 4.1 The rule, and why a wish is not enough

"The governing rule is no surprises: continuous communication means a formal review never contains new information" (WP p.19). Her version is the manager's diligence: "If you've managed well, there are no surprises" (Book p.401). The record's is stronger and Session 4 built the first half of it: the check-in is placed by the stack rather than by a lead's memory, so the conversation the review summarizes cannot silently stop (S4 P15). Session 12 built the second half: the only record of coaching is the person's own line, so there is no lead's file for a review to open (S12 P10, P48). This page builds the third half, the review's own: the review is bounded by the page, it stops when it would exceed the page, and the record proves which it did.

### 4.2 Bounded: the stack assembles, the lead adds nothing

Before the block, the stack assembles the review from the records in 3.1 into the review record's fields (I1): the lines with dates, the states, the counts, the connections, the infrastructure items. The lead's pre-read is that assembly and the records it cites, and the lead writes nothing (S12 4.1). The lead's card (I3) has no field for the lead's observation, summary, or view. So the review's content is fixed before anyone speaks, and it is the same content the person can open on their phone the day before.

### 4.3 Stops: what happens when a review would contain something new

A lead who arrives at a review holding something about the person's work that is on no record the person can read has a hypothesis, and the hypothesis has one destination, the check-in (S12 P5, P6). At the review the lead does not say it. The review proceeds on the page. After the block the lead asks the stack to place a check-in (S12 P16), and the hypothesis is said there, once, as an observation of behavior and impact, after the person's own account. The review never becomes the placed check-in and the placed check-in is never held inside the review's block; the two are different mechanisms with different refusals, and a review that absorbed a hypothesis would have become her manager's review with the writing removed.

Three cases, each with its path. Something the lead saw and the person did not write: a hypothesis, to the check-in, as above. Something a record shows that the person has not read: the lead reads the record with the person at the review, because the record is the person's to read and the review is where the page is read whole; a capture the person wrote and forgot is not new information. Something excellent the lead has not said: the record's rule runs both ways, "If something is excellent, they know before it too" (WP p.19), and a strength the lead has not named at a check-in is named at the next one; at the review the lead may read aloud what the record shows, which is section 5.3's formal half of "you really have talent," and may not add a judgment the record does not carry.

### 4.4 Proves: the read-back and the candor line

At the end of the block the lead reads the review record back and asks one question in the person's language: was anything said in this review that was not on your page or on a record you could read. The person's answer is a mark on the record, nothing new or something new, in the person's words if they choose to say what. The mark is the mechanism's proof and its enforcement. A review marked "something new" is a candor line on the lead's entry at the leads' review, read as a failure of the check-in to carry what it should have, "read on the lead's entry and not a diligence lapse" (`17tn048qcjg`), with the new item placed at a check-in by the stack and nothing about it written on the person page. The count of reviews marked "something new" per lead per period is on the leads' review record as a count on the mechanism, never per person, and at the reset it is read beside the check-in's interval: reviews that contain new information are the check-in failing, and the fix is on the check-in and the lead's entry, never on the review's interval and never on the person.

The form enforces it a second way: every item on the review record carries the date of the line or the state it cites, and an item with no citation cannot be entered. What cannot be cited cannot be reviewed.

**P9 (Recommended).** "No surprises" is built as three halves: the stack assembles the review from the records the person can read and the lead adds nothing; a lead holding something not on a record says nothing of it at the review and asks the stack to place a check-in, where it is said as a hypothesis; and the review ends with the read-back and one question, whether anything said was not on the page, whose answer is a mark on the record.

## 9\. The compensation architecture, designed without a figure

### 9.1 What the record fixed, and what it left

Fixed, and not re-derived: the team's compensation is built into the menu price on the backend; it is not a service charge, is never presented as one, and is not a tip, so no tip-aggregation tool exists (WP p.20, p.22); it is distributed to the whole team, the culinary team included; on a point system weighted by the days each person works; calculated daily rather than quarterly; paid on a reasonable rhythm; benefits are planned from the start; cross-strand fluency is recognized in compensation; teaching is paid; the percentage is unfinalized (WP p.11, p.19, p.20, p.22). Left, and designed here: what the pool is a share of; who is in it, seat by seat; how a person's points are set and what may never set them; what a day is; how the daily calculation is done and shown; the payment rhythm's owner; how base pay and the pool relate; how a salaried seat sits on a day-weighted system; and every parameter's owner.

The architecture has two layers, and the record describes only the second. Base pay is the hourly wage of an edge seat or the salary of a salaried seat, which the record never names and everywhere assumes: "a paid practical" (WP p.18), "the training plan with pay dates" (WP p.18), and every mechanism this program placed "as shift." The pool is the built-in share the record describes. Every person on the roster holds both. No figure for either appears in this program; the wage and salary values, and wage and hour law, are the founders' with counsel (finding 19.1).

### 9.2 The pool, as a mechanism

The pool is the day's built-in share: a defined proportion, the percentage the record calls unfinalized, applied to a defined base, computed by the stack at the close of each day from the point-of-sale record. Three things define it and each is a parameter with an owner:

The percentage. The record's open item (WP p.22). Owner: the founders, set with Dominic's domain against the financial model. Financials are not a context source for this program; this page states no value and no range.

The base: which revenue counts. The record says where the compensation is collected, in the menu price, and not what the pool is a share of. The base is a list of revenue classes, in or out, and the team's constraint on the list is one sentence: the base must not shrink when a server uses the generosity range, because a comp that reduces the pool makes the team pay for its own generosity, and the record wants the range "fully used, not minimized" (WP p.26). The team recommends that a comped item counts in the base at its menu price and that recovery spend does not reduce the day's pool; which other classes are in (the morning window, events, retail, a private tasting) is the founders' with Dominic's domain, versioned on the parameters register (I5).

One pool per day, for the whole house. The record's picture is dinner-shaped, "the nights they help fill" (WP p.20), and the record's thesis is one restaurant "met at different times of day" (WP p.33). The team recommends one pool per calendar day across every open period, shared by everyone who worked that day in any period, and not a pool per period. Three reasons. A per-period pool makes the periods compete for the same people's days and pays the morning window's team out of the morning window alone, which is the daypart bolted on that the record's first page names as the structural failure (WP p.2). Cross-period fluency is a marker the record needs (S8 P36), and a person who holds the window at seven and the floor at seven is one person on one day. And "no hierarchy of importance" (WP p.20) applied to periods means the person who set the room for dinner at ten in the morning shares dinner. The alternative, a per-period pool, is stated so the founders can refuse the recommendation: it would pay each period's team from its own revenue and would need a rule for the shared seats, and the team found no sentence in the record that asks for it.

### 9.3 Who is in it

Every seat on the roster that worked the day, in every domain and both strands: servers, hosts, runners, the beverage seats, the kitchen's Station Leads, Chefs on the line and on prep, the Steward, and the salaried seats on the rule in 10.1. A person in training is in it from their first paid hour (11.4). A candidate on a paid practical is not, because a candidate is not on the roster and the practical's employment form is counsel's (`86akh7qwd`). The overnight cleaning crew is contracted labor outside the web (S8 finding 21.10) and is outside the pool by that fact; finding 19.13 states what that costs the record's "whole team." The founders are not the team; whether the partners hold points is Session 16's and founder-gated (`86akh3x9m`). Under branch A the chef partner is a founder; under branch B the chef seat is a salaried seat in the pool (10.1).

### 9.4 A person's points: the inputs, and what may never be one

A person's points on a day are set by four things and nothing else, each on a record the person can read:

1. The seat held, at the horizon held. Every seat sheet carries, at each of field 6's horizons, the seat's point weight (I6). The weight is a seat property, the same for every person holding that seat at that horizon, and it is published on the seat sheet and on the transparency sheet's item 6. Its value is the founders' with Dominic's domain. This is the unlock-tied progression Session 8 named as this page's (S8 P30): an unlock recorded by the lead moves the person to the next horizon's weight on the date of the record, which is the record's "direct line between learning and earning" (WP p.19) made mechanical.
2. The markers. Cross-strand fluency and cross-period fluency, each an on-or-off addition while current inside Session 8's window (S8 P35, P36), and the teaching marker while a mentored plan is open (11.1). A marker's value is a parameter; a marker is never graded.
3. The days. The weight the record fixed: the person's points count on each day they worked, and a day is defined in 9.5.
4. The seat's day-weight rule for a salaried seat (10.1), which is the one place a seat's days are counted by rule rather than by shift.

What may never set or move a person's points, each with its rule: a review's states, mark, or the person's half (section 12); a check-in line or anything said at one (S4 P15); a rating, which does not exist (P12); a designation held or withheld (S10 P33); a recognition given or received (S11 P13); a lead's judgment of any kind, because "No one's advancement waits on a manager's attention or judgment" (WP p.11) and pay is advancement's downstream; a lagging indicator as a driver beyond the pool's own base (`86akh5ux8`), so a person's points never rise or fall with food cost, labor percentage, or a period's revenue; a customer's rating, a tip, or a per-server sales attribution, because the relationship is the server's book and the pool is shared (WP p.25); hours worked, because the record chose days; a period worked, because one pool per day already carries it; the person's history before the house, tenure as such, or a negotiation at hire, because "one rule for everyone by construction" is the whole defense of the system (`17tn048qc2c`). The points a person holds are a function of records, and no person in the house, the founders included, moves them by hand.

### 9.5 A day, defined

The record says "weighted by the days each person works" and never says what a day is (finding 19.3). A day is a calendar day on which the person worked a scheduled shift of any length in any period, counted once: a person who works the morning window and dinner on one day has worked one day, which is why cross-period fluency is a marker and not a second day (10.2). A check-in, a review, a career conversation, a training block, a paired service, a module hour on shift, and the orientation block are scheduled shift and count, because they are paid as shift (S4 P15; S7 P15; S9 P43) and the record does not shift costs to employees (WP p.19). A scheduled day not worked does not count. A day the house closes after the schedule posted, and what the scheduled people are paid for it, is a wage question for the founders with counsel and a parameter on the register, with the team's reading that the day's pool is zero and the base pay is the founders' call. The definition of a day is on the mechanics page in every house language.

### 9.6 The daily calculation, and who can see it

At the close of each day the stack computes the day's pool from the base and the percentage, sums the points of every person who worked that day (each person's seat weight at their horizon plus their current markers, and the salaried rule's days), and writes each person's share as their points over the day's total points, times the pool. The person's share, their points and how they are composed, and the day counted are on the person's pay page (I4) by the next morning, in the person's language, the way the thank-you note follows the night (WP p.26). The share accrues to the pay date. No lead computes anything, no lead sees another person's page, and the calculation runs on the point-of-sale record and the schedule, two systems the record names as unsettled and settled respectively (finding 19.8). Until the OS surface carries it, the calculation is a build on the backbone and the partners' page records it as manual, by Session 7's rule for every mesh gap (S7 P31).

What the person can see of the arithmetic is section 13.2's question and it is founder-gated at the pool's total.

### 9.7 The payment rhythm

"Calculated daily rather than quarterly and paid on a reasonable rhythm" (WP p.20). The rhythm's owner is the record's silence (finding 19.4). The team recommends: the pool share is paid on the same pay date as base pay, in the same payment, never later than base pay and never on a slower cycle, because a share that arrives a month after the nights it was earned is not the "upside of a full night" (WP p.20) as anyone feels it; the cycle's length is bounded by pay-date law, which is counsel's, and by what the payroll platform carries, which is to confirm; the Realist's read is that an hourly team in this labor market is paid weekly or every two weeks in practice and that a rhythm slower than the market's makes "reasonable" false at the first pay date. The rhythm is a parameter on the register, founder-gated with counsel, and it is a duration and not a figure.

### 9.8 Base pay against the pool: the record's silence, stated

The record describes the pool and never describes base pay (finding 19.1). The two relate as two layers on one pay date: base pay is the seat's hourly wage or salary and is what the training plan's "pay dates" (WP p.18) and the paid practical pay; the pool is the share. The team states three constraints and no value: base pay for an edge seat is an hourly wage set per seat, the same for every person in the seat, and published on the sheet like the weight (one rule for everyone); the pool is never a substitute for base pay and no offer states a total that depends on the pool, because the pool is a share of nights not yet filled and "never assert the unproven" (WP p.36) applies to a candidate before it applies to anyone; and every wage, overtime, tip-law, and pay-transparency question the two layers raise is counsel's, including whether a share built into menu price is or is not a tip in law, which the parked carryover already holds outside the program (`86ajgnhzk`). The wage values are the founders' with Dominic's domain and counsel.

The company runs on Rippling, chosen for what it delivers, including access to cost-effective benefits for frontline employees and a fast path to full benefits as the business supports them" (WP p.20). ## 10\. Salaried seats, the lead's entry point, fluency in pay, unlocks, and designations

### 10.1 A salaried seat on a day-weighted system

Finding 18.7 stands: "salaried roles" appears once in the record and the day-weighted pool has no place for it (S6 18.7). Session 8 supplied the structural test, ownership of a domain or program across every open period, with a shift shape that is not a set of services, and the list: the Operations Lead, the Maitre d, the chef seat, the Head of Beverage, with the Station Leads chef-gated (S8 P33, P34). The founders ratify the list (`86akh9tbw`). This page prices the class as a mechanism.

A salaried seat holds two things: a salary as its base pay, and points in the pool. The team recommends that a salaried seat is in the pool and not outside it, and states the alternative. In: the seat's points on every day the house is open in the pay period, by rule and not by shift, because a seat that owns a domain across every open period has worked every day the domain ran; the seat's weight is a seat property like any other; and the seat's upside on a full night is therefore real, which "no hierarchy of importance" (WP p.20) and "the people who make the food share in the nights they help fill" require of the seat that runs the pass as much as of the seat that runs the line. A lead in the pool holds no conflict, because a lead moves no one's points and holds no discretion over the pool; the only thing a lead can do to the pool is fill the room. The alternative, salaried seats outside the pool on salary alone: it is simpler to administer, it removes the appearance that a lead is paid from the same share as the people whose reviews they hold, and it makes a full night worth nothing to the two seats that hold it. The team recommends in; the founders decide, with Dominic's domain.

### 10.2 What a lead enters at, named without altitude

Session 8 read a lead's entry as two webs: the lead holds a center seat in the house's web whose connections are the seat's by construction, and enters their own inverted web at its center like every person, at the lead seat's entry horizon (S8 P31; founder-gated at P32). In the architecture, what a lead enters at is a name with no rank in it: the seat's salary at the seat's entry horizon and the seat's point weight, both seat properties on the lead's sheet, the same for whoever holds the seat. A lead's own unlocks (the other lead's domain read through the leads' review, the periods held as they open, cross-domain fluency like anyone's, S8 10.3) add markers and connections as they add anyone's, and nothing else moves a lead's pay. "Further along for a lead is the same measure as for a runner" (S8 10.3), and in pay it is the same mechanism. There is no seat above a lead's to enter and no rank to name; the entry point is the sheet's first horizon, priced. The founders ratify (`86akh9tbz`).

### 10.3 Cross-strand and cross-period fluency, recognized in pay

The record commits cross-strand fluency to be "recognized in compensation as meaningful" (WP p.11), Session 8 defined it so it can be demonstrated (S8 P35), and this page prices it as a mechanism: a marker, on while the connections are current inside the window and off when the window lapses, adding a parameter's value to the person's points on every day it is on. It is recognized as a marker and never as a dimension, a rank, or a seat (S8 P35; S6 6.2). Its value is founder-gated (P26).

Cross-period fluency: the record does not name it and Session 5 and Session 8 argued it into the structure as the second marker (S5 P62; S8 P36), leaving whether pay recognizes it to this page (`86akh7qxy`). The team recommends yes, on the same mechanism, for one reason the day's definition makes plain: a person who holds two periods works one day on a day they hold both, so without a marker the second register earns nothing, and the record's own thesis is that the second register is what the industry's bolted-on daypart could not supply (WP p.2, p.33). The alternative, no marker, would leave cross-period fluency recognized only in connections and the succession pool's order (S8 P36), and the team states it and does not recommend it.

### 10.4 Advancement and unlocks against pay

Each unlock on the person's own seat (field 6's horizons) moves the person's weight to the next horizon on the date the domain lead records the form the assessor scored (S8 P37). ## 11\. The routed pay lines: mentoring, authorship, completion, training, translation, and the two values set with Dominic's domain

### 11.1 Mentoring

The form: a teaching marker on the mentor's points on every day a plan they mentor is open, from the mentee's first paid hour to the plan's close as a state, and never per the mentee's outcome, because a mentor paid for a plan held becomes a judge of the person they answer questions for, and the mentor "gives no verdict on the person to anyone" (S7 P42). A mentor may hold one open plan at a time (S7 P41), so the marker is never stacked. Its value is founder-gated (P26).

### 11.2 Module authorship

"Any qualified team member can create training modules and get paid for teaching everyone else" (WP p.19). Session 7's constraint holds: paid on approval against the module standard (S7 I11) and never on a module written, so that volume is not the pressure to fill (S7 P35). Whether the author also earns per completion by others: the team recommends no, because it would pay authors to push modules onto people and would rank authors by reach, which is the pressure Ma warns against (Deck 3.0, p.10) and a per-person count by another route (S9 P32). The record's "the more modules completed, the more earned" is read in 11.3.

### 11.3 Completion

The record's sentence names the learner: "The more modules completed, the more earned, a direct connection between personal growth and financial reward" (WP p.19), and Session 7 fixed that completion is paid "for completion recorded by the platform, not for a module opened" (S7 P35). The team's reading is that the record's "more earned" for completion is carried by the architecture without a separate payment: every module hour is on scheduled shift and paid as shift (S7 P10), and every completed set leads to an unlock that moves the person's weight (10.4), which is the "direct connection" the sentence promises. A separate amount per module completed is not recommended, because it pays for completing rather than for holding, because the readiness read and not the completion is what validates (S7 P23), and because a per-module amount is a volume incentive the standard exists to refuse. This is a reading of a record sentence against its neighbors and the founders ratify or refuse it; the alternative, a flat amount per completion recorded by the platform under Session 7's constraint, is stated.

### 11.4 Training pay

Session 7 found that the transparency sheet carries "the training plan with pay dates" and the record never says what training pay is (S7 19.5). The form: a person in training is paid base for every scheduled hour from the first paid hour, and holds points in the pool at their seat's entry-horizon weight from the first paid hour, so that a trainee's day counts as a day and their share is the seat's. The team recommends no training weight below the entry weight, because a reduced weight is a probation label written in pay, which the person page refuses in words (S7 P25), because "nothing is required of a person before their first paid hour" and nothing is owed after it at a discount (S7 P15), and because "no one touches a table until they are actually ready" (WP p.19) already means the first weeks are the house's investment and not the person's. The alternative, a training weight, is stated and not recommended; the choice is founder-gated because it is a weight. The paid practical is base pay only, outside the pool, on the employment form counsel sets (`86akh7qwd`).

### 11.5 Translation and interpreting

Translation of a house-written page is paid "on the module authorship model" (S11 P18; `17tn048qc2c`): a flat payment per page version approved by the page's owner and a second holder of the language, paid with base pay, never as a point, and again per version; software-drafted translations are paid on the holder's approval work and marked with the disclosure line (S11 8.4). Interpreting is a designation held for a check-in, a review, a brief, or a why block, on the clock, and like every designation it carries no pay line beyond shift (S10 P33; S11 P19). The founders and the first leads are the pre-opening translators, which puts unpaid founder work and paid lead work on the same pages; the leads' translation is paid as this section states and the founders' is Session 16's.

### 11.6 The generosity range's value and the financial recovery threshold: the setting method

## 12\. Review and pay: separated, and what carries her "pay for performance"

### 12.1 The decision

No review output reaches pay. Not the states, not the person's half, not the "nothing new" mark, not the upward half, not anything said in the block. No check-in line reaches pay (S4 P15). No feedback act of any kind is a pay input (`17tn048qcjg`). The point system's inputs are the four in 9.4, and every one of them is a record written by the stack from a form, a schedule, or a sheet, never by a person's judgment of a person.

### 12.2 The argument

Six reasons, each already in the record or the ledger. The record's review is "development, growth, and the path forward rather than a verdict" (WP p.19), and a review that moves pay is a verdict whatever else it says, because the person in the chair hears the number before the path. The check-in refuses pay (S4 P15) and feedback never touches pay (S12), so a pay-tied review would be the one conversation in the house where the person's work and their wage are in the same room, which is the room the program spent eleven sessions emptying. The person page refuses ratings (S7 P25), so there is nothing on it a pay mechanism could read. The no-surprises rule cannot survive a pay-tied review: a raise is news and its absence is news, and her own chapter is a manual for delivering that news ("Managing disappointment," Book p.417), which is the surprise the record forbids. "No one's advancement waits on a manager's attention or judgment" (WP p.11), and pay progression is advancement's downstream; a lead who could move pay at a review is the manager the advocate exists to remove, and the politics the record says begin where the path is unclear (WP p.11) begin again at the review. And legibility: a person can compute their pay from the seat, the horizon, the markers, and the days (13.2) only because nothing in the computation is anyone's opinion, and the day a review enters it, "am I being treated fairly" (Book p.414) stops being a question the arithmetic answers.

### 12.3 What carries her "pay for performance"

Her philosophy is "higher rewards for higher performance" delivered through a rating (Book p.413). At Sŏn the same function is carried by four mechanisms with no rating in them. The unlock: the person who learns a horizon and holds it on an assessor's read earns the next weight on the date of the record, "a direct line between learning and earning" (WP p.19), which is pay for performance with the performance defined as a competency held rather than a manager's impression of a half. The markers: the person who holds both strands or both periods, demonstrated and current, earns the marker while it is current. The pool's weighting: the person who works the days earns the days, and "the upside of a full night" (WP p.20) is earned by the people who filled it, kitchen included. And the days themselves. What is not carried, deliberately: differential reward for doing the same seat better than another person in it, because the house has no rating of "better" and refuses to build one, and because the record's account of who does the essential work is that the people at the edge carry it together and "no single element bears the load" (WP p.8). A person who holds a seat's horizon and never unlocks earns the horizon's weight and the pool's growth, and is not "successfully meets" or "partially meets"; they hold the seat. Session 14's high, middle, and low performers must not add a pay tier to this (carryover B1).

### 12.4 Her "comparisons," answered by the rule

"So-and-so is getting paid more than me even though they're doing the same job" (Book p.417). At Sŏn the answer is arithmetic and it is public: the same seat at the same horizon with the same markers on the same days earns the same share, and any difference is a horizon, a marker, or a day, each on the person's own pay page. A person cannot see another's page, because review content beyond the person and their lead is founder-gated and a pay page is the person's (S11 P33); a person can see the rule, because the seat weights are on the seat sheets and the transparency sheet's item 6 hands the mechanics to every candidate (WP p.18). Her "geographical location to tenure to when market data was refreshed" (Book p.417) has no counterpart, because none of those is an input. Whether the house publishes every seat's weight to the house, and not only to the candidate for that seat, is section 13.3's and recommended.

**P36 (Recommended).** No review output, check-in line, feedback act, or anything said in a block reaches pay, and the point system reads no person's judgment of a person; review and pay are separated by design, because the record's review is not a verdict, the check-in refuses pay, the page refuses ratings, no surprises cannot survive a raise, the advocate replaces the manager, and legibility requires that nothing in the computation is an opinion.

**P37 (Recommended).** Her "pay for performance" is carried by the unlock, the markers, the pool's weighting, and the days, with performance defined as a competency held on an assessor's read, a fluency demonstrated and current, and the nights filled; no differential reward for doing the same seat better exists, and no later session adds a pay tier. A comparison is answered by the rule, which is public, and never by another person's page.

## 13\. Legibility and transparency

### 13.1 What a person can see of their own pay, and when

They open the system and see it" (WP p.11) applied to pay, and it is her "instill trust in your systems" (Book p.415) built as a surface rather than said as a posture.

### 13.2 What a person can see of the pool's calculation: the arithmetic, held at one figure

To check their own share by arithmetic a person needs the day's total points and the day's pool. The total points is a house figure and not a lagging figure; the team closes it as visible on every pay page. The day's pool is a share of the day's revenue at a known proportion, and a person who can see the pool and knows the percentage from item 6 can compute the day's revenue, which is exactly the lagging figure the transparency line holds founder-gated: "whether any lagging figure appears on any surface the house reads, and which" (S11 P33; `86akh5uby`). The record pulls the other way: the mechanics are handed to every candidate "No surprises and nothing withheld" (WP p.18). This is finding 19.5, the record against the line.

The team recommends that the person sees the whole arithmetic of their own share, the day's pool included, because a share a person cannot check is legible by trust and not by arithmetic, and the record's whole answer to the industry's check-line dishonesty is a mechanism a person can verify (WP p.20). The cost is stated plainly: every person on the roster can derive the day's revenue, and the founders own whether that is acceptable. The alternative, also stated: the pay page shows the person's points, the day's total points, and the person's share, and holds the pool's total, so that the share is checkable against the total points and the rule but the revenue is not derivable; the cost is that the one figure in the arithmetic the person cannot see is the one that decides the amount. ## 14\. The compensation conversation, and where a pay question lands

### 14.1 No pay event, so no conversation about one

Her compensation conversation follows a review and delivers a change or its absence (Book p.416). At Sŏn there is no change to deliver: a weight moves on a form's date and the pay page shows it; a marker turns on when the window's conditions are met and the page shows it; the pool rises and falls with the nights and the page shows it daily. There is no raise, no bonus, no refresh, no merit cycle, and no moment at which a lead tells a person what they will earn next year. What exists instead is three things her guide is reaching for, each already a mechanism: the once-per-person explanation, the daily statement, and the destinations of a question.

### 14.2 The once-per-person explanation: the offer conversation and the mechanics module

Her "educate yourself" (Book p.414) is a requirement on every lead and every founder who hands item 6: they can explain the mechanics in their own words and answer a candidate's question about them without a figure beyond the sheet (S6 7.1, module 6). The compensation mechanics module (I8) is the form: completed on scheduled shift by every person in onboarding as part of the training plan's paid time, and by every lead and the founders before the first interview they run, teaching the mechanics page and the pay page and nothing about anyone's pay. The offer conversation (S6, `86akh9te2`) is where a person first hears their own numbers, from the sheet, in the correspondence register, with item 6's mechanism already in their hands from the first interview; it is the one time a lead speaks a figure, and the figure is the sheet's.

### 14.3 The daily statement

Her "instill trust in your systems" (Book p.415) is the pay page (13.1). Her "Am I being treated fairly?" is answered by the arithmetic every morning and her "Am I being recognized for my performance?" by the weight moves and the markers on the same page (Book p.414). No conversation is scheduled to say either.

### 14.4 Where a pay question lands

The check-in refuses pay (S4 P15), and this page reads that refusal exactly: the check-in refuses a pay negotiation, a pay promise, and a pay figure spoken by the lead. It does not refuse the path, and a question about the path (what unlocks next, what a marker needs, which seat after this one) is the third question's and the career conversation's, answered from the sheet and the fluency record with no figure spoken, because the page shows the figure. Every other pay question has a destination that is not the check-in, on the destinations page (S10 I3):

A question about the arithmetic of one's own share (why was Tuesday's share what it was): the pay page first, which shows the composition; then the correction path (16.6) if a record is wrong.

An error (a day not counted, an unlock not moved, a marker not on): the pay correction path, an item to the Operations Lead's domain, because "Cash, payroll, HR, compliance... in software" is that domain's (WP p.22), with the channel's window, acknowledged with an owner within seven days and corrected or declined with a written reason within thirty (S4 P30), and the correction paid on the next pay date. An error is a capture about a mechanism and never a question about a person.

A question about the rule (why is the weight what it is, why one pool per day): the mechanics page first; then the channel, as an item about a mechanism with an owner and a window (S11 6.8), which the leads' review reads and the reset may change by versioned decision.

A question about what another person earns: no destination, because a pay page is the person's; the rule is the answer (12.4).

A question about what one could earn: the path, at the check-in or the career conversation, as above.

### 14.5 What a lead may and may not say about pay

May say: the mechanics in their own words; what the sheet says the seat's wage and weights are, at the offer; that an unlock was recorded and the weight moves on that date, at the check-in where the form is read (S8 P37); where a question goes; that they do not know and will find out, which is her own "if you don't know the answer to a question, say that" (Workbook p.111) kept whole. May not say: a figure not on the person's own page or the sheet; another person's pay or share; a promise or a prediction of the pool, a marker, or an unlock's date; any sentence linking the review, a check-in, a designation, or a recognition to pay; "you'd earn more if" beyond the path the sheet shows; and any sentence from her disappointed-person script, "the system will not provide additional rewards if the contribution hasn't been high enough" (Book p.418), because the system has no such lever and a lead who implies one has invented a rating.

### 14.6 Her "managing disappointment," answered

A person disappointed by their pay at Sŏn has three destinations and no conversation to be managed in: the arithmetic (an error, to the correction path), the path (an unlock or a marker, at the check-in from the sheet), and the rule (an item on the channel). Her reframe, "How can we work together to make sure that six months from now, you feel that your compensation and perceived impact... are more closely aligned?" (Book p.418), survives as the career conversation's track and the check-in's path read, with the pay half removed: what would move the weight is on the sheet, and the lead names what the building must supply for the person to reach it, which is dimension 4. Her example line that compares a person's increase to a share of the company is refused with its number (Book p.418, md 295). Her "it may be that they really want to talk about getting promoted" (Book p.418) is the seat after this one, on the person's own path line, and the ready-now pool's order, which the person can see.

**P41 (Recommended).** There is no compensation conversation because there is no pay event; her guide's functions are carried by the offer conversation, the compensation mechanics module completed on shift by every person and by every lead and founder before their first interview, and the pay page. The check-in refuses a pay negotiation, promise, and figure, and answers the path from the sheet with no figure spoken.
