# Session 1: post-extraction work items

Typed subtasks for the post-extraction parent task (`86akh1hdg`). Each carries exactly one type: founder decision, document, process, instrument, or structure. Each rationale stands alone without the session's context.

## Founder decisions

### 1. Ratify the six founder operating principles

**Type:** Founder decision

**Rationale.** Sŏn's record (the white paper and the brand canon) holds the material for a set of principles governing how its founders operate, but does not state them as principles. Six have been derived and argued on the Session 1 page of the Operating System doc: the house runs without its founders; employees are the users and the founder's cognition is not the default; name what is open; blame the process, and the founders' processes first; authority lives in the domain, founders included; bandwidth before results, founders included. Each is marked recommended and cites the record it rests on. The founders ratify, amend, or reject each one. Until ratified, later sessions treat them as recommended, not settled.

### 2. Ratify the metronome position

**Type:** Founder decision

**Rationale.** Standard scaling advice says to set the pace faster than is comfortable. Sŏn's record sets service tempo by attunement and the daypart's register, never by a clock; sets the build sequence (which daypart opens when, whether lunch exists) by proof, deliberately slowly; and runs the build work itself fast, by multiplication and the stack rather than by founder effort. The Session 1 page argues these as two metronomes, with the build metronome split into a fast tempo and a gated sequence, and names the hazard that the build habit of speed leaks into the room during the first quarter of dinner service. The founders ratify or amend the position. Sessions 4 and 10 inherit it.

### 3. Ratify the management and leadership position

**Type:** Founder decision

**Rationale.** The standard distinction between leadership (direction, change, adaptive problems) and management (execution, stability, technical problems) does not map onto Sŏn's two parallel co-leads, who each carry both modes across a what/how split. The Session 1 page recommends that Sŏn not overlay the distinction on the leads as a second axis, and instead adopt it as a triage rule on problems (does this have a fix, or does it need a judgment?), hold direction at the center as the written vision the leads serve, and treat stability as a property of the system rather than something a lead produces by hand. The founders ratify or amend.

### 4. Set the founder decision-rights rule

**Type:** Founder decision

**Rationale.** The record holds that authority lives in the domain and that each founder owns their domain outright. It holds exactly one rule for a decision that crosses domains: whether lunch is ever built is an equal decision among all three partners. There is no general rule for which decisions are domain-owned, which are joint, what happens when a domain decision has consequences in another domain (a stack choice that changes the floor; a service decision that changes labor), or how a deadlock among three equal partners resolves. Each founder drafts their own version from inside their domain in prompt D of their working-with-me document; the three of them set the rule together. Session 2 (founding documents) and Session 3 (accountability) need the rule and carry a carryover for it.

### 5. Decide who carries the concept's voice to the team

**Type:** Founder decision

**Rationale.** The record makes one founder the public voice externally and puts mission and values before mechanics in onboarding, but does not say which founder speaks the concept to the team internally at a rhythm, or whether the two leads carry that voice when the founders are not in the building. A house built to run without its founders implies the leads must be able to. The founders decide which of them holds the internal voice and how it is handed to the leads. Session 2's founder job descriptions depend on the answer.

### 6. Reconcile the two accounts of the chef seat

**Type:** Founder decision

**Rationale.** The record holds two statements of the culinary seat that are not the same. The white paper makes the chef partner an equal founding partner who owns the culinary vision. The brand canon, in its held items pending an executive chef hire, states that the brand owns the culinary identity and the chef executes within it. These were written at different times. The two existing founders resolve which governs, or how the two are reconciled, before a chef partner is engaged, because it is the seat they are offering. The chef partner's own reading is invited in their working-with-me document once seated, and the position itself is a three-partner decision. Session 2 carries a carryover on this.

## Documents

### 7. Brandon writes his working-with-me document

**Type:** Document

**Rationale.** The Session 1 page holds a complete working-with-me spine (eleven prompts, each with guidance on what it asks, how to answer well, and what a weak answer looks like) and a seat-specific preamble for the founder who owns the concept, the operating systems, the experience, and the public voice. Three self-exercises (values, work style placement, strengths inventory) precede the writing and are specified there. The document is completed alone, from the spine, with all answers left blank until the founder fills them. It is exchanged with the other founders; the timing of the exchange with the chef partner is open and routes to Session 7, but this founder's document can be written now.

### 8. Dominic writes his working-with-me document

**Type:** Document

**Rationale.** Same spine and same three prerequisite exercises as item 7, with the seat-specific preamble for the founder who owns the capital, the financial architecture, and the technology build. The preamble points three prompts at that seat's particular weight: what in the model and the stack lives only in the founder's head; how the other founders get a read from the financial domain without a figure being asserted outside it; and how a founder whose domain did not come from hospitality intends to learn the floor. Can be written now.

### 9. The chef partner writes their working-with-me document (chef-gated)

**Type:** Document

**Rationale.** Same spine, with a preamble written as a prompt to a person not yet identified. The preamble tells the chef partner what the house already holds about their seat (the kitchen's strand structure, the Mahk standard, the distinction between culinary execution during service and culinary direction overall) and states plainly that the record currently holds two accounts of the seat (see item 6), which the chef is asked to read and respond to. Nothing is filled in on their behalf. This item is issued to the chef partner at the point Session 7 decides: before signature as diligence, or in the first ninety days as onboarding.

### 10. Build the founder knowledge-transfer register

**Type:** Document

**Rationale.** The record's founding claim is a company built so it does not depend on either founder's memory. Prompt B of each founder's working-with-me document asks them to list what they know that the house depends on and that is not yet written anywhere the house can read it, with a date for each item. The three lists are consolidated into one register: what is still in a founder's head, what breaks if they are unreachable, and when it moves into the system. The register is reviewed at the founder cadence and closed item by item before opening. It is the audit of how far the founding claim is currently true.
