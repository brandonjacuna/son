# Session 1: founder operating principles, the metronome, and the working-with-me document

**Status:** recommended positions for founder ratification. Nothing on this page is final.

**Source frame, stated once:** this page translates the introduction and first chapter of *Scaling People* (Claire Hughes Johnson) into Sŏn's operating terms. The book is not cited in the body. Where the page cites "the record," it means the white paper (cited as WP p.NN) and the brand canon (cited by section number). The record is what Sŏn has already decided; this page builds on it and does not re-derive it.

---

## How to read this page

**Marks.** Every position carries one of four marks inline.

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| [Recommended] | The team's position, ready for founder ratification |
| [Founder-gated] | Requires founder input the team cannot supply: values, interiority, working style, or a decision only the partners can make |
| [Chef-gated] | Deliberately left open for the chef partner to shape |
| [Team-filled] | Populated once the team exists |

**Two standing corrections apply to every section.**

1. **Sŏn compounds without headcount growth.** The standard apparatus for scaling a company assumes the organization grows by adding people, and most of its advice is timed to that growth. Sŏn's growth is in what the system knows, the depth of each customer relationship, and the archive (WP p.06, p.37). The web "never acquires altitude, only reach" (WP p.09), and the leadership structure is lean by design (WP p.22). Where this page says "as the house grows," read "as the system holds more," never "as headcount rises."

2. **Two different things are called an operating system.** Section 4 separates them. For this program and every session after it: "operating system," unqualified, means the human system of strategy, accountability, and cadence. The software is always "the stack" or "the OS surface," never "the OS" or "the operating system" on its own.

## 1. Founder operating principles

### What the record holds

The founding posture of Sŏn is stated once in the record and governs everything after it: the company is built so it does not depend on either founder's memory, and "the mark of these founders is a company built to run without them" (WP p.05 to 06). The record also holds the company's three non-negotiables, no exploitation ever, no toxicity ever, no disposable people (canon 01), and the metabolic sequence: bandwidth, then joy, then retention, then results (WP p.12, p.17; canon 01). It holds the founders' domains: one owns the concept, the operating systems, the experience, and the public voice; the other owns the capital, the financial architecture, and the technology build; the chef partner owns the culinary seat as an equal founding partner (WP p.03, p.05).

What the record does not hold is any founder's personal values, work style, strengths, or failure modes. Those are not in the white paper or the canon, and this page does not supply them.

### Trust, and where it comes from at Sŏn

Standard founder principles are, at bottom, about building trust between a manager and the people who report to them, one conversation at a time. At Sŏn, trust is extended by the structure in advance. The server's judgment is trusted and pre-authorized before the first shift: the generosity range, routine recovery, the table relationship (WP p.13, p.26; canon 08). Authority sits in the domain, not in a person above it (WP p.07). The founders' principles therefore describe how to hold a structure that has already extended trust, not how to earn it. A founder who re-centralizes trust in themselves, by requiring approval the structure did not require, is not being careful. They are converting a code-bearing house back into a load-bearing one.

Six principles follow from what the record holds. A seventh section names what only the founders can supply.

### Principle 1: the house runs without its founders [Recommended]

**What it says.** Anything a founder knows that the house needs is a liability until it is in the system. The founder's job is to move it there.

**Why the record requires it.** The first-time-team objection is answered in the record not with experience but with architecture: "a code-bearing organization built so it does not depend on either founder's memory" (WP p.05 to 06). The memory failure mode, institutional knowledge living in people, is named as the reason nothing compounds (WP p.13). The chef-dependency objection is answered the same way: "a business whose value lives in one person is load-bearing in exactly the way Part I diagnoses" (WP p.37). The record does not exempt the founders from this. It aims the principle at them first.

**What it requires in practice.** Each founder keeps a running account of what still lives only in their head: the concept decisions not yet written, the model assumptions not yet documented, the vendor relationships held personally, the reasons behind a build choice. That account is a to-do list for the system, not a description of the founder's value. The working-with-me template in section 7 carries this as prompt B.

**A corollary the record already states.** There is no hierarchy of importance (WP p.20): the pre-shift briefing and the signature dish, the phone and the final plate, are equally important, so "there is never a need to adjudicate what deserves care, because the answer is always yes." For the founders this removes a class of decisions. A founder does not spend judgment ranking what deserves attention. The system holds the standard for everything; the founders hold the standard for the system.

### Principle 2: employees are the users, and the founder's cognition is not the default [Recommended]

**What it says.** Self-awareness at Sŏn has a specific structural target: the way a founder thinks must not become the unexamined shape of the system.

**Why the record requires it.** "Restaurant founders build systems for themselves without realizing it, then blame employees when those systems do not work for anyone else. Sŏn has done it too, which is how the team knows the trap" (WP p.17). The record's answer is that systems are designed for the people who use them, with continuous feedback loops, and that the test is whether it works for a server six months into the industry, not whether it works for the founder.

**What it requires in practice.** Every instrument, cadence, and interface a founder designs is checked against a user who is not the founder. A founder who talks to think will build meetings; a founder who thinks to talk will build documents. Neither is wrong, but the house needs to know which it is getting and why, and the leads need the right to say the design does not work at the edge. The self-knowledge that makes this possible (work style, information preferences, decision mode) is founder-gated content. The principle that it must be surfaced and designed around is not.

**The complementary team is already on the cap table.** The founding structure hands the two domains outside the kitchen to founders who own them outright and makes the culinary seat a founding one (WP p.03, p.05). The domains are complementary by design. Whether the people are complementary in work style, and where their preferences collide, is not in the record. That is what the working-with-me exchange exists to surface.

### Principle 3: name what is open [Recommended]

**What it says.** A founder states what is decided as decided, what is open as open, and never presents the second as the first, to an investor, a customer, the team, or each other.

**Why the record requires it.** The record practices this on itself. It closes Part I with "Naming these keeps the account honest: the foundation is built, and a handful of pieces are still being decided rather than presented as settled" (WP p.15). It names the open stack decisions "because a settled-looking answer to an open question is the tell of a deck that has not been pressure-tested" (WP p.22). It refuses to name future brands because "the discipline is to never assert the unproven" (WP p.36, p.38). The canon's first aesthetic framework, Jaeyeonmi, forbids anything that conceals what it is (canon 01), and the master register is "present without performing" (canon 01). Crisis communication is "transparency first" (WP p.31). Recovery at the table is "be direct about what happened" (WP p.26).

**What it requires in practice.** Three habits. First, every founder-level document carries an open-items section, as the white paper does. Second, a founder who is asked for a position they do not hold says so rather than improvising one; "I have not decided" is a complete answer. Third, disagreement between founders is stated in the room where the decision is being made, not carried out of it. The standard failure is the manager who filters so heavily that the real issue is never said. The Sŏn failure is subtler and more likely: the founder who is so fluent that an open question sounds settled. Fluency is the risk this principle guards against.

**The gap.** The record establishes the principle externally, toward investors and customers. Its internal application, how the partners disagree with each other and what a founder does when they think another founder is wrong in their own domain, is not in the record and cannot be written by the team. Prompt E of the working-with-me template asks each founder to state it for themselves. [Founder-gated]

### Principle 4: blame the process, and the founders' processes first [Recommended]

**What it says.** When something fails, the first question is what allowed it, not who. The founders apply this to their own domains before anyone else's.

**Why the record requires it.** "When something goes wrong, the first question is not who failed. It is what allowed this to happen. Systems are designed to prevent, catch, and recover from errors. Management absorbs blame from staff. Post-mortems are private and process-focused" (WP p.17). The failure mode reference (WP p.14) exists so that no one intervenes at the person before naming which structural failure is present. Universal problems are design problems (WP p.17).

**What it requires in practice.** The founders are the first domain owners, so a failure in the model, the stack, the concept, or the food is diagnosed in the founder's process before it is diagnosed in a lead or a team member. This is the founder-level version of candor: not saying the hard thing to a report, but saying it about one's own domain, in writing, in the post-mortem. The record's no-surprises rule (WP p.19) applies upward as well: a lead should never learn in a review that a founder has been unhappy with a system for a month.

**Where it meets Principle 3.** A post-mortem that names the process is also an open-items entry. The two principles are one discipline seen from two sides: name what is open before it fails, and name what allowed it after.

### Principle 5: authority lives in the domain, and the founders are the first domain owners [Recommended, with one founder-gated seam]

**What it says.** Each founder owns their domain outright and is accountable to its outcomes. Neither reports to the other. The chef partner is an equal principal in the culinary domain.

**Why the record requires it.** "Authority lives in the domain. The person who owns the domain is accountable to its outcomes" (WP p.07 to 08). The founders' domains are stated as ownership, not oversight: "Two of its founders own everything outside the kitchen" (WP p.03); "the chef partner owns the culinary vision as an equal founding partner" (WP p.03). The rigid anchor points of the spider silk are "small, specific, explicitly defined, interlocking" domains, and "small and distributed is provably stronger than large and centralized" (WP p.09). That is the record's own argument against a founder who reaches into another founder's domain.

**What it requires in practice.** A founder does not decide inside another founder's domain. They bring a tension to it. The concept founder does not choose the point-of-sale spine; the capital founder does not set the emotional standard of the room; neither writes the menu. Where a decision sits on the seam between domains, the founders decide together, and the record gives one worked example: whether lunch is ever built "is an equal decision among all three partners, the chef partner included" (WP p.36).

**The seam.** The record holds that one joint decision and no general rule. Which decisions are domain-owned, which are joint, what happens when a domain decision has consequences in another domain (a stack choice that changes how the floor works; a service decision that changes labor cost), and how a deadlock resolves among three equal partners: none of this is in the record. The team can name the categories; it cannot set the rule. This routes to Session 2 (founding documents) and Session 3 (accountability) as a new carryover and is a founder decision in the post-extraction work. [Founder-gated]

### Principle 6: bandwidth before results, founders included [Recommended; the founder-level instrument is founder-gated]

**What it says.** The founders are subject to the same metabolic sequence as everyone else in the building. A founder running at zero bandwidth is a cooling failure, not a display of commitment.

**Why the record requires it.** "Bandwidth, then joy, then retention, then results. This is mechanical, not aspirational" (WP p.17). The cooling failure mode is "no mechanism for preventing burnout because it has no way to measure thermal load... It runs people at full capacity until they stop, and calls it dedication until it becomes turnover" (WP p.13). The content engine is sized explicitly so the volume is "achievable without burning the founders out" (WP p.30). The record does not say founders are exempt. It says the opposite, by designing the founders' own workload around the constraint.

**What it requires in practice.** Each founder can state what they look like at zero bandwidth and what the other founders should do about it (prompt H of the template). The house has leading indicators for the team's bandwidth (employee NPS, cultural labor score, WP p.22). It has none for the founders. The record's nervous system does not currently read the center. What that read should be is founder-gated and routes to Session 16, which owns the founder's own time and energy. [Founder-gated]

### What the principles do not contain [Founder-gated] [Chef-gated]

The six principles above are derived from the record and can be ratified or rejected as written. The following are not in the record, cannot be written by the team, and are marked so no later session mistakes their absence for a decision.

| Missing | Who supplies it | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|
| Each founder's three values at work, with the activity and the trade-off | Brandon; Dominic; the chef partner when seated | Working-with-me, prompt G |
| Each founder's work style (the quadrant, at work and at home, and under load) | Same | Working-with-me, prompt C |
| Each founder's strengths, innate and acquired, and what each costs | Same | Working-with-me, prompt F |
| How each founder wants to be disagreed with, and how they disagree | Same | Working-with-me, prompt E |
| The founder decision-rights rule (domain-owned versus joint; deadlock) | All three partners together | Session 2, Session 3; post-extraction founder decision |
| Which founder carries the concept's voice to the team internally, and whether the leads carry it in the founders' absence | Brandon and Dominic | Post-extraction founder decision; Session 2 founder job descriptions |
| The chef partner's read of the culinary seat, including the two accounts of it the record currently holds (see section 7.4) | The chef partner, then all three | Session 2 carryover |

The line the team holds: it may propose a value, a style, or a strength for a founder as a question. It may not record one as a position. The first attempt at this session did that, and it was un-made.

## 2. The metronome

### The standard advice, in one line

Set your pace faster than is comfortable, because performance plateaus break only under discomfort, and a fast-growing company sets that pace for you whether you choose it or not.

### Why the advice does not transfer whole

There is no growing headcount at Sŏn to set the pace. So the question becomes: which of Sŏn's tempos, if any, should be deliberately uncomfortable, and who sets each one? The input carried into this session was that Sŏn has two metronomes, a build metronome that takes the faster-than-comfortable advice and a service metronome that is set by the room. That reading is argued here against the record rather than restated.

### What the record holds on tempo

The record is dense with tempo decisions, and they do not all point the same way.

**Service tempo is set by attunement and register, never by a clock.** The step-back architecture is "attunement-triggered, not timer-triggered" (canon 08). The table walk is "below street pace, no narration" (canon 08). The dinner-to-late-night transition is "table-state dependent, not clock-dependent" and "nothing cleared prematurely" (canon 08). Mahk requires that "the room is slow enough that a customer is actually present to the experience" (canon 01). Dinner is "opulent, unhurried" (canon 01). Late night is "the room after the room," the same sequence in a "looser register" (canon 01, 08). The morning, by contrast, is a standing order and grab-and-go (WP p.05; canon 01): fast by design, because the customer's need is speed. The behavioral logic is constant across all four dayparts; the expression changes (canon 08). Service tempo, then, is a property of the daypart's register and, within a service, of the table. It is not a dial the founders turn.

**Where the record does want speed at the service moment, it engineers it upstream.** The matcha and the espresso programs front-load the labor in the back so the counter step is short and consistent: "higher consistency, the same peak, faster service" (WP p.04 to 05). The overnight cleaning crew moves closing labor off the servers so end-of-shift time goes to the thank-you note (WP p.26). The stack absorbs the integration work so the leads' time goes to the floor (WP p.21). The pattern is consistent: tempo at the moment of service is bought by moving work away from that moment, never by pushing the people in it.

**The build sequence is gated on proof and deliberately slow.** Dinner opens first and "runs on its own for at least a quarter, so the heart is solid before anything is added to it." The other core dayparts follow "once dinner is steady." Lunch and brunch are "not to be revisited until the restaurant has operated for a full year," and whether lunch is built at all is a three-partner decision with "no good answer" as a real option (WP p.36). The platform ceiling is opened "gated on the proof, never a promise made before the building earns it" (WP p.38).

**The build tempo, inside that sequence, is fast, and fast by system.** The content engine targets high daily volume, and "the multiplication, not heroic effort, is what makes the volume real," sized to run "without burning the founders out" (WP p.30). Most content moves "at the speed of the moment" with a small share held for review (WP p.31). In hiring, "speed is respect": the decision is made quickly and development starts immediately (WP p.18). Feedback is continuous, and leading indicators are "tracked regularly rather than annually" (WP p.22). Compensation is calculated daily (WP p.20).

**Individual learning is self-paced.** The next skill set "unlocks automatically after a set period. It is self-paced" (WP p.11). No one's advancement waits on a manager's attention, and no one's pace is set by one either.

### The position [Recommended]

The two-metronome reading is right in its cut and incomplete in its detail. Sŏn has two metronomes, and the first of them has two parts.

**The service metronome is set by the canon and the table.** The founders do not set it faster. A faster-than-comfortable service tempo would violate Mahk, the step-back architecture, and the master register at once. The founders' job with respect to service tempo is to protect it: to engineer speed upstream where a daypart calls for speed (the morning), and to hold the room slow where the register calls for slow (dinner). Whether a given service holds its tempo at 8:15 on a Friday is the realist's test, not the architect's; this page names the position and routes the test.

**The build metronome has a tempo and a sequence, and they run on different rules.** The tempo of the build work (content, hiring, feedback, decisions, the stack) is fast, and the faster-than-comfortable advice applies to it with one governor: fast by system, never by grind. Multiplication, front-loading, and the stack are how the record makes speed sustainable. A build tempo that is uncomfortable because the founders are working past their bandwidth is not the metronome doing its job. It is the cooling failure (Principle 6). The sequence of the build (which daypart opens when, which concept follows, whether lunch exists) is gated on proof and is the one place the record explicitly rejects "faster." The founders will be tempted to add a daypart because the room is there and the kitchen is staffed. The record opened with why that fails (WP p.02) and closed with the sequence rule that prevents it (WP p.36).

**The founders' own learning tempo is not in the record.** The standard advice is aimed at an individual's plateau: set a personal metronome past the comfortable rate. The record's learning system is self-paced by design, but that system is built for the team, not the founders. Who sets a founder's learning pace, and against what, is founder-gated and routes to Session 16. [Founder-gated]

### The hazard to name for later sessions

The build habit is speed. The service register is presence. The founders will carry the first into the second unless something stops them, and the most likely moment is the first quarter of dinner service, when the build engine is idling and the founders are in the room. The position here is that the founders' presence on the floor is subject to the service metronome, not the build one. Session 4 (operating cadence) and Session 10 (team environment) inherit this.

## 3. Management and leadership

### The standard distinction, in one line

Leadership sets direction and drives change; it solves adaptive problems, the ones that keep moving, and it disappoints people at a rate they can absorb. Management executes and creates stability; it solves technical problems, the ones that have an answer. Both are necessary; senior managers must grow into leaders; experienced people need mostly a leader and inexperienced people mostly a manager.

### What the record holds

The record cuts the management layer on a different axis. "Every management function in a restaurant resolves into one of two orientations. The first is strategic: what needs to happen, what the standard is, what the organization is building toward. The second is coordinative: how it happens, how execution moves" (WP p.10). These become two parallel co-leads, the Operations Lead and the Maitre d, "co-leads of different systems serving the same center, the culinary and conceptual vision" (WP p.10). Management sits at the center of the web "because that is where the most connections intersect. This is a systems function, not a rank" (WP p.09).

Two further things the record holds bear directly on the distinction. First, stability at Sŏn is produced by the system, not by managers: "the operating layer handles much of what a traditional operations manager once did by hand," and the Operations Lead "is accountable to their outputs, not their manual production" (WP p.10). Second, change enters through the process loop: an incident is captured at shift close, the process is updated, and "the next server inherits a system that has already learned from it" (WP p.13).

### Does what/how map onto leadership/management?

Partly, and the partial fit is the trap. "What needs to happen, what the standard is" sounds like leadership; "how execution moves" sounds like management. But the record assigns both orientations to management functions, puts them in two parallel roles rather than two modes of one person, and requires each lead to carry both of the standard modes. The Maitre d sets the emotional standard of the room (direction) and develops the team (stability). The Operations Lead reads the systems (stability) and updates the architecture when a process fails (change). The two axes are not the same axis.

Overlaying the standard distinction on the two leads would do one of two things. It would rank one lead above the other, the "leader" and the "manager," which the parallel structure forbids and which is the load-bearing model's final failure mode (WP p.10). Or it would split each lead into two people, a leader and a manager per domain, which reintroduces exactly the manual-integration headcount the record removed (WP p.22).

### The position: Sŏn adapts the distinction, in three moves [Recommended]

**Move 1: the distinction applies to problems, not to people.** Technical problems, the ones that have an answer, route to the process loop and the domain owner: name the failure mode, fix the process, capture it. The record already does this (WP p.14, p.17). Adaptive problems, the ones with no settled answer where the situation keeps moving (a shift in who is in the room, a competitor, a daypart that is not finding its customer), route to judgment: the domain owner's judgment inside their domain, and the founders' judgment together when the problem crosses domains or touches the concept. The value of the standard distinction at Sŏn is as a triage question asked of every recurring problem: does this have a fix, or does it need a judgment? A lead who keeps applying process fixes to an adaptive problem is building the wrong thing. A founder who keeps applying judgment to a technical problem is holding the house in their head.

**Move 2: direction lives at the center as a thing, not a rank.** The culinary and conceptual vision is what the leads serve (WP p.10). It is held by the founders as domain owners of the concept and the food, and it is written into the record and the canon, which is what makes it code-bearing rather than a person's charisma. This is where the standard idea of leadership lives at Sŏn: in the vision, and in the founders' obligation to hold it against pressure. It follows that the founders are the ones who must disappoint people at a rate they can absorb, and the record has already scheduled the disappointments: the daypart that does not open yet, the lunch that may come out of the plan, the concept that stays unnamed until proven (WP p.36).

The gap in this move: the record makes Brandon the public voice externally (WP p.03) and puts mission and values before mechanics in onboarding (WP p.18), but it does not say who speaks the concept to the team internally, or whether the two leads carry that voice when the founders are not in the building. A house "built to run without them" implies the leads must. Which founder holds the internal voice, and how it is handed to the leads, is a founder decision. [Founder-gated]

**Move 3: management decreases along the inverted web; direction is constant.** The playground philosophy requires "training architecture that progresses from procedure mastery to autonomous judgment" (canon 01). Onboarding puts why before how (WP p.18). The invisible advocate removes the manager from advancement (WP p.11). So the standard observation that experienced people need mostly a leader and inexperienced people mostly a manager is already the shape of Sŏn's progression structure. What changes as a person develops threads is how much of the "how" they receive. What does not change is the standard they are held to.

### The departure, stated plainly

The standard frame holds that management creates stability. At Sŏn, stability is a property of the system. The leads maintain it; they do not produce it by hand. A lead who is producing stability personally, holding the schedule in their head, being the integration layer between the floor and the kitchen, chasing the linen order, is not a good manager doing extra. They are the CPU failure and the node-overload failure at once (WP p.13, p.14), and the fix is to restore the system, not to praise the lead. This is the single most important reframe for every session from 3 onward, because most of the standard management apparatus assumes the manager is the stabilizer.

One more clarification for the record. The standard frame notes that management structures have worked for thousands of years and treats decentralized experiments as forays. Sŏn's web is not one of those forays. It has a center, two named leads, documented competencies, and explicit domain ownership. It is a management structure. It is not a load-bearing one.

## 4. Come back to your operating system

### The conflation, named

In the standard sense, an operating system is the human system a team runs on: clear missions, stated goals, metrics that matter, the same meeting structures, weekly and quarterly cadences, and a consistent approach to hiring and developing people. It is the box you constructed so that people can think inside it. The record uses the same words for something else: a five-layer software stack whose top layer is "the OS surface, the home where the software agents live" (WP p.21). The stack is "the nervous system" (WP p.12, p.21).

A later session that reads "operating system" in the standard sense, finds the OS surface in the record, and concludes the operating system exists will have made a category error. A session that treats the human cadence as something the stack should replace will have made the opposite one. Both are live hazards, and this section exists to close them.

### The distinction [Recommended]

| | The operating system (human) | The stack (software) |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | What people do at a fixed rhythm: the mission they hold, the goals they set, the metrics they read, the meetings they keep, the way they hire and develop | What carries information between those rhythms: the data layer, the mesh, the AI layer, the OS surface (WP p.21) |
| What it produces | Accountability and shared context | Signal, memory, and the removal of manual integration |
| Who owns it | The founders as a partnership; the two leads for the floor | The capital and technology founder (WP p.05) |
| How it fails | Sequence corruption in the human rhythm: the shift brief not happening, the review not held, the goal not set | Bus failure or memory failure in the software: the data not moving, the knowledge not captured (WP p.13) |
| Vocabulary in this program | "Operating system," "operating cadence," "the cadence" | "The stack," "the OS surface," "the nervous system" |

The relationship between them: the cadence reads the stack, and the stack records the cadence. The shift brief is a cadence ritual fed by the stack's preference surfacing; the shift close is a cadence ritual that writes to the stack's memory (WP p.13 to 14). The leads' reading of leading indicators is a cadence act on stack output (WP p.22). Neither substitutes for the other. A perfect stack with no cadence is a nervous system attached to nobody. A perfect cadence with no stack is the traditional house, where the operator is the integration layer (WP p.21).

### What Sŏn already holds of the human operating system

The pieces exist in the record, distributed, and not yet assembled as one system.

| Component | What the record holds | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Mission and intent | The concept, the three engines, the positioning, the non-negotiables, the three aesthetic frameworks, the service philosophy | WP throughout; canon 01 |
| Metrics that matter | Leading indicators: employee NPS, cultural labor score, customer recognition rate. Cultural measures: retention, internal promotion, engagement, participation. Lagging indicators named as the X-ray after the fracture | WP p.12, p.22 to 23 |
| Floor cadence | The shift brief; the shift close with incident capture; the pre-shift briefing weighted equal to the signature dish | WP p.13 to 14, p.20 |
| Review cadence | Reviews every three to six months, development-focused, no surprises, feedback in both directions including on management effectiveness | WP p.19 |
| Feedback channel | A structured channel synced to the execution system, with the loop closed | WP p.22 |
| Onboarding cadence | A ninety-day plan with regular check-ins | WP p.18 |
| Hiring system | Self-scheduled, format and language choice, trained interviewers, paid practical, the transparency sheet, no ghosting | WP p.17 to 18 |
| Development system | Documented competencies, the learning platform, the invisible advocate, cross-training | WP p.11, p.19 |
| Brand cadence | Daily pulse, weekly read, monthly strategic review, revenue attribution | WP p.31 |

### What is absent, and where it routes

| Gap | Routes to |
|---|---|
| A goals layer: the record has metrics with no targets attached and no goal-setting cycle at the house or team level | Session 3 |
| A planning cycle: how the year, the quarter, and the daypart launch are planned and reviewed | Session 3, Session 4 |
| The founder cadence: how the three partners meet, decide, and review each other's domains. The record has a floor cadence and a review cadence and nothing for the center | Session 4, new carryover |
| The leads' cadence: how the Operations Lead and the Maitre d sync as parallel co-leads with no one above them | Session 4 |
| Ongoing communication of priorities to the team after onboarding: the transparency sheet and the why-before-how orientation are one-time; nothing in the record carries priorities forward at a rhythm | Session 4, Session 11 |

Assembling these into one named operating system, with the stack as its nervous system and not its substitute, is the work of Sessions 3 and 4. This page fixes the vocabulary so that work starts clean.

## 5. The prerequisites, read as a gap list

## 6. The working-with-me document

### What it is at Sŏn [Recommended]

A founder's written account of how they work, exchanged among the founders and later issued in a shorter form to the two leads. It is the code-bearing move applied to the founder's self-knowledge: what a founder knows about their own edges, decision mode, and load signals moves out of their head and into a document the other partners can read, so that the people they work with do not have to learn it by collision.

It has three jobs at Sŏn that a standard version does not.

1. **It is a seam document between non-overlapping domains.** The founders will rarely watch each other work. One is in the room and on camera; one is in the model and the build; one is in the kitchen. A manager and their reports see each other daily and can correct by observation. Co-founders with separate domains cannot. The document carries what observation would otherwise supply.

2. **It is the first document the incoming chef partner reads that is about the people, not the concept.** The record and the canon tell a chef what Sŏn is. Nothing tells them who Brandon and Dominic are to work with. Two existing founders' documents, written before the chef is seated, are part of what the chef is joining.

3. **It is an inventory of what still lives in the founder's head** (prompt B). This is the part that makes it a founding instrument rather than a personality profile.

### What it is for

Mutual awareness among three people who own everything and report to no one. Fewer collisions in the first year, when the build metronome is fastest. A written basis for the founder cadence that Session 4 designs. A model the leads will later be asked to produce for themselves.

### What it must refuse to do [Recommended]

- It is not a job description. Session 2 writes those. Prompt A states the domain in a paragraph and points to the job description when it exists.
- It is not a statement of company values. The canon's non-negotiables and the record's intent carry those. Prompt G is the founder's own values at work, which are theirs and not the company's.
- It is not a performance instrument, a rubric, or a scorecard, and it is never used as one.
- It does not bind the other founders. It describes; it does not contract. What a founder asks for in prompt J is a request, not a term.
- It does not set the founder cadence. A document cannot decide when three people meet. Prompt I covers reachability only; the cadence is a joint decision for Session 4.
- It does not assert brand facts or financial figures. If a founder needs a figure to explain their seat, they say the figure lives in the model.
- It does not perform. It is written in the master register: present without performing (canon 01). A founder describes the self they are, not the self they intend to be. The rule against performed conviction applies to it.
- It does not fill in anyone else's. The chef partner's document is written by the chef partner. Nothing in section 7.4 is an answer.

### When it is exchanged: open, and routed [Founder-gated, routed to Session 7]

The question of when the document changes hands is open. There are two candidate timings. One treats it as pre-signature diligence: the incoming chef partner reads the two existing founders' documents, and writes their own, before the partnership is signed, on the logic that an equal partner is doing diligence on the people as well as the concept. The other treats it as an onboarding instrument, written by the new person some weeks in, after they have seen the house and can describe how they work in it. The first attempt at this session closed this question in favor of the first timing without standing to do so, and that closure was un-made. Session 7 owns the question (`86ajgnj8t`) and closes it.

What this page can say: the timing question only bites for the chef. Brandon and Dominic can write theirs now, because they are already in the partnership. The post-extraction work items assign them accordingly. Whether the chef's document is a condition of signing or a product of the first ninety days is for Session 7.

### Revision [Recommended]

The document is dated and versioned. It is revised when the founder's seat changes materially and, at minimum, after the first quarter of dinner service, when every founder will have learned something about how they work in a room that exists. A shorter version is issued to each lead when the lead is seated (section 7.6).

## 7. The working-with-me template

This section is the complete content of the template: one Sŏn spine shared by all three founders, with a short seat-specific preamble for each. A founder can fill it in unaided from what is written here. Every founder's actual answers are blank. This is the container.

It is not a formatted fillable artifact. That packaging is gated on the document methodology (`86ajgn2z5`).

### 7.1 Before you start: instructions common to all three

**Who reads this.** Your two co-founders read the full version. The two leads, when seated, read the issued version (section 7.6). Assume the reader knows the concept and does not know you.

**How long.** As long as it takes to be specific and no longer. A weak document is long because it is vague. Two to four pages is the expected range.

**Do these three exercises first, alone, before writing anything.** They take an afternoon. Their outputs feed prompts C, F, and G, and without them those prompts get answered from self-image rather than evidence.

- *The values exercise.* Imagine yourself at eighty, looking back at your working life. Ask what gave it meaning. Write the ten values that show up. Cut to five. Cut to three. For each of the three, write the specific activity that tells you that you are working in accord with it, one positive outcome of holding it, and one trade-off it costs you. The trade-off is the part that matters; a value with no trade-off has not been thought through. Name values as activities where you can ("I do not ship a thing I would not put my name on") rather than as single words, which say less than they seem to.

- *The work style placement.* Two axes: introverted to extroverted (do you gain energy from people or from being alone? do you talk to think or think to talk?), and task-oriented to people-oriented (do you reach first for the problem or for the people in it?). The four corners have working names: Analyzer (introverted, task), Director (extroverted, task), Collaborator (introverted, people), Promoter (extroverted, people). Place yourself three times: at work in an ordinary week, at home, and under load (the last week before a deadline, or a bad service). The three placements are usually not the same, and the differences are the useful part. This is shared vocabulary, not a verdict. It does not decide what you can do.

- *The strengths inventory.* List the three or four things you are best at. For each, split what it is made of into skills (tactical things you can do: build a model, write a brief, run a pass) and capabilities (the broader ability to deploy those skills in a situation you have not seen before). Mark each as innate (as easy as breathing; you never had to learn it) or acquired (you built it, and you can say when). Then, for each strength, write the sentence: "This strength is also a weakness when..." and finish it honestly. Then write what guardrail, if any, you have built against that.

**Rules for the writing.** These are the house's standing rules and they apply to this document.

- The customer is the customer. Never "guest."
- No em dashes. Use commas, colons, semicolons, or a new sentence.
- Sentence case for every heading.
- No daypart code names. Say early morning, lunch, dinner, late night.
- No financial figures. If a figure is needed, say it lives in the model.
- No brand facts from memory. If you cite the canon, cite it.
- No performed conviction. Not "I am committed to," not "I believe in," not "my goal is." Say what you do.
- Profanity is spoken. It does not go in the document.

**How to answer well, in general.** Specific over categorical. An example over an adjective. The thing you would rather not write is usually the thing the reader most needs. Where you do not know the answer, write "I do not know yet" and move on; prompt K collects these. A blank is more useful than a performance.

**What a weak document looks like.** It describes the founder the writer would like to be. It uses words from a leadership book. It has no trade-offs. It says "collaborative" without saying what happens when a decision is needed by Friday. It contains nothing the other two founders could not have guessed.

#### A. My seat and its edges

**The prompt.** State what you own, in a paragraph. Then state where your domain ends: the three or four decisions nearest your edge that belong to someone else, and who. Then state what you are accountable to: the outcomes your domain is judged by. If a job description exists, point to it and do not repeat it.

**What it is really asking for.** Not a role and its goals. Co-founders with separate domains do not need each other's goals; they need each other's edges. This prompt asks for the border, drawn from your side, so the other two can draw theirs and the three maps can be compared for gaps and overlaps.

**How to answer well.** Name the edge cases, not the center. Everyone knows the concept founder owns the concept. What the others need is: does the concept founder own the uniform wear test, or the Maitre d? Does the capital founder own the choice of reservation platform, or the concept founder, because it shapes the arrival? Does the chef own the beverage program's food pairings? Write the ones you are unsure of; those are the ones that matter.

**What a weak answer looks like.** A title and a list of responsibilities copied from the record. No edges. No mention of anything you are unsure you own.

**The prompt.** List what you know that the house depends on and that is not yet written anywhere the house can read it. For each item: what it is, what would break if you were unreachable for a month, and by when it will be in the system.

**What it is really asking for.** This is the code-bearing principle applied to you. The record says the house is built so it does not depend on either founder's memory (WP p.05 to 06). #### C. How I work

#### K. What I do not know yet

**The prompt.** The prompts above you could not answer honestly, and why. The things about how you work in a house that does not exist yet that you will only learn when it does. The date you will revisit this document.

**What it is really asking for.** Principle 3 applied to the document itself. A founder who has never run a room at this scale, or never worked with these two people, does not know some of the answers. The house is built to name what is open. So is this.

**How to answer well.** Short. Honest. Dated.

**What a weak answer looks like.** Empty, because everything above was answered with confidence.

### 7.6 Issuing the spine to a lead later [Team-filled]

When the Operations Lead and the Maitre d are seated, each founder issues a shorter version to them, and each lead is asked to write one on the same spine for the founders and for each other. The issued version keeps prompts A, C, D, E, H, I, and J, with J rewritten for the lead as reader. It omits B, F, G, and K, which are for the partners. The lead's own version adds one prompt the founders' version does not need: how they will discuss development and progression with the people in their domain, and how they want to be included in the team's work. That prompt is written when the leads exist and is not drafted here.

## 8. Specifications

For each instrument the material recommends: whether Sŏn uses it, what it must contain, what it must do, what it must refuse to do. These specifications are what the downstream clusters consume when the instruments are built. No instrument is built here; instrument production is gated on the document methodology (`86ajgn2z5`).

### 8.1 The values exercise

**Does Sŏn use it.** Yes, as a founder self-instrument, completed alone, feeding prompt G of the working-with-me document. Later, as a lead self-instrument on the same terms. [Recommended]

**What it must contain.**
- The eighty-year-old prompt: looking back at a working life, what gave it meaning.
- The reduction: ten values, then five, then three.
- For each of the three: the activity that signals working in accord with it; one positive outcome; one trade-off.
- The why-and-story frame: why this value matters to you and one example of it appearing in your life.
- A note that values are best stated as activities or decision rules rather than single words.
- The example list of values, as examples only.

**What it must do.**
- Produce three values each with a real trade-off.
- Be completable alone in under two hours.
- Keep the full working private by default; only the three values, activities, and trade-offs export to the working-with-me document.
- Ask, at the end, where the writer expects their values to collide with another founder's or with a house decision.

**What it must refuse to do.**
- Derive company values or a mission from the founders' shared personal values. The standard exercise offers this as an option; Sŏn declines it. The record and the canon carry the company's intent; the founders' personal values are theirs and are not aggregated into a mission.
- Rank one founder's values against another's.
- Treat a value as a verdict on the person, or use it in any performance conversation.
- Require the values be named with words from the canon's forbidden lexicon. The canon governs brand voice, not a founder's private vocabulary, but a value stated as an activity avoids the problem and is a better answer regardless.

### 8.2 The work style grid

**Does Sŏn use it.** Yes, as shared vocabulary among the founders and, later, the leads, with the explicit caveat that it is not deterministic and does not decide what a person can do. Placement is done three times: at work, at home, under load. [Recommended]

**What it must contain.**
- The two axes, each stated as a question: introverted to extroverted (energy from people or from solitude; talk to think or think to talk), task-oriented to people-oriented (reach first for the problem or the people).
- The four working names: Analyzer, Director, Collaborator, Promoter, each with a two-sentence description of what it is good at and what it is worse at.
- The three placements: at work, at home, under load.
- A Sŏn-specific addition: the strand question. The record's two strands require different intelligences, the outward-facing relational and expressive under load, the inward-facing architectural and timing-precise (WP p.10 to 11). The grid asks which strand's intelligence the writer recognizes as their own, and whether they hold both.
- The caveat, printed on the instrument: a preference is not a limit, and the grid is never used to stereotype.

**What it must do.**
- Give three people who work in separate domains a common vocabulary for their differences within one conversation.
- Surface the under-load placement, which is where founder collisions actually happen.
- Feed prompt C of the working-with-me document.

**What it must refuse to do.**
- Be used in hiring, in role assignment on the floor, or in any progression decision. The question of how work style demand differs by daypart, and whether service pressure selects for some quadrants over others, is owned by Sessions 5, 6, and 8 (`86ajgnhh3`). This page notes one refinement for those sessions as an input, not a conclusion: the claim that service selects for Directors and Promoters describes the outward-facing strand; the inward-facing strand, architectural and timing-precise, is where the Analyzer's intelligence lives in service, and the record's strand structure already reserves that place. The scarcity claim is therefore about one strand, not about service.
- Be treated as an assessment with a score.
- Replace the strand structure. The strands are the record's vocabulary for the floor; the grid is a vocabulary for individuals.

### 8.3 The strengths inventory

**Does Sŏn use it.** Yes, as a founder self-instrument feeding prompt F, and later for leads. [Recommended]

**What it must contain.**
- Three or four strengths, named.
- For each: the skills it is made of (tactical, binary: can do or cannot) and the capabilities (the broader ability to deploy skills in an unfamiliar situation).
- For each skill and capability: innate (as easy as breathing) or acquired (built, and when).
- Two Sŏn additions. The trade-off column: "this is also a weakness when..." completed for each strength, with the guardrail built against it or "none yet." The dependency column: whether the house currently depends on this strength in the writer personally, or whether it has been moved into the system.

**What it must do.**
- Produce, for each strength, its cost and its guardrail.
- Distinguish what the writer can teach (acquired) from what they cannot (innate), because the learning platform will want the teachable ones.
- Tie back to prompt B: every strength the house depends on personally is an item for the knowledge-transfer list.

**What it must refuse to do.**
- Become the competency map for any floor position. The documented competency requirements per position belong to the progression structure (WP p.11) and are specified in Session 8 and built by the learning cluster.
- Compare founders to each other.
- Record a capability without an example of it having been used.
- Be used in any compensation or review conversation.

### 8.4 The management prerequisites checklist

**Does Sŏn use it.** Once, as the gap list in section 5, and then it is retired. It is not adopted as a recurring readiness test. [Recommended]

**What it must contain.** The mapping in section 5: each standard item, what it assumes, what the record holds, what is absent, and where the gap routes.

**What it must do.**
- Route every gap to a session or to counsel.
- Hand off, rather than resolve, the items owned by existing carryovers: founder job descriptions (Session 2), the 1:1 (Sessions 4 and 12), levels and ladders (Sessions 8 and 13), compensation philosophy (Session 13), the review instrument (Session 13).
- Name the assumptions Sŏn has replaced by design: a manager above, ladders and levels, the desk-native 1:1, the periodic feedback event.

**What it must refuse to do.**
- Score Sŏn. Absence of a standard structure is not a failure where the record has replaced it.
- Be re-run against Sŏn without translation. Sessions 3 and 4 produce Sŏn's own definition of "the operating system is running," and that definition replaces this checklist.
- Imply that ladders and levels are a prerequisite. Whether a single house needs them is open (`86ajgnj4e`).

### 8.5 The working-with-me document

**Does Sŏn use it.** Yes, for all three founders, on one spine with seat-specific preambles, and later for the two leads in a shorter issued form. [Recommended] The timing of the chef partner's exchange is open and routes to Session 7. [Founder-gated]

**What it must contain.**
- The header: name, seat, date, version, who has read it.
- The eleven prompts of the spine (section 7.5), each with its four-part guidance printed on the instrument so the writer does not need this page.
- The common instructions (section 7.1), including the three prerequisite exercises and the standing rules.
- The seat-specific preamble for the writer's seat (sections 7.2 to 7.4), with the chef partner's preamble marked chef-gated and containing the record's two accounts of the seat.
- An open-items section (prompt K) and a revision date.

**What it must do.**
- Be completable by a founder alone, from the instrument, without a facilitator.
- Make legible to a person in a different domain: the writer's edges, decision mode, disagreement mode, load signals, reachability, and dependencies.
- Move the writer's self-knowledge and their unrecorded house knowledge (prompt B) into a document the house can read.
- Produce three comparable documents, so the founders' edges (A), decision drafts (D), and dependencies (J) can be laid side by side and the gaps and overlaps found.
- Support a shorter issued version for leads (section 7.6) without rewriting.
- Be revised on a stated date and when the seat changes.

**What it must refuse to do.**
- Be a job description, a contract, a performance instrument, a scorecard, or a statement of company values.
- Set the founder cadence or the decision-rights rule. It collects each founder's draft of the second (prompt D) as input to a joint decision.
- Assert brand facts or financial figures.
- Perform. The master register applies: present without performing. No performed conviction.
- Be filled in on anyone's behalf. The chef partner's is written by the chef partner.
- Use the daypart code names, the word "guest," or em dashes.
- Be issued to the team below the leads. It is a founder-and-leads instrument.
- Be packaged as a formatted fillable artifact before the document methodology exists (`86ajgn2z5`).
