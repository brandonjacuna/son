# Session 3: post-extraction work items

Typed subtasks for the post-extraction parent task. Each carries one type (founder decision, document, process, instrument, or structure), a priority, the section of the Session 3 page it came from, and a description that stands on its own. Instruments are specifications only and are gated on the document methodology; the Session 3 page, section 14, holds each specification in full.

## Founder decisions

**Confirm the data hub of record for the stack.** *Priority: urgent. From section 13.1.* The white paper (p.21) names a specific tool as "the hub for everything that is not CRM," with the reservation platform as the identity spine beneath it and the loyalty engine above it, and describes the AI layer as reading across that hub. The program's source boundary states the same tool is no longer in use for any purpose. The operating system's metric layer, shift close, feedback loop, alert register, and readiness test all run on whatever holds the hub role. Dominic confirms what currently holds it and whether the white paper's five-layer stack description is current. Until confirmed, no operating-system document names a hub tool.

**Ratify the interim two-partner decision rule.** *Priority: urgent. From section 4.4.* Until the chef seat is filled there are two partners and two equal votes have no majority. The team's argued interim: for every decision outside the reserved classes, the domain owner decides after consulting the other partner where the consequence crosses domains; for reserved-class decisions, both partners or neither, with the status quo as the default when they disagree, so a disagreement is a hold rather than a deadlock; and the lunch decision is frozen, not held, because the record makes it three-partner and two cannot take it. Requires Dominic. The operating agreement must be read first for any voting rule it already holds. Extends the existing founder decision on the three-partner deadlock rule.

**Ratify the decision-rights rule and the eight founding register entries.** *Priority: high. From sections 4.1 and 4.2.* The rule: authority sits with the closest competent owner; the range within which that owner acts without asking is stated in advance; escalation triggers are named before the event, each with a destination. Three operating rules attach: a trigger names its destination; a range states what the owner may not do; ranges are set one level out from the position that holds them. The eight instances the record already assigns (lunch, sonic governance, floor authority, the David protocol, the design token system, token naming, content approval, the server's generosity range) are rewritten in that form on the Session 3 page, five of them marked incomplete on a named part. Brandon ratifies the rule and the entries; Dominic sees the two that touch his domain.

**Ratify the reserved-class list, extended by three.** *Priority: high. From section 4.3.* Session 2's candidate list of decisions reserved to all partners (building or removing a service period; changing a non-negotiable; versioning the charter; versioning canon at tier 1 or 2; admitting a new principal) is extended by three from this session: setting or changing a charter metric's definition; setting the two leads' ranges; and naming, for roles, the loyalty layer, and future concepts. Brandon and Dominic ratify the list.

**Name the owners the record leaves unnamed in the decision-rights register.** *Priority: high. From sections 4.2 and 9.2.* Five entries name a function without a seat: "management sign-off" at the sonic system's second tier, under two parallel leads; "leadership review" for the content framework's highest tier; the person who triggers the pre-service scene and the dinner-to-late-night transition (canon says "same person, same moment" and never names the person); the owner of the David flag in the customer record; and who holds emergency authority during a service. The team's recommendations are on the page (the owner of the room for the transition; the Maitre d for the David flag; Brandon, with Dominic for investor-facing matters, for leadership review). Brandon rules; the kitchen's receiving side of the David flag is chef-gated.

**Rule whether the floor manager and the Maitre d are one role or two, and which owns the room.** *Priority: high. From section 13.3.* The white paper frees the Maitre d from the host stand "to hold the floor and the team." Canon 1.0 gives the room to "the floor manager," lists a floor manager and hosts among uniform roles with no Maitre d, and places a Maître d' at the porch steps. The deck 3.0 lists Maître d' and floor manager as two distinct uniform roles. The server's escalation destination, the transition trigger, and emergency authority all currently name "the owner of the room" and cannot name a seat. Brandon rules on the role identity; Session 8 designs the structure from the ruling.

**Define the cultural labor score.** *Priority: high. From section 8.5.* The white paper (p.22) names three leading indicators and gives the cultural labor score one line: "beyond hours and cost, how the team is actually doing." It is a charter metric candidate and the least defined of the three. The team specifies what it must do: read bandwidth rather than output, be captured at service tempo rather than annually, and not reduce to hours. Brandon, who originated the concept, supplies what it is: the question or observation it is built from, the population, and the rhythm.

**Define "dinner is steady" as the gate that opens the early morning.** *Priority: high. From section 5.4.* The white paper phases the opening on dinner running "on its own for at least a quarter, so the heart is solid before anything is added to it," and the next periods follow "once dinner is steady." Steady is not defined. The Session 3 page supplies the readiness test rows and the leading indicators that could read it. The founders decide which rows and which indicators constitute the gate; the chef partner shapes the kitchen's half once seated. No target value is set until a baseline exists.

**Populate the existential-risk register.** *Priority: high. From section 6.4.* Her rule that leadership must "invest in critical work that no one else will naturally step up to prioritize" survives intact. The record names candidate risks without listing them: the chef dependency; undercapitalization and weak controls; food safety; key-person loss; customer acceptance of compensation built into price; sound; the stack breaking; and, added by the team, the founders' own bandwidth across floor, build, and content engine. The founders decide which risks are on the register, in what order, and which founder owns each. The register is the source of "what keeps you up at night" at the house review.

**Rule whether the operating system is built for three service periods or four.** *Priority: high. From section 13.2.* Both canon versions state four dayparts at the address and specify lunch fully, with nothing marked held. The white paper states a core of three, with lunch and brunch "held in reserve, not part of the core and not assumed," subject to an equal three-partner decision that may remove lunch entirely. On status this is a disagreement, not an altitude difference. The operating system resolves its own dependency by running whatever set of periods holds a ratified daypart charter. The founders rule whether canon's four-daypart statement is a design readiness or a decision, and whether canon is versioned to mark lunch as held. Routed to the canon reconciliation Session 2 filed.

**Draw the transparency line.** *Priority: normal. From sections 8.4 and 11.2.* Her fourth test of internal communications is transparency: "everyone has clarity about what will be shared, when, and with whom." The record commits to sharing the org chart, the lead, the compensation mechanics, and the advancement path with every candidate before hire, and says nothing about operating figures, individual review content, or the financial model. The team's recommendation: goals, metrics, owners, competency paths, ranges, and the decision-rights register visible to everyone; individual reviews to the person and their domain lead; the financial model and lagging figures to the founders and whoever they name. The founders draw the line.

**Approve or decline a quarterly whole-house gathering.** *Priority: normal. From section 11.6.* Sŏn's team never co-occurs by design, so her company-wide meeting has no form at Sŏn without closing a service period or finding a shared window. The team recommends one gathering per quarter, tied to the house review and, at its first two instances, to the principles revision Session 2 scheduled. Either form costs something; no figure is stated. The founders decide; Session 4 places it on the cadence if approved.

**Decide whether Horizon 3 appears in the internal operating system.** *Priority: normal. From section 5.2.* The white paper holds the platform ceiling (concepts by category and a membership tier) and states it "never appears externally." The team home is read by every hire. Whether the horizon appears internally at all, even by category, is Brandon's and Dominic's call.

**Confirm the transparency of ratified positions to the third seat.** *Priority: low. ## Processes

**Run the alert-actor rule.** *Priority: high. From section 10.7.* For every automated alert the stack can raise, a person is named in advance to act on it, with their range on receipt and the path by which they report cause and fix. The named actor is never a founder by default; an alert that routes to a founder is a design fault. Alerts route to the domain owner whose outcome they touch. No alert is enabled without an entry in the alert register.

**Operate the cross-domain and lead-to-lead escalation rules.** *Priority: high. From section 4.3.* Class B: the owning domain decides; any affected domain is consulted before the event; an unresolved objection holds the decision and escalates to the founder whose domain holds the disputed outcome, or to both partners if it belongs to both. Class C: a disagreement between the two parallel leads escalates the same way, experience to Brandon's seat, cost to Dominic's, both to the partners. Class D: emergency authority and closure as specified. Class E: no stack change that alters what a lead or server does during service lands without the affected domain consulted through the feedback channel.

**Split every seam-crossing metric at the domain boundary.** *Priority: normal. From section 9.3.* No metric has two owners. A metric that crosses the seam between the two parallel leads is split into a stack-side measure owned by the Operations Lead (the record travels; the profile is one across periods; the invisible advocate's mechanics run) and a floor-side measure owned by the Maitre d (recognition delivered; the customer was invited across; the team's health). Where a metric cannot be split, its owner is the daypart charter's "held by" entry.

**Run the pre-baseline goal cycle.** *Priority: normal. From section 7.7.* Pre-opening goals are the readiness test rows. First-quarter goals are binary states and baselining goals of the form "a baseline exists for X, computed by the stack, reviewed at the leads' review." No target on a rate is set before a baseline exists. From the second quarter, targets are stated as relative movement from the baseline.

**Reserve a maintenance line and a risk line in every company and period goal set.** *Priority: normal. From section 6.4.* Each goal set at the company and service-period units carries one line for system maintenance, owned by the Operations Lead, and one for a named existential risk, owned by a founder, so that the work no one prioritizes has a named owner before the period begins. No ratio is attached.

## Instruments

All gated on the document methodology. Each item specifies; none builds. Full specifications are in the Session 3 page, section 14.

**Decision-rights register format.** *Priority: high. From sections 4 and 14.* One entry per decision class or instance: owner, range including refusals, triggers with destinations, source, mark, incomplete flag; the reserved-class list; the Class B through E rules; the interim partner rule. Refuses entries by analogy, two owners, financial values, and "escalate" as a destination.

**Goal page template.** *Priority: high. From sections 7 and 14.* One page per unit (company, service period, domain, person): three to five objectives as states, one to five key results each, parent commitment, committed or aspirational class, owner, the reserve lines for company and period pages, the three-state score, a change log. Refuses activities, verbs, function-split goals, orphan goals, pre-baseline targets, and a second page.

**Metric definition card and register format.** *Priority: high. From sections 8 and 14.* The entry structure for the definitions register and the card each mechanism reads. Refuses a metric with no owner or two, a lagging charter metric without a written argument, a memory-dependent definition, and any target value.

**Alert register format.** *Priority: high. From sections 10.7 and 14.* Every automated alert with threshold and its owner, named actor, the actor's range, the report path, and the escalation destination. Refuses an alert with no actor, a founder as default actor, and a financial threshold stated on the page.

**Shift brief and shift close structures as operating-system carriers.** *Priority: high. From sections 9.2, 10.1, and 14.* The brief's operating-system load: designations, transition trigger owner, emergency authority, flagged records including every David flag, surfaced preferences, spoken changes, the period goal headline. The close's: every incident with range status, generosity use, recovery, escalation and destination, the thank-you record, captured operating metrics, and an owner per process change. The kitchen's contents of both are chef-gated.

**House review outline and phase-gate review form.** *Priority: normal. From sections 10.4 and 14.* The house review's sections as translated from her QBR outline: candid summary, narrative on charter and operating metrics, reflection per period and domain, the feedback channel's top asks, outlook against the risk register, the five failure modes as standing scorecard, the tension map in place of a headcount table, prior action items, lagging figures as an appendix owned by Dominic's domain. The phase-gate review applies the same form to the readiness test and the "steady" condition.

**Leads' review agenda and lead's weekly note format.** *Priority: normal. From sections 10.2, 10.3, and 14.* The weekly cross-period mechanism's agenda, opening on the leading indicators and closing, if at all, on lagging figures, and the one-page note each lead files before it.

**Team home content specification.** *Priority: normal. From sections 11.7 and 14.* For every unit, the workbook's table (mission or reason to exist, objectives with owner, key metrics, accountability mechanisms, cadence), plus the decision-rights register, competency paths, the strategy lists, the definitions register and standing vocabulary, the document list with versions and owners, and the web rendered as the record's designed artifact. Distinct from the OS surface in name and purpose; readable between services in the team's languages. Where it lives is founder-gated.

**Readiness test checklist.** *Priority: high. From sections 12 and 14.* The twenty rows this session wrote plus Session 4's cadence rows, each with check method and checker, each answerable yes or no, none met by a document's existence alone.

## Structure

**Run the requisite-variety test on the leadership structure.** *Priority: high. From section 6.3.* The record's leadership delta (roughly eight lines across the day against fourteen at a named peer's single dinner service) is a claim, not a proof, and her own critique of benchmark ratios applies. Before opening: map each of the eight disturbance classes on the page (customer-side, kitchen-side, floor-side, people-side, system-side, transitions, external, brand-side) to the regulator that absorbs it, the stack, a pre-stated range, a lead, or a founder, against the actual leadership-line list. After dinner's first quarter: re-run against the shift-close incident log. Any class that resolves to a founder by default or to nothing is a structural finding. The line count and load are a stated gap until the list exists.

**Confirm the four goal-bearing units.** *Priority: normal. Every goal is set by the unit that holds the information and laddered up to a long-term commitment. Session 8's team-structures work consumes this and must not reintroduce a division or team unit.

**Map the charter metrics to seats.** *Priority: high. From sections 8.2 and 9.3.* Each charter metric candidate carries a proposed owner on the page: employee NPS and retention to the Maitre d; recognition rate split between the Operations Lead (the record travels) and the Maitre d (recognition delivered); cross-period movement split between Dominic's data architecture through the Operations Lead and the Maitre d with the servers' books; the invisible advocate's mechanics to the Operations Lead; the cultural labor score to Brandon's seat pending its definition; kitchen metrics chef-gated. The map is finalized once the long-term commitments are ratified and the floor manager ruling lands.
