## 1. Her ask against the record, and how much is already decided

### 1.1 Her ask

Her section opens on a claim: "Rituals and common practices weave the fabric of a team" (Book p.304). What follows is three instruments for weaving it. Offsites: two or three days a year for senior teams, at least one a year for junior ones, planned against the team's development stage on Tuckman's four phases, balanced between task-focused and team-focused activities, opened by a personal check-in, warmed by an icebreaker, structured by purpose, agenda, limit, and notes, and closed by a one-word check-out (Book pp. 304 to 311). Meetings: the team's most frequent activity, costly, only as good as the relationships beneath them, laid on groundwork (common understanding of who is on the team, what each does, how they prefer to work, and agreed norms), given roles (a DRI, a facilitator, a notetaker), owned mutually, given one or two purposes, run to norms (logistics, no undiscussable topics, inclusion, disagree and commit, a parking lot, action items, reconfiguration every three to six months), split between operator mode and creator mode, run with openers and closers, closed by confirming the purpose was achieved, and made decisive by an explicit decision method, a framework, and a decision log (Book pp. 311 to 328). Leadership team meetings: the "first team" a leader commits to above their own reports, Stripe's Monday three hours on snippets and metrics, and a supplemental leadership group for new senior leaders (Book pp. 328 to 330).

Read as one, every instrument assumes a manager who creates the environment by convening people and running the room. The team is made in the meeting; the manager is the maker.

### 1.2 What the record already holds

The record holds a different premise, and it holds most of the ground. The team is a structure, not a meeting: "great nights feel like distributed intelligence rather than coordinated direction... This is not a management achievement. It is the structure functioning correctly" (WP p.8). Culture is named as one of the two phases of the team's material: "The elastic connective tissue is organizational culture: the connective flexibility that allows the structure to flex under stress without fracturing. Culture is not soft. It is the phase that keeps the strong phase from becoming brittle" (WP p.9). Health is bandwidth: "what the system has available after the work of survival is complete... No one has room to feel what is happening, to respond to what is arriving, to improve what is working" (WP p.12), and the order is "Bandwidth, then joy, then retention, then results" (WP p.12; Canon 1.0, section 01; Deck 3.0, p.4). The day already has a rhythm: "Beginning of shift, prepare for the customer. During service, serve them. End of shift, reflect and strengthen the relationship" (WP p.26). Errors are the process's: "the first question is not who failed. It is what allowed this to happen... Management absorbs blame from staff. Post-mortems are private and process-focused" (WP p.17). Employees are the users, with "continuous feedback loops so the tools evolve based on real usage" (WP p.17). Feedback "runs both directions" (WP p.19). Recognition is peer-to-peer "in the moment" (WP p.19). Dignity is physical: a real employee area with secure lockers "is not a perk. It is basic dignity, and dignity is a precondition for presence" (WP p.20). Generosity is pre-authorized: "their judgment is trusted" (WP p.26). And the record's own culture measure names psychological safety directly: "engagement results across psychological safety and values alignment" (WP p.23).

Canon holds the frame. The Playground Philosophy: "The brand's operational obligation across every concept, every daypart, and for customers, founders, and employees, is to build conditions for agency. A playground is a structured space with legible rules and open-ended results. Clear enough to feel safe; open enough to stay" (Canon 1.0, section 01). Its test: "does this build or foreclose conditions for agency? If it forecloses agency without a documented reason, it violates the philosophy" (Canon 1.0, section 01). The deck states the two failure directions: "Rules so tight the result is foreclosed" and "Openness so loose the space stops feeling safe" (Deck 3.0, p.14). The non-negotiables sit above everything executional: "No exploitation ever. No toxicity ever. No disposable people" (Canon 1.0, section 01; Deck 3.0, p.4). Ma forbids "service staff who manufacture activity because standing still feels wrong" and "copy that rushes to fill silence" (Canon 1.0, section 01). Nunchi is "the human expression of the Playground Philosophy at the floor level" and Jeong "cannot be scripted" (Canon 1.0, section 01). The master register is "present without performing" (Canon 1.0, section 01; Deck 3.0, p.27).

## 2. The playground test, made operational

### 2.1 What canon states, and what canon specifies

Canon states the obligation three ways and specifies it, in the main, one way. Canon 1.0's Playground section prohibits six things and requires five (Canon 1.0, section 01). Of the eleven, nine are customer-facing: scripted service protocols, spatial routing through a single sequence, menu and brand language that resolves ambiguity, customer data systems that make the customer reconstruct their history, digital interfaces that route, spatial design that holds possibility in reserve, customer history infrastructure, and brand language that names and invites. Two touch the employee: the prohibition on "training systems that measure procedure execution without building toward judgment," and the requirements of "training architecture that progresses from procedure mastery to autonomous judgment" and "service authority distributed to the closest-to-customer position." None touches the founders. The deck adds no list; it states the two failure directions and the requirement of "Conditions for agency for customers, founders, and employees alike" and "Structure legible enough to be trusted, open enough to be inhabited" (Deck 3.0, p.14).

So the employee side of the philosophy arrives at this page with two mechanisms named by canon (a training architecture that reaches judgment, and authority at the edge) and a general obligation. That is the honest starting point for Brandon's question. The philosophy's employee half is neither a slogan nor a system in canon; it is two specified requirements and a claim about everything else.

The tier is held. Canon 1.0 places the Playground Philosophy at tier 2 with positioning and leaves the company non-negotiables unranked; Deck 3.0 places the non-negotiables at tier 2 and the Playground at tier 3 (Canon 1.0, section 02; Deck 3.0, p.32; S2 sections 3 and 4; `86akh3rx4`). Under Deck 3.0 a mechanism that builds agency by breaching a non-negotiable loses; under Canon 1.0 the non-negotiables have no tier to lose from. Every verdict on this page reads the non-negotiables as outranking the philosophy, which is the deck's order, and marks that as held.

### 2.2 The test as a procedure that a mechanism can fail

Canon's test is one question. Run on a mechanism it needs six, so that a mechanism can fail it in a way someone can point to:

1. **The condition.** Which condition for agency the mechanism claims to build, named from the list in 2.3. A mechanism that names none is not a culture mechanism and is tested only by the entry test.
2. **The legible rule.** What the mechanism states in advance, where it is written, and who can read it before acting. A playground's rules are legible or it is not a playground.
3. **The open-ended result.** What the person decides inside the rule, and what the mechanism refuses to decide for them. A rule with no result inside it is a script.
4. **The tight edge.** Does the rule foreclose the result: does it script the act, prescribe the sequence, measure the procedure without the judgment, or let one person's standard stand as the rule? "Rules so tight the result is foreclosed" (Deck 3.0, p.14).
5. **The loose edge.** Does the openness remove the safety: is there an act with no stated limit, a trigger with no destination, a topic with no place to go, a failure with no capture, a night with no one holding the room? "Openness so loose the space stops feeling safe" (Deck 3.0, p.14).
6. **The documented reason, and the read.** Where the mechanism forecloses agency, the reason is written on the mechanism map or the register, because "If it forecloses agency without a documented reason, it violates the philosophy" (Canon 1.0, section 01). And the diagnostic line that would read the condition's absence is named, because a condition no line can read is a condition no one will notice losing.

A mechanism passes when all six are answered and neither edge is crossed. It fails on the tight edge, the loose edge, or a missing reason. It is incomplete when a question cannot be answered from the record, which is a different state from failing and is recorded as such.

### 2.3 What "conditions for agency" means for an employee at Sŏn

The phrase is decomposed here into conditions specific enough that each can be checked against a mechanism. The decomposition is the team's, derived from canon's own definition (legible rules, open-ended results, safe enough, open enough to stay) and the record's people sections, and it is what section 3 tests. Ten conditions:

1. **Legible rules.** The person can read, before acting, what the rules of the seat, the service, and the house are.
2. **A stated range.** The person has authority to act without asking, with its limits and its escalation destinations written in advance.
3. **Judgment built, not procedure only.** The training reaches "autonomous judgment" and something reads whether it did (Canon 1.0, section 01).
4. **Safety to err.** An error is the process's first, captured, and never a verdict on the person.
5. **Safety to speak.** A question, a dissent, a report of a failure, and a concern about a lead each have a destination, and something reads whether they arrive.
6. **Being known.** The house holds the person in their own words, in their own language, and knows what the building must supply for them.
7. **A path.** The next thing is visible before it is asked for, and no one's advancement waits on a manager.
8. **Time that is not taken.** The paid window, the check-in on the clock, the close, and slack exist and are read as bandwidth.
9. **A say in the rules.** What the person reports changes the process, and what the floor reports reaches the rules.
10. **A place of their own.** The physical condition the record names as "a precondition for presence" (WP p.20).

Two things her material wants that are not on the list, and why. "Belonging among the team" is not a condition the philosophy names; canon's "open enough to stay" is the outcome the conditions produce, and the record's order puts retention third, after bandwidth and joy, as an output (WP p.12). Section 14 tests whether the steady state needs a belonging mechanism anyway. "Vulnerability," her fourth interpersonal risk, is not a condition the house builds; a house that asks for it is performing, and canon refuses performing (section 6).

### 2.4 The verdict method

Each condition in section 3 gets one of three verdicts. **Mechanism:** a named mechanism builds it, passes the four-part entry test (bandwidth cost, what it buys, who runs it, where its record lives; S4 P2), passes the six-question test above, and its absence would be read by a diagnostic line that exists. **Partial:** a mechanism builds it for one object and not another (the house but not the roster; the floor but not a lead), or one of the entry test's four parts or the read is missing, or the mechanism exists and cannot yet run because a gated item holds it. **Slogan:** nothing builds it; the philosophy asserts it and no mechanism, existing or specified by an earlier session, produces it; the verdict names what would make it a mechanism and who must decide.

**P3 (Recommended).** The playground test is run on a mechanism as six questions: the condition, the legible rule, the open-ended result, the tight edge, the loose edge, and the documented reason with the read that would notice the condition's absence. Every mechanism on the map is run through it at the reset, and any mechanism proposed after this page is run through it at entry beside the four-part test.

**P4 (Recommended).** "Conditions for agency" for an employee at Sŏn decomposes into ten conditions as listed in 2.3. Each is tested against a mechanism in section 3 and given one of three verdicts: mechanism, partial, or slogan.

**P5 (Recommended).** The non-negotiables outrank the Playground Philosophy on every verdict here, which is Deck 3.0's order; the position is marked as held under `86akh3rx4` because Canon 1.0's hierarchy does not rank them.

## 3. The conditions, one by one

Each condition: the mechanism that builds it, what it costs, who runs it, where its record lives, how the diagnostic would read its absence, the two edges, and the verdict. Most mechanisms already exist; this page names them as culture mechanisms, which is the thing no earlier page did.

### 3.1 Legible rules

The brief's standing change line, so that "any change to a range, trigger, goal, or mechanism since the last brief" is spoken before it is met (S3 shift brief; S8 P49).

**Cost, runner, record.** Already funded: the sheet and the card are read on paid time; the team home is a build. The domain lead owns the sheet and the card; the Operations Lead holds the register's record; the owner of the room speaks the change line. Records: the sheet's version log, the person page's orientation entry, the team home.

**The read.** Skill invisibility: "employees do not know what advancement requires because the competencies are not documented or accessible" (WP p.14), read by S9 4.6 from blank horizons and unscheduled unlocks; sequence corruption, where a change reached the floor first at the brief or not at all (S9 B2). A rule that exists only in a lead's head is node overload by the ledger's stability rule.

**The edges.** Tight: a sheet or a card that scripts the act rather than bounding it; guarded by the range card's form, which states what the person may and may not do and not how to do it, and by canon's prohibition on "service delivered by script rather than felt knowledge" (Canon 1.0, section 01). Loose: a seat opened without a sheet, which the inventory refuses (S8 P26).

**Verdict: mechanism, with a dependency.** The why block is empty until the charter is ratified (S7 P12), so the rules a person reads first are, today, unwritten; and where the team home lives is founder-gated (S3 P11.9). The mechanism is complete; two gates hold its content.

### 3.2 A stated range

**Mechanism.** The decision-rights rule: "Authority sits with the closest competent owner; the range within which that owner acts without asking is stated in advance; the triggers that escalate are named before the event" (S3 4.1), with canon's floor authority ("server owns the table"), the generosity range ("Every server carries a bottle to gift as they see fit," WP p.26), routine recovery pre-authorized "inside known limits," and escalation triggers "defined before service, not improvised at the table" (Canon 1.0, section 08; Deck 3.0, p.79). The range travels with the offer as a sheet field (S5 P12, field 10) and is on the range card.

**Cost, runner, record.** None beyond the register's upkeep; the domain lead sets ranges one level out; the register on the team home; every use of the range is captured in the close and reviewed by the lead, "a capture trigger rather than an escalation trigger" (S3 4.2, instance 8).

**The read.** Under-use: "the range is to be used, and under-use is the failure the record names" (S3 4.2, instance 8); the close's range-use count by section is a tension-slack source (S9 P9) and this page reads a range unused across a quarter as the tight edge on the person's seat. Over-escalation: calls deferred to a lead inside the server's range are a node-overload signal on the lead (S9 P10). A trigger with no destination is a structural hole (S3 4.1, rule 1).

**The edges.** Tight: a lead who "backs up without intercepting" (Canon 1.0, section 08) and intercepts; the check-in's "what is in the way" aggregated by friction is where that surfaces, never by the person's name (S9 4.7). Loose: a range with no "may not" (S3 4.1, rule 2).

Until then the card "states the hold in words" (S7 section 3), which is a legible rule with an open number inside it. A pre-opening cohort will be trained on a range whose money edge is a sentence, and the Realist's read is that on a Friday a sentence holds better than a number no one has rehearsed, but not for a whole quarter.

### 3.4 Safety to err

**Mechanism.** Blame the process: "when something goes wrong, the first question is not who failed. It is what allowed this to happen... Management absorbs blame from staff. Post-mortems are private and process-focused. The system gets fixed so it does not happen again, to anyone" (WP p.17). The capture at the close, "the system captures the incident in the shift close, the appropriate lead reviews, the process that allowed the failure gets updated" (WP p.13), in less time than the thank-you note (S4 P12). The close carries nothing about a person and no post-mortem (S4 P12). The person page carries no verdict, no rating, no free text about the person (S7 P25 to P29). The check-in refuses verdicts and discipline (S4 P15). The evidence line names no person (S9 P8). The one path a read reaches a person is tension slack, last (S9 P41). Principle 4 applies it to the founders' own domains first (S1).

**Cost, runner, record.** The capture's seconds at the close, a stack failure if longer than the note; the person who held the table captures, the reviewing lead updates, the post-mortem is that lead's off the floor; the capture log, the process's version.

**The read.** The capture rate and the process-update rate are item 1 of the leads' review every week (S4 P26), and S9 placed the capture rate and the error-reporting rate as the diagnostic's safety source (S9 3.3). A capture written by a lead on behalf of the floor is sequence corruption (S9 3.3); a falling capture rate on a rising incident count is the loose edge of safety read as a count.

**The edges.** Tight: a capture form that asks who; the close's spine refuses it. Loose: a failure that is genuinely a person's (a non-negotiable breached) treated as the process's; the non-negotiable flag goes to both leads the same day (S7 P45), which is the one case the record treats as a person's, and section 6 places what the environment does with it.

**Verdict: mechanism.** The most complete condition on the page, because the record wrote it as mechanics and Sessions 3 to 9 refused every verdict-shaped instrument that would have undone it. The response by type of failure (basic, complex, intelligent) is S12's, and the page states that dependency in section 6.

### 3.5 Safety to speak

The non-negotiable flag to both leads (S7 P45).

**Cost, runner, record.** The check-in's minute, the channel's entry; the lead of the person's domain, the item's owner, the Maitre d for the pulse; the person page's line, the channel's items and their state, the pulse composite.

**The read.** Item 5 of the leads' review, items open past the loop-closure window (S4 P26); feedback items past thirty days as a cooling-failure source (S9 P13); the count of check-in lines under "what is in the way," aggregated by friction. Who decides: the founders, `86akhb2jg`, already urgent in the ledger; this page adds the environment's reason to the urgency.

### 3.6 Being known

## 4. Designed autonomy against drift, both ways

Brandon's gloss on the session task, designed autonomy rather than freedom, is not canon's wording and is not quoted here as canon. What canon states is the two edges (Deck 3.0, p.14). This section asks what holds each edge over time, because a playground is not built once; it drifts, and the drift has a direction on each side.

### 4.1 The tight edge: rules tightening until the result is foreclosed

Three drifts the Realist expects on a real floor, and what holds each.

**Scripted service.** The rush's first instinct is a script, because a script is reproducible by a green crew. Canon forbids it three times: "service protocols built entirely on scripted delivery" (Playground), "service delivered by script rather than felt knowledge" (Mahk), and Jeong "cannot be scripted into the opening of a service interaction" (Canon 1.0, section 01). The one scripted line canon itself fixes, "We have you" at the threshold (Canon 1.0, section 08), is a legible rule with an open result behind it, which is the difference.

**Procedure-only training.** A module that measures procedure and stops is the drift canon names at tier 1 or 2 depending on the version. What holds it: the module standard must carry canon's judgment requirement as a row (section 3.3; `86akhb2kk`); the readiness form's range row; and the invisible advocate, which unlocks the next set on a period and not on a manager's judgment that the person is "ready for more," which is where procedure-only training usually hides.

## 5. The team that forms and dissolves every service

### 5.1 Her stages, and the three objects they would have to describe

Tuckman's forming, storming, norming, performing (Book pp. 306 to 308, Table 7) describes a fixed group of people meeting repeatedly over time, with a manager who is directive in forming and consultative by norming. Her guidance follows the stage: build safety and norms while forming, frame conflict as healthy while storming, celebrate and hone while norming, raise the rate of challenge while performing, and "know that this phase is not permanent" (Book p.307).

Sŏn has one team in her sense, the house, and her word means three things (S8 P6). Her stages have to be tested against each, and each gives a different answer.

**The service roster.** The people the brief named for one service, across every domain; it exists for one service and dissolves at the close (S8 3.2). It cannot form, storm, or norm as a group, because it does not meet again as that group. The Realist's read is exact: a roster has to perform on its first service, so every stage before performing has to be done before the roster exists, by the structure the roster instantiates. Forming is the brief: the designations named (who holds the room, the door, the sequence, the pass), the readiness block, the period's goal, the one thing, and the roster is formed in the time it takes to say them (S4 P10; S8 section 8). Norming happened once, for the house, and the roster inherits it. Performing is the record's own picture of a great night, "every element in relationship with every other, information moving laterally without routing through a chain of command" (WP p.8), and it is produced by the structure or not at all.

**The house.** The one team forms once, pre-opening, through the cohort in two waves and the training services (S7 P8, P10). It storms, if the word is kept, in dinner's first quarter, which is exactly why the freeze exists: the record's own quarter "so the heart is solid before anything is added to it" (WP p.36) is her storming phase with a rule that the norms do not move while it runs. **The cohort.** Her forming guidance is the training sequence's own shape and needs no translation: "be more directive," "provide extra support, direction, and modeling in activities," "actively work to build psychological safety by setting up collaborative, supportive team norms" (Table 7) are the modules, the live session, the observed and paired services with the mentor, the why block delivering the non-negotiables first, and the readiness read (S7 P10; S6 P38). ## 6. Psychological safety, the environment's half

### 6.1 Her definition, placed act by act

Her definition, from Project Aristotle, is "the ability to take interpersonal risks like asking questions, offering a dissenting opinion, discussing a failure, or expressing vulnerability without fear of negative repercussions," which she calls "a central requirement for teams to progress from one phase to the next" (Book p.306). Session 9 placed the signal (the capture rate, the error-reporting rate) as a source the diagnostic reads and handed the system that produces candor to Session 12 (S9 3.3). This page places the environment's half: what makes each of her four acts safe by construction, before any coaching or feedback conversation exists.

**Asking a question.** The mentor, who "answers questions" (WP p.18) and is "the person a new hire asks before they ask the lead" (S7 P42); the check-in's "what is in the way"; the feedback channel. Safe because none of the three writes anything about the asker: the mentor writes nothing on the person page, the check-in's line is the person's own, and the channel's item names a friction.

**Offering a dissenting opinion.** The joint half-page as the form of a hold, with a lead's refusal to hear a unilateral case (S8 P50); the check-in's upward question; the dissent line in the hiring packet (S6 section 19). Safe because a dissent at Sŏn is a written entry on a record with a named destination, not a thing said in a room to a person who decides; and because the decision-rights rule means the dissenter is never asking someone above them to change their mind, only asking the owner to consult, which the owner must do before the event under Class B (S3 4.3).

**Discussing a failure.** The capture and blame the process (section 3.4). Safe because the post-mortem is "private and process-focused" (WP p.17) and "Management absorbs blame from staff" (WP p.17), and because the close carries nothing about a person.

**Expressing vulnerability.** Refused as a mechanism. The house does not ask, and no opener, prompt, or ritual invites it: a person brings what they bring to the check-in and it is written only if they say it and only in their words. Canon's register is "present without performing" (Canon 1.0, section 01), her personal check-in example (a parent's surgery, apologies for distraction, Book p.309) is precisely a performed vulnerability, and the non-negotiables' refusal of intrusion (S9 P44, on the career conversation's origin questions) applies. What the house offers instead is the thing the record names: "dignity is a precondition for presence" (WP p.20), which is a locker and a break space, not a prompt.

### 6.2 What safety is made of here

Six things, all mechanisms already on the map: blame the process (WP p.17); the capture at the close (WP p.13; S4 P12); private, process-focused post-mortems off the floor (WP p.17; S4 P12); the check-in's refusals of verdicts, task assignment, pay, and discipline (S4 P15); nothing on a person page but states and the person's own words (S7 P25 to P29); and the non-negotiables above the philosophy (section 2.1). A seventh is the range: a pre-authorized act is not an interpersonal risk, "pre-authorizing it signals trust to staff" (WP p.26), so the range itself is a safety mechanism, and the size of the range is the size of what a person can do without fear.

What the environment does not supply, and states as dependencies. The response by type of failure (basic, complex, intelligent), which decides whether a capture is met with a process fix or something else, is S12's. What happens when a non-negotiable is breached by a person, the one failure the record treats as a person's, is S14's and S15's; the environment's rule is only that the flag goes to both leads the same day (S7 P45) and that it is never said at the brief or the close.

### 6.3 The measure, and a finding

The record commits "engagement results across psychological safety and values alignment" as a cultural measure (WP p.23). That is a survey-shaped measure, and Session 9 refused the survey as a form (S9 P5, I10). The record's own measure of safety therefore has no instrument the program accepts. What stands in: the capture rate and the process-update rate, on item 1 of the leads' review every week (S4 P26), and the error-reporting rate the designer's seat instruments (S9 3.3); the pulse's one question; and check-in lines aggregated by friction. A baseline for all three is team-filled from the first quarter. Section 20 records the finding.

### 6.4 "No topics are undiscussable," and correcting bad meeting behavior

Her norm is that a team agrees to bring lurking issues to the table because it will smell them eventually, and she calls it the meeting form of "Say the thing you think you cannot say" (Book pp. 317 to 318). At Sŏn the rule is inverted and stronger: every topic has a named destination, and the undiscussable topic is the one with no destination, which is the trigger with no destination the ledger forbids (S3 4.1, rule 1). A friction goes to the check-in or the channel; a dissent to the hold; a failure to the capture; a non-negotiable to both leads; a lead's own concern to the leads' review as a Class B or C entry; a founder's to the partners' page. Her "stinky fish" as a periodic reminder is the leads' review's item 5, items open past the window, read every week whether or not anyone remembers them (S4 P26). What the environment cannot yet route is a concern about a lead that the lead does not hold (section 3.5), and that is the one undiscussable topic left in the house until `86akhb2jg` closes.

Her "correct bad meeting behavior" has two halves. Inside the room: her rule that it is fair to call out a violated norm in a nonjudgmental way (Book p.327). ## 10. Operator mode and creator mode

Her distinction: operator meetings have agendas and are for "decisions, updates, priorities, and alignment"; creator meetings are for brainstorming, "much less structure," and may produce nothing but ideas; a good team has both, and the creator time is built "primarily through offsites" (Book pp. 319 to 320). Her example is an operator question about a balance sheet that became an unconstrained five-year vision.

**Where creation happens at Sŏn, and when.** On the build metronome, which is "fast by system, never by grind" (S1 section 2), and in the pre-opening cadence, which is one long creator period at the partners' weekly working meeting (S4 9.2). In service: the mechanism reset and the principles revision, annually, at a closed hour (section 7); the charter drafting group for a new period, convened when a period is drafted and dissolved when it is ruled (S8 P41); the menu turn, the chef's (S4 P36); the content engine, Brandon's, daily (WP p.31); and module authorship by any qualified team member, paid (WP p.19; S7 P33). The floor's creator mode is the last two of those and the feedback channel's "suggest improvements" (WP p.22); the floor is never convened to brainstorm, because a convened floor is a meeting in service hours or a closed service.

**What never happens in the room during service.** Any creation. The seam protection forbids a build meeting in the building during service hours (S4 P9), and her balance-sheet moment, an operator item that turns into a strategy brainstorm, is exactly what the protection exists to stop from happening in a dining room in dinner's first quarter, when "the build engine is idling and the founders are in the room" (S1 section 2). Inside the leads' review it is refused by the hard stop: an item that opens into a creator conversation goes to the partners' page as an ask with an owner. At the partners' review pre-opening it is the meeting's own purpose, and in service it belongs to the reset. The service metronome is set by the register and the table (S1); creation runs on the other clock.

**Her personal typology** ("some people are operators, and some people are makers or creators," Book p.319): refused as a type. The strands are two kinds of work and not two kinds of person (S5 P7), and the house reads no one for temperament (section 13).

**P25 (Recommended).** Creation at Sŏn runs on the build metronome and at closed hours: the pre-opening partners' review, the annual reset and revision, a charter drafting group dissolved at its ruling, the menu turn, the content engine, and paid module authorship. ## 13. The work style grid's team use

### 13.1 What is settled and what is open

The grid is out of recruiting (S5 P8), lives inside the working-with-me document as a self-awareness exercise for founders and leads (S1 8.2; S7 P53), and never enters structure, scheduling, or placement (S8 P40). Three carryovers (`86akh7qq7`, `86akhb2wj`, `86akhcz6v`) route one question here: whether it has any team use, "as language people use about themselves and never as an instrument that reads a person for a seat."

### 13.2 The decision

The grid has no team use beyond the placement Sessions 1 and 7 already hold, and this page adds one narrow use in one direction. The reasons:

1. A language a team uses "about itself," on a roster that dissolves every service, is a language people use about each other. Her own illustration is a leader in a meeting saying a colleague is "acting a bit 'red'" (Book p.328), which is a read of a person spoken in a room, and the house refuses a read of a person anywhere but the person's own line (S9 P42).
2. The record's vocabulary for difference is the strands, "two kinds of work, not two kinds of person" (S5 3.3), and canon's for the floor is Nunchi and Jeong. A second vocabulary about persons would compete with the one about work, and on a floor the vocabulary about persons wins, because it is easier to say.
3. Four names are a typology, and a typology on a floor becomes a placement within a week: "the room needs a Director tonight" is the sentence S8 P40 exists to forbid, and it would be said at the brief where designations are named.
4. An hourly person is never asked to write a working-with-me (S7 P49), so there is no surface on which a floor person could place themselves that is not a form the house holds about them; and the person page refuses a rating (S4 P18), which a self-placement read by a lead is.
5. The one use her material has for it that the house needs, "a common language" for founders and leads who "work in separate domains" (S1 8.2), is already met where it lives.

**The one narrow use.** A lead's working-with-me is readable by the lead's domain on the team home (S7 P49). The lead's self-placement is therefore language the domain may use about the lead, upward, at the check-in's fourth question and on the channel, because the lead wrote it about themselves for that reason. The reverse never happens. A person who wants the vocabulary for themselves can read Session 1's instrument on the team home and place themselves in their own head; nothing records it.

**How misuse is caught.** The grid's four names appearing on any person page, scorecard, schedule note, designation block, brief record, close, sheet, or leads' review line fails readiness row 120 (section 18), which is a search like S9's row 115. A check-in line in a person's own words saying a lead placed them on it is a range breach on the lead's check-in entry (section 17.5) and goes to whoever holds a lead's check-in (`86akhb2jg`); until that closes, it goes to the partners' review as a load reading on the lead.

**P30 (Recommended).** The work style grid has no team use beyond its placement inside a lead's working-with-me document. Its one team-facing use is upward and one-directional: a lead's domain may use the lead's own self-placement about the lead. It never appears on any record about a person, never names a designation's need, and never enters a room as a read of anyone; its four names on any such record fail a readiness row.
