## 1. Her ask against the record, and how much is already decided

### 1.1 Her ask

Her diagnosing section asks a manager to "take stock" of a team, especially a new one, against three things: "whether it has the structures, plan, and people it needs to deliver excellent results" (Book p.283). Her method is a survey of the group before a meeting or offsite, read against Lencioni's five dysfunctions, then a person-by-person placement on the skill-will matrix with a prescribed response per quadrant (Book pp. 283 to 286). Her restructuring section adds an effort-impact matrix for ordering the work, two legitimate triggers for a reorganization (structure not matching strategy, a talent issue) and one misused one (an underperforming team), three rules of timing, and a three-phase method with its own clock: a month, one to two weeks, one to two days (Book pp. 287 to 294). Her rebuilding section says the bedrock of a team is its people, and asks the manager to understand each one through a career conversation, then to delegate to them by a two-axis framework, with a DRI for anything that needs a single accountable name (Book pp. 294 to 303).

Read as one, her three sections assume a manager who inherits or builds a team, reads its people, and changes its shape from above. The reading is the manager's, the placement is the manager's, and the change is announced.

### 1.2 What the record already holds

The record holds a diagnostic, and it sits at the end of Part I rather than in a people section: "Use this as a diagnostic against any operational problem. Before intervening at the people level, identify which failure mode is actually present" (WP p.14). It names five modes and a fix for each. Tension slack: "a role is occupied but the person is disengaged, the thread present but not carrying load, and other nodes are compensating without visibility. The fix is not replacing the person. It is diagnosing which adjacent nodes are overloaded and re-establishing tension in that domain." Node overload: "one domain owner is absorbing load that belongs to adjacent domains, usually a manager doing too much operational work because the parallel structure has blurred. The fix is restoring the Operations Lead and Maitre d separation." Sequence corruption: "information is not flowing to where it is needed before it is needed... The fix is system maintenance, not people management." Skill invisibility: "employees do not know what advancement requires because the competencies are not documented or accessible, and politics fills the vacuum. The fix is documentation and system access." Cooling failure: "bandwidth is at zero, the team has no capacity beyond surviving the shift, joy is absent, and retention is suffering. This is a bandwidth problem, not a morale problem. The fix is removing friction from the work" (WP p.14).

Three of the five fixes say in words that they are not a person-level move: not replacing the person, not people management, not a morale problem. The other two are a structural restoration and a documentation task. None of the five names a person as the thing to change. That is the record's whole diagnostic posture, and it is stated as a rule of order: the mode is identified before anyone intervenes at the person.

Behind the reference sit the machine's four failures, which are its source: CPU failure (expensive cognition throttled by the architecture around it), memory failure (knowledge living in people and leaving with them), bus failure (information that moves only by verbal handoff and relationship), and cooling failure (no way to measure thermal load) (WP p.13). And behind those sit the structural claims the diagnostic reads against: tension loss rather than slot vacancy on a departure (WP p.8 to 9), the two parallel leads (WP p.10), the inverted web and the invisible advocate (WP p.11), bandwidth as the metabolic rate and leading indicators as the nervous system's signal (WP p.12), and the code-bearing version of a key person leaving, "a bounded, temporary, calculable" cost because the knowledge stayed in the system (WP p.14).

Canon adds two things the record's reference does not carry. The three company non-negotiables, "No Exploitation Ever. No Toxicity Ever. No Disposable People," sit above every executional system (Canon 1.0, section 01; ranked at tier 2 in Deck 3.0, p.32, and unranked in Canon 1.0's hierarchy, a difference the ledger holds open under `86akh3rx4`). And the Playground Philosophy requires "training architecture that progresses from procedure mastery to autonomous judgment" and prohibits "training systems that measure procedure execution without building toward judgment" (Canon 1.0, section 01), which is a test on the skill-invisibility fix that the record's "documentation and system access" does not state.

## 2. What a diagnosis reads at Sŏn

### 2.1 The five objects, and which are teams

Session 8 settled that Sŏn has one team in her sense, the house, and that her word translates three ways: the house for the collective, the domain for a manager's reports, and the service roster for who is on tonight (S8 P6; carryover `86akhcz6u`). A domain, a strand, and a service period are not teams. A diagnosis at Sŏn reads five kinds of object, and the two that are not among her three are read as often as the three are:

| Object | What it is | Is it a team | What the diagnostic reads of it |
|---|---|---|---|
| The house | The whole company as one integrated entity, "distributed intelligence rather than coordinated direction" (WP p.8) | Yes, the only one | The five modes' quarterly scorecard; the leading indicators as the spine; the readiness test's failures; the variety pass's classes resolving to nothing or to a founder |
| The domain | A set of seats with one owner, the domain lead (S5 P33) | No | The check-in load the lead's seat holds and its moves and stretch; the domain's open seats and their age; the training-infrastructure lines naming the domain; the captures by domain; the domain's goal page state; the tension-loss rows in the domain |
| The service roster | The people the brief named for one service across every domain; exists for one service and dissolves at the close (S8 3.2) | No, and it is the closest thing to her "boat" | The brief's designation block and whether every function was held; the close's incidents with their range status; the range used across sections; the designations named and onto whom |
| The seat | A row on the inventory, the only way a seat exists (S8 P26) | No | The row's fields, including field 15 (why the seat is open, S5 P12); the seat's age open; the designations the row carries and how often each was named onto the seat's holders; the sheet's version history |
| The service period | A period of one restaurant, opened by a ratified charter with non-empty dependent interfaces (S8 P7) | No | The charter's provided and dependent interfaces and the weekly interface read (S8 I10); the period's goal page; the period's incidents mapped to disturbance classes; the period plan's records |

The person is a sixth object, and it is the one the diagnostic reaches last and reaches only by the path in section 14. A person is never the first object of a read. The record's order, "before intervening at the people level, identify which failure mode is actually present" (WP p.14), is enforced by the form of the line: every evidence line names one of the five objects above, and none carries a person's name (section 4.1).

### 2.2 The order of every read

Every read the diagnostic makes, at any rhythm, runs in the same order, and the order is what keeps the record's rule mechanical rather than a matter of a lead's restraint:

1. Name the object read: house, domain, service roster, seat, or service period.
2. Name the failure mode present, from evidence on a mechanism's record: a capture, a close, a brief's record, a check-in line in the person's own words, a schedule fact, an inventory row, a readiness row, a variety pass entry. A mode with no source on the record is not named.
3. Name the mechanism the record's fix touches: a range, a trigger, an interval, the brief, the close, the stack's surfacing, a sheet, a designation's rule, the inventory, a seat's count under a charter, the schedule.
4. Only where the mode is tension slack, and only after the domain's fix in step 3 has been made and read back, may the read reach a person, and then only as section 14 states, and it hands off at once.

A read that arrives at a person without passing steps 1 to 3 has failed the diagnostic's own form, and the decision-rights entry in section 16.1 sends it back to its writer. This is also how the diagnostic answers her first question, "Are the right people doing the right jobs?" (Book p.283): at Sŏn that question is asked of the sheet, the horizon, the range, and the schedule before it is ever asked of a person, and by the time those have been read the question has usually dissolved into one of the five modes.

**P3 (Recommended).** A diagnosis reads five objects, the house, the domain, the service roster, the seat, and the service period, and every evidence line names which. Two of the five are not teams and are read as often as the three that translate her word. The person is reached last, by one path, and is never an evidence line's object.

**P4 (Recommended).** Every read runs in one order: the object, the mode from a record, the mechanism the fix touches, and only then and only for tension slack, a person. The order is enforced by the line's form and the decision-rights entry, not by a lead's restraint.

## 3. Her diagnostic questions and frameworks, each translated, absorbed, or refused

### 3.1 Her questions

"How is the team doing?" should be answerable "at any time" (Book p.283). At Sŏn it is, because the answer is the leading indicators the stack reads continuously and the leads' review reads weekly (S4 P26), not a manager's impression gathered for the occasion. "Are the right people doing the right jobs?" is section 2.2's order. "How happy, motivated, and fulfilled are team members?" is the employee NPS pulse, monthly, anonymous, on the OS surface (S4 P29), the check-in's upward question in the person's own words (S4 P15), and the cultural labor score once it exists (`86akh5u9x`); no lead answers it on the team's behalf. "Do they work well together?" is the record's description of a great night, information "moving laterally without routing through a chain of command" (WP p.8), read from the close: incidents resolved inside range, recoveries, escalations with destinations. "Are deadlines being met, and are the metrics looking good?" is the goal-page table with its three states and the bandwidth flag (S4 P35). Her final test, whether "every team member and the team overall" is having a positive impact (Book p.283), is refused in its second half: the diagnostic reads the house, and the person is section 14's.

### 3.2 The survey and the offsite

Her method is a survey sent by "the team leader, an HR partner, or an outside facilitator" before a meeting or offsite (Book p.283). Refused as a form. A survey asks for an impression on a date; the record's nervous system is "what allows the organization to feel what a restaurant without it cannot feel, early enough to respond rather than repair" (WP p.12), and Session 7 refused her manager milestone survey on the same ground (S7 P36). What does the survey's work is already running: the pulse, the feedback channel with its loop-closure window (S4 P30), the check-in's upward question, and the close written "at the edge by the people who felt the room" (S3 shift close). What a survey would add is a second, slower, self-reported channel beside four that already read the same population, which fails the four-part entry test on what it buys. The offsite is Session 10's; this page does not place one.

### 3.3 Lencioni's five dysfunctions

Refused as the diagnostic's frame, for two reasons and with a mapping so nothing she reads is lost. First, the dysfunctions are read at the team's interior, and "before intervening at the people level, identify which failure mode is actually present" (WP p.14) is the record's rule against opening there. Second, each dysfunction, where it is present, is a symptom one of the five modes explains, and the mode carries a fix where the dysfunction carries a conversation:

| Her dysfunction | What it looks like on Sŏn's records | The mode the diagnostic reads it as |
|---|---|---|
| Absence of trust | Incidents captured late or not at all; the error-reporting rate the designer's seat instruments as the leading indicator of safety; captures written by a lead on behalf of the floor | Sequence corruption (the capture is not happening) or cooling failure (no bandwidth to report) |
| Fear of conflict | Class B and C holds that never appear on the leads' review record while the interface read shows handoffs breaking | Sequence corruption at the interface; node overload where one lead absorbs the other's side |
| Lack of commitment | Goal pages with no state change all quarter; goals met at a bandwidth cost | Sequence corruption of the goal cycle; cooling failure |
| Avoidance of accountability | Decision-rights entries incomplete; "escalate" with no destination; a founder as an alert's named actor | Node overload at the center; a structural hole the variety pass records |
| Inattention to results | Lagging figures read first; leading indicators unread | Sequence corruption of the review's own order (S4 P26) |

The psychological safety half of her first dysfunction is the designer's seat as org-design theory and is not designed here; the diagnostic reads its signal (the capture rate, the error-reporting rate) and hands the system that produces candor to Session 12.

### 3.4 The skill-will matrix and the effort-impact matrix

The skill-will matrix is section 13's; its team-level half is absorbed there and its person-level half is refused and routed. The effort-impact matrix (Book p.287) is refused as an instrument and absorbed as a habit the reset already has: Session 4 reads every mechanism "against its own cost and buy" and continues, changes, or ends it (S4 P39). Her four quadrants are that read's four outcomes. What the diagnostic adds to ordering is not effort against impact but the record's own causal order among the modes: sequence corruption is fixed first because a degraded nervous system corrupts every other read; node overload second because an overloaded lead cannot run the other fixes; skill invisibility and cooling failure next by the record's fixes; and tension slack last, because it is the one mode whose read can reach a person and the record wants it read only after the adjacent nodes are known.

### 3.5 "Model the new behaviors and hold the team accountable to the plan"

Her manager, after the diagnosis, models the behavior and holds the team to a plan (Book p.284). Refused as a posture, absorbed as a mechanism. At Sŏn a diagnosis becomes a versioned change (section 7), the change reaches the team by four channels and no announcement (S8 P49), and whether it worked is read back on the next scorecard by the same source that raised it. The lead's modeling is the mentor's paired services (S7) and nothing else; a lead who holds the team to a plan by presence is producing stability by hand.

### 3.6 The solid and dotted lines callout

Session 8 refused solid and dotted lines (S8 P15, I12), and this page does not reopen it. What her callout raises that Session 8 did not answer is the "boat": the people a leader needs "no matter whom they report to" (Book p.282). At Sŏn the boat is the service roster, named at the brief across every domain for one service, and it is already the only cross-domain unit the structure has; a lead does not assemble one, because designations are named by each domain lead onto seats that carry the mark (S8 18.9), and nothing else is needed.

**P5 (Recommended).** Her diagnostic questions are answered by mechanisms that already run: the leading indicators, the pulse, the upward question, the close, and the goal-page table. The survey is refused as a form; the offsite is Session 10's.

**P6 (Recommended).** Lencioni's five dysfunctions are refused as the frame and mapped to the five modes as tabled. The effort-impact matrix is refused as an instrument and absorbed as the reset's cost-and-buy read; the diagnostic orders fixes by the record's causal order among the modes: sequence corruption, node overload, skill invisibility and cooling failure, tension slack last.

**P7 (Recommended).** Her post-diagnosis posture (model and hold accountable) is replaced by the versioned change and its read-back. Her boat is the service roster and needs no assembling.

## 4. The diagnostic specified: the evidence line, and the five modes one by one

### 4.1 The form of one evidence line

Carryover `86akh67wy` asks what a failure-mode evidence line looks like. Session 4 fixed where it sits: one per mode at the house review, "the evidence line from the quarter's captures and closes" (S4 P33), with the weekly feeders on the leads' review agenda under load readings and the week's captures (S4 P26, items 2 and 6). This page fixes its form. An evidence line has seven fields and no others:

1. **Mode.** One of the five, in the record's words. Never a sixth, never a blend.
2. **Object.** House, domain (named by seat of its lead), service roster (named by service and date), seat (named by inventory row), or service period (named by charter). Never a person.
3. **Signal source.** The mechanism record the line is read from, named so the reader can open it: the close of a dated service, a capture, a brief's record, a check-in line, a schedule entry, an inventory row and version, a readiness row, a variety pass entry, a pipeline read line, an infrastructure line, the pulse composite, the feedback channel. A line with no source is incomplete and is not read.
4. **Count.** How many times the signal appeared, as a count of services, reviews, check-ins, captures, or weeks. Never money, never a percentage this program generated, never a figure from anywhere the source boundary excludes.
5. **Compensation.** Which adjacent seats, designations, or mechanisms are carrying the load or absorbing the failure, named by seat or by mechanism. This is the record's "which adjacent nodes are overloaded" (WP p.14), and it is the field that makes tension visible where the record says it is invisible.
6. **The fix the record names,** and the mechanism it touches: for tension slack, re-establishing tension in the domain; for node overload, restoring the separation, carried under the ruling by a designation, the stack, or a procedure; for sequence corruption, system maintenance; for skill invisibility, documentation and access; for cooling failure, removing friction, with the allocation consequence marked as founder-gated (`86akh3t00`).
7. **State.** Present; not present; or cannot read yet, with the reason (no baseline, no roster, no closes, the definition open, the kitchen chef-gated).

Three rules on the line. It is written in the record's vocabulary and canon's register: declarative, ending at its last load-bearing word (Canon 1.0, section 09). It names no person, carries no verdict, and adds no dimension to any review, so that it can never become the back door into Session 13's four dimensions (S6 P33). And it is the same line at every rhythm: the weekly feeder is the line with fields 1 to 3 and 5, the quarterly scorecard line adds 4, 6, and 7, and the reset reads four quarterly lines side by side.

**P8 (Recommended).** The failure-mode evidence line has seven fields: mode, object, signal source, count, compensation, the record's fix and the mechanism it touches, and state. It names no person, carries no figure and no verdict, and is the same line at the leads' review, the house review, and the reset.

### 4.2 Who writes and who reads

Each domain lead writes their domain's lines into their memo for the house review (S4 P32), and marks weekly feeders on the leads' review record under items 2 and 6 as the review runs; the chair records them. The Maitre d writes the floor's, both strands, and the Head of Beverage's line for the bar's seats sits under the Maitre d's read as the lead whose outcomes it serves (S8 P9). The kitchen's lines are chef-gated. The center, the partners, integrates the domains' lines into one line per mode for the house, which is what the house review's scorecard section reads (S3 P10.4). The Operations Lead holds the record and writes no line about a floor person, because the Operations Lead holds no floor check-in (S8 P21); the Operations Lead does write the system-side lines, since sequence corruption's evidence is largely the stack's. The reset reads the year's lines; the gate review reads the lines beside the readiness rows.

### 4.3 Tension slack

The record: a role occupied, the thread present but not carrying load, "other nodes are compensating without visibility. The fix is not replacing the person. It is diagnosing which adjacent nodes are overloaded and re-establishing tension in that domain" (WP p.14).

Evidence it reads, and from which record. From the close: incidents on one seat's section resolved by another seat's range, recoveries made by the room's holder on a section whose server did not escalate, a designation's function performed by someone not named to it, all as counts of services. From the check-in: "what is in the way" lines, in the persons' own words, that name an adjacent seat's load (a runner's line that a section's server does not call; a server's line that the sequence never arrives on their side), never a lead's reading of anyone's manner. From the thermal-load lines the close carries (S4 agenda item 6): a rise on the seats adjacent to one seat, read as a week. From the schedule: a section re-drawn by the room's holder mid-service repeatedly to route around one seat. From the pipeline read: a seat the lead holds open rather than fills below the bar, which Session 5 reads as a slack reading on whether the house can absorb the hole (S5 P14), and which this page reads as the same line with the seat unfilled rather than occupied.

The form of the line: tension slack | domain, outward strand, dinner | close records of the named services; check-in lines of the two adjacent seats | services this quarter on which an adjacent range was used on the section | the two adjacent server seats and the room's holder | re-establish tension in the domain: re-section, the rotation of designations, the range card re-read, the schedule | present, or cannot read yet.

What the line may conclude: that the domain's tension is slack at a named seat, and which adjacent seats are carrying it. What it may not: that the person is disengaged (the record's word is a description of the mode, not a finding the diagnostic makes about anyone), that the person should be replaced, moved, reviewed, coached, or placed on a quadrant; it writes nothing on any person page and travels to no review. Where it hands off: after the domain fix is made and read back, and only then, by section 14's path.

**P9 (Recommended).** Tension slack is read from the close's cross-section range use, the adjacent seats' own check-in lines, the thermal-load lines, the schedule's re-sectioning, and the hold-open slack reading, as counts of services. The line names the seat and the adjacent seats carrying it and concludes nothing about the person. The fix is the domain's, and the read reaches a person only by section 14.

### 4.4 Node overload

The record: "one domain owner is absorbing load that belongs to adjacent domains, usually a manager doing too much operational work because the parallel structure has blurred. The fix is restoring the Operations Lead and Maitre d separation" (WP p.14). The ledger adds: a lead who produces stability by hand is node overload, and every mechanism runs without a lead's memory (S3 P3.3).

Evidence it reads, and from which record. From the schedule and the stack: a check-in moved twice (leads' review) or three times (house review), and the interval's stretch as the stack reports it when the roster exceeds the lead's windows (S4 P19, P20). From the pipeline read: a seat open past three consecutive leads' reviews (S5 P14). From the brief and close: a call deferred to a founder on the floor (S8 18.8's trigger); a designation's function held by the Maitre d because none was named (row 95); a service run on the stack's failure with the book, the ranges, or the close held from a lead's memory (row 103). From the partners' page: a lead pulled onto a live risk (S8 P42); a founder as an alert's named actor (S3 P10.8); a second plan extension, which Session 7 already routes as a node-overload or infrastructure entry (S7 P29). From the training-infrastructure line: the same mentor load recurring, which carryover `86akhb2wr` names as this mode's signal (S7 I16). From the variety pass after the first quarter: a disturbance class whose incidents landed on a lead's memory rather than the regulator the pass named (S8 P14). From the pre-opening cadence: the hiring partner still opening every seat and deciding every packet after the leads are in the building (S6 P15's six-week condition), which is node overload at the center.

Read under the ruling. The record's fix names a separation between two seats. Brandon's ruling makes the room one seat, and Session 8 read the line the record leaves undrawn: live coordination of the room is the how and is the Maitre d's; the schedule, labor, compliance, and the stack are the operational work and are the Operations Lead's and the stack's (S8 21.6). So "restoring the separation" reads, on the floor, as: whatever operational work has drifted onto the Maitre d's seat goes back to the Operations Lead's systems, and whatever live function of the room the Maitre d cannot hold on an absent service goes to the room designation, the door, and the sequence. A load finding on the seat is carried by a designation, the stack, or a procedure, never by a second seat, a floor manager under another name, or an assistant of any title (ledger; S8 P4).

The form of the line: node overload | domain, the floor, by its lead's seat | the stack's interval report; the leads' review record's moved check-ins; the brief records naming the room | check-ins moved twice this quarter, by count; services on which the room was held by the seat's holder on a day the seat was not scheduled | the room designation, the sequence, the mentor pool | restore the separation: return the operational item to the Operations Lead's system, widen the eligible holders of the room, lengthen the interval after the plan (argued at the reset), print the degraded-mode set | present, or cannot read yet.

What the line may conclude: which lead's seat is absorbing which adjacent load, and the mechanism that should carry it. What it may not: that the lead is a poor lead or a good one; that a second seat is needed; that praise is due for holding it by hand. A node-overload line on a lead's seat goes to the partners' review the week it is written, because the lead cannot version the fix to their own seat's load.

**P10 (Recommended).** Node overload is read from moved check-ins and the interval stretch, seats open past three reviews, calls deferred to a founder, functions held by a lead because no designation was named, anything held from a lead's memory, a lead pulled onto a live risk, the recurring mentor load, the second extension, and the variety pass's incidents landing on a lead. Under the ruling the fix is carried by a designation, the stack, or a procedure, never a second seat. A line on a lead's seat goes to the partners' review the same week.

### 4.5 Sequence corruption

The shift brief is not happening, or the system is not surfacing preferences, or post-shift documentation is not captured. The nervous system is degraded. The fix is system maintenance, not people management" (WP p.14).

## 5. Signals of the same kind: recurrence, and what a diagnosis does with an object that repeats

### 5.1 Thresholds of recurrence, in counts of reviews and services

The house already carries three recurrence rules: twice for a moved check-in (S4 P20), three consecutive leads' reviews for an open seat (S5 P14), and a quarter of every service for a designation (S8 18.9). This page generalizes them so that any signal on any object has a stated recurrence at which it moves, and the stack surfaces the move rather than a lead remembering it:

| Recurrence | Where the read goes | What it becomes |
|---|---|---|
| One occurrence | The leads' review that week, under items 2 or 6 | A weekly feeder: mode, object, source, compensation |
| The same signal on the same object across three consecutive leads' reviews | The partners' review, that week | A possible node-overload entry or a structure question, in the record's words for three weeks of compensation |
| The same mode on the same object on two consecutive quarterly scorecards, after the record's fix was applied at the first | The mechanism reset's agenda | A mechanism question: the fix named the wrong mechanism or the wrong mode; continue, change, or end |
| A designation held by one person on every service for a quarter | The mechanism reset | A question about a seat, answered in section 9.3 under the ruling |
| A mode present at a gate review on any object | The gate review's record | A failed readiness row's mode; the gate does not open around it |

No threshold is set in money, and none is set in a figure this program generated; each is a count of reviews or services, which is what the house can count on the stack from the first training service.

### 5.2 A domain that repeats

Carryover `86akh7r0c` asks what a diagnosis does with a domain that produces recruiting signals repeatedly. The answer is that the object of the read moves up: a repeating seat signal becomes a domain read, and a domain read asks three questions in order, none of which is whether the lead hires well.

A domain repeats when any of these holds: a seat in it has passed three consecutive reviews open twice inside a year; two of its seats have opened by tension loss inside one quarter; or its lead has held a seat open as a slack reading on two consecutive quarterly scorecards. At that recurrence the partners' review reads the domain, and the questions are:

1. Is the count wrong? The seat count per charter is a field on the charter and a row count on the inventory (S8 I1). A domain that keeps opening seats it cannot fill at the bar may be carrying a count the charter set before a service ran; the fix is a structure change entry on the count, with the lines as its reason (section 7).
2. Is the sheet wrong? Field 6's entry horizon, field 11's shift shape, and field 13's physical demands are the fields a seat stays open on. A sheet whose horizon is set above what the entry pool holds keeps a hole; the fix is a versioned sheet, which re-reads every open candidate (S5 P13).
3. Is the lead's seat overloaded? A domain whose lead is holding the room, the door, and the check-in rotation on the seat's absent services will produce open seats as a symptom, because the pipeline read's action is the lead's (S5 P28). This is section 4.4's line and section 12's read.

Only after those three does anything about the domain's hiring get read, and then by Session 6's calibration read, which reads the packets against the pages and writes to neither (S6 P41), never by the diagnostic. A domain that produces tension-loss departures repeatedly is read as cooling failure first (section 4.7), with the exit interviews as the building's lines; the diagnostic does not conclude that the domain's people leave because of its lead, because the record's rule is that universal problems are design problems (WP p.17) and a domain is not a lead's character.

### 5.3 A seat that repeats, and a service period that repeats

A seat opened by tension loss twice inside a year is a sheet question before it is anything else: the horizon, the shift shape, the physical demands, and the range are the fields that make a seat hard to hold, and each is versioned. The person who left is not the object; the exit interview's building lines are.

A service period that repeats is read through its charter. A period whose incidents cluster in one disturbance class across a quarter has the variety pass re-run for that period (S8 P46). A period whose dependent interfaces field goes empty in practice, which the interface read surfaces (S8 I10), goes to the gate review as a period that has become a separate restaurant, which the ledger says is not opened and, if open, is the record's own "worse version of dinner" (WP p.2). ## 7. From diagnosis to change

### 7.1 The record's own loop, and the two kinds of change

The record already has the loop: "the system captures the incident in the shift close, the appropriate lead reviews, the process that allowed the failure gets updated, and the next server inherits a system that has already learned from it" (WP p.13). And canon has the rule for what a change is: "A living document changes by versioned decision, never by drift" (Deck 3.0, p.94), with Jaeyeonmi's "maintenance philosophy that distinguishes care from correction" (Canon 1.0, section 01). Session 4 used the distinction for the reset's rarity (S4 6.6). This page uses it to say which changes need a version.

Care is a process update from a capture: the close records an incident, the lead reviews it inside seven days, the process that allowed it is updated, and the update is logged against the capture with an owner. It is daily, unversioned in the deck's sense, and it is the only change the first quarter's freeze allows in the room. Correction is any change to a mechanism's rhythm, spine, range, trigger, or rule, to a designation's rule, to a seat, a line, or a period, and it is versioned: on the mechanism map for a mechanism (S4's cadence page), on the inventory for structure (S8 P49), on the goal page for a goal (S4 P35), on the sheet for a seat's fields (S5 P13). A diagnosis produces both kinds, and the line's field 6 says which by naming the mechanism.

### 7.2 The four paths

A diagnosis becomes a change by one of four paths, and the path is set by what the fix touches:

1. **A mechanism.** A range, a trigger, an interval, a window, the brief's block, the close's fields, a surfacing rule. Proposed by the domain lead at the leads' review with the evidence line as its reason; a parameter inside a range the review already holds is set there and logged; anything else waits for the reset, where it continues, changes by versioned decision, or ends (S4 P39). Between resets a mechanism's rhythm and spine do not move.
2. **A designation's rule.** Eligibility, a rotation floor, what the room designation holds. A structure change entry on the inventory (S8 17.1 lists "changing a designation's rule"), proposed through the leads' review, versioned by the partners' review, and made at the reset when it is a load finding on the Maitre d's seat (section 12). Naming a designation for a service is not a change and needs no entry.
3. **A seat.** Creating, retiring, re-opening by tension loss, changing its count under a charter, or versioning its sheet. Session 8's path whole: a versioned inventory entry with its reason first, proposed through the leads' review, versioned by the partners' review, reaching the team by the affected person's check-in, the sheet's version, the brief, and the team home, in that order, and by no announcement (S8 P49, I5).
4. **A leadership line or a period.** A Class A candidate at the partners' review on the variety pass at a gate, argued and ratified before the period opens (S8 18.3, 18.4). Never from a single quarter's line; the pass is the ground.

### 7.3 The reason is the line

Session 8's structure change entry must contain "the reason written first, in the record's terms (tension lost, a class resolving to nothing, a gate, a ruling)" (S8 I5). This page closes the coupling: the reason field of a change entry, and the reason field of a mechanism's versioned decision on the map, is the evidence line, carried over verbatim with its seven fields. A change with no line is a change with no reason, and Session 8's form already refuses it. A change whose line names a person as the reason is refused twice, once by the line's form and once by the entry's.

The consequence for restructuring is exact. At Sŏn nothing is restructured because a manager judged the team; something is versioned because a dated line on a mechanism's record named a mode, an object, and the record's fix. That is the whole of her "10 percent figuring out the plan" (Book p.287), and it is written before the change, not narrated after.

### 7.4 The read-back

Every change carries the mode and the object it was made to clear. The next scorecard reads the same line, from the same source, and writes one of three states beside the change's version: cleared (the signal is absent on the source that raised it), not cleared (present on two consecutive lines after the fix, which is section 5.1's reset trigger), or cannot read (the source has not run since the change). No change is read back by a lead's impression that it helped; it is read back by the mechanism record that raised it. A change that cleared is left alone; the reset does not re-open a cleared line. A change that did not clear says one of two things, that the fix named the wrong mechanism or that the line named the wrong mode, and the reset decides which before it names a second fix. This is her "build in some checkpoints to determine whether the skill is rising to meet the will" (Book p.286) with the object moved from the person to the mechanism.

What is changed at the reset and what may be changed between resets, in one list. At the reset only: any mechanism's rhythm, spine, interval, or range width; any designation's rule; the mentor pool's composition after the first wave (S7 P42); the response window's house standard (S5 P29); the recurrence thresholds themselves. Between resets: a process update from a capture; a parameter inside a held range; a sheet's version; a seat opened by tension loss; a structure change entry that a gate or a departure requires; the naming of designations. Inside the first quarter: only the second list's first, second, and fourth items, and the sheet on the build side.

**P24 (Recommended).** A diagnosis produces care or correction: a process update from a capture is care and is logged; a change to a mechanism, a designation's rule, a seat, a line, or a period is correction and is versioned on the map, the inventory, the goal page, or the sheet.

**P25 (Recommended).** Four paths from a line to a change: a mechanism through the leads' review and the reset; a designation's rule through a structure change entry; a seat through Session 8's path whole; a line or a period through the variety pass at a gate. The evidence line is the change's reason field, verbatim, and a change with no line is refused.

**P26 (Recommended).** Every change is read back on the next scorecard by the source that raised it, as cleared, not cleared, or cannot read; a change not cleared on two lines goes to the reset, which decides whether the mechanism or the mode was wrong before naming a second fix.

### 8.1 Reorganizations, and her three triggers

## 9. A departure, an open seat, and a designation held every service for a quarter

### 9.1 A departure is tension re-establishment, and what the diagnostic reads first

## 14. Where a diagnostic read reaches a person

### 14.1 The one path

Tension slack is the only mode whose read can reach a person, because it is the only mode the record describes at a person: "a role is occupied but the person is disengaged" (WP p.14). It is also the mode whose fix the record states most firmly away from the person: "not replacing the person." The path is therefore narrow and ordered, and every step is on a record:

1. The line names the seat and the adjacent seats carrying its load, from the close, the adjacent seats' own lines, and the schedule (section 4.3). The person's name is on no line.
2. The domain fix is made and read back: re-sectioning, the designations rotated, the range card re-read with the seat's holder at their check-in, the schedule's shape. This is "re-establishing tension in that domain" (WP p.14), and it is a change with a read-back like any other (section 7.4).
3. The only person-side read the diagnostic takes is the person's own: the check-in's "what is in the way," in their words, on their page, read back before saving (S4 P15). The lead's reading of the person is not a source and is never written.
4. If, after the domain fix, the seat still does not carry its load across the recurrence in section 5.1 (three consecutive leads' reviews, or two consecutive quarterly lines), the diagnostic may write one thing and only one: that the seat remains occupied and its load remains carried by named adjacent seats after the domain's fix, with the dates. It may not write disengaged, low will, a problem, a fit question, or anything about the person's manner.
5. It hands off there. To Session 12, for what the check-in's coaching content does with a line the person has heard and a load the domain has tried to re-establish. To Session 14, only if the pattern the check-ins have recorded, and the person has heard at each, becomes a performance conversation, which Session 14 designs and the diagnostic does not. Never to Session 15 directly; managing out is not a destination the diagnostic has, and a read that arrives there has skipped two sessions' work.

Two things this path does not touch. A non-negotiable breach is not a diagnostic read; it is the flag rule and the both-leads trigger (S4 P20; S6 P51), and it runs on its own path regardless of any line. And a hire that does not hold inside the plan is not the diagnostic's; Session 7 hands it to Session 14 with its record (S7 P46), and the diagnostic reads the plan's infrastructure lines only as the modes they name.

### 14.2 What the diagnostic writes about a person, stated as a refusal

Nothing. A person's name appears in no evidence line, no scorecard section, no house memo, no reset record, and no leads' review record's diagnostic mark. The only page a person appears on is their own, written at their check-in in their words. The diagnostic reads that page for the friction the building supplies and for the path the person named; it writes on it never. That refusal is what keeps the record's "No Disposable People" (Canon 1.0, section 01) a property of the instrument rather than a hope about its users, and it is what makes the four dimensions hold at review (S6 P33): a diagnostic that wrote about people would be the fifth.

**P41 (Recommended).** A read reaches a person by one path, for tension slack only: the seat and its adjacent seats named; the domain fix made and read back; the person's own check-in line as the only person-side source; one permitted statement after recurrence, that the seat remains occupied and its load carried by named seats; then hand-off to Session 12, and only through it to Session 14, never to Session 15.

**P42 (Recommended).** The diagnostic writes nothing about any person anywhere. A person's name is on no line, memo, or record it produces; the person page is read for the building's friction and the person's path and is never written by the diagnostic.

## 15. The career conversation

### 15.1 Her conversation, and what the record holds

Hers is a scheduled sixty-minute "get-to-know-you" conversation "after a few months of working together... but before any sort of formal performance review" (Book p.295; Workbook p.90), walking the person's history from childhood through school and past jobs to a five-year projection, closing with "two or three development goals to keep an eye on together" (Workbook p.92), and serving as a reference point at reorganizations, reviews, and delegation decisions (Book p.296).

The record has no career conversation and would not need one as she frames it. "The path is explicit before they start it. Every position has documented competency requirements visible to anyone. Employees do not need to ask what it takes to advance. They open the system and see it" (WP p.11). "Advancement requires preparation, not permission" (WP p.14). A conversation in which a lead learns a person's ambitions in order to "make better decisions about what that person should focus on" (Book p.295) is the manager's attention the invisible advocate exists to remove. What the record does hold is the review "every three to six months" focused on "the path forward" (WP p.19), cross-training as "career development, not only operational insurance" (WP p.19), and the person's own development outward "deeper into their domain and across into adjacent ones" (WP p.11). Session 7 placed the path read at the fifth check-in: "The first unlock's date from the sheet, opened on the person's own account; the seat after theirs, named by them; what they want to learn next, including another domain's track" (S7 P27), and handed the rest here (carryover `86akhb2wr`) with two conditions: it opens on the person page and adds nothing the person has not heard.

### 15.2 Placed, on the clock, opening on the person page

The career conversation at Sŏn is one scheduled conversation, placed by the stack in the month after the plan closes and before the person's first review, which matches her timing exactly and the record's review interval. It is paid as shift, scheduled as a block in a pre-service window with slack or at a closed hour, longer than the check-in and shorter than a shift's first hour; its length is a parameter for the reset, set at thirty minutes to start. It is between the person and the domain lead; for a lead, its holder is whoever holds a lead's check-in (`86akhb2jg`). The kitchen's is chef-gated with the kitchen's check-in (S4 P16).

It opens on the person page's path line, the one the fifth check-in wrote in the person's words, and on four documents the person can already read: the seat sheet's field 6 horizons and item 7's advancement path written as outward movement (S8 P30); the tracks, visible from day one including another domain's (S7 P39); the fluency record, where cross-strand and cross-period fluency are connections demonstrated by readiness forms and never asserted (S8 P35, I7); and the seat after theirs, named by them. It adds nothing the person has not heard: no reading of them, no lead's view of their potential, no comparison.

What it does: re-reads the path line with the person and lets them re-write it; names, in the person's words, one track they want next, inside the two strands and the open periods, which is their one goal's development form (S4's count of one per person); names what the building must supply for that track (a form placed on the schedule, a module in their language, a paired service in the other strand), which becomes a training-infrastructure line if the building has not supplied it; and records the mentor relationship's shape after the plan as the person states it, which Session 10 designs.

What it refuses. A five-year plan, "which causes most of us to shut down" (Book p.295), and the record's own rule against asserting the unproven. A title, a level, a rank, or a promise of a seat, because Sŏn has no levels and no ladders and advancement is the advocate's (S8 P30). A promise of pay, which is Session 13's and never a lead's. A date beyond the sheet's unlock period. The person's history before the house: "where you grew up," "where you went to school," "what you did right after college" (Workbook p.91) are refused as questions, because the hiring rule refuses years of experience and credentials as reads (S5 P13), the seat sheet never asked for them, and a lead asking about family and origins after hiring is the intrusion the non-negotiables exist to forbid; if the person brings their own history, it is theirs to bring and is not written. Development goals in the lead's words: the wrap-up's "write down two or three development goals" (Workbook p.92) is refused as the lead's act; the person names one track and it is written in their words and read back. Any use as an input to delegation, placement, or a designation; the record's placement rule is the seat's mark and a complete form (S8 18.9), not a conversation.

Her workbook's questions, translated where they can be: "your favorite job... least favorite" becomes "what work do you want more of here, and less of," inside the strands and periods, which is a question about the seat and not the past; "what sort of work do you see yourself doing in the future" becomes "which seat after this one, which strand, which period," never a title; "who are the people you'll be working with, where will you be living" is refused. Her three purposes: "establishing that you care" is refused as a purpose, because care at Sŏn is the mechanism that advocates without a lead's attention, not a performed conversation; "the person's narrative arc" becomes the person's account of what they want next, inside the house's tracks; "a longer-term career direction that you can both refer to" becomes the path line on the person page, which the check-in re-reads whenever the stack shows a new unlock or a period opens.

### 15.3 Rhythm, and its relation to the review and the diagnostic

Once, after the plan. After that the path is re-read at the check-in, not re-held as a conversation, when the stack shows an unlock scheduled, when a period's charter is ruled and the period plan opens, and when the person asks. The review every three to six months (S13's) opens on the person page and finds the path line there, which is how "no surprises" holds for the path as it does for everything else (S4 P18). The diagnostic reads the conversation's outputs only as building lines: what the building has not supplied for a named track is an infrastructure line, and the same track unsupplied for the same reason across seats is skill invisibility. A career conversation that surfaces a non-negotiable goes to both leads by the flag rule; one that surfaces a friction goes to the infrastructure line; one that names another domain's track goes to that domain's lead through the person's own lead, as Session 7's cross-domain rule states (S7 P39, P40).
