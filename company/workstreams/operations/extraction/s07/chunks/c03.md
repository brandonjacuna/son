## 5. Why before how: the charter delivery, and the blocking dependency stated

### 5.1 The record's commitment, and what it needs

"Why comes before how. Orientation to the mission, the values, and the behavioral standard happens before operational training, because a person needs the reason before the mechanics" (WP p.18). Carryover `86akh3w9a` is exact about the source: the content is drawn from the charter, not from a version this page writes, and if the charter is unratified the page records that the record's own commitment is unmet and names the charter as the blocking dependency rather than filling the gap with brand language. It is unratified: the mission sentence (`86akh3tdr`) and principles version one with the long-term commitments (`86akh3tg0`) are open in the ledger.

Her version of the same commitment is the "why" first, then the "what," then the "how" (Book p.216), delivered by leadership through "stories, not bullet points" (Book p.216). The record's order is the same and its three terms are narrower and better: the mission, the values, and the behavioral standard. This page uses the record's three.

### 5.2 What the charter holds now, and what it does not

The behavioral standard is ratified. It is the three non-negotiables, No Exploitation Ever, No Toxicity Ever, No Disposable People, and the order bandwidth, then joy, then retention, then results (Canon v1.0, section 01; Canon v3.0, p.4), and it is what the candidate was already handed as the transparency sheet's item 9, "the house's commitments to the candidate before they are the candidate's to the house" (S5 P34). The language constant, "The word is customer" (Canon v3.0, p.5), is ratified canon and is part of the standard as this page reads it, because it is a behavior every person in the house performs in every sentence.

The structure is the record. The web with no top and no bottom, the outside edge as the most sensitive position, growth outward, strands not levels, the domain lead as a systems function and not a rank, the invisible advocate, blame the process (WP p.9 to 14, p.17). These are decided, and orientation delivers them as they stand.

The mission sentence and the principles are not ratified. Orientation's why block has a first sentence it cannot yet say and a set of principles it cannot yet read aloud. Session 6 met the same gap in the third read's principle-embedded questions and held them (S6 P22); Session 4 met it in the goal page's commitment field, which "points at a placeholder until they land" (S4 P41). This page holds the why block's first two parts the same way: the block has a slot, the slot is empty, and an empty slot is not filled with brand positioning ("Korean restraint. Texas warmth. Polished but playful," Canon v3.0, p.3), which is a brand's account of itself to a customer and not a house's account of why it exists to a person who works in it. Positioning is delivered in orientation as positioning, in the brand module, and never as the mission.

### 5.3 The why block's content, in the record's order

The why block is the first content of the first shift, after the walk of the building and before any module, station, or section. It is spoken, in person, by the person section 5.4 names, in the languages the cohort speaks, with an interpreter where one is needed, and it is not a slide deck. Its order:

1. The mission, as ratified. The sentence, said once, and what it was chosen against. Empty until `86akh3tdr` closes.
2. The principles, as ratified, each with one moment from the record or the house's own captures in which it decided something. Empty until `86akh3tg0` closes. When the house has run, the moments come from the shift-close captures and the post-mortems, which is where Session 4 said the principles are tested (S4 P39); pre-opening, from the record's own stories.
3. The behavioral standard: the three non-negotiables, read as the house's commitments to the person first; the order bandwidth, joy, retention, results, with the record's own account of why the order is the decision (WP p.12, p.17); and the language constant, with the record's reason for it, that "customer keeps the transaction visible" and "You cannot truly belong somewhere that is performing for you" (WP p.25).
4. The structure the person has entered: the web, drawn on paper or on the wall from the person's own node outward (S5 P32); the outside edge as the most sensitive position, in the record's words (WP p.9); what the domain lead is and is not (S5 P33); the invisible advocate and the seat after theirs, opened on the person's own account so they see it (WP p.11; S5 P60); and blame the process, with the record's own worked example of the server, the incident, the shift close, and the next server inheriting the fix (WP p.13).
5. The stories. Her rule is right and canon sharpens it: "Specificity is the mechanism of memory" (Canon v1.0, section 01). The stories are the record's own and they are told as what happened: the room in New York that added lunch and brunch and both stayed flat, and what that taught about structure (WP p.2); the coffee shop on the wrong side of the road (WP p.3); the broken computer everyone has used (WP p.12); the fourteen leadership lines around one dinner service (WP p.22). None is a founder hero narrative; each is an argument about structure told as an event, which is what "stories of those principles in action" (Book p.216) means when the principle is structural.

What the why block refuses: a video in place of a person; a synthesized presenter of any kind (section 8.3); a printed values poster, which the record names as the thing culture is not (WP p.18); a quiz; a signature acknowledging the values; any content that names its own effect; positioning presented as mission; canon's forbidden word for the customer in any story, including quoted ones, which are paraphrased; a daypart code name; any figure.

### 5.4 Who delivers it

Her ask is that "company leadership should take part in onboarding new hires" (Book p.216). At Sŏn "Brandon Acuña-Cardona is the public voice and the origin of the operating concepts" (WP p.3), and the ledger holds open who carries the concept's voice to the team internally (`86akh2qq0`; spec `86akh3u2y`). The why block is exactly that function at its first and most consequential moment.

The team's position: pre-opening and through dinner's first quarter, a founder delivers the why block in person to every wave and every later hire, because there is no one else who holds the concept and because the first quarter's freeze (S4 P9) is not a reason for the founders to be absent from the one block that is theirs. After the mechanism reset, the holder of the concept's voice internally delivers it, and if that question has not closed by the reset, the founder continues and the reset records it as a load on the founder's seat. A domain lead delivers the fourth part, the structure, from the second quarter, because the structure is the lead's daily object; the lead never delivers the first three, because the lead is a systems function inside the structure and not its author. The mentor is present at the block and delivers none of it.

**P16 (Founder-gated).** The why block's first two parts, the mission and the principles, are empty until the charter is ratified (`86akh3tdr`, `86akh3tg0`). The record's own onboarding commitment, that orientation to the mission and the values precedes operational training, is unmet until then, and the charter is the blocking dependency. The team does not fill the slots with brand language and marks them. With it, who carries the concept's voice internally after the first quarter (`86akh2qq0`) is founder-gated and the team recommends the founder continues until it closes.

**P17 (Recommended).** The why block runs in the record's order, mission, principles, behavioral standard, structure, stories, as the first content of the first shift, spoken in person by a founder pre-opening and through dinner's first quarter, in the cohort's languages, with the structure delivered by the domain lead from the second quarter and the stories drawn from the record as events. It refuses video, a synthesized presenter, a poster, a quiz, an acknowledgement signature, positioning as mission, and any figure.

### 5.5 The blocking dependency, argued

The argument for making charter ratification a start-date condition (P12) is the record's own sequence rule: "a person needs the reason before the mechanics" (WP p.18). A cohort onboarded to the non-negotiables and the structure alone has received the behavioral standard and the web, which is most of the why, and a house that had to choose would rather have that than nothing. But the record does not say "most of." The commitment is the mission, the values, and the behavioral standard, in that order, and a cohort onboarded without the first two would be told the mission later, after their operational training, which is the sequence the record forbids. The leads are the exception because the leads are the people who will close the charter with the founders, and their orientation to an unratified charter is a known incomplete on their own plan (section 6.4) rather than a commitment unmet.

What this costs: if the charter is not ratified by the time the hiring calendar wants the cohort's first start dates, the start dates wait, and the gate slides. That is the same shape as Session 6's P16 on the compensation architecture, and the team's recommendation is the same: ratify ahead rather than slide. The charter's ratification is already a readiness row (S3 row 5) and the gate cannot open around it; this position only moves it earlier, to the cohort's start dates, which precede the gate by the training-service count.

## 6. The first shift, the first week, the first ninety days

### 6.1 The sequence, and why it is the interviewer's sequence

Session 6 fixed the training form for interviewers: modules read, a live session, an observed stage, a paired stage read side by side, then solo, all on scheduled shift, with the record written by the stack (S6 P38). The brief asks whether operational training uses the same sequence and why.

It does, and the reason is not economy of design. The sequence is the one canon's training requirement produces when it is taken seriously: "training architecture that progresses from procedure mastery to autonomous judgment" (Canon v1.0, section 01). Modules carry the procedure as knowledge; the live session carries it as demonstration; the observed service carries it as the procedure seen at tempo; the paired service carries it as the procedure done at tempo with a person beside you; the solo is the procedure done alone, and judgment begins there. Her section's own kitchen voice describes the industry's alternative, "thrown onto the job" (Book p.216), and the record describes it as "Shadow someone for two shifts, catch fragments because they are busy, get thrown in before ready" (WP p.19). Two shifts of shadowing is the observed step with the other four missing. The sequence is what the record's paragraph is asking for, and the interviewer training is the first place the house wrote it down.

Two things differ from the interviewer's sequence, and they are stated. The paired step, for a floor or kitchen seat, is not one paired service but as many as the seat's entry procedures need, and the count is on the seat sheet's training plan (item 5) and not a house constant, because a runner's entry procedures and a server's are not the same length. And the solo step is gated by the readiness read (section 6.5), scored by an assessor, where the interviewer's solo is gated by the paired read of two scorecards; the difference is that an interviewer's solo produces a scorecard the Maitre d reads afterward, and a server's solo produces a customer's evening, which no one reads afterward.

The steps, defined once:

Modules read. The seat's first module set (section 8), on Trainual, taken on scheduled shift in the check-in's unit of time per module, self-paced within the first week, in the person's language. Modules are read before the live session and never in place of it.

The live session. The mentor walks the section or station with the person, outside service, in the building: the setup, the sequence, the positions, the surfaces the seat uses on the stack, the close. For a floor seat this is the section's setup and the step-back positions walked on the floor with no customer in the room; for a kitchen station, chef-gated in every specific; for the bar, the well. One block, on shift, in the pre-service window of a service the person then observes, or in a training service.

The observed service. The person is on the schedule beside the mentor for a service, carrying nothing: not a table, not a station, not a runner's tray. They attend the brief and do not speak at it, they stand in the step-back coverage position beside the mentor, they watch the pass, and they attend the close and watch the capture. The customer is never told a person is observing. One observed service for an edge seat; more where the seat sheet says so.

The paired services. The person works the section or station with the mentor or the seat holder beside them, carrying what the mentor hands them, on services the leads' review has not flagged short, placed by the stack with the mentor as a teaching body (S6 P31's rule for practicals, applied). The count is the seat sheet's. The person never carries a recovery, a generosity decision, or a David flag alone during a paired service; those are the seat holder's range until the solo (S5 P41's rule for candidates, applied through the paired step).

The readiness read. In the last paired service, or a separate one, an assessor who is not the mentor scores the seat's entry procedures on the practical's rows (S6 I4), the two people rows, the range stated, the language constant, and the felt-knowledge block, and submits the readiness form (I7). Section 6.5.

The solo. The first service the person holds a section or station without the mentor beside them, placed on the schedule by the domain lead only on a passed form, never a new period's first service, never dinner's first service (P10), never a service the leads' review flagged short. The mentor is in the building, on their own section, and reachable. The four-week load read starts here.

**P18 (Recommended).** Operational onboarding uses the interviewer training's sequence, modules read, a live session, an observed service, paired services, then solo, because it is the sequence canon's procedure-to-judgment requirement produces and the record's sink-or-swim paragraph describes by its absence. Two differences: the paired count is the seat sheet's, and the solo is gated by an assessor's readiness form.
