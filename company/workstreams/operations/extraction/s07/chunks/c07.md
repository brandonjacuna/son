### 14.3 The manager transitions guide

Her guide is for "a report ... transitioning to another team," three steps: the current manager previews, schedules a transition meeting in which the employee shares scope and strengths and the two managers discuss "past performance and development conversations we've had," and the new manager follows up (Book p.252 to 253; Workbook p.78).

At Sŏn a person's domain lead changes in two cases: a lead seat changes hands, or the person moves domain through the internal loop (S6 P69) and the domain plan (P30). In both, the hand-off is the person page, and the meeting is refused. The reasons are the page's own rules. The person page is written only at the check-in, read back, and readable by the person at any time (S4 P18), so there is nothing a former lead knows about the person that the person has not heard and that the page does not hold; her meeting exists to transfer a manager's private account, and Sŏn's structure holds no private account. The new lead reads the person page before the person's next check-in, and at that check-in the person and the new lead read it together, with the person saying what on it they want carried forward and what has changed; the line written is the person's. Nothing is passed between the two leads about the person outside the page, and a former lead's "additional info that I think might help" (Book p.252) is refused in exactly those words. The one thing her guide gets right for Sŏn is the date: the stack records the day the check-in's holder changes, so that the person knows who holds their next check-in before it arrives. Where the transition is a lead's departure or replacement, the mechanics beyond the person page are Session 15's, and the carryover says so.

**P51 (Recommended).** A change of domain lead is carried by the person page, not by a transition meeting: the new lead reads the page before the person's next check-in, the person and the new lead read it together at that check-in, and nothing about the person passes between leads outside it. The stack records the day the holder changes. Lead-succession mechanics beyond the page are Session 15's.

### 14.4 Internal mobility, and her survey

Her survey at every team change asks who initiated the move, whether it develops or redirects, whether the managers spoke, satisfaction on a scale, and whether the move allows greater impact (Book p.217). At Sŏn an internal move is one of three things and each has its record: an unlock, which the invisible advocate scheduled and the person page holds (WP p.11); a seat filled from the ready-now pool through the internal loop, whose record is I15 of Session 6 (S6 P69, P71); or a period move through the period plan (P30). None is initiated by a manager, because "No one's advancement waits on a manager's attention or judgment" (WP p.11), so her first question has one answer. Her "running away from something" signal is the check-in's upward question and dimension 4 read on the building, which exist for every person every month and not only at a move. Her satisfaction scale is refused as a rating on a person page (S4 P18) and as a rating anywhere; the employee NPS is the house's one scaled question (WP p.22). The survey is refused, and the internal promotion rate the record commits (WP p.23) is read from the internal loop record (S6 P71).

**P52 (Recommended).** The internal mobility survey is refused. An internal move is an unlock, a pool hire, or a period move, each with its record; the "running away" signal is the check-in's upward question and dimension 4, monthly for everyone; the internal promotion rate is read from the internal loop record.

## 16. Decision rights for onboarding

Every entry is in the ledger's form: owner; range stated as may and may not; triggers, each naming a destination. An entry the record cannot furnish an owner for is marked incomplete and named as an open question, never filled by analogy (S3 4.1). Session 5's entry for committing a start date (S5 12.6) and Session 4's for the check-in (S4 P20) are cross-referenced, not repeated; the entries below are the ones onboarding adds.

### 16.1 Naming a mentor

Owner. The domain lead of the seat, at the offer; pre-opening, for the first wave, the founders under P8.

Range. The lead may name any person on the strand's pool in the domain who has said yes, matching for language and overlapping shifts. The lead may not name themselves; may not name the assessor who will run the readiness read; may not name a person not on the pool; may not name a person with an open plan already; may not name for temperament or for who would get along; may not name a person whose page carries a non-negotiable line.

Triggers. No eligible person on the pool goes to the leads' review as a training-infrastructure load and the start date is not set (S5 12.6). A mentor stepping back goes to the lead, who names another with no line on either page. A mentor found to have written on a person page or given a verdict goes to the Maitre d as a team-health entry and returns the mentor to the mentor module before mentoring again.

Complete for outward-facing floor seats and beverage; incomplete for runner and host seats until their domain lead is named (S5 P64; `86akh7r10`); kitchen seats chef-gated.

### 16.2 Placing the first solo

Owner. The domain lead of the seat.

Range. The lead may place the first solo on the schedule on the day the readiness form reads ready or any day after, on a service the leads' review has not flagged, with the mentor in the building, never as sole coverage for the section or station. The lead may not place a solo without a passed form; may not run the readiness read; may not pass a person on their own read; may not place a solo on a new period's first service or on dinner's first service (P10); may not place a solo as coverage for a short night.

Triggers. A solo placed without a form, which the scheduling surface should refuse and which row 79 checks, goes to the Operations Lead's record as a system failure and to the leads' review. A form reading not yet twice on the same rows goes to the leads' review as a training-infrastructure line. A first solo on a flagged service goes to the leads' review as a slack reading.

Complete for floor and beverage; kitchen chef-gated.

### 16.3 Running the readiness read

Owner. The assessor, a trained person from the seat's strand who is not the mentor and did not run the paired services; the Maitre d for the standard and the assessor pool; kitchen assessors the chef partner's.

Range. The assessor may score the seat's entry rows, the people rows, the language row, the range row, and the felt-knowledge row, in the block, and recommend ready or not yet with rows named. The assessor may not score presence, speed, nunchi, fit, or how the section felt; may not score from memory after the block; may not score a person they mentored; may not score load on a training-service read; may not pass a felt-knowledge row before the tastings; may not carry the form anywhere but the person page.

Triggers. A form with a composite, a verdict, or a refused row goes to the Maitre d and returns the assessor to the assessor module. A form on a person the assessor mentored is void and goes to the Operations Lead's record. A not-yet form goes to the person at the next check-in with its rows.

Complete for floor and beverage; kitchen chef-gated.

### 16.4 Authoring and approving a module

Owner. The author, any team member holding the validated competency the module teaches; the approver, the domain lead for the domain, the chef partner for the kitchen, the Operations Lead with counsel for compliance and food-safety content, and canon's owner for content carrying brand facts, the arrival line, the register, or the uniform.
