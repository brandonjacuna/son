## 18. Instrument specifications

Specifications, never instruments. Nothing here is built, and no session builds any of it until the document methodology exists (`86ajgn2z5`). Each specification states what the instrument is, whether Sŏn uses it, what it must contain, what it must do, what it must refuse to do, where it lives and whether that is configuration, a build, or to confirm, who owns it and on what revision rhythm, and what it is gated on. Numbering is this page's own, I1 onward; earlier sessions' instruments are cited by session and number. The instruments come in the order a person meets them: what arrives, the plan block, the first shift, the range card, the modules and their standard, the mentor, the readiness form, the load read, the felt-knowledge block and the tasting record, the check-in reads, the onboarding read, the lead's plan and pre-reads, the lead's document, the period and domain plans, the infrastructure line. The instruments her material proposes that Sŏn refuses come last, each with what does its work.

Whether Sŏn uses it: yes; it is the record's "information is delivered, expectations are set" (WP p.18) as one document. Must contain: the start date and the first shift's hours as a paid orientation block with no table or station; where to arrive and the first hour; the mentor's name and seat; the seat sheet, current version; the transparency sheet, current version; the range card (I4) marked as to be read together; the first two weeks' schedule with the check-in visible; a link to the seat's first modules, marked visible and not assigned; the person page; the languages the house speaks; the line that nothing in it is required before the first shift; and a line naming the non-negotiables as the standard until a handbook exists. Must do: be generated by the stack from the sheet and the person page on acceptance; go in the person's chosen language in the correspondence register; match the offer in every term; carry no house item hidden in a legal task. Must refuse: "welcome," "enjoy," "excited," an exclamation point, a founder's story, a line naming its own effect; a module assigned or due; a figure; canon's forbidden word for the customer; a promise about a service period without a ratified charter; any term not on the sheet.
