### 6.5 The first solo, and the readiness read

"Competency validation: no one touches a table until they are actually ready" (WP p.19) is the record's one gate inside onboarding and the record does not say who reads readiness, against what, or in what form. Two accounts of the advancement gate sit in the record, the system auto-scheduling a practical assessment and the person scheduling a competency conversation with a lead who signs off (S6 18.2), and this page does not resolve them. For the first solo it does not need to: the readiness read is not an advancement and it is not the first unlock. It is the entry horizon of field 6 read once, at the end of the paired services, by a person trained to read it.

The readiness read. In a paired service the leads' review has not flagged short, an assessor from the seat's strand, trained under the assessor module (S6 P37, module 9), placed on the schedule as an extra body for the block, who is not the person's mentor and did not run any of the person's paired services, scores: the seat's entry procedures on the practical's rows, reproduced, reproduced with correction and the count, or not reproduced (S6 I4); the two universal rows, asked before guessing and cleaned as they went; the three people rows, with an evidence line and a non-negotiables mark; the language row, every sentence heard to a customer and to a colleague, with "customer" held and no forbidden word; the range row, the person asked, off the floor before or after the block, to state their range and its triggers unprompted, with the answer recorded in the person's words; and the felt-knowledge row (section 9), for the dishes that exist, in the chef's or the Head of Beverage's form. The form carries the block's start and end, the mentor's name, the assessor's name and training record, and the assessor's recommendation: ready, or not yet with the rows named. No composite, no score, no verdict, no row for presence, speed, nunchi, or how the section felt about the person (S6 P29 applied). The form goes to the person page as a milestone with its rows, which the person reads, and it is the one form on the person page, because it is a milestone and not a scorecard: it carries no dimension marks and no recommendation beyond ready or not yet, and Session 13 does not score against it.

Not yet. A "not yet" names the rows and the rows are what the platform teaches; the person takes the module for each row again and one more paired service, the assessor reads again, and nothing about it is on the person page but the rows and the next read's date. A second "not yet" on the same rows is the training-infrastructure line at the leads' review (S5 12.6's trigger), read first as a question about the module, the mentor's load, and the paired count, and only after that as anything else, which is blame the process (WP p.17) applied where the record's own training paragraph says the industry fails. What a third "not yet" concludes is not this page's; the plan may extend once (section 7.6), and past that the question is Session 14's (section 13).

The lead reads the form and places the solo. The domain lead may not place a solo without a passed form, may not pass a person on their own read, and may not run the readiness read themselves, because the lead decides on the packet and the lead holding the person's check-in cannot also be the one who read them, for the same reason the assessor and the decider are separate at hiring (S5 P41). The Realist's condition on the solo: it is placed on a service the leads' review has not flagged, with the mentor in the building, and never as the only coverage for the section.

Pre-opening the readiness read runs in a training service and reads procedure without load, and the form says so, exactly as the cohort's practicals do (S5 P42). The first four dinner weeks are the load read (S6 P31), and a cohort member whose load lines in those weeks do not hold is a training and slack question, never a readiness failure, because the form said in writing that it did not read load.

**P23 (Recommended).** The first solo is gated on a readiness form scored in a paired service by a trained assessor who is not the mentor and did not run the paired services, on the practical's rows plus the language row, the range row, and the felt-knowledge row, with a recommendation of ready or not yet and the rows named; the form is a milestone on the person page and not a scorecard. ### 6.6 Service-ready, defined once

The phrase appears in the session task and nowhere in the record, and this page uses it only in the sense defined here. A person is service-ready in a seat when their readiness form reads ready: the seat sheet's entry procedures reproduced in a paired service, the people rows held, the language constant held, the range and triggers stated unprompted, and the felt-knowledge block passed for the dishes that exist. Service-ready is per seat and per period; a person service-ready at dinner is not service-ready at the early-morning window until that period's plan (section 7.7) has run. Service-ready is the entry horizon of field 6 and nothing past it: not the first unlock, not cross-strand fluency, not nunchi, not "trained," in Crenn's sense that "it's not like, 'Now you're trained'" (Book p.216). The word the record uses is "ready," and this page's definition is the record's word with a form behind it.

**P24 (Recommended).** Service-ready means a readiness form reading ready in the seat, for the period, at the entry horizon of field 6, and nothing past it. It is the only sense in which the phrase is used in this operating system.

## 7. The ninety-day plan

### 7.1 What it is

"A ninety-day plan sets expectations and goals with regular check-ins and course correction, so early success is planned rather than hoped for" (WP p.18). Session 4 placed the check-ins (every two weeks, six across ninety days, S4 P19) and said a person's first goal is the plan's (S4 P42). Session 5 said the cohort is onboarded against the first unlock (S5 P60). Carryover `86akh67ny` asks this page to design the plan's content so its six check-ins carry it, and to say what its expectations and goals look like on the person page.

The plan is not a document a lead writes for a person. It is the plan block on the person page (section 7.2), written by the stack from the seat sheet at acceptance, with milestones as states, one goal, and six check-in reads. It is the same for every person in the same seat, because the sheet is the same, and it varies for a person only in the dates the stack fills, the fourth-read baseline the person supplied, and the check-in lines. Her manager's "special one- to two-hour session" to translate the operating system to the team level (Book p.216) has no object: the seat sheet, the range card, and the person page are the translation, and they existed before the person did.

The plan runs from the start date for ninety days and is not shortened by an early solo or lengthened by a late one, except by the one extension in section 7.6. Her 30/60/90 for a knowledge worker, with its deliverables at each mark, is refused as a form: a cook's first thirty days do not produce a deliverable, they produce a station held, and the marks are the seat's milestones and not the calendar's.

### 7.2 Its form on the person page

Beneath the ten entries, the plan block holds:

The goal. One sentence, as a state, per Session 4's rule (S4 P40, P41): the person holds the seat solo on the schedule, with the range stated, by the plan's close. Its measure is binary: the readiness form reads ready and a solo service has run. For a lead, the goal is the domain running on the cadence (P21). No attainment figure, no baseline, no target on a rate (ledger).

The milestones, each a state with a date or "not yet," and each with who writes it:
1. Orientation block held: written by the stack from the schedule.
2. The seat's first modules complete: written by the stack from Trainual's record.
3. The live session held: written by the stack from the schedule, with the mentor's name.
4. The observed service held: from the schedule.
5. The paired services at the sheet's count held: from the schedule, one line per service.
6. The felt-knowledge block passed: from the readiness form, or "not yet: dishes not tasted" where the menu has not reached the cohort.
7. Readiness form: ready, with the date; or not yet with the rows.
8. First solo held: from the schedule.
9. Four-week load read: four lines, one per week, written by the lead at the check-in.
10. The range used: one line, written by the lead at the fourth check-in, from the person's account.
11. The platform opened beyond the first set: written by the stack from Trainual.
12. The path read: the first unlock's date from the sheet and the seat after theirs named, written by the lead at the fifth check-in.
13. The plan closed: written by the lead at the sixth check-in, with the goal's state.

The fourth-read baseline. What the person said they need from the building, in their words, from the ten entries, and beneath it one line per check-in on what the building supplied, written by the lead from the person's account.

The six check-in dates, placed by the stack.

What the block refuses: a scorecard field or a dimension mark (S6 row 71); a rating; a free-text assessment of the person; a milestone written by the mentor; a milestone written by the lead outside a check-in (S4 P20); a milestone about nunchi, Jeong, presence, or fit; a wage or a rate; a "probation" label or any state that implies one.

**P25 (Recommended).** The plan block on the person page carries one goal as a state with a binary measure, thirteen milestones as states with dates written by the stack where it can see them and by the lead at check-ins where it cannot, the fourth-read baseline with one supplied line per check-in, and the six check-in dates; it refuses scorecard fields, ratings, free-text assessment, mentor writing, out-of-check-in writing, and any probation label.

### 7.4 The six check-ins, check-in by check-in

The check-in's spine is fixed and this page does not touch it: what is working, what is in the way, what changed, the upward question, one line written and read back, the next date (S4 P15). What this page adds is what each of the plan's six check-ins reads inside "what changed," so that the plan is carried by the check-ins and not by a lead's memory. The lead reads the plan block before the check-in and the block tells the lead which read this one is.

Check-in 1, week two. The fourth-read baseline read against what arrived: the person said they needed these things; here is what the house supplied, and what it did not. This is dimension 4 read on the building for the first time (S6 P33), and the line written is the first supplied line. Milestones 1 to 4 confirmed as the stack shows them. The person's language in training confirmed as the one they chose.

Check-in 2, week four. The paired services so far, in the person's account: what the mentor hands them and what they carry; what in the section or station is not what the module said. That last is an authoring line: a module the floor contradicts goes to the module's author through the feedback channel, with the person's knowledge, and the platform learns (WP p.19, "constantly expanding"). The readiness read's date, if placed, confirmed. The felt-knowledge block's state.

Check-in 3, week six. The readiness form read together, rows and all, whichever way it read. If ready, the first solo's date and the load read's start. If not yet, the rows, the modules to retake, the next read's date, and the infrastructure line if it is a second not yet. This is the check-in her section's manager would have used to deliver a verdict, and it delivers the rows.

Check-in 4, week eight. The load read's first lines. The range: has the person used it, on what, and did they escalate on a trigger; the line written is the range-used milestone in the person's words. This is where row 4 is checked for the person, unprompted, a second time (section 12). Anything in the four solo weeks that the person met and the modules did not cover.

Check-in 5, week ten. The path. The first unlock's date from the sheet, opened on the person's own account; the seat after theirs, named by them; what they want to learn next, including another domain's track (section 10); and the mentor relationship's shape after the plan, which is the person's to say. The line written is the path milestone.

Check-in 6, week twelve or thirteen. The plan closed: the goal's state, held or not held; the supplied lines read as a set, which is the person's read of whether the building kept its word; the next check-in's interval moving to monthly (S4 P19); and the ninety-day calibration read noted as something the Maitre d will do with the packet and the page side by side, writing to neither (S6 P41), which the person is told and which is not about them. If the goal is not held, section 7.6.

What no check-in in the plan carries: a verdict on the plan, a pay conversation, a probation decision, a mention of any interviewer's scorecard, or a milestone about the person's manner.

**P27 (Recommended).** The six check-ins inside the plan carry it: week two the fourth-read baseline against what arrived; week four the paired services and the module-versus-floor line; week six the readiness form read together; week eight the load read and the range used; week ten the path and the first unlock; week twelve the plan's close and the move to monthly. ### 7.5 What the plan may not conclude

Section 2.3 holds the list and it is restated here in the plan's own terms because the plan is the place a house is most tempted. The plan's close is a state, held or not held, and a set of supplied lines. It is not a review; the review every three to six months (WP p.19) is the review, and it opens on the person page and contains nothing new (S4 P18). It is not a probation ending, because the plan has no probation state (P25) and the record has no such word. It is not a hiring verdict; the ninety-day calibration read reads the interviewer, not the person (S6 P41). And it is not a reading of whether the person stays. Where a plan whose goal is not held is read, and what is fixed, is Session 14's (S6 18.6; section 13), and this page hands it there with what onboarding has recorded.

**P28 (Recommended).** The plan's close is a state and a set of supplied lines; it is not a review, a probation ending, a hiring verdict, or a reading of whether the person stays. A plan whose goal is not held is handed to Session 14 with its record.

### 7.6 Extension

The plan may be extended once, by the domain lead with the person at a check-in, by a stated interval to a stated date, when a milestone was blocked by the building: a module missing in the person's language, a mentor absent or overloaded, paired services that ran short or were cancelled, a felt-knowledge block that could not be passed because the dishes had not been tasted, a readiness read the schedule could not place. The extension names the building's fix beside the new date, and it goes to the leads' review as a training-infrastructure line. The plan may not be extended for a person's not holding without a building fix named, because that is a person-level move dressed as a plan change; may not be shortened; may not be extended twice by the lead, and a second extension is the partners' review's, as a node-overload or infrastructure entry (WP p.14). A plan that ends with its goal not held, after one extension, is Session 14's question and not this page's.

**P29 (Recommended).** The plan extends once, by the lead with the person, to a stated date, for a building-caused block with the fix named, as a leads' review line; never for a person's not holding without a fix, never shortened, never twice by the lead.

### 7.7 The period plan and the domain plan

Two shorter plans for a person already in the house. The period plan, when a period is added by ratified charter and a person takes a seat in it: the period's module set, one live session on the period's register and procedures, one observed service in the period, paired services at the count the period's sheet states, a readiness read in the period, and the first solo, with no new ninety-day plan, no new mentor unless the person asks, and the check-ins on their existing monthly interval with the period's milestones read inside them. Cross-period fluency is recorded on the person page when the readiness read passes (S5 P62). The domain plan, when a person moves domain through the internal loop (S6 P69): the receiving domain's module set and sequence, the receiving domain's mentor, and a readiness read on that domain's rows, with the plan's length the receiving sheet's and the check-ins moving to that domain's lead. Neither plan re-runs orientation's why block, and neither carries a fourth-read baseline unless the person offers a new one at the first check-in.

**P30 (Recommended).** A period added by charter runs a period plan (module set, live session, observed, paired, readiness read, solo) inside the person's existing check-in interval with no new ninety-day plan; a domain move runs a domain plan on the receiving domain's sequence with its lead and mentor. Neither repeats the why block.

## 8. Training infrastructure and the learning platform

### 8.1 The record, and what it leaves to build

The record commits a platform "built like the content the team actually uses, engaging and personalized and constantly expanding," authored by "Any qualified team member" who is paid for it, feeding the progression structure, with "competency validation" as its gate (WP p.19). It does not say what a module is, who approves one, in what languages it exists, what canon requires of its every word, how the platform records completion so the invisible advocate can read it, or what stands in for it before it exists. The industry it is written against is named exactly: "Shadow someone for two shifts, catch fragments because they are busy" (WP p.19), which is training held in people's memory, which is memory failure (WP p.13).

### 8.2 The bought stack, tool by tool

Trainual is the learning platform's first form and the interim before any built surface. Configuration: one path per seat, generated from the seat sheet's field 6 entry horizon and item 5 of the transparency sheet, holding the seat's first module set in order; the modules in every language the seat's field 14 names; completion recorded per module per person; the assessor module and the mentor module as paths of their own; the interviewer training modules (S6 I5) as paths on the same platform once it exists, which Session 6 said they would be. A build: the completion record copied to the person page as milestone 2 and milestone 11, and to the invisible advocate's read of dimension 2 (S6 P33), because the platform's record is what the system reads instead of a lead's impression (WP p.11). To confirm: whether Trainual's completion record can be read by the stack without a person re-entering it, which is the record's "No manual resume re-entry" (WP p.17) applied to training; whether paths can be versioned so that a module revised by versioned decision shows the version each person completed; whether a person's language can be set per account so that the path renders in it; and whether authoring by a team member can be held for approval before publication (section 8.3). If any of the four cannot be confirmed, the gap is a build or the interim is manual on the team home, and the interim is recorded on the partners' page rather than absorbed by a lead.
