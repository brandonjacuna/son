### 11.3 Paid how, carrying what, refusing what

Paid. As shift. Every hour a mentor spends beside the person is a scheduled hour, placed by the stack as a teaching body on the paired services and the live session (S6 P31's rule), and the record's rule that "If it matters, Sŏn pays for it. Employees should not subsidize it" (WP p.19) closes any version in which mentoring is an unpaid favor. Whether mentoring is recognized beyond shift, as teaching in the record's paid-teaching sense (WP p.19), is Session 13's (P35), and this page states no figure and files the coupling. What the mentor is not paid for: pre-arrival contact beyond the one message on shift (P13), and nothing else is asked.

Carrying. The mentor carries the house's ways: what the module could not say about how this section, this pass, this well actually runs; the live session; the person's questions, in the first week before the first check-in and after; the paired services as the teaching body; the standard, by being the person the new person watches most, which is the record's "makes the early experience reflect the standard" (WP p.18); the arrival line and the language constant by example; and the feedback channel's use, by showing the person where "what is in the way" goes between check-ins (WP p.22). The mentor is the second of the ledger's three communication channels, "carried by the domain lead or mentor" (S5 P34, item 4), and is the person a new hire asks before they ask the lead.

Refusing. The mentor is not the assessor and never scores the readiness read (P23). The mentor does not hold the check-in and writes nothing on the person page, ever; anything the mentor thinks the lead should know is said to the person first and then, if the person agrees, goes to the check-in in the person's own words, because a person page that accumulates a mentor's notes is her shared 1:1 document with the sharing removed (S4 P18). The mentor gives no verdict on the person to anyone, and a lead who asks a mentor "how are they doing" is asking for the impression the system was built to refuse; the lead reads the person page. The mentor does not assign, schedule, or discipline. The mentor does not deliver the why block, the range card, or the legal onboarding. The mentor does not stand between the person and their lead: the new person's check-in is theirs and the mentor is not in the room. The mentor never sees the packet (P13). The mentor is not a supervisor, a trainer with a rubric, or a buddy with a checklist, and the mentor module teaches the refusals before it teaches anything else.

Duration. Through the plan, and after it as the person chooses. At the fifth check-in the person says what shape they want the relationship to have (section 7.4), and the stack stops placing the overlap at the plan's close. A mentor may step back before the plan closes by telling the lead, and the lead names another from the pool without a line on either person's page.

**P42 (Recommended).** The mentor is paid as shift, placed by the stack as a teaching body, with any recognition beyond shift Session 13's. The mentor carries the house's ways, the live session, the first week's questions, the paired services, the standard by example, and the feedback channel's use. The mentor never scores, never writes on the person page, gives no verdict to anyone, does not assign or discipline, does not deliver the why block or the range card, is not in the check-in, and never sees the packet. The relationship runs through the plan and after it as the person chooses.

## 12. What onboarding delivers of the range, the triggers, the web, and the non-negotiables

### 12.1 Row 4 as an onboarding output

Session 3's readiness row 4 reads: "Every server and floor manager can state their range and their escalation triggers unprompted," checked by asking three of each, and Session 3 named a failed row 4 "a range that was written and not delivered, which is an onboarding failure routed to Session 7" (S3 12.3). Carryover `86akh5ut1` fixes that "direct lead" means domain lead and that the range travels with the offer; the offer states it aloud (S6 P57). Delivery is this page's, and it has five moments.

At the offer: the range read aloud "as what the holder decides without asking" (S6 P57), from the transparency sheet's item 8 and the seat sheet's field 10, and the candidate has held the sheet since the first interview.

On the first shift: the range card (I4) read aloud together at orientation, the range first and the triggers after, each trigger with its named destination, and the person saying it back in their own words. The card is the sheet's field 10 rendered for one person, and it carries, for a server, the record's own content: the server "owns the table relationship and resolves it using their authorized judgment and generosity range" (WP p.13); "Every server carries a bottle to gift as they see fit" (WP p.26); routine recovery "pre-authorized inside known limits" (Canon v3.0, p.79); and the three escalation triggers, "financial recovery above threshold / kitchen failure affecting multiple sections / genuine emergency" (Canon v1.0, section 08), each with the destination Session 3 supplied as a recommendation (S3 4.2, instance 3). The card states in words what is unset: the threshold's value and the known limits' content are set with Dominic's domain and are not on the card until they are (S3 4.2), so a server pre-opening is taught a range whose one number is missing and is told that it is missing, rather than taught a number this program invented.

In the modules: one module on the range, with two written worked examples, a recovery inside the range and a trigger met and escalated to its destination, and the record's own worked example of the incident, the shift close, and the next server inheriting the fix (WP p.13). The examples are written from the sheet and never from a real incident with a customer in it (S6 P42's rule applied).

At the paired services: the person watches the seat holder use the range and, where the holder hands it to them, uses it with the holder beside them; a paired service in which the range is never used is a paired service on a night where nothing needed recovering, and the count on the sheet allows for that.

At the readiness read and the fourth check-in: the range row on the form, stated unprompted off the floor (P23); and at week eight, the range used, in the person's account (P27). Row 4's check, "ask three of each; all six answer without looking" (S3 row 4), is thereby passed by every person who passed the readiness read, and a failed row 4 after this page points at one of the five moments.

What the range delivery refuses: teaching the range as permission a lead grants on the night; teaching under-use as safe, when the record names under-use as the failure ("a line item to be fully used, not minimized," WP p.26); teaching a trigger without its destination; teaching a number.

**P43 (Recommended).** Row 4 becomes an onboarding output through five moments: the range aloud at the offer, the range card read together and said back at orientation, one module with two written examples, the range watched and handed at paired services, and the range stated unprompted at the readiness read and read as used at the fourth check-in. The card states the unset threshold and limits as unset. Every seat with a range, not only servers and floor managers, runs the five moments.

### 12.2 The web, delivered

The person enters at the outside edge (WP p.9) and is told so as the most sensitive position and not the bottom. The web is drawn at orientation from the person's own node outward, exactly as item 1 of the transparency sheet already rendered it (S5 P32): their node, the adjacent nodes, their domain lead, the two leads, and the center, with no line running upward. The person's own inverted web, "the employee is at the center of their own knowledge map" (WP p.11), is drawn beside it on their person page as the tracks, so that the two pictures the record holds, the house's web with the person at the edge and the person's web with them at the center, are both taught and neither is mistaken for the other (section 19, finding 19.9). The web is delivered again, without a drawing, every time the person is told who decides something: the module for each procedure names the owner of the range it touches, and "escalate" is never the destination (S3 4.1).

**P44 (Recommended).** The web is delivered at orientation as two drawings, the house's web from the person's node outward and the person's own tracks from their center outward, and again inside every module that names an owner. No drawing shows a line above the person's seat.

### 12.3 The non-negotiables, delivered twice

The first delivery is the why block (P17), as the house's commitments to the person. The second is at the end of the first shift, as what the person may raise and where: any person may raise a non-negotiable concern with evidence, at any time, to the Maitre d and the Operations Lead together (S4 P20's trigger), through the feedback channel or in person, and a concern raised is never a line on the raiser's person page. What follows a raised concern is Session 14 and 15's to write (S4 P20), and this page says so to the person in those words rather than pretending a mechanism exists. The language constant is taught as part of the standard: the customer is the customer in every sentence, "Internal communication included" (Canon v3.0, p.5), with the record's reason (WP p.25), and the readiness read's language row holds it.

**P45 (Recommended).** The non-negotiables are delivered twice: as commitments in the why block and, at the end of the first shift, as what the person may raise, with evidence, to the Maitre d and the Operations Lead together, never as a line on their own page. What follows is Sessions 14 and 15's and the person is told so.

## 13. Her hiring mistakes, answered and routed

### 13.1 Her section, and the seam it sits on

"For most hiring mistakes, you won't realize the error until a few months into the person's tenure. In that case, it boils down to a performance issue" (Book p.224). Some surface "right away, sometimes even before the person's start date," and her four signals are confidential information posted, a misrepresented background, poor treatment of people before the start date or during onboarding, and "arrogance and bad behavior toward their new team members in the first week" (Book p.224). Her rule: "The main thing is to do something" (Book p.224). Her loop: a retrospective, and data (Book p.225 to 226).

The section sits on three seams. Session 6 found that the record's "blame the process" has no hiring object and routed where a hire that does not hold is read to Session 14 (S6 18.6). Managing out is Session 15's. Low performers are Session 14's. This page writes only what onboarding owns and routes the rest by carryover; it does not design managing out, a probation, or a termination.

### 13.2 What the ninety-day plan reads as a hire not holding

A state, never a verdict. The plan reads: a milestone not reached by its expected date and by which link (modules incomplete, live session not held, paired services short, a not-yet form with rows named, a solo not placed, load lines not holding, the range unused); a supplied line that reads "not supplied" against something the person said they needed; and an infrastructure line at the leads' review. That is the whole of what the plan reads, and it reads it as the record's own sequence: "when something goes wrong, the first question is not who failed. It is what allowed this to happen" (WP p.17).

### 13.3 What the house fixes before it reads a person

In the building, in order, before any question about the person: the module (was it in the person's language, did the floor contradict it, is it version one); the mentor's load (two open plans, an overlapping schedule that did not overlap); the paired count (was it the sheet's, did the services run short); the readiness read (did it run in a flagged service, was the assessor also the mentor by error); the schedule (was the solo placed as sole coverage, on a flagged night); the felt-knowledge sequence (had the dishes been tasted); and the building's supply against the fourth-read baseline (the locker, the uniform, the language, the shift shape the person said they needed). Each of these is a training-infrastructure line and is fixed by versioned decision where it is a document and by the schedule where it is a placement. This is her hiring manager's retrospective (Book p.225) with its object moved from the manager's process to the house's, and with one addition her section does not have: the fourth-read baseline gives the house a written statement, in the person's own words, of what they said they would need, so the house's first question has a document to be asked against.

Only after that, the hiring system: the ninety-day calibration read opens the packet beside the page and reads whether the marks corresponded (S6 P41), and a hire who did not hold under load in the first four dinner weeks "is first a question about the practical's rows and the training plan, and only after that a question about the interviewer" (S6 P41). Onboarding has no view into the packet and asks for none.

### 13.4 What the plan may not conclude, and what is routed

The plan may not conclude that the person should leave, that the hire was a mistake, that the plan should be shortened, or that a probation has failed, because none of those is a state the plan holds. The plan may extend once for a building fix (P29). Past that, a plan whose goal is not held, with its record, is handed to Session 14, which owns where a hire that does not hold is read and what is fixed, and Session 15, which owns managing out. The carryovers say what onboarding hands over: the plan block whole, the infrastructure lines, the supplied lines, and the record that the building's fixes were made first.

Her immediate cases. A non-negotiable breach between acceptance and the first shift: the person is in Greenhouse's accepted state, and the non-negotiable flag with evidence, raisable by anyone, ending a candidacy, overridable by no one (S6 P51; S5 12.3), is the mechanism, applied as it is at any stage, with the Operations Lead and counsel holding the employment form of an accepted offer withdrawn, which this page does not write. A non-negotiable breach in the first week: the same flag, raised to the Maitre d and the Operations Lead together (P45), and what follows is Session 14 and 15's. Her "consistently late to meetings" (Book p.224), the minor and feedback-worthy case: at Sŏn a person late to a paired service is a schedule fact the stack holds and a line at the second check-in in the person's own account; it is not "feedback quickly and strongly" from a manager, it is a question about what is in the way, and only a pattern the check-ins have recorded and the person has heard becomes anything else. Her "there's no need to overexplain things internally; a short note to their team" (Book p.225): the brief carries no line about a person, and the close carries nothing about a person (S4 P12); a departure is a seat opened by tension loss on the sheet with what the node held (S5 P14), and nothing more is said to the floor.

**P46 (Recommended).** The plan reads a hire not holding as a state: a milestone not reached and by which link, a supplied line reading not supplied, and an infrastructure line. Before any question about the person, the house fixes the module, the mentor's load, the paired count, the readiness read's conditions, the schedule, the felt-knowledge sequence, and the building's supply against the fourth-read baseline, then the calibration read reads the hiring system. The plan may not conclude departure, mistake, or a failed probation; a plan whose goal is not held after one extension is handed to Sessions 14 and 15 with its record.

**P47 (Recommended).** A non-negotiable breach between acceptance and the first shift is the flag rule as at any stage, with the employment form of a withdrawn acceptance the Operations Lead's with counsel; a breach in the first week is the flag to both leads with what follows Session 14 and 15's; a minor pattern is a check-in line in the person's account and never a manager's "feedback quickly and strongly"; nothing about a person is said to the floor.

### 13.5 Her closed loop, placed

Her ATS data, time in process, funnel, interviewer stats: the pipeline read (S5 I22) and the calibration read (S6 I7) already carry the first two and the third. Her engagement prompts: the employee NPS is the record's (WP p.22) and Session 4 set its rhythm (S4 row 28); this page adds no question to it. Her pulse prompts about onboarding: the onboarding read (P36). Her milestone survey to managers: refused; the plan block is the record and a manager's survey is an impression. Her hiring-versus-performance synthesis and sentiment analysis: refused; the calibration read reads evidence lines against the person page side by side and writes to neither (S6 P41), and nothing mines interview evidence. **P48 (Recommended).** Her closed loop lands on mechanisms that exist: the pipeline read, the calibration read, the employee NPS, the onboarding read, and the candidate pulse.
