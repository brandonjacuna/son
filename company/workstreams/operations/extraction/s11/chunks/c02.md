**P8 (Recommended).** Managing through uncertainty is the live-risk line's daily written state, the crisis rule inward, and the transparency line: what happened, the real impact, the fix, and when there is no fix yet, that there is none yet. Hoffman's method is the existential-risk register's.

## 4. The communication architecture's premise

Five facts the architecture is built on, none of them her chapter's.

**Non-co-presence by hour.** The trigger is the hour, not headcount (S3 P11.1). Under any ratified set of periods the person who opened the day is not in the building when it closes, and the roster of one service does not meet the roster of the next. Every rule below is written so that no service period's team learns of a decision later, or differently, than another's.

**The roster dissolves at every close.** Her team persists between meetings; Sŏn's service roster exists from the brief to the close and is a different set tomorrow (S8 P6; S10 section 5). A spoken word at a brief reaches the people on that service and no one else. Nothing that must reach the house may exist only as something said.

**The house has languages.** The record lets a candidate "choose the interview language" and promises that someone "who speaks it" runs the interview (WP p.17); the seat sheet's field 14 names the languages the seat works in (S5 I2); the team home is read "in the languages the team speaks" (S3 P11.8). The list of those languages is a fact about the roster, and it is a constraint on every surface (section 8).

**No one sits at a desk or reads between services.** The writing culture is structured capture at the edge and short prose at the center (S3 P11.5). A server reads the team home on a phone in the pre-service window or off the clock by choice. Nothing on any surface may assume an hour at a laptop.

**Nothing is owed before the first paid hour, and nothing is owed off the clock.** "Information delivered before arrival is available, not owed" (S7 P15), the check-in is on the clock (S4 P15), and no gathering is attended unpaid (S10 row 121). Applied here: no message on a person's phone off shift requires an answer, no feedback prompt is due, no recognition is expected, no schedule change is effective by a message the person has not been on shift to read. Every surface is read on paid time, and what a surface may push to a personal device off shift is the schedule the person already agreed to and nothing that asks anything of them (section 6.10).

To these the Realist adds the floor condition: the architecture's floor half must survive a Friday at tempo run by the crew that is actually there, a server at the end of a double, and a thirty-second brief. The readiness block is "a checklist the rush cannot delete" (S4 P10). Every rule that reaches the floor is written in that light: it either sits in the pre-service window or the close, or it is on a record a person reads when they choose.

**P9 (Recommended).** The architecture's premise is non-co-presence by hour, a roster that dissolves at every close, a house that works in several languages, a team that does not read between services, and nothing owed before the first paid hour or off the clock. Every surface and every rule below is tested against those five and against a compressed Friday brief.

### 5.1 The hard rows, argued

**Recognition names a person; the environment names no person in a room.** The two rules meet at item 4 of the brief. The reconciliation is in what a recognition is and where it lives. Its destination is a record, the platform, which is the rule's requirement (S10 P14). What the brief does is read a record aloud, as it reads the readiness block; the room's owner is a reader and not a speaker, "not editorialized" (S4 P10). A recognition is a peer's own words about an act in a service, never the house's read of a person. The ledger's prohibition is on "a read or a correction of a person," and this page states the boundary as a rule the recognition platform enforces: a recognition names what a person did, in one service, and never what a person is; it carries no rating, no comparison to anyone, and no rank; and the room's owner declines to read one that is a correction dressed as praise, a read of a person's state, or a comparison. A recognition that fails those is not deleted; it is left on the platform unread and the peer is told why by the room's owner in person, off the floor. Read that way, item 4 is the one place the roster hears a person named, and it hears the person's peers, in their words, about an act. That is the record's reason for the platform: it "lands differently than management praise because it comes from the people who understand the work" (WP p.19). The finding underneath, that reading a peer's words in a lead's voice at a brief is itself a change of speaker, is section 20's.

**The structure change and the departure both use the brief's change line, and Session 3 called the departure an announcement.** The three positions (S3 P11.6, S7 P47, S8 P49) are consistent once Session 3's tension map is read as coverage, which Session 9 already did (S9 P30). This page narrows Session 3's wording once more and surfaces it: "announced" in S3 P11.6 and "in person before in writing" are read as the brief speaking coverage under its standing change line, which is spoken before the team home shows the inventory change only because the brief runs before the next roster reads. Nothing is said about the person, and the founders ratify the narrowing beside S3 P11.6 rather than this page overwriting it.

**The one thing carries no framing.** Her pass-down comes with the manager's frame, "I think the right way to think about this is..." (Book p.361). At Sŏn the one thing is one sentence, the same sentence at every brief that week, spoken by the room's owner (S4 P10). The reason travels with it as the evidence line's own words, verbatim, because the evidence line is the reason field of every change (S9 P24). A lead who adds a frame has put a person between the record and the roster, which is the relay the ledger's departure from her hierarchy refuses. The one exception her example does preserve is the record's: a founder speaking the why block in person (S7 P17), which is the founders' voice and not a lead's framing of it.

**The phone is a customer surface until it produces a message for the room.** Slang AI handles phones (the session task). What a customer says on the phone is customer-facing and is canon's; it enters this architecture only where it produces internal information. That information has three destinations and only three: the book on the identity spine, for a reservation, a change, a note for a table, or an allergy; the brief's readiness block, which reads the book for tonight (S4 P10); and the capture, for a complaint, which is an incident the system should know. The rule is the record's own: "the system is not surfacing preferences" is sequence corruption (WP p.14), so a phone-originated message that lives in whoever answered the phone and not on the book is a bus failure by definition. No phone message reaches the room by a person relaying it by voice alone; if a person takes it, they write it to the book before they say it. Section 6.9 holds what answers the phone against canon.

**Schedule changes and off-clock messages.** The stack posts the schedule and places the check-ins (S4 P15). A change to a shift reaches a person on a personal device off the clock because the record's own reason for the stack is that the person does not have to hold the schedule in their head. That is the one thing a surface may push to a person off shift, and it asks nothing of them: a change is effective for a shift only after the person has confirmed it where the surface carries a confirmation, or has been on the clock to read it. Everything else, a recognition prompt, a feedback prompt, a module, a message from a lead, waits for paid time or the person's own choice to look.

**P10 (Recommended).** The inventory as tabled is the house's one account of every speech act: each with an originator, a surface, a language rule, an hour, an audience, a record, a read-back where one exists, and refusals. Every speech act's destination is a record; the brief and the slot read records aloud and originate nothing but the designations' lines. The one thing carries no lead's framing; the reason travels as the evidence line's own words.

**P11 (Recommended).** A recognition is reconciled with the rule that the environment names no person in a room by what it is and where it lives: a peer's own words about an act in one service, on a record, read at the brief verbatim by the room's owner as reader; never a read of a person, a rating, a comparison, a rank, a correction, or a lead's praise. One that fails those is left unread and the peer told why in person. The narrowing of S3 P11.6's "announced" to coverage under the change line is surfaced for ratification beside it, not overwritten.
