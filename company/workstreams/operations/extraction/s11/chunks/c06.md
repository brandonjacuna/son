## 15. Sŏn's position on team composition, to the ceiling

### 15.1 What the record and canon support

The record says nothing about who the team should be, and it says several things about how the house treats whoever the team is. Those are the ceiling, and they are more than a silence. The house selects on four reads, competency for the strand at entry, growth potential as self-directed mastery, cultural alignment as the non-negotiables and comfort with distributed authority, and what the building must supply (WP p.18; S5 3.1), and on nothing else; the ledger fixes that no fifth read is added and nothing about who a person is enters a scorecard. It interviews in the candidate's language and answers every candidate (WP pp. 17 to 18). It treats a person's language as a fact it serves, not a qualification it screens, except where a seat's work requires a language (S5 P23). It refuses "fit," "attitude," and "culture fit" on every sheet (S5 I2). It measures the building against the person and not the person against a type (the fourth read). It treats every position as essential because "every role is essential because it carries information the structure depends on" (WP p.8), and "there is no hierarchy of importance" (WP p.20). Canon's non-negotiables, "No exploitation ever. No toxicity ever. No disposable people" (Deck 3.0, p.4), are conduct rules that reach every person the same way. Canon's positioning is a set of three qualities and "No quality leads the others" (Deck 3.0, p.3), and canon's one persona rule is that personas "calibrate emphasis. They do not constrain" (Canon 1.0, section 02).

None of that is a composition position. All of it supports a statement about the house's conduct toward composition, which the team drafts as the part it has standing to draft.

### 15.2 The statement the team drafts, and marks recommended

Sŏn states the following about the composition of its team, and it is a statement about what the house does and does not do, not about who the team should be:

The house selects every person on four reads and on nothing about who they are. Every stage of every hire runs in the language the candidate chose, by someone who holds it, and every posting states the languages the process runs in. The house records nothing about who a person is on any record about them beyond the languages they work in, and it reads no person by any attribute. The house reads its own reach: where every candidate for a seat comes from and which stage declines whom, as patterns and never as targets, and when its reach narrows it widens its reach and never lowers or raises its bar for anyone. The house sets no target for the composition of its team, and it states this so that no one is ever read against one.

Why the team can draft that and mark it recommended: every sentence is a rule the record or a prior session already holds, gathered and stated as a position. The last sentence is the one the record does not hold by name, and it follows from two the record does hold: the four reads and no fifth, and nothing about a person on any record about them. A target would require a read of who each person is, which the record's own rules forbid. So the record's mechanics entail the absence of a target, and the team states the entailment.

### 15.3 What the source-dominance read does once the statement exists

Session 5 recommended the read regardless of a position: source and stage data read at the leads' review for whether any single source dominates a seat's pipeline and whether any stage declines a group of candidates at a rate the others do not, with no target and no figure (S5 P26; readiness row 58). Session 6 kept it as a pattern read with no claim of significance at the house's volume (S6 8.2, P47). Without a position, the read could report and not act (`86akh7r2f`).

Under the statement in 15.2 the read acts, and it acts on the house's reach and its process, never on a candidate and never on a bar. Source dominance, one source supplying every candidate for a seat across a stated count of reviews (a reset parameter, set by the reset like the recurrence thresholds, S9 P17), triggers a change to the seat sheet's sourcing mix (S5 P27): a posting placed on the board in a language the process runs in but the posting did not reach, the board placed where the build's audience does not look, the house's pool read again, the lead's reach named as the dominant source and the other three activated. Stage rate, one stage declining candidates from one source or one chosen language at a rate the others do not, triggers the calibration read's existing acts: the interviewer note, the module and paired stage, an anchor revision proposed at the reset (S6 P44). Neither trigger touches a scorecard, a decision, or a candidate's outcome, and neither is a target: the read asks whether the house's reach is narrow, and the answer is an act on the reach. What the read may not do: infer who a candidate is from a name, a language, an address, or a source; record any such inference; or slow a seat for the pipeline's shape. The read's record is the leads' review's line and the calibration read's monthly record, anonymized (S6 P44), and it names no candidate.

### 15.4 What the house may and may not measure, record, or act on

May: the source tag, the stage outcomes and on-sheet decline reasons, the interview language and format chosen, per seat and per stage, in aggregate, read as patterns (S5 I4, I22; S6 P44); the languages a seat works in, on the sheet; the languages a person chose, on the person page's field for it and nowhere else; the house's language list.

May not: record on any scorecard, sheet, person page, schedule, brief, close, designation block, or leads' review line anything about who a person is beyond the seat's language field; read a person's chosen language as anything but a fact to serve; ask a candidate or a team member about their origins, family, or background (S9 P44); infer any attribute from any record; select an interviewer, a mentor, an assessor, or a designation's holder for who they are; select for nunchi or Jeong (ledger); set a target for the composition of any seat, domain, roster, or the house; hold a seat open for a pipeline's shape.

### 15.6 What is founder-gated: whether the house states more

Her chapter's position is that a team should be diverse, and the record does not say so. Whether Sŏn states an aspiration about the composition of its team, in any words, is a values statement the founders make, and this page develops the case to the ceiling the record allows without making it.

The record's one structural argument for caring about composition is its own: the outward strand's intelligence is "relational and expressive and emotionally attuned under load" and "the customer feels this strand" (WP p.10); the edge is "the most sensitive position" and "feel[s] the customer, the room, and the cultural temperature of the building before anyone else" (WP p.9); the house draws "Different first customers at different times" (WP p.35) and reads its customers' "Demographic composition... at the door" (WP p.27). A team whose reach comes from three narrowing sources reads fewer rooms than one whose reach is wide. That is an argument about reach and reading, and it is already carried by 15.3's action on reach. The record's one structural argument against a stated aspiration is also its own: a stated composition goal needs a read of who each person is, and the record's people system refuses every such read.

So the ceiling is this: the house can state everything in 15.2 as fact about its conduct, and it can act on its reach. Whether it says anything about who it hopes its team will be is the founders'. Three options, argued. State nothing beyond 15.2: the lightest, consistent with every rule the record holds, and the one the team recommends, because a position on who the team is that the house cannot read or measure without breaking its own rules is a sentence and not a mechanism, and this program does not write sentences as positions. State an aspiration in the record's terms, that the house's reach is as wide as its customers' door: consistent with 15.3 and readable, since it is a statement about reach; it adds one sentence to the architecture page and no read. State an aspiration in her terms, a diverse team: a values statement, unreadable at the house's volume without a self-identification record, and the one the team does not draft. The founders choose; the team recommends the first, and notes that the second is available to them without a new read.

## 16. Bias measurement's data

Session 6 P47 asked what data the bias read runs on. The answer, in the diagnostic's form: what data, from where, read by whom, on what rhythm, with what refusals.

**What data.** Per seat and per stage: the source tag written by the stack at application (the build's audience, the board, the house with the referrer named, the lead's reach; S5 I4); the pool tag where the candidate is returning; the interview language and the format the candidate chose; each stage's outcome in the four states (S5 P29); the on-sheet decline reason (S5 P10); the interviewer per stage; the dates against the response window. Nothing else. No name in any read's record; no attribute of any kind; no self-identification unless the founders rule (P37), and then only as 15.5 states.

**From where.** The applicant system, Greenhouse, as configured by S5 I4 and I22; the calibration read's monthly record; the leads' review's pipeline line (S5 P28). All records that exist; the read adds no field but the ones S5 already specified.

**Read by whom.** Weekly, the leads' review, for source dominance per open seat and stage rate per stage, as its item 6 line (S5 I22). Monthly, the calibration read, the Maitre d, pre-opening the partner who is not the hiring partner (S6 P44). At the reset, the whole record, with the count that defines dominance revisited as a parameter.

**On what rhythm.** Weekly, monthly, at the reset; and at ninety days per hire, the outcome read (S6 7.6), which is where the house learns whether its reads predicted what the building saw and is the only place a bias in the reads could show as a pattern.

**Refusals.** No target for any source's share or any stage's rate. No figure presented as a standard. No claim of significance (S6 8.2). No name in the record. No inference about who a candidate is from any field. No act on a candidate, a scorecard, or a decision. No read that slows a seat. No language field read as anything but the language the process ran in.

**What the read produces.** A line on the leads' review record: which seats' pipelines one source dominates and for how many reviews; which stages decline one source's or one language's candidates at a rate the others do not. An act on the reach (P36). A note per interviewer where the calibration read finds one (S6 I8). A parameter proposal to the reset.

**P39 (Recommended).** Bias measurement's data is the source tag, the pool tag, the chosen language and format, the stage outcomes and on-sheet decline reasons, the interviewer, and the dates, per seat and per stage, from records S5 already specified, read weekly at the leads' review, monthly by the calibration read, at ninety days per hire, and at the reset, with no target, no figure, no significance claim, no name, no inference, and no act on a candidate.
