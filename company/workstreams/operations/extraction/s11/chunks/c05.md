## 11. The unblocking practice

Session 8 refused the workbook's process as an escalation up two lines and absorbed its form: the joint half-page as the record of a Class B or C hold, the refusal to hear a unilateral case, and a window (S8 P50). The practice, from the book's version (Book pp. 372 to 377), which is textually the workbook's (extraction notes).

**Who calls it.** Either party to a disagreement about a decision inside another owner's range, or between two owners, once the decision's owner has consulted and the objection stands. Her rule that "if you find yourself in a position where you're blocked by the lack of a resolved decision, it's your duty to instigate this process" (Book p.373) survives as a duty, because a disagreement carried unwritten is a Class B hold never written, which Session 10 named as sequence corruption at the interface (S10 P29). Her first test, whether the decision is reversible and "does it really matter" (Book p.372), is the register's own: a reversible decision inside an owner's range is the owner's, and the objection goes on the record as a dissent and the decision stands (S10 P26).

**The form.** One half-page, written jointly by the parties, in the language they share, translated under section 8.4 where the record's readers need it: the problem, stated as the outcome for the customer or the house and not as either party's position, which is her "goal of the joint enterprise in the voice of the user" (Book p.373) with the record's word; the options considered; the trade-offs both parties see, with the ones they could not resolve marked. Canon's six tests run on it, so it is short. No names appear beyond the seats that hold the ranges.

**Where it goes and who decides.** Inside one domain: the domain lead, in range, on the domain's record. Across domains: the owning domain's lead decides after the consultation the half-page records, and the half-page is the Class B hold on the leads' review record (S3 4.3). Between the two leads: Class C, the half-page on the partners' page, decided by the founder whose domain holds the outcome or by the reserved-class rule where it is one (S3 4.5). Never up two lines, never to a shared manager, never "recursively... until a decision is reached (or the stack overflows)" (Book p.374), because there is no one above both parties at Sŏn and the register already names who decides.

**The window.** Her five business days (Book p.373) is the loop window's shape (S8 P50): the half-page is on the leads' review record by the next leads' review, which is within seven days; the owner decides and writes the reason within the loop-closure window, thirty days (S4 P30); a Class C half-page is read at the next partners' review. A half-page open past the window is a standing item at the house review with its owner named, like any item past the window.

**The unilateral case.** Her three questions a manager asks before hearing one party alone (Book p.374) survive as the owner's refusal: an owner asked to decide by one party asks whether the parties have tried to agree, whether they have written the half-page, and whether the absent party was told the matter would come alone; three yeses and the owner asks the absent party to write their half before deciding; fewer and the owner declines to hear it. Her "It's not acceptable to refuse to unblock jointly" (Book pp. 374 to 375) is the rule that a party who declines to write jointly has their view recorded by the other party as "declined to write," and the owner decides on the half-page that exists.

**The record and the read-back.** The half-page, the decision, and its reason field stay on the record they were written on; the decision is read back on the next scorecard like any change (S9 P26); and her closing example, where the deciding manager "documents the rationale in reply to the escalation email" (Book p.377), is the reason field, on the record and not in a message.

**What it refuses.** A meeting called to resolve it, beyond the review at which its record is read; a decision in a room; a lead hearing one side at the brief or the close; a half-page about a person, whose destination is the check-in or the flag; a half-page used as a performance input.

**P29 (Recommended).** The unblocking practice: either party's duty to write; one joint half-page, the problem as the outcome for the customer or the house, the options, and the trade-offs, under the six tests; decided by the range's owner inside one domain, as a Class B hold across domains, as Class C between the leads, never up a line; on the record by the next leads' review and decided with its reason within the loop-closure window; an owner refuses a unilateral case until the absent party has been asked to write; the decision read back on the next scorecard. Sessions 12 and 15 take a disagreement that is about a person and a lead who repeatedly hears one side.

## 12. Feedback upward and sideways

### 12.1 What the record commits

"Feedback runs both directions. Team members weigh in on management effectiveness, operational improvements, and culture, because the best ideas and the earliest warnings both come from the people doing the work" (WP p.19). Three surfaces carry it: the check-in's fourth question (S4 P15), the channel (S4 P30), and the platform (S4 P10). Sections 6.7 and 6.8 placed the tools. This section fixes what each may never become and where each read goes.

### 12.2 Upward: the channel and the check-in's fourth question

"Management effectiveness" is the record's phrase and the house has no manager; what a person weighs in on is the house, a mechanism, a tool, a space, a process, and their lead's holding of the lead's range. The channel carries the first four. The fifth, a concern about a lead, is the one topic with no destination the lead does not hold until `86akhb2jg` closes (S10 P15), and this page does not fill it: a channel item about a lead is a person-subject item and is routed, not carried, and the destination reads "held" on the destinations page until the founders rule. What the channel can carry about a lead's domain is a friction with a mechanism the lead owns, named as the mechanism, which is the same rule the diagnostic runs on everything (S9 P1). The check-in's fourth question is the second door: "the person's read on the lead and the house" (S4 P15), said to the lead in the lead's own check-in with the person, written only in the person's words if the person wants it written, and the friction half carried to the channel by the lead with the person's knowledge (S4 4.4). A lead's working-with-me is the vocabulary a domain may use upward about the lead (S10 P30).

The read: channel items aggregated by friction and by mechanism join cooling failure's evidence line (S9 P13), the closure rate and items past the window join the leads' review's item 5 and the reset's environment reads (S10 I6), and nothing about a person is read from the channel. A channel that carried a mood question, a satisfaction score, a rating of a lead, or any prompt asking how the team is doing would be the survey the program refused (S9 P5, I10) and would put a read of persons on a surface the whole house sees; section 6.8 refuses it as configuration.

### 12.3 Sideways: the platform

A recognition is a peer's act about a peer's act (section 5.1). Its read is that recognitions exist and are read; the count of recognitions read at briefs per period joins the environment's reads as a count of services (S10 I6), and no count per person is read by anyone, because a per-person count is a ranking and a ranking is a read of a person (S9 P32; S10 P33). Mentoring, module authorship, and anything the house pays for are Session 13's (S7 P35) and never run through the platform as points.

### 12.4 What each may never become

The channel may never become: a survey, a poll, a rating, a suggestion box with no owner and no window, a vote that decides, a place a person is named, a performance input, or a lead's instruction surface. The platform may never become: a leaderboard, a ranking, a points ledger, a reward with money value, a manager's praise channel, a survey, a values scorecard, a performance input, a read of a person, or a prompt pushed off shift. The check-in's fourth question may never become: a rating of the lead, a form, or a line the lead writes about the person. The pulse may never move onto the channel or the platform, because it is anonymous and they are not.

### 12.5 The pulse and the employee NPS

The pulse is one question monthly, anonymous, on the OS surface, off the floor, read at the leads' review in its week and as a quarterly composite (S4 P29). It is the record's employee NPS, "would you recommend working here, tracked regularly rather than annually" (WP p.22). Its carrier is to confirm and is neither Nectar nor Canny by default, because both are named surfaces and the pulse's anonymity is its whole design; if either tool can carry one anonymous monthly question with no other prompt, it may, to confirm, and the choice is the Operations Lead's. The cultural labor score's line stays empty (`86akh5u9x`).

**P30 (Recommended).** Upward feedback runs on the channel (about the house, a mechanism, a tool, a space, a process) and the check-in's fourth question (about the lead and the house, in the person's words); a concern about a lead has no destination the lead does not hold until `86akhb2jg` closes and the destinations page reads "held."
