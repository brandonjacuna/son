### 5.4 Inside the window

The check-in interval moves to every two weeks for the window's length, which is the plan's interval and the plan's mechanics (S4 P19; S7 P27): the stack places them, the spine is unchanged, the second question is heard as the building, and the line is the person's. Her weekly progress checks (Workbook p.121) are refused as weekly and as the manager's; the two-week check-in is the window's rhythm and the person's words are its only record. A placed check-in is still available once per interval (S12 P16).

The person's one goal for the window is the seat held at the horizon, binary, set at the opening block by the person with the lead, as any goal is (S4 7.3). The building's supply continues to be read at every check-in against what the person wrote at the block; a "not supplied" line inside the window pauses the window's count until it is supplied, on the plan's own logic (S7 P29), and is an infrastructure line at the leads' review.

The modules for the horizon's rows are available on the platform as they always were; a paired service with a mentor from the pool may be placed by the stack at the person's request (S7 7.7's period-plan logic), and the mentor never scores, never writes, and never reports (S7 P42; S10 P32). No lead assigns a task, a project, or a deliverable inside the window (S9 P38); her milestones "[Project 1] by [deadline]" (Workbook p.121) have no object on a floor and are refused.

The window's length is a count of services, a reset parameter, set to start at the sheet's paired-service count plus the count the four-week load read covers (S7 7.2, milestones 5 and 9), because that is the count the house already believes is enough to read whether a seat holds; her three-month ceiling (Book p.437) is a duration and is not adopted, and the team flags the count for the founders. The window may be extended once, by the lead with the person at a check-in, for a building-caused block with the fix named, as the plan extends (S7 P29), and never for the person's not holding, never shortened, never twice by the lead, and never ended early by anyone; her "I may terminate the PIP at any time" (Workbook p.121) is refused whole, because a window that a lead can close early is a lead's lever.

### 5.5 The close: the assessor's read

At the window's end the stack schedules the readiness form at the horizon held, on the seat's own rows (S7 I7, the same rows as the practical plus language, range, and felt knowledge), read by an assessor from the pool in a real service, never the mentor and never the lead (S7 P24; S8 P37). For the attendance trigger the close is the count over the window, a schedule fact the stack writes, and no form. For a tension-slack trigger the form is read and the adjacent seats' lines over the window are read beside it by the diagnostic, because a seat whose form reads ready while the adjacent lines still rise is a domain question and not a person's (S9 4.3).

The form reads ready: the window closes as a state, held, written by the stack from the form on its date, with the rows; the lead records it at the next check-in and may not override it (S8 P37); the check-in interval returns to monthly; nothing else is written, and nothing is said at any room. The block stays on the person page as a dated state, like a plan's close, and is read by no review, because the review reads the progression state and never the window (S13 P6, extended in 12.2). Her "you must maintain this level of performance moving forward, and further underperformance may result in termination without a further PIP" (Workbook p.122) is refused whole: a second window opens on the same triggers as the first, or it does not open.

The form reads not yet: the window closes as a state, not held, with the rows in the assessor's words, read at the next check-in row by row with the lead adding nothing (S12 P21). What follows is section 8: the person chooses among the moves that are open, with the lead, inside a stated count of services, a reset parameter; and where no move is open or the person declines them, the record goes to the partners under the interim two-partner rule as Session 15 designs (5.8).

A second window on the same seat inside a stated count of reviews after a held close is a seat question before a person question: the diagnostic reads the seat as a seat that repeats (S9 P19), the sheet's horizon and shift shape are read, and the window opens only after that read; a stated count of windows on one seat class in a year is a sheet question at the reset regardless of who held the seat.

### 5.7 How "no surprises" holds here

Five ways. The window's triggers are counts of check-ins at which the person heard the hypothesis or the statement and wrote their line, so a window cannot open on anything the person has not heard as many times as the threshold counts. The team home's performance page (I10) states, in every house language and before anyone is in one, what a window is, what opens it, how long it runs, how it closes, and what it can and cannot lead to, so that the block is never the first time a person learns the mechanism exists. The block reads the trigger back from records the person can open, and the state is read aloud before it is saved. The close is a form read together row by row, in the assessor's words, and the moves are on the page. And the one thing a person cannot see coming is refused: no lead's hypothesis on the outcome exists, so no one in the house knows how the window will close until the form is read.

### 5.9 The four-part entry test and the six-question playground test

Entry test (S4 2.2). What it buys: a bounded, honest answer to a question the record otherwise leaves to linger as tension slack nightly, which is the record's own "other nodes are compensating without visibility" (WP p.14) made visible and ended; and the refusal of every mechanism a house reaches for instead, the quiet re-sectioning that never ends, the schedule that thins one person's shifts, the designation quietly withheld, the lead's file. Who runs it: the stack opens and closes it, the lead holds the block and the check-ins, an assessor reads the form, the person writes. Where its record lives: the window block on the person page, and the counts of windows opened, held, not held, paused, and extended per seat class per period on the leads' review record and the reset, never per person.

Playground test (S10 P3). The condition: a person knows where they stand and chooses among real options, which is agency for the employee in canon's own words (Canon 1.0, section 01). The legible rule: the triggers are counts the person can read, the horizon is on the sheet, and the page is public. The open-ended result: held, a move the person chooses, or the partners' decision, none of which is decided by the lead. The tight edge: a window opened on a lead's impression, or without the building's fixes cited, which the trigger's form refuses. The loose edge: a window that never closes, or a seat left not holding for a year with the adjacent seats carrying it and no one saying so, which is disposal by neglect and the record's tension slack left to run; the close date on the schedule and the count on the leads' review are the reads that notice. The documented reason: this section, and the read that would notice the condition's absence is the reset's count of windows against the count of tension-slack lines that never reached one.

## 6. The non-negotiable breach, to the ceiling this session can reach

### 6.1 What is fixed, and what is open

Fixed: the flag is raised by anyone, with evidence, to both leads the same day, overridable by no one, said in no room (S6 P51; S4 P20; S7 P45; S11 5); it is never a check-in item, a hypothesis, a typed failure, a placed check-in's reason, or a lead's line on the person page, and what follows is not coaching (S12 P36); a breach ends a candidacy at any stage (S5 12.3); the eligibility rule for every designation reads "no non-negotiable line on their page" (S8 P25); the review's dimension 3 reads the fact of a line and never discusses one (S13 P7). Open, from `86akht39y` and S12 P36: the destination after the flag; the writer and form of the non-negotiable line; a breach by a lead; and the definition of toxicity, which canon names and neither artifact defines (S12 finding 18.9), beside exploitation (S13 finding 19.12).

The ceiling this page can reach is the mechanism up to the decision about staying, and the form of the line. The decision itself is Session 15's and the founders'; the definitions are canon's owner's with the founders; employment law is counsel's.

### 6.2 The destination after the flag: the flag read

The flag goes to both leads the same day. What follows, on the next closed hour or the next pre-service window with slack and never later than the next service the person works, is the flag read: the two leads together, off the floor, on the clock, reading the flag's record (I5) and the record it cites (a capture, the flag's own form with the evidence stated), and writing one of three states on the flag's record, jointly, as Class C (S11 P29's form for a decision between the leads): a non-negotiable act, and which of the three (exploitation, toxicity, disposal); a process failure that arrived as a flag, returned to the capture path with the reason written; or cannot read, which goes to the partners' review that week with the flag's record and nothing added. Where the flag concerns a lead, or reaches only one lead because the other is its subject, the read is held by the other lead with a founder, and a breach by a lead is otherwise Session 15's and the founders' (6.6). The flag read writes nothing on the person page; it writes on the flag's record, which is the leads' record (S11 5), and a flag that reads "process failure" leaves no trace on any record about the person.

The person hears it before anything is written about them. The lead who holds their check-in, with the other lead present as the flag's second reader, tells the person, off the floor, on the clock, in their language, what was flagged and what the record cites, in the register the house uses for everything it says (Canon 1.0, section 09), and hears the person's account. This is not a check-in, not a hypothesis, and not a room the roster is in; it is the one conversation in the house with three people in it, and the reason is that a line with one reader is a lead's file. The person's account is theirs, written beside the line in their words if they choose, and it is the only thing about the breach a person writes.

### 6.3 The non-negotiable line: its writer, its form, and what reads it

The writer: both leads jointly, from the flag read's state, and never one lead alone, never the stack from a capture, never the founders on a lead's behalf except where a lead is the subject. The form, fixed and closed: the non-negotiable named (one of the three, in canon's words); the date of the act; the flag record's citation; the flag read's date and its two readers by seat; and the person's own words beside it if they wrote any. Nothing else: no narrative of the act, no severity, no "first" or "second," no consequence written on the line, no lead's view, and no text in the register of a case. It is written on the person page as a state with a citation, which is the only kind of thing the page carries (S7 P25), and read back to the person before it is saved. It exists in the person's language.
