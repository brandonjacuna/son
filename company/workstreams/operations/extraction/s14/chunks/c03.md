## 4. Work that is not holding: the order of reads, with the building first and the person last

### 4.1 Who notices, and what they may do in the moment

On a Friday, work that is not holding shows as a re-fire, a sequence call after the plate left the pass, a table that waited, a section whose range another server used, a no-show at crew call, a station whose mise ran down by the second turn, a designation's function performed by someone not named to it. The people who notice are the room's holder from the coverage position (Canon 1.0, section 08), the pass's owner, the adjacent seats, and the person themselves. What any of them may do in the moment is an operating instruction, "the object of the work as subject, a present act as verb" (S12 P15), a re-section by the room's holder as coverage, a range used across a section, and the degraded-mode set if a seat is down (S8 I8). What none of them may do is say anything about a person on the floor, at the pass, or in any room the roster is in (S12 P15; S10 P14). The night is survived by coverage, and the record of it is the close.

### 4.2 The reads, in order

The record's rule is "before intervening at the people level, identify which failure mode is actually present" (WP p.14), and thirteen sessions turned that sentence into an order. Nothing on this page opens until every step below has run and is on a record the person can read.

1. The capture and the close. The incident is captured in the writer's own words, naming a process and never a person as cause (S4 P12; S11 5).
2. The type. At the capture review the reviewing lead types it on the process update: basic, complex, or intelligent (S12 P28). A complex failure has no person in it. An intelligent failure is never corrected. A basic failure's fix is the process's placement first: was the checklist on the surface the person opens, was the module in their language, was the 86 read at the brief.
3. The diagnostic's mode. The leads' review reads the week's captures and load readings as feeders, and a mode is named on an object, house, domain, service roster, seat, or period, never a person (S9 P1, P8).
4. The domain's fixes, made and read back. Re-sectioning, the designations rotated, the range card re-read, the schedule's shape, the par, the pacing at the door, each a change with the evidence line as its reason and a read-back at the next scorecard (S9 P24 to P26; S9 14.1 step 2).
5. The seat sheet. Field 6's horizon, field 11's shift shape, field 13's physical demands, and field 10's range, read for whether the seat describes a seat the pool does not hold; a seat that repeats is a sheet question before anything else (S9 P19).
6. The building's supply against the fourth-read baseline. What the person said they need, in their words, and what the building supplied, at every check-in (S5 P16; S7 7.4). A "not supplied" line is the building's failure and stops the order here until it is supplied (4.4).
7. The person's own lines. The check-in's second question, heard as the building, in the person's words, on their page (S4 P15; S12 4.3). This is the only person-side source anything on this page reads.
8. Only then, a hypothesis at the check-in, once, as a behavior in a service and its impact, after the person's account (S12 P5, P6), and only for a basic failure that recurred on the same seat with the placement fixed, or for the tension-slack statement after the domain's fix (S12 P24, P28).

The order is enforced by form, not by diligence: the horizon window's trigger (5.2) reads records that only exist if steps 1 to 8 ran, and the trigger record cites them by date. A window that cannot cite a domain fix with a read-back, a supplied line, and the person's lines across the count cannot open.

## 5. The horizon window: the destination, designed

### 5.1 What it is called, and what it is not

The horizon window is the one mechanism this page adds. It is a bounded count of services, opened by the stack on a trigger read from records, during which the question of whether a person holds their seat's horizon is put explicitly, in writing as a state, and answered by the same instrument that put them in the seat: the readiness form at the horizon held, read by an assessor from the pool in a real service. It is named for what it reads, the horizon on the seat sheet, and for what it is, a window with a stated close.

It is not a check-in and does not wear its form: the check-in refuses discipline and writes only the person's words (S4 P15; S12 P10), and the window's opening block is a fourth kind of block on the schedule beside the check-in, the review, and the career conversation. It is not a review and does not wear its form: the review has no rating, reads only what the person can read, and may never become a managing-out file (S13 P17), and the window is never cited at a review. It is not a performance improvement plan: no document in a lead's voice exists, no lead sets its goals or measures its progress, no lead may end it early, and it carries no termination warning and no maintenance clause. It is not a probation: the plan has no probation state (S7 P25) and neither does the window; the word does not appear on any surface. It is not a coaching record: the hypothesis still has no page (S12 P5), and the window's block holds states, citations, and the person's words. It is not a diagnostic read: the diagnostic never opens at a person (S9 P1), and the window opens at a seat whose records the diagnostic has already read as a mode and fixed. It is not a room: two people, the person and the lead who holds their check-in, in the person's language, with an interpreter designation where needed (S11 P20).

### 5.2 The trigger: what opens it, from which records, and who

The stack opens a window. No lead requests one, and no lead can prevent one; the lead is told, as the person is told, by the schedule. Four triggers, each a count on records the person has already read at check-ins, each with its citations written on the window block by the stack:

1. The hypothesis count. A hypothesis about a behavior in a service, said at consecutive check-ins across the recurrence thresholds (S9 P17), with the captures, rows, or range count unchanged after the building's fix and no line from the person naming it (S12 P8). The stack reads this from the check-in dates and the records' version dates; the lead's hypothesis is written nowhere, so what the stack reads is the person's own lines against records that did not change, which is the count Session 12 handed.
2. The tension-slack statement. The one permitted statement, that the seat remains occupied and its load carried by named seats after the domain's fix, read at consecutive check-ins across the thresholds with the person's lines beside it and the domain's fixes unchanged (S12 P24).
3. The basic-failure recurrence. A basic failure typed at the capture review, recurring on the same seat past the thresholds with the process's placement fixed and read back (S12 P28; S9 P25), and then the hypothesis count above.
4. The plan not held. A ninety-day plan whose goal is not held after one extension for a building fix that was made (S7 P29; 4.4).

A fifth source is attendance, which is a schedule fact and not a hypothesis: shifts scheduled, worked, missed, and late by count, which the stack already holds and the person can read on the scheduling surface (S7 13.4). A count of missed services inside a stated window, a reset parameter, opens a window on the same rule, after the building's questions have run at the check-in (the shift shape against field 11 and the person's stated shape, transit, the schedule's language, the crew-call time against the pre-service window) and the person's lines have been recorded. Her "consistently late" case (Book p.224; `86akhb2w8`) is a check-in line in the person's account first, then a count, and never a manager's "feedback quickly and strongly."

What can never open a window: a lead's impression; a review's states or mark; a recognition or its absence; a designation held or not held; a channel item; a customer's complaint or rating; the reach read or the calibration record; a scorecard or anything in the hiring packet; a mentor's or a peer's account; a work style placement; a count of placed or moved check-ins; a failure count per person, which does not exist; a lagging figure; a founder's word; the person's own request, because a person who asks whether they hold the seat has a career conversation's question and not a window's. The thresholds are reset parameters in counts of check-ins and services (S9 P32), and they are the same counts that reach the partners' review for any object; this page sets no new values and flags the counts as the founders' with the reset.
