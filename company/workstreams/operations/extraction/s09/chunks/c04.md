## 13. Delegation, DRIs, and the skill-will matrix, against the decision-rights rule and the failure mode reference

### 13.1 Delegation lands on the register, not on a manager

Her delegation is a manager handing work down: "general work assignment," covered "automatically" in 1:1s, and "a one-off assignment" (Book p.296). At Sŏn authority is not handed down; it is placed with the closest competent owner by the register, with the range stated in advance, triggers each with a named destination, and ranges set one level out (S3 section 4). Her first form is the seat sheet: field 5 (the work), field 6 (what great looks like at two horizons), field 10 (the decision-rights range), and the range card the person carries (S7 I4); the check-in refuses task assignment (ledger), so nothing about the job is "covered in 1:1s." Her second form, the one-off, is either a process change with an owner on the leads' review record (S8 I17's substitute for the working group) or a designation for a service; a lead may not hand a task to a person at a check-in or at the brief, and an owner is named by domain, never by a person's development goals.

Her "delegating is the key to getting your own work done" (Book p.296) has no Sŏn object. A lead has no work to hand down; the lead's work is the domain's outcomes, and the record has already moved "much of what a traditional operations manager once did by hand" onto the stack (WP p.10). A lead who has work to delegate is holding operational work the register placed elsewhere, which is the mode this page reads it as.

### 13.2 Her under- and over-delegating signs, read as modes

"A lead who does not delegate" is read through node overload, as the brief directs, and her lists map sign by sign:

None of the eight is a dial a lead turns between more and less delegating. Each is a mechanism, and the diagnostic names the mechanism.

### 13.3 The delegation framework, translated to "ranges set one level out"

Her two axes, impact and trapdoor, and her rule that everything outside high-impact-trapdoor is delegated (Book pp. 300 to 301), translate to the register's rule that a range states what an owner may do without asking and what they may not, set one level out. Her upper-right quadrant, "Do," is what the record already reserves: the reserved decision classes at the partners' review (S3, extended by `86akh5u8m`), Class A structure, the non-negotiable flag that no one overrides (S6 P51), and the financial recovery threshold above the server's range (Canon 1.0, section 08). Her other three quadrants, "Delegate," are the inside of a range. What her framework adds that the register can use is a test for writing a range: a trapdoor outcome above the seat's range must appear as a trigger with a named destination, so that the irreversible decision is never inside a range by default and never lands on "escalate."

Her three reasons a manager keeps adjustable work (Book pp. 301 to 302): modeling, which at Sŏn is the mentor's paired services and "see one, do one, teach one" mapped onto the training sequence and module authorship, "Any qualified team member can create training modules" (WP p.19), never a lead doing the work to show it; urgency, "learn from it and don't let it happen again," which is the capture and the process update; and resource constraints, "you're not scaling yourself or your team adequately" (Book p.302), which is her own name for node overload, and the record's answer is the pool and the seat count, not the lead rolling up their sleeves. A founder on the floor doing the work is the same mode at the center (S3 P10.8).

### 13.4 DRIs

Refused as a construct. Session 8 already refused the working group with a DRI (S8 I17), and her DRI callout adds the general case: a named individual "who would be held accountable for meeting a goal or ship date" whose job is "to get the decision made" even when "not the decision-maker" (Book p.298). At Sŏn the owner on the decision-rights register is the decision-maker inside a range, a process change has an owner on the leads' review record, and the room's holder for a service is named at the brief; each is a named owner with a range, which is more than a DRI carries. Her point that the term works because "everyone knows what it means" and belongs in onboarding is absorbed: the register's vocabulary (owner, range, trigger, destination) is what onboarding delivers in the why block's sequence (S7). Her chain of DRIs relying on one another is the interface read (S8 I10). Nothing gets a decision made at Sŏn by being accountable for it without owning it; that is the trigger with no destination the ledger forbids.

### 13.5 The delegation conversation

Her seven steps for handing an assignment (Book pp. 302 to 303) survive, for a process change with an owner, as the leads' review record's form and Session 4's change record: the change in one line, the owner, the date, what it displaces (S4 P35). Two of her steps are refused. "Explain why the work assignment is right for the person," matching "the project to the individual's skill set or development goals" (Book p.303), is refused because an owner is named by domain, development is the invisible advocate's (WP p.11) and not a lead's assignment, and a lead who assigns work to develop someone has reintroduced the manager's attention the advocate exists to remove. "Get buy-in" is refused as a step because a range is on the sheet before the seat is accepted (S5 P12, field 10) and a person who holds a seat holds its range; there is nothing to buy into per assignment.

### 13.6 The skill-will matrix, tested against four rules

The matrix places a person on two axes and prescribes the manager's response per quadrant (Book pp. 284 to 286). Tested against the four rules the brief names:

Against "identify which failure mode is actually present" (WP p.14): the matrix opens at the person and the rule forbids opening there. Refused as a first read of anyone.

Against the four scorecard dimensions and nothing added at review that was not read at hiring (S6 P33): "will," motivation, is not one of the four. The nearest dimension, self-directed mastery, is read in role from the invisible advocate's own record, a mechanism record, not a lead's placement. A quadrant is a fifth dimension and a back door into Session 13's review. Refused.

Against the check-in's refusals: a check-in refuses verdicts (ledger), and "high skill, low will" is a verdict. Refused inside the check-in.

Against the record's fixes, her four prescriptions map, and each maps away from the person: "Change team members" (low skill, low will) is Session 14's and Session 15's and is never the diagnostic's; "Set expectations" (low skill, high will) is the sheet's two horizons and the plan's milestones; "Delegate and empower" (high skill, high will) is the range; "Raise the bar" (high skill, low will) is tension slack's read, which the record answers by "diagnosing which adjacent nodes are overloaded" and "not replacing the person."

What Sŏn uses, and for whom. No one is placed on the matrix. Two of her team-level readings survive as mechanism reads: "skill too much... the team will lose motivation" (Book p.285) is a person past the sheet's horizon with no next unlock, which is skill invisibility, the path not visible or the advocate not running, and never a person's will; "will too much... overpromise and under-deliver" (Book p.286) is the goal counts, at most one per person (S4), and the bandwidth flag. Her motivation questions, "Is it new, episodic, or persistent? Could the person be experiencing something in their personal life?" (Book p.286), are the person-level half and route to Session 12 (coaching, hard feedback, the check-in's conversational content) and Session 14 (high, middle, and low performers), with the diagnostic's contribution being only section 14's hand-off.

**P38 (Recommended).** Delegation lands on the register: the seat sheet and the range card for the job, a process change with an owner or a designation for the one-off, and no task handed down at a check-in or a brief. Her under- and over-delegating signs are read as the modes tabled, each a mechanism and none a dial.

## 14. Where a diagnostic read reaches a person

### 14.1 The one path

Tension slack is the only mode whose read can reach a person, because it is the only mode the record describes at a person: "a role is occupied but the person is disengaged" (WP p.14). It is also the mode whose fix the record states most firmly away from the person: "not replacing the person." The path is therefore narrow and ordered, and every step is on a record:

1. The line names the seat and the adjacent seats carrying its load, from the close, the adjacent seats' own lines, and the schedule (section 4.3). The person's name is on no line.
2. The domain fix is made and read back: re-sectioning, the designations rotated, the range card re-read with the seat's holder at their check-in, the schedule's shape. This is "re-establishing tension in that domain" (WP p.14), and it is a change with a read-back like any other (section 7.4).
3. The only person-side read the diagnostic takes is the person's own: the check-in's "what is in the way," in their words, on their page, read back before saving (S4 P15). The lead's reading of the person is not a source and is never written.
4. If, after the domain fix, the seat still does not carry its load across the recurrence in section 5.1 (three consecutive leads' reviews, or two consecutive quarterly lines), the diagnostic may write one thing and only one: that the seat remains occupied and its load remains carried by named adjacent seats after the domain's fix, with the dates. It may not write disengaged, low will, a problem, a fit question, or anything about the person's manner.
5. It hands off there. To Session 12, for what the check-in's coaching content does with a line the person has heard and a load the domain has tried to re-establish. To Session 14, only if the pattern the check-ins have recorded, and the person has heard at each, becomes a performance conversation, which Session 14 designs and the diagnostic does not. Never to Session 15 directly; managing out is not a destination the diagnostic has, and a read that arrives there has skipped two sessions' work.

Two things this path does not touch. A non-negotiable breach is not a diagnostic read; it is the flag rule and the both-leads trigger (S4 P20; S6 P51), and it runs on its own path regardless of any line. And a hire that does not hold inside the plan is not the diagnostic's; Session 7 hands it to Session 14 with its record (S7 P46), and the diagnostic reads the plan's infrastructure lines only as the modes they name.

### 14.2 What the diagnostic writes about a person, stated as a refusal

Nothing. A person's name appears in no evidence line, no scorecard section, no house memo, no reset record, and no leads' review record's diagnostic mark. The only page a person appears on is their own, written at their check-in in their words. The diagnostic reads that page for the friction the building supplies and for the path the person named; it writes on it never. That refusal is what keeps the record's "No Disposable People" (Canon 1.0, section 01) a property of the instrument rather than a hope about its users, and it is what makes the four dimensions hold at review (S6 P33): a diagnostic that wrote about people would be the fifth.

**P41 (Recommended).** A read reaches a person by one path, for tension slack only: the seat and its adjacent seats named; the domain fix made and read back; the person's own check-in line as the only person-side source; one permitted statement after recurrence, that the seat remains occupied and its load carried by named seats; then hand-off to Session 12, and only through it to Session 14, never to Session 15.

**P42 (Recommended).** The diagnostic writes nothing about any person anywhere. A person's name is on no line, memo, or record it produces; the person page is read for the building's friction and the person's path and is never written by the diagnostic.

## 15. The career conversation

### 15.1 Her conversation, and what the record holds

Hers is a scheduled sixty-minute "get-to-know-you" conversation "after a few months of working together... but before any sort of formal performance review" (Book p.295; Workbook p.90), walking the person's history from childhood through school and past jobs to a five-year projection, closing with "two or three development goals to keep an eye on together" (Workbook p.92), and serving as a reference point at reorganizations, reviews, and delegation decisions (Book p.296).

The record has no career conversation and would not need one as she frames it. "The path is explicit before they start it. Every position has documented competency requirements visible to anyone. Employees do not need to ask what it takes to advance. They open the system and see it" (WP p.11). "Advancement requires preparation, not permission" (WP p.14). A conversation in which a lead learns a person's ambitions in order to "make better decisions about what that person should focus on" (Book p.295) is the manager's attention the invisible advocate exists to remove. What the record does hold is the review "every three to six months" focused on "the path forward" (WP p.19), cross-training as "career development, not only operational insurance" (WP p.19), and the person's own development outward "deeper into their domain and across into adjacent ones" (WP p.11). Session 7 placed the path read at the fifth check-in: "The first unlock's date from the sheet, opened on the person's own account; the seat after theirs, named by them; what they want to learn next, including another domain's track" (S7 P27), and handed the rest here (carryover `86akhb2wr`) with two conditions: it opens on the person page and adds nothing the person has not heard.
