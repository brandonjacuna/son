## 7. From diagnosis to change

### 7.1 The record's own loop, and the two kinds of change

The record already has the loop: "the system captures the incident in the shift close, the appropriate lead reviews, the process that allowed the failure gets updated, and the next server inherits a system that has already learned from it" (WP p.13). And canon has the rule for what a change is: "A living document changes by versioned decision, never by drift" (Deck 3.0, p.94), with Jaeyeonmi's "maintenance philosophy that distinguishes care from correction" (Canon 1.0, section 01). Session 4 used the distinction for the reset's rarity (S4 6.6). This page uses it to say which changes need a version.

Care is a process update from a capture: the close records an incident, the lead reviews it inside seven days, the process that allowed it is updated, and the update is logged against the capture with an owner. It is daily, unversioned in the deck's sense, and it is the only change the first quarter's freeze allows in the room. Correction is any change to a mechanism's rhythm, spine, range, trigger, or rule, to a designation's rule, to a seat, a line, or a period, and it is versioned: on the mechanism map for a mechanism (S4's cadence page), on the inventory for structure (S8 P49), on the goal page for a goal (S4 P35), on the sheet for a seat's fields (S5 P13). A diagnosis produces both kinds, and the line's field 6 says which by naming the mechanism.

### 7.2 The four paths

A diagnosis becomes a change by one of four paths, and the path is set by what the fix touches:

1. **A mechanism.** A range, a trigger, an interval, a window, the brief's block, the close's fields, a surfacing rule. Proposed by the domain lead at the leads' review with the evidence line as its reason; a parameter inside a range the review already holds is set there and logged; anything else waits for the reset, where it continues, changes by versioned decision, or ends (S4 P39). Between resets a mechanism's rhythm and spine do not move.
2. **A designation's rule.** Eligibility, a rotation floor, what the room designation holds. A structure change entry on the inventory (S8 17.1 lists "changing a designation's rule"), proposed through the leads' review, versioned by the partners' review, and made at the reset when it is a load finding on the Maitre d's seat (section 12). Naming a designation for a service is not a change and needs no entry.
3. **A seat.** Creating, retiring, re-opening by tension loss, changing its count under a charter, or versioning its sheet. Session 8's path whole: a versioned inventory entry with its reason first, proposed through the leads' review, versioned by the partners' review, reaching the team by the affected person's check-in, the sheet's version, the brief, and the team home, in that order, and by no announcement (S8 P49, I5).
4. **A leadership line or a period.** A Class A candidate at the partners' review on the variety pass at a gate, argued and ratified before the period opens (S8 18.3, 18.4). Never from a single quarter's line; the pass is the ground.

### 7.3 The reason is the line

Session 8's structure change entry must contain "the reason written first, in the record's terms (tension lost, a class resolving to nothing, a gate, a ruling)" (S8 I5). This page closes the coupling: the reason field of a change entry, and the reason field of a mechanism's versioned decision on the map, is the evidence line, carried over verbatim with its seven fields. A change with no line is a change with no reason, and Session 8's form already refuses it. A change whose line names a person as the reason is refused twice, once by the line's form and once by the entry's.

The consequence for restructuring is exact. At Sŏn nothing is restructured because a manager judged the team; something is versioned because a dated line on a mechanism's record named a mode, an object, and the record's fix. That is the whole of her "10 percent figuring out the plan" (Book p.287), and it is written before the change, not narrated after.

### 7.4 The read-back

Every change carries the mode and the object it was made to clear. The next scorecard reads the same line, from the same source, and writes one of three states beside the change's version: cleared (the signal is absent on the source that raised it), not cleared (present on two consecutive lines after the fix, which is section 5.1's reset trigger), or cannot read (the source has not run since the change). No change is read back by a lead's impression that it helped; it is read back by the mechanism record that raised it. A change that cleared is left alone; the reset does not re-open a cleared line. A change that did not clear says one of two things, that the fix named the wrong mechanism or that the line named the wrong mode, and the reset decides which before it names a second fix. This is her "build in some checkpoints to determine whether the skill is rising to meet the will" (Book p.286) with the object moved from the person to the mechanism.

What is changed at the reset and what may be changed between resets, in one list. At the reset only: any mechanism's rhythm, spine, interval, or range width; any designation's rule; the mentor pool's composition after the first wave (S7 P42); the response window's house standard (S5 P29); the recurrence thresholds themselves. Between resets: a process update from a capture; a parameter inside a held range; a sheet's version; a seat opened by tension loss; a structure change entry that a gate or a departure requires; the naming of designations. Inside the first quarter: only the second list's first, second, and fourth items, and the sheet on the build side.

**P24 (Recommended).** A diagnosis produces care or correction: a process update from a capture is care and is logged; a change to a mechanism, a designation's rule, a seat, a line, or a period is correction and is versioned on the map, the inventory, the goal page, or the sheet.

**P25 (Recommended).** Four paths from a line to a change: a mechanism through the leads' review and the reset; a designation's rule through a structure change entry; a seat through Session 8's path whole; a line or a period through the variety pass at a gate. The evidence line is the change's reason field, verbatim, and a change with no line is refused.

**P26 (Recommended).** Every change is read back on the next scorecard by the source that raised it, as cleared, not cleared, or cannot read; a change not cleared on two lines goes to the reset, which decides whether the mechanism or the mode was wrong before naming a second fix.

## 8. Her team changes and restructuring, translated or refused

### 8.1 Reorganizations, and her three triggers

"Needing to change the org structure can also be a sign of high growth or a strategic shift" (Book p.288). Sŏn has no organization to reorganize in her sense: it has seats, lines, designations, and periods, and its growth is periods and reach, "outward, not upward" (WP p.9). Her first trigger, structure not matching strategy, has one Sŏn form: a period's charter ruled at a gate, which adds seats, a designation set, interfaces, and a register block and no line unless the variety pass requires one (S8 P8). Her second trigger, a talent issue, "a leader was fired or is leaving the company or changing roles" (Book p.289), is a departure, which is a tension re-establishment and a seat re-opened with the pool read first (S5 P60), and a change of domain lead carried by the person page (S7 P51); nothing about the domain's other seats is rethought because its lead changed, and "manager changes almost always lead to some team changes" (Book p.289) is the load-bearing model's habit that the inventory's rows, which carry no names, refuse by form. Her misused third trigger, the underperforming team, is where she and the record agree and she says it from the other side: "Ask yourself whether the issue is that the team isn't structured to execute on the strategy or whether you actually have a talent problem" (Book p.289) is "before intervening at the people level, identify which failure mode is actually present" (WP p.14). At Sŏn a domain that repeats is diagnosed in section 5.2's three questions and is never restructured to mask the answer.

### 8.2 "Don't leave the ice cream on the counter"

Her rule is speed once others are involved, because "misinformation creates instability" (Book p.290). Absorbed whole as the change entry's own clock. A proposed row is read at the leads' review the week it is proposed and versioned at the partners' review the same week or the next; the reason is written before the entry, so nothing is designed in a limbo the floor can hear about. Her phase 0 secrecy, "a very small number of people" (Book p.292), has no Sŏn form and needs none: the first place a change is spoken is the leads' review, the first person to hear it is the affected person at their check-in, before the sheet's version and before the brief (S8 P49), and the four channels' order is what prevents the misinformation she fears. A change that reaches the brief before the person's check-in has broken the order and fails row 102.

### 8.3 Structure versus stability

"It will always feel like a reorg is coming at the wrong time" (Book p.290). At Sŏn stability is a property of the system and not of the moment (ledger), and the question of timing is answered by two rules already made: structure changes at gates and at the reset, seats and designations between (section 7.4); and nothing in the room's cadence changes inside dinner's first quarter (section 6.2). Her caution against reorganizing again soon, "if you've turned the heat up too high, or if it might go higher still" (Book p.291), is the reset's rarity, once a year after the first two, and Ma's argument that a mechanism no one would miss is ended rather than tuned (S4 6.6). Her "bridge toward a future state with a less-than-ideal structure" and "be transparent with the team about the interim state" (Book p.291) is already the record's form for pre-opening: the variety pass's classes resolving to a founder are named as such on the partners' page (S8 P13), which is the transparent interim.

### 8.4 "Is this the person you want to break your structure for?"

Refused entirely, and the refusal is structural. The inventory's rows carry no names (S8 17.1). A seat may not be created by re-titling or for a person (S8 18.1). A designation is never permanent (S8 18.9), and the reset reads one held every service for a quarter as a question about the seat's hours and the designation's rule, never as a person's claim (section 9.3). A lead seat is filled from the pool with a written reason on the partners' page if it is not (S5 P60), so there is no path by which "a critical person threatening to leave if they don't get more responsibility" (Book p.291) obtains a line or a seat. Her horizontal and vertical opportunities are Session 8's outward movement and one-service designations (S8 P30). What survives is her own warning: "if your teams believe you're optimizing for an individual rather than for the team, you'll lose their trust" (Book p.291), which is the record's "Politics are a load-bearing structure problem" (WP p.11), and the refusal is how the house keeps them out.

### 8.5 The three phases

Refused as phases with a clock. What survives from each: phase 0's "a design for the new structure with every person accounted for" (Book p.292) is the entry's affected rows before and after (S8 I5); phase 1's "at least two, if not three, conversations" (Book p.293) is the affected person's check-in and the next one, on the clock, with the person's own line written and read back, and nothing framed for their growth by a lead; phase 2's "tell the teams and people who are most impacted first" (Book p.294) is the channels' order, and "announce the change to the wider group within a day" is the brief's spoken line the next service, which is not an announcement but the third channel. "Get the ice cream back in the freezer" is the version log's date.

### 8.6 Moving people, merging and splitting, and communicating change

A person moves seats at Sŏn by the internal loop, the pool and the seat's paid practical in a real service (S5 P60, P61), or into a period by the period plan (S7 P30), never by assignment; a lead who moves a person between seats without a practical has made a hire without a packet. Merging domains is bundling the leads, which Session 8 refuses under any pressure and names as the load-bearing model's final failure (S8 18.3; WP p.10). Splitting a domain is adding a line, which only the variety pass at a gate does (S8 18.3). Her "reorgs are often an opportunity for leaders and team members to take on more responsibility and step up" (Book p.294) is designations held more often and the pool's nearest-node rule (S8 P45). Communicating change beyond the four channels, how the house tells itself things, is Session 11's (carryover `86akhcz6y`), and this page adds only that the channels' order is a diagnostic read: a change the floor heard first at the brief is sequence corruption on the entry.

**P27 (Recommended).** Reorganizations are refused as a form. Her first trigger is a period's charter ruled at a gate; her second is a departure read as tension re-establishment with the lead change carried by the person page; her misused third is the record's own rule that the failure mode is identified before a person is read. A domain that repeats is diagnosed, never restructured.

**P28 (Recommended).** Her speed rule is absorbed as the change entry's clock and the four channels' order; her structure-versus-stability caution is answered by changes at gates and the reset, the first-quarter freeze, and the reset's rarity; her three phases survive as the entry's rows, the person's two check-ins, and the brief as the third channel.

**P29 (Recommended).** Restructuring around a person is refused structurally: no names on rows, no seat by re-titling, no permanent designation, no lead seat outside the pool without a written reason. People move by the internal loop or the period plan; domains are never merged and are split only by the variety pass; communication depth is Session 11's.

## 9. A departure, an open seat, and a designation held every service for a quarter

### 9.1 A departure is tension re-establishment, and what the diagnostic reads first

The record: "Thread removal is tension loss, not slot vacancy... every adjacent thread compensates at cost. This is the honest account of what turnover costs. Not a backfill problem. A tension re-establishment problem" (WP p.9). And the code-bearing version of a key person leaving: "the tension that node held redistributes, which costs something, the web is weaker until the node is re-established, but the knowledge does not leave. It lives in the system as documented processes, captured customer preferences, and skill maps of adjacent roles, and the ready-now pool surfaces who is furthest along" (WP p.14).

What the diagnostic reads first, the week of the departure, in order: the inventory's tension-loss row, field 15, "what the departing node held" (S5 P12), which the domain lead writes the day the departure is known; the leads' review's one line, what the node held and what the adjacent nodes are carrying (S5 P14's trigger), which is the departure's evidence line with the mode left open, because a departure is not itself a mode; the pool, read before the seat opens externally, with the reading recorded on the sheet (S5 P60); and the adjacent seats' thermal-load lines and check-in lines over the following weeks, which are where the compensation the record names becomes visible.

The second read is the one the record's five modes do not carry by name and its machine does: memory failure, "Every departure takes knowledge with it" (WP p.13). The diagnostic asks, of the departed node, whether what it held was in the system: the processes it ran (documented, or in its head), the customer preferences it carried (captured in the record, or in its book), the skill maps of the adjacent seats (on the sheets and person pages, or nowhere). Each answer that is "in its head" is written as sequence corruption (the capture did not happen) or skill invisibility (the map was not documented), so that the departure's cost is "bounded, temporary, calculable" (WP p.14) in the only unit this program carries, which is which mechanism now has to be maintained, and not a figure.

The third read, when the seat is re-filled: whether the re-established node carries the tension. The adjacent seats' lines fall back, the range use across sections stops, the schedule's re-sectioning ends. That is the read-back of section 7.4 applied to a seat, and it is the moment the departure's cost closes.

What is said to the floor. Three pages already hold pieces of this, and they have to be read together rather than overwritten. Session 3 wrote that a key-person departure is told at the next brief of every open period, in person, by the domain lead, before it is written, with its tension map (S3 P11.6). Session 7 wrote that "nothing about a person is said to the floor" and that a departure is a seat opened by tension loss on the sheet with what the node held, "and nothing more" (S7 P47). Session 8 wrote that a change reaches the team by four channels and by no announcement (S8 P49). This page reads all three as one rule with the content narrowed: the brief carries the structural fact as coverage, spoken, under its standing line "any change to a range, trigger, goal, or mechanism since the last brief" (S3 shift brief): which seat is open, which seats hold its functions this service, and which designation carries what the node held. That is Session 3's tension map, and it is what Session 3 meant by "the team hears the structural read rather than a rumor about a slot." It carries nothing about the person: not why they left, not a farewell, not a judgment, which is Session 7's rule. And it is the third channel, not an announcement, which is Session 8's. The three pages are consistent once the tension map is understood as coverage; where Session 3's wording ("announced") reads wider than that, this page narrows it and surfaces the narrowing rather than overwriting the position, and the practice is Session 11's.

### 9.2 An open seat is a load reading on the domain

Session 5 fixed the triggers (S5 P14) and Session 4's item 6 is where they land. What the diagnostic adds is the order of what is read while the seat is open, before the pipeline is read as a recruiting question: the adjacent seats' thermal-load lines and range use across sections, as a count of services; the designations doubled onto one person because the open seat's holder would have carried one; and the seat's own sheet, field 6's horizon, field 11's shift shape, and field 13's physical demands, read for whether the seat stays open because the sheet describes a seat the pool does not hold. Only after those is the pipeline read a recruiting question, and even then Session 5 says it is not a recruiting failure.

The hold-open case, "a seat the lead wants to hold open rather than fill below the bar" (S5 P14), is where the bandwidth order matters. The bar holds; Session 5 and the designer's seat both say a hole is held rather than filled below it. What the diagnostic reads is whether the adjacent seats' lines rise across the weeks it is held, and at three consecutive leads' reviews of a rising line the house's answer under the order, bandwidth first, is that covers come down before the floor is thinned (S3's attachment to `86akh3t00`), never that the bar drops. That consequence is founder-gated, and the line states it as a dependency.

### 9.3 A designation held every service for a quarter

Session 8's trigger: "A designation held by the same person on every service for a quarter goes to the mechanism reset as a question about whether it is a seat" (S8 18.9). This page answers what the question is, under the ruling, because the answer is not "make it a seat."

The read has three branches, and the reset takes whichever the record shows. First, the same person held the room because the Maitre d's absent services are a fixed set and one eligible holder was scheduled on them: a load finding on the Maitre d's seat's hours, carried by the schedule (the stack places the seat's services differently) or by the designation's rule (a rotation floor across the eligible holders), never by a second seat, a floor manager under another name, or any permanent elevation (ledger; S8 P4). Second, the same person held it because no one else had a complete readiness form and range card: skill invisibility or a training-infrastructure line, fixed by widening the eligible pool through forms placed on the schedule (S8 18.9's range). Third, the same person held it because the lead named them by preference: the record's "politics" (WP p.11), corrected by a rotation floor written into the designation's rule at the reset, since 18.9 already forbids persisting a designation without re-naming it and does not yet require rotation. In none of the three is the holder's performance read from the count, because a designation is not performance (carryover `86akhcz7b`), and in none of the three does the door or the sequence designation become a seat either.

The seat question, then, is a question about the Maitre d's seat's hours and the designation's rule. The Lead Host stays a designation (S8 P24), and the reset's entry is a structure change on the designation's rule with the quarter's brief records as its line.

**P30 (Recommended).** A departure is read in three passes: the tension-loss row, the leads' review line, the pool, and the adjacent seats' lines first; then whether what the node held was in the system, with each "in its head" written as sequence corruption or skill invisibility; then, when the seat is re-filled, whether the re-established node carries the tension. The floor hears the structural fact as coverage at the brief and nothing about the person; the practice is Session 11's.

**P31 (Recommended).** An open seat is read for the adjacent seats' lines, the doubled designations, and the sheet's horizon, shift shape, and demands before the pipeline is read as a recruiting question. A seat held open above the bar is read for the adjacent lines' rise, and the answer to a rise is covers before the floor, held on `86akh3t00`.

**P32 (Recommended).** A designation held by one person every service for a quarter is, at the reset, a question about the Maitre d's seat's hours and the designation's rule, in three branches: the schedule or a rotation floor; the eligible pool widened by forms; a rotation floor against preference. It is never a second seat, and the holder's performance is never read from the count.

## 10. (Re)building the team, translated against a house that compounds

### 10.1 Her rebuilding, and the record's compounding

"Whether you've hired a new team, inherited a team, or restructured to create a new team, the bedrock of your team is the people on it" (Book p.294), and the manager's first task is to "evaluate the team's skill and will and form your own impression of each person's contribution." The record's first claim is the opposite of bedrock in people: "an organization that holds its knowledge in the system gets smarter as it ages, where one that holds knowledge in people resets with every departure" (WP p.8), and "the cost of losing a person falls as the system holds more of what people know" (WP p.14). The house is not rebuilt after a departure; it compounds through one, and the diagnostic's job in section 9 is to read whether it did.

Three properties of the record bound what rebuilding can mean. The house compounds without headcount growth (ledger), so there is no new team to build; there is a period to add, with seats. New people always enter at the outside edge (WP p.9), so a rebuilt node is an edge node, and the only center seats are the leads, hired from outside only pre-opening and, after dinner's first quarter, only with a written reason on the partners' page that no one in the pool is on the track (S5 P60). And the building "rarely hires a manager from outside at all" (WP p.19), so rebuilding a domain's lead is the pool's work before it is recruiting's.

### 10.2 Her "deeply understand each team member," placed
