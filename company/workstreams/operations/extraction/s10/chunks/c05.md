

**Where creation happens at Sŏn, and when.** On the build metronome, which is "fast by system, never by grind" (S1 section 2), and in the pre-opening cadence, which is one long creator period at the partners' weekly working meeting (S4 9.2). In service: the mechanism reset and the principles revision, annually, at a closed hour (section 7); the charter drafting group for a new period, convened when a period is drafted and dissolved when it is ruled (S8 P41); the menu turn, the chef's (S4 P36); the content engine, Brandon's, daily (WP p.31); and module authorship by any qualified team member, paid (WP p.19; S7 P33). The floor's creator mode is the last two of those and the feedback channel's "suggest improvements" (WP p.22); the floor is never convened to brainstorm, because a convened floor is a meeting in service hours or a closed service.

**What never happens in the room during service.** Any creation. The seam protection forbids a build meeting in the building during service hours (S4 P9), and her balance-sheet moment, an operator item that turns into a strategy brainstorm, is exactly what the protection exists to stop from happening in a dining room in dinner's first quarter, when "the build engine is idling and the founders are in the room" (S1 section 2). Inside the leads' review it is refused by the hard stop: an item that opens into a creator conversation goes to the partners' page as an ask with an owner. At the partners' review pre-opening it is the meeting's own purpose, and in service it belongs to the reset. The service metronome is set by the register and the table (S1); creation runs on the other clock.

**Her personal typology** ("some people are operators, and some people are makers or creators," Book p.319): refused as a type. The strands are two kinds of work and not two kinds of person (S5 P7), and the house reads no one for temperament (section 13).

**P25 (Recommended).** Creation at Sŏn runs on the build metronome and at closed hours: the pre-opening partners' review, the annual reset and revision, a charter drafting group dissolved at its ruling, the menu turn, the content engine, and paid module authorship. ## 11. Decision-making in meetings

### 11.1 Her five methods, against the register

Her methods: autocratic, consensus, democratic, consultative, delegated (Book p.324), and her rule is to be explicit about which one is running and who decides (Book p.321). At Sŏn the method is fixed by the register before any room exists, which is her rule made structural: "the range within which that owner acts without asking is stated in advance" (S3 4.1). The owner decides inside a range, which is her autocratic method bounded by a range and a trigger. Class B is consultative by rule: "the owning domain decides; any domain whose outcomes are affected is consulted before the event, not informed after" (S3 4.3). Class A is neither consensus nor democratic: with two partners it is "both or neither, with the status quo as default" (S3 4.5, held under `86akh5u77`), and with three the record's one instance is lunch as "an equal decision among all three partners" (WP p.36; S4 P57, founder-gated). Delegated is the register's own form, "ranges set one level out" (S3 4.1, rule 3), and never a per-decision act. Nothing at Sŏn is decided by a vote in a room, and nothing is decided by a room. Her closing example, "I'm the decision-maker. I'd like to consult all of you, but I will ultimately decide" (Book pp. 324 to 325), is the register's entry read aloud, and it is read before the meeting rather than at it.

The interim two-partner rule is the founders' own meeting decision process and it is not this page's to land; the partners' review "runs on whatever rule is ratified and holds the status quo until one is" (S4 P54).

### 11.2 Disagree and commit, and the senior person speaking last

**Disagree and commit** (Book p.318): absorbed, with the disagreement written. A decision inside another owner's range is theirs; an objection has a destination, the Class B or C hold as a joint half-page (S8 P50); once the owner decides, the objection is on the record and the decision stands. What replaces "commit to supporting its success" as a norm is the read-back: every change is read back on the next scorecard by the source that raised it (S9 P26), so a decision that was not supported shows as a mechanism not cleared, on a record, and not as a person's attitude. Her leader saying "I'm going to disagree and commit" (Book p.327) survives as a founder writing a question on a domain page and leaving the domain owner's decision standing (S4 P53).

**The most senior person holds their opinion until the end** (Book p.326; the editor's pull quote at Book pp. 329 to 330): survives whole where it has an object. The record's rooms are parallel, so in the leads' review there is no senior person. Where a founder attends the leads' review by standing invitation (S4 P22), the founder speaks last, and the rule is written into section 17.6's entry. The monthly domain read is speaking last by construction: each partner writes questions on the other's page after reading it whole, and the domain owner answers (S4 P53). The lead-and-partner conversation is on the lead's page with the partner writing questions (S4 P59). Her exception, a leader putting forward a provocative idea to elicit a reaction, belongs to the partners' review pre-opening and to no room in service.

### 11.3 Frameworks, logs, and the personality callout

**Decision-making frameworks** (RAPID, SPADE, a seven-step process; Book p.326): refused as frameworks. The register's form, owner, range, triggers with destinations, is the one framework the house teaches, and it is what the why block delivers as vocabulary (S9 13.4). A second framework beside it would be two rules for one decision.

**Decision logs** (Book pp. 326 to 327): survive as the records the mechanisms already write, and never as "a spreadsheet of every decision your team has made." The partners' page is "a decision log by versioned decision" (S4 P53; Deck 3.0, p.94); the goal page carries its change record with the reason and what it displaced (S4 P35); the inventory has a version log (S8 P49); the mechanism map has a log (S9 I4). Her reason for mixed success, that logs "are most effective when they're widely adopted and rigorously kept current" (Book p.327), is why Sŏn's logs are the mechanisms' own records and not a separate artifact anyone has to remember to keep. Her "edit the log when a decision has been changed and explain why" is the versioned change with its reason field (S9 P24, P25).

**"Decision-making and personality types"** (Book p.325): refused. Her exercise places each person on a square of the decision process and reads where they are stuck. At Sŏn no decision is the group's, so there is no process for a group to stand on, and the grid is never used to read another person (section 13). What her exercise wanted, a way to say "we are blocked and here is why," is the hold's half-page: the problem, the options, the trade-offs, written jointly (S8 P50).

**P26 (Recommended).** Her five decision methods are replaced by the register: the owner decides inside a range; Class B is consultative by rule; Class A is both or neither with the status quo as default, held under `86akh5u77`; delegation is ranges set one level out. Disagree and commit survives with the disagreement written as a hold and the commitment replaced by the read-back. The senior person speaks last survives for a founder in any room and is the domain read's own form.

**P27 (Recommended).** Decision frameworks are refused for the register's one form; decision logs survive as the partners' page, the goal page's change record, the inventory's log, and the mechanism map's log, never as a separate spreadsheet; the personality-types exercise is refused and its function is the hold's joint half-page.

**P28 (Founder-gated).** The interim two-partner rule as the founders' meeting decision process (`86akh5u77`, extended and not filed twice), and the three-partner rule beyond the one instance the record states (S4 P57).

## 13. The work style grid's team use

### 13.1 What is settled and what is open

The grid is out of recruiting (S5 P8), lives inside the working-with-me document as a self-awareness exercise for founders and leads (S1 8.2; S7 P53), and never enters structure, scheduling, or placement (S8 P40). Three carryovers (`86akh7qq7`, `86akhb2wj`, `86akhcz6v`) route one question here: whether it has any team use, "as language people use about themselves and never as an instrument that reads a person for a seat."

### 13.2 The decision

The grid has no team use beyond the placement Sessions 1 and 7 already hold, and this page adds one narrow use in one direction. The reasons:

1. A language a team uses "about itself," on a roster that dissolves every service, is a language people use about each other. Her own illustration is a leader in a meeting saying a colleague is "acting a bit 'red'" (Book p.328), which is a read of a person spoken in a room, and the house refuses a read of a person anywhere but the person's own line (S9 P42).
2. The record's vocabulary for difference is the strands, "two kinds of work, not two kinds of person" (S5 3.3), and canon's for the floor is Nunchi and Jeong. A second vocabulary about persons would compete with the one about work, and on a floor the vocabulary about persons wins, because it is easier to say.
3. Four names are a typology, and a typology on a floor becomes a placement within a week: "the room needs a Director tonight" is the sentence S8 P40 exists to forbid, and it would be said at the brief where designations are named.
4. An hourly person is never asked to write a working-with-me (S7 P49), so there is no surface on which a floor person could place themselves that is not a form the house holds about them; and the person page refuses a rating (S4 P18), which a self-placement read by a lead is.
5. The one use her material has for it that the house needs, "a common language" for founders and leads who "work in separate domains" (S1 8.2), is already met where it lives.

**The one narrow use.** A lead's working-with-me is readable by the lead's domain on the team home (S7 P49). The lead's self-placement is therefore language the domain may use about the lead, upward, at the check-in's fourth question and on the channel, because the lead wrote it about themselves for that reason. The reverse never happens. A person who wants the vocabulary for themselves can read Session 1's instrument on the team home and place themselves in their own head; nothing records it.

**How misuse is caught.** The grid's four names appearing on any person page, scorecard, schedule note, designation block, brief record, close, sheet, or leads' review line fails readiness row 120 (section 18), which is a search like S9's row 115. A check-in line in a person's own words saying a lead placed them on it is a range breach on the lead's check-in entry (section 17.5) and goes to whoever holds a lead's check-in (`86akhb2jg`); until that closes, it goes to the partners' review as a load reading on the lead.

**P30 (Recommended).** The work style grid has no team use beyond its placement inside a lead's working-with-me document. Its one team-facing use is upward and one-directional: a lead's domain may use the lead's own self-placement about the lead. It never appears on any record about a person, never names a designation's need, and never enters a room as a read of anyone; its four names on any such record fail a readiness row.

## 14. Community after the cohort, the mentor after the plan, and designations as rotation

### 14.1 Community in the steady state

Her onboarding wanted "a sense of community with other new hires," and Session 7 found it met pre-opening by the cohort itself, "the community her section wants is the cohort itself and needs no mechanism" (S7 section 3). A later hire arrives alone into a running house. Whether the steady state needs a mechanism is carryover `86akhb2wj`'s question.

The team's position: no new mechanism, and an honest partial. What does the work for a person arriving alone: the mentor from the pool, same strand and domain, matched for language and overlap (S7 P41); the paired services as "the teaching body" (S7 P42); the brief's one thing naming the seat's new holder as a fact (S6 P71) and, on this page, the slot's fourth item naming their first solo as a thread added; the peer recognition platform, where the roster's voice reaches them; and the house's own precedent that community is transmitted by mentoring and not manufactured, since the first wave mentors the second (S7 P8). A batched start date, so that later hires arrive as a small cohort, is a hiring-calendar choice the hiring partner and the leads can make when the pipeline allows (S6 3.9) and it is noted as an option, not recommended as a culture mechanism, because a calendar that waits for a cohort holds a seat open longer than the pool read allows.

What remains partial is the thing section 3.6 named: the record built belonging in full for the customer and for the team wrote a break space. Belonging among the team is not a condition the philosophy names and is the outcome the conditions produce (section 2.3), and the read of it is the pulse and, over time, retention read as tension-loss departures by count (S9 P13). The one direct mechanism the house has is the whole-house gathering, founder-gated. This page does not invent another, because every candidate (a social, a meal, an outing) is either the staff meal the record is silent on (P12) or a manufactured activity Ma refuses.

### 14.2 The mentor as a standing relationship

Session 7: the relationship runs "through the plan and after it as the person chooses," said at the fifth check-in, with the stack stopping the overlap at the plan's close (S7 P42). Session 9 recorded the shape the person states in the career conversation block, in the person's words (S9 I7). What the house does with it after the plan, designed here:

Nothing formal, by design. The house records only the shape the person stated and takes no further act, because a recorded standing relationship is a line about two people, and the person page refuses lines about anyone but the person in their own words. What the house may do: where the person states "continue," the stack may treat overlap with the former mentor as a scheduling preference of the person's, like any preference on the sheet, honored where the schedule allows and never required, and written nowhere on the mentor's page. What the house refuses: the mentor becoming a sponsor or an advocate ("The system is the advocate," WP p.11); a coach, which is S12's and founder-gated as a spend for leads (S7 P22); a channel for the lead, which S7 P42 already refuses; or a person the lead asks about the former mentee. The mentor's later place in the house is the pool: the former mentee becomes eligible to mentor once their own plan has closed with its goal held (S7 P41), which is how the relationship reproduces without being recorded. The mentor's own mentoring is recognized beyond shift only if Session 13 says so (S7 P35).

### 14.3 Designations as rotation, never status

Session 8 holds that a designation is named at the brief, lasts one service, and is never permanent (S8 P22, P24, P25); Session 9 holds that a designation held by one person every service for a quarter is a reset question about the seat's hours and the designation's rule, "never a second seat and never a read of the holder" (S9 P32). Carryover `86akhcz6v` asks this page to hold designations rotating as a development mechanism and not a status.

The rule this page adds is the rotation floor, written as part of each designation's rule on the register (section 17.4): among the seats eligible for a designation on a service (a complete readiness form and range card, no non-negotiable line; S8 P25), no eligible person holds it on every service they work in a quarter while another eligible person held it on none. The floor is a rule about the roster's shape and not about anyone's performance; its read is the count per quarter the stack surfaces to the reset (S9 I3's third rule), and the reset's three branches are Session 9's (the schedule or a rotation floor; the eligible pool widened by forms placed on the schedule; a rotation floor against preference). Development is the reason: holding the room once is the way a range is rehearsed at scale, and the eligible pool widening by forms is the path making itself visible.

Never a status, stated as refusals: a designation carries no pay line (S13's), no mark on the person page beyond the brief's record, no title, and no place on a sheet; it is never named as a reward or withheld as a sanction, and feedback to a holder is Session 12's (`86akhcz71`); its count is never read as the holder's performance (S9 P32; `86akhcz7b`). A lead who names the same person every night "because they are good at it" has, by the third quarter, made a permanent room holder under another name, which is a refused carrier (S9 P36).

**P31 (Recommended).** The steady state needs no new community mechanism: the mentor from the pool, the paired services, the brief naming the new holder and the first solo, and the recognition platform do the work for a person arriving alone, and belonging among the team is read as an outcome by the pulse and tension-loss departures. The partial is named: the record builds belonging for the customer in full and for the team as a break space, and the one direct team mechanism is the gathering, founder-gated.

**P32 (Recommended).** After the plan the house records only the shape the person stated and takes no further act; a stated "continue" is a scheduling preference of the person's, honored where the schedule allows and written on no one's page. The mentor never becomes a sponsor, a coach, or a channel for the lead; the relationship reproduces through the pool.

**P33 (Recommended).** Each designation's rule carries a rotation floor: no eligible person holds it on every service they work in a quarter while another eligible person held it on none, read by the stack's count at the reset and never as the holder's performance. A designation carries no pay line, no page mark, no title, and is never a reward or a sanction.
