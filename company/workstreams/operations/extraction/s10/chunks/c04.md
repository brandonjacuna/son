## 8. Meetings at Sŏn: her meeting material against the mechanism map

### 8.1 What a meeting is here

Her chapter's premise is that "when you think of teams, you think of meetings" (Book p.311). The house has no meeting in her sense during service: the brief opens a service and the close ends it, and both are spoken to a roster that is about to work or has just finished (S4 P10, P12). Everything else that gathers people sits at closed hours (S4 P9). What the map holds that is meeting-shaped: the brief, the close, the check-in, the leads' review, the partners' review, the domain read, the lead-and-partner conversation, the house review, the gate review, the reset, the principles revision, and the whole-house gathering if approved (S4 10.2). Her material is read against each, and what applies is mostly already carried.

### 8.2 Her groundwork and common understanding

"They're only as successful as the relationships between the people involved... invest in creating a common understanding and setting meeting norms up front" (Book pp. 312 to 313): "who's on the team, what each person's role is, and how they prefer to work" (Book p.313). At Sŏn the common understanding is written and read before anyone sits down: the web rendered (S5 `86akh7ru5`), the inventory's rows, the seat sheets, the register, the lead's working-with-me for how the lead prefers to work (S7 P49), and the person page for the person's own account. Her "agree on team norms" is refused as an act a group performs: the norms are the non-negotiables, the register, and the spines, written once, versioned, and the same for every gathering. A team that agreed its own norms would have written a rule with no version and no owner above it, which is drift. Her "extended kickoff meeting to proxy some of the functions of an offsite" (Book p.313) is, for the leads, their first partners' review and first leads' review (S7 14.1), and for the house, the cohort's orientation block.

### 8.3 Her roles

**The DRI**, "the person responsible for the meeting's success" (Book p.313): refused, as Session 9 refused the DRI generally (S9 P38, P39, I15). A meeting-shaped mechanism at Sŏn has an owner on the map (the owner of the room for the brief and the close; the alternating chair for the leads' review; the partners for their review; the center for the house review), and that owner has a range, which is more than a DRI carries.

**The facilitator**, who keeps time, gets through the agenda, tracks participation, and "feels empowered to move the meeting along" (Book pp. 314, 321): absorbed into the chair, whose "only added duty is the clock and the record" (S4 P22). Her rotating facilitator is the alternating chair, and the reason is stronger than hers: "a permanent chair is the first step back toward a general manager" (S4 5.1). The facilitator's participation-tracking ("we've heard a lot from Jen," Book p.318) is refused as a role: the leads' review has three seats and the brief is spoken to a roster that does not debate; the participation rule the house does carry is the domain read's written questions, where every partner writes and no one speaks over another (S4 P53).

**The notetaker**: refused as a role; the record is the mechanism's own. The brief's block is on the OS surface (S4 P10); the close is written by the people who held the tables (S4 P12); the leads' review's notes are the chair's on the team home (S4 5.2); the partners' page is the decision log (S4 P53); the house memo is the record (S4 P32). Her rule that "the best notes... include the top-level discussion topics, decisions made, and follow-ups or action items... with each follow-up assigned an owner and an agreed-upon time frame" (Book p.314) is already every review's form, with the one rule hers lacks: an owner "is a lead, a domain, or a unit, never a person below another" (S4 5.2). Her notes as "psychological safety... if there's a disagreement down the road, you can refer to the meeting notes" is the versioned decision's own purpose (Deck 3.0, p.94). Her notes "shared more widely if you have a transparent culture" is the transparency line, held (`86akh5uby`).

### 8.4 Mutual ownership, purpose, and structure

Her signs of ownership (norms maintained by participants, participation broad, roles rotating; Book p.315) hold at the leads' review by construction: two co-leads, alternating, no one above them. Her "model ownership. Be on time, try not to reschedule" (Book p.315) is the stack's: the leads' review has a day, the check-in is placed by the stack and its moves are counted (S4 P20), and a review that runs into the brief shortens its agenda (S4 5.1). Her "one or two purposes" and PAL (Book pp. 315 to 316) are every review's fixed agenda; the leads' review has eight items in a fixed order and the house review a fixed procedure (S4 P26, P32). Her "Limit" is the hard stop, and the Sŏn rule is sharper: the stop is before the brief and holds every week.

### 8.5 Her norms, each

**Logistics** (Book pp. 316 to 317). Meeting time: fixed on the map, and the leads' review's day moves once, when the early morning opens, to the lowest-tempo day (S4 P22, P25). Delegation: the chef's delegate at the leads' review is chef-gated (S4 P24); a partner cannot delegate a Class A vote (S3 4.5); the room's owner at the brief is the room designation when the Maitre d is absent (S8 P25), which is delegation by rule and never per meeting. Pre-reads: the memo twenty-four hours ahead with a reading block (S4 P32); the leads' review reads the record on the team home; the brief has none, its block is on the surface for anyone who arrives late (S4 P10). Action items: updated on the record before the review, and read first at the house review (S4 P32). Electronics: "no service radio in the room; no stack surface open except the agenda's reads" (S4 P27), and on the floor the phone is pocket-carried with "no visible screen in the dining room" (Canon 1.0, section 08).

**No topics are undiscussable**: section 6.4.

**Be inclusive** (Book p.318): the leads' review's alternation and the domain read in writing; her rule against restating another's idea without credit has no mechanism and needs none in a three-seat room.

**Disagree and commit** (Book p.318): section 11.2.

**Have a parking lot** (Book p.318): refused as a device. An item off the spine at the brief goes to the check-in or the channel; an item off the agenda at the leads' review goes to next week's agenda or the channel, with an owner; there is no lot, because "otherwise, the parking lot will become the town dump" (Book p.318) is exactly what the loop-closure window exists to prevent.

**Respect action items** (Book pp. 318 to 319): every review's notes carry owners and dates, and the leads' review opens on item 2, the captures waiting on a lead past seven days (S4 P26).

**Reconfigure the meeting every three to six months** (Book p.319): the mechanism reset, after the first quarter, after the first year, and annually, reading every mechanism's record, cost, and buy and continuing, changing, or ending it (S4 P39), plus the house review's quarterly reflection paragraph on the review itself (S4 P32). Her three options, refresh the norms, evolve the people and topics, start over, are the reset's three outcomes. Her "consider polling participants or having a neutral person do so" is refused as the survey; the reset reads records.

### 8.6 Her cost argument, and the ledger

The bandwidth ledger is that calculation, done in time rather than figures, per team member per month, per lead per week, per partner per week (S4 10.3), and its total for an hourly person is the brief, the close, fifteen minutes of check-in, one pulse question, a recognition when they choose, the review every three to six months, and one paid shift per quarter if the gathering is approved. ## 9. The brief's team-content slot, and the gathering's team-making half

### 9.1 The slot

Session 4 fixed the brief's spine and bounded a fifth slot, "team content, owned elsewhere, bounded by what time remains before the scene trigger," and its specification refuses "any item about a person; any content that displaces the readiness block; any trigger for a transition in the room" (S4 P10, 13.1). The Realist's rule from that page: the operational block is "a checklist the rush cannot delete," and the team content sits after it, never inside it (S4 3.1). Carryover `86akh67xe` asks this page to design the slot's content inside those bounds.

**What goes in it.** One item per brief, spoken by the owner of the room, chosen from a fixed list in this priority:

1. **The designations' line.** Each designation's holder for the service (the room, the door, the sequence, the pass) says one sentence on what they need from the roster tonight. This is her "meeting roles" reduced to the roster's, and it is the only place a roster hears its own shape in its holders' voices. On a compressed window it is the whole slot, and it is the one item that also serves readiness, which is why it is first.
2. **What the system learned.** One capture from the last close, read as process and never as person: what allowed it, what was updated, what the next server inherits. This is blame the process made audible, and it is the mechanism by which safety to err becomes a thing the roster has heard and not only a rule it read. The reviewing lead chooses it; the room's owner speaks it.
3. **A principle against a moment.** Once a week at most, one sentence from the charter's principles read against something the house did in the last week, spoken by the room's owner. This is the principles revision's raw material (S2 P6.5) reaching the floor in the only form the floor can hear it, and it waits on principles version one (`86akh3tg0`).
4. **A thread added.** A readiness form passed or a fluency connection recorded since the last brief, named as a fact, because the record says a person's growth "adds threads to the collective web" (WP p.11) and the roster is the web on the night. Named like a recognition: read, not editorialized. It is fourth because it names a person, and the rule below governs how.

**What may never be in it.** Anything about a person beyond a fact the person page already shows (a form passed, a first solo held), and never a read, a rating, a comparison, or a correction. A script for the service. A survey question or a poll. An opener about how anyone feels. A personal share or an icebreaker. A sales or upsell instruction. A lagging figure. A structure change announced, because a change reaches the team by four channels and never by announcement (S8 P49). A build item, because the seam forbids it (S4 P9). Anything that runs past the scene trigger.

**Length.** The slot has no floor and its ceiling is bounded by the scene trigger. The team sets its ceiling, as a reset parameter, at five minutes on a full pre-service window and zero on a compressed one, where a compressed window is one on which the readiness block ran in its thirty-second form. Five minutes is a duration and not a financial figure; it is flagged for the reset, which reads the first quarter's brief records for how often the slot ran at all.

**Who holds it.** The owner of the room for that period runs the brief (S4 P11, ruled to the Maitre d), and on services the Maitre d does not work the room designation runs it (S8 P25), holding the slot with the same list and the same refusals. The room designation's card (S8 `86akhcz4g`) gains the slot's list. The kitchen's item, if the pass owner speaks at item 1, is chef-gated.

**By period.** Dinner as stated. The early morning's brief is a window brief whose slot is item 1 alone, because its designation set is smaller and its window shorter (S8 P8). Late night carries no second brief and no second slot: where periods run back to back with the same crew, the second brief is "a thirty-second readiness insert, never a second gathering" (S4 P14). Lunch has no charter and no brief.

**How it is read.** The slot produces no line about anyone. Its only record is the brief's record on the OS surface noting which item ran or that the slot was dropped, so that the reset can count briefs with a slot against briefs without across the quarter. A slot dropped on most briefs of a quarter is read as sequence corruption on the brief's window, a window problem and not a lead's, and it is one of the two environment-side sources section 15 adds to cooling failure's line.

### 9.2 The whole-house gathering's team-making half, if approved

Session 4 wrote the gathering as conditional and founder-gated: on the same closed day as the house review, after it, paid as a scheduled shift, carrying the review's one thing, the quarter's goal-page states, the recognitions of the quarter, and the next quarter's goals, and nothing about a person (S4 P38). The bandwidth ledger already prices it: one paid shift per quarter per person (S4 10.3). This page designs the half Session 4 left, inside those bounds.

**The team-making half, in order after Session 4's content.**

1. **The threads of the quarter.** The forms passed and the fluency connections recorded in the quarter, read as facts like recognitions. This is the web growing, said aloud once a quarter to the whole web.
2. **What the house learned.** The captures of the quarter that changed a process, as process. The floor wrote them; the house hears what it wrote.
3. **The principles read back.** The moments of the quarter against the principles, spoken by the center, which is the principles revision's read-back reaching the room that produced the moments (S2 P6.5). At the first two gatherings this is the revision itself, as Session 3 tied it (S3 P11.7).
4. **Coverage.** The seats opened in the quarter and the pool read, as coverage only and nothing about anyone who left (S9 P30).
5. **One open question.** One question the reset or the house review could not close, put to the house with the feedback channel as its destination and a stated window. This is the item that makes "a say in the rules" a mechanism (section 3.9): the floor is asked, once a quarter, in the one room it is in, and its answer has a place to go.

**Refused in it.** A personal opener or closer, an icebreaker, the work style exercise, a survey, a speech about culture, and any structure change announced there rather than already reached by the four channels. Nothing about a person beyond a fact on their page.

**If declined.** The review's outcome reaches the house by the three channels only (S4 P38). What the house loses: the one occasion the whole house is in one room, so that the record's "every element in relationship with every other" (WP p.8) has fewer relationships across periods and services than the structure can otherwise produce; the principles' read-back stays written; and the floor's say in the rules stays indirect, read from its records and never asked. The team's recommendation stands with Sessions 3 and 4: approve, at a cost in closed service the founders weigh, with the content bounded as above.

**P20 (Recommended).** The brief's team slot carries one item from a fixed list in priority: the designations' one line each, what the system learned from the last close as process, a principle against a moment once a week at most, and a thread added as a fact. **P21 (Recommended).** If the whole-house gathering is approved, its team-making half carries, after Session 4's content, the quarter's threads, what the house learned, the principles read back, coverage, and one open question put to the house with the channel as its destination. It refuses an opener, an icebreaker, the grid, a survey, a culture speech, and any announcement. If declined, the floor's say in the rules stays indirect and the page says so.

## 10. Operator mode and creator mode

Her distinction: operator meetings have agendas and are for "decisions, updates, priorities, and alignment"; creator meetings are for brainstorming, "much less structure," and may produce nothing but ideas; a good team has both, and the creator time is built "primarily through offsites" (Book pp. 319 to 320). Her example is an operator question about a balance sheet that became an unconstrained five-year vision.
