## Standing rules on every word you write

- The customer is the "customer." Never "guest." Absolute, all contexts, internal included.
- **No em dashes.** Use commas, colons, semicolons, or restructure. This is canon and it is also a program rule.
- No performed conviction: never "we believe," "we hope," "our goal is," "we are committed to." Declarative over aspirational.
- Sentence case for all headings and labels.
- **No daypart code names** (Good Energy, Dosi, Luxx) in any output. Refer to dayparts by time of day or service period. Canon uses the code names; you do not reproduce them.
- Brand facts defer to brand canon. Do not assert a brand fact from memory. Everything you need is quoted above; if you need something not quoted, mark it as a gap rather than recalling it.
- **No financial figures generated, estimated, or recalled.** If a figure is needed, state that financials are not a context source for this program and flag the gap.
- Profanity never appears in written output.
- Canon's voice rules apply inward: copy never names its own effect; every noun carries its own weight; the sentence ends at its last load-bearing word.

## Form of the deliverable

A single markdown page, structured in numbered sections with numbered sub-sections, in the shape of the Session 3 page. Prose, not an outline. Declarative, standalone, wiki-ready: a person who has not read the book should be able to read this page and run from it.

Every position is numbered and marked with exactly one of:
- **(Recommended)** — the team's position, ready for founder ratification
- **(Founder-gated)** — requires founder input the team cannot supply
- **(Chef-gated)** — deliberately left open for the chef partner
- **(Team-filled)** — populated once the team exists

Nothing is marked final or landed. Argue founder-gated work to the ceiling one founder can reach, stake the recommendation, then mark what Dominic, the chef, or the team must close. Never present as landed what only the group can land.

Quote her and cite the page for every claim you attribute to the book. Quote the record and cite it for every claim you attribute to Sŏn. Where the record is silent, say so plainly and mark the gap.

End the page with:
1. **A completed readiness test section** presenting the cadence rows you wrote, numbered from 21, in S3's three-column form, with a line stating the test is now complete.
2. **Instrument specifications.** For each cadence instrument this session implies (agenda templates, the shift-native 1:1 form, the goal page, the scoring record, the house review memo, the daypart charter's placement, the founder cadence's record, the mechanism-reset review, whatever else you land on): what it must contain, what it must do, what it must refuse to do. Specifications, never instruments. These are consumed by downstream clusters later and gated on the document methodology.
3. **A findings section** for anything you find inside Sŏn's record: conflicts, silences, or two accounts of the same thing. Session 1 found two, Session 2 found three, Session 3 found four. Report what you actually find, including none.
4. **A marks register:** a table of every numbered position with its mark, and a count by mark.

Also return, after the page and clearly separated under a heading `# POST-EXTRACTION WORK ITEMS` and a heading `# CARRYOVER ITEMS`:

**Post-extraction work items.** Each with a title, a type (exactly one of: founder decision, document, process, instrument, structure), and a body explaining what it is and why it surfaced. These become ClickUp subtasks.

**Carryover items.** Each with a title, the target session or sessions by number and the reason, a priority (urgent, high, normal, low), and a body written so the receiving session can act on it without reading this page. Route only what genuinely couples to a later session.

Write the page. Take the time it needs.
