## 1. What the record holds about the founders and their domains

**WP p.03.** "Nearly every serious restaurant company is led by a chef, and that model has produced the best rooms in the country. Sŏn is built on a different distribution of founding domains. Two of its founders own everything outside the kitchen, and together they cover what the business actually requires. One owns the concept, the operating systems, and the experience. The other owns the capital, the financial architecture, and the technology build. That is what makes the culinary seat a founding one rather than a hire: the chef partner owns the culinary vision as an equal founding partner, freed from carrying the rest of the business alone. Brandon Acuña-Cardona is the public voice and the origin of the operating concepts. Dominic Thomas owns the financial model and the machine."

**WP p.05.** "Dominic Thomas is the second founder and an equal principal, and he owns the half of the business the industry most often gets wrong. He comes from institutional wealth management... In the company he is the hybrid of two roles most restaurants never staff with this caliber of person: the CFO who owns the financial model and the capital architecture, and the CTO who owns the data architecture and the technology build."

**WP p.05.** "Sŏn simply hands the two domains outside the kitchen to founders who own them outright, which lets the chef partner own the food as a founding seat rather than carry the whole business."

**WP p.06.** "The preparation that answers the objection is not more years. It is a code-bearing organization built so it does not depend on either founder's memory... paired with a founding team that covers the two domains where first-time operators most often fail: the experience and the systems on one side, the capital and the controls on the other. The mark of these founders is a company built to run without them."

**Note for the session.** The record assigns Brandon "the operating systems" and Dominic "the data architecture and the technology build." WP p.21-22 then describes the stack's proprietary layer as "the OS surface and the agent layer." The record does not say which founder owns the OS surface. This is the seam the founder-job-description carryover (`86ajgnhp7`) predicted: two founders whose stated domains both land on the same object.

## 2. What the record holds about the chef seat (the conflict)

**White paper account.**

- WP p.03: "the chef partner owns the culinary vision as an equal founding partner, freed from carrying the rest of the business alone."
- WP p.10: "The Chef Partner owns culinary execution during service, which is not the same as culinary direction overall."
- WP p.10: The Operations Lead and Maitre d "are parallel. Neither reports to the other. They are co-leads of different systems serving the same center, the culinary and conceptual vision."
- WP p.36: "whether lunch is ever built at all is an equal decision among all three partners, the chef partner included. If there is no good answer for lunch, it comes out of the plan entirely. That is a real option, not a courtesy."
- WP p.37: "The culinary vision is real and it is led by the chef partner, but the business does not rest on the survival of any one person in it. That is the difference between a chef's restaurant and a company."

**Canon account.**

- Canon 02, Endorsed Architecture Model, non-negotiables at the architecture tier: "culinary identity belongs to the brand, not the chef."
- Deck p.33, under "THE SOLE MANDATORY STRUCTURAL CONSTANT": "The endorsed model: one master Sŏn, visible across all four dayparts through the glyph. Culinary identity belongs to the brand, not the chef."
- Canon, What Remains, [HELD] — Pending Executive Chef Hire, item 1: "Session 1B — Culinary Philosophy (Korean Hospitality Consultant). The brand owns the culinary identity. The chef executes within it. Mahk standard is an explicit hiring criterion."
- Deck p.89: "The brand owns the culinary identity. Mahk is an explicit hiring criterion. The frameworks above it cannot change. [Requires the chef:] The dishes and the technique that execute within it."
- Canon 01, Mahk requires: "the Executive Chef can articulate what each dish is reaching for, not just what it tastes like."
- Canon, What Remains, item 3, Tableware Taxonomy requires: "chef's relationship to Bangjja Yugi, Baekja, Buncheong, and Cheongja (a hiring criterion)."
- Deck p.87-89 heading: "HELD · PENDING EXECUTIVE CHEF HIRE." Deck p.92: "Chef determines, then the industrial designer brief opens."

**The sharpened reading available to the session.** The two documents may not be describing the same seat. The white paper says "chef partner," a founding seat with an equal vote on whether a daypart exists at all. The canon says "Executive Chef," a hire, gated by hiring criteria the brand sets. A hiring criterion is an instrument applied to a candidate; it is not an instrument that can be applied to a founding partner on the same terms. The conflict is therefore not only about who owns culinary identity. It is about whether the third seat is a partner or a hire, and the two artifacts answer differently.

## 3. What the record holds about authority and decision rights

### 3a. Canon claims total decision authority over the company

- Deck p.01, cover, marked "GOVERNING": "Brand and experiential guidelines. The source of truth for every decision made under this name." Marked "LIVING DOCUMENT · VERSION 3.0."
- Canon preamble: "Every decision in this document is closed unless marked [HELD] or [DEFERRED]... The six-tier authority hierarchy governs all decisions. Higher tiers override lower tiers without exception." Marked "Version 1.0."
- Deck p.93: "TO RESOLVE ANY CONFLICT, READ UP THE TIERS. Higher tiers override lower tiers. Without exception."
- Deck p.94: "When this document conflicts with a habit, the document wins. When it conflicts with itself, the higher tier wins." And: "A living document changes by versioned decision, never by drift."

Neither artifact states a rule for what happens when canon conflicts with a different founding document. Both state a rule only for canon against habit, and canon against itself.

### 3b. The two canon artifacts state different six-tier hierarchies

This is a verified conflict inside the record. Both artifacts are on the allowlist. Both present the hierarchy as closed and governing.

| Tier | `brand-guidelines.md` section 02 (Version 1.0) | Deck p.32 and p.93 (Version 3.0) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cultural Frameworks (Jaeyeonmi, Ma, Mahk) | Cultural frameworks (Jaeyeonmi, Ma, Mahk) |
| 2 | Positioning + Playground Philosophy | **Company non-negotiables** (no exploitation, toxicity, or disposable people; employee-first) |
| 3 | Jeong + Nunchi (service philosophy) | Positioning and philosophy (the three qualities; Playground, Jeong, Nunchi) |
| 4 | Brand Architecture (endorsed model, 선 on every surface) | Architecture and identity (the 선 mandate, endorsed model, closed systems) |
| 5 | Voice and Tone | Executional systems (voice, type, color, space, sound, service, digital, photography) |
| 6 | Persona Profiles | Persona profiles |

The material difference: the deck ranks the company's people non-negotiables at tier 2, with override authority over positioning, philosophy, architecture, and every executional system. The text version does not place them in the hierarchy at all. For an operating charter this is the load-bearing question, because it decides whether "no disposable people" outranks a brand or service decision or merely sits beside it.

### 3c. The record holds decision rights in instance form, repeatedly

The carryover (`86akh2qt2`) records that the record holds "exactly one rule for a decision that crosses domains." Reading the full record, the record in fact holds a recurring pattern, stated case by case and never generalized:

- **Lunch.** WP p.36: "whether lunch is ever built at all is an equal decision among all three partners, the chef partner included."
- **Sonic system.** Canon 07 and Deck p.75: "Tier 1 (Brandon only) — criteria, BPM ranges / Tier 2 (management sign-off) — dinner, late night, morning exterior track additions / Tier 3 (floor management) — Dosi context, interior morning staff-facing, level adjustments. No streaming algorithm input at any tier."
- **The floor.** Canon 08: "Floor authority: server owns the table — attunement calls, approach timing, whether a dish gets a spoken note or arrives in silence. Floor manager owns the room — coverage, pattern recognition, backs up without intercepting. Routine recovery pre-authorized. Escalation triggers: financial recovery above threshold / kitchen failure affecting multiple sections / genuine emergency."
- **The David protocol.** Canon 08: "David protocol: system decision, never floor call... Never improvised."
- **The design system.** Canon 12: "Governed exclusively by the Design Systems Architect. A request requiring a value outside the token system is an RFC, not a design decision."
- **Naming.** Deck p.51: "Semantic [tier]: Three-party sign-off. Versioned on change."
- **Content.** WP p.31: "Roughly eighty percent is green-light... Roughly fifteen percent is strategic review... Roughly five percent is leadership review."
- **Generosity.** WP p.25-26: "Staff can be generous without asking permission. Every server carries a bottle to gift as they see fit." WP p.13: the server "resolves it using their authorized judgment and generosity range."

The shape these share: authority is assigned to the closest competent owner, the limits inside which they may act without asking are pre-stated, and the triggers that escalate are named before the event rather than judged during it. The record has been applying a decision-rights rule without ever writing it down.

### 3d. The structural commitments authority must satisfy

- WP p.07-08: "A load-bearing organization asks: who is holding the weight and who is beneath whom? Authority flows downward from an apex... A code-bearing organization asks: what does the system know and how does that knowledge move?... Authority lives in the domain. The person who owns the domain is accountable to its outcomes."
- WP p.09: "The rigid anchor points are domain ownership: small, specific, explicitly defined, interlocking... Small and distributed is provably stronger than large and centralized. This is the scientific argument against concentrated authority."
- WP p.11: "The path is explicit before they start it... The absence of a clear path is where politics begin. Politics are a load-bearing structure problem."
- Canon 01, Playground Philosophy requires: "service authority distributed to the closest-to-customer position."

## 4. What the record holds about people, and the compensation question

**Canon 01, Company Non-Negotiables (text version).** "No Exploitation Ever / No Toxicity Ever / No Disposable People. Employee-first operating philosophy: bandwidth → joy → retention → results."

**Deck p.04.** "Three lines that do not move. No exploitation ever. No toxicity ever. No disposable people. The operating philosophy is employee-first. The company protects four things in order, and the order is the decision. FIRST Bandwidth / THEN Joy / THEN Retention / THEN Results."

The deck adds a sentence the text version does not carry: "the order is the decision." That converts a sequence into a decision rule, and it is the closest thing in the record to a stated tiebreaker for an operating decision.

**WP p.12 and p.16-17.** "Bandwidth is the organizational metabolic rate. It is what the system has available after the work of survival is complete... Bandwidth, then joy, then retention, then results. This is the metabolic sequence." And: "This is mechanical, not aspirational."

**WP p.16.** "The restaurant industry treats labor as a cost to minimize. Sŏn treats it as an asset to invest in. It is not charity, it is strategy."

**WP p.17.** "Sŏn flips it. Employees are the users."

**WP p.17.** "when something goes wrong, the first question is not who failed. It is what allowed this to happen... Management absorbs blame from staff. Post-mortems are private and process-focused."

**WP p.19-20, "We pay for what matters."** "If it matters to the experience, Sŏn pays for it. Costs are not shifted to employees... A real employee area with secure lockers and a comfortable break space is not a perk. It is basic dignity, and dignity is a precondition for presence."

**WP p.20, "How the team is paid."** "Compensation is built to bring the culinary team into the upside it is usually walled out of, and to do it without a charge added at the table. It is not a service charge, and it is never presented as one... That built-in compensation, set at a defined percentage that is still being finalized, is distributed to the whole team, the culinary team included, on a point system weighted by the days each person works. It is calculated daily rather than quarterly and paid on a reasonable rhythm."

**WP p.20, "No hierarchy of importance."** "there is no hierarchy of importance. Everything is treated as equally important... The principle also solves a management problem: there is never a need to adjudicate what deserves care, because the answer is always yes."

**WP p.11.** Cross-strand fluency "has genuine additional value, visible in the web as more connections and recognized in compensation as meaningful."

**WP p.15, Open items.** "Compensation architecture, how cross-strand fluency is recognized and paid, a separate deliverable."

**Session note.** As with decision rights, the record holds a substantial set of compensation commitments stated case by case, and names the absence of the architecture as an open item. What is missing is not the content. It is the statement of the content as a philosophy that can be applied to a case the record did not anticipate.

## 5. Record facts that bear on how a charter may be written

**No growth to organize around.** WP p.21-22 compares Sŏn's eight leadership lines across the full day against fourteen at a peer running one dinner service. WP p.35-36: dinner opens first and runs at least a quarter alone; the other core dayparts follow; lunch and brunch are "held in reserve, not part of the core and not assumed," not revisited for a year, and may be removed from the plan entirely.

**The company does not name itself to customers.** WP p.36: "The parent company is not a brand and is not named in customer-facing contexts either. It is a hospitality group built on a different structure than its peers, and it stays in the background."

**Never assert the unproven.** WP p.36: "Naming a future brand asserts a thing the venture has not yet proven, and the discipline is to never assert the unproven, to a customer or to a reader." WP p.39 (Horizon): "the discipline is to never assert the unproven to an investor or a customer. The reach is real. The roadmap is earned."

**The voice rules constrain a charter written in Sŏn's own voice.** Canon 09, longer-form digital: "Brand position named directly. No 'We believe,' no 'journey,' no founder hero narrative." Canon 09 Rule 1: "Copy never names its own effect." Rule 3: "The sentence ends at its last load-bearing word." Forbidden lexicon includes: authentic, passion, journey, curated, thoughtful, intentional, memorable, community-driven, crafted.

**Two record-integrity notes the charter has to absorb.**
- The canon's own pointer to its record of truth, in both artifacts (canon .md footer; deck p.10 and p.94), is ClickUp doc `2ky45bmy-15773`. That doc is archived and on this program's exclusion list. **Financials.** Financials are not a context source for this program. The record states the embedded compensation percentage is "still being finalized" and the white paper carries "no ask, no terms, and no figures" (WP p.02). No percentage is to be reproduced from any profile or from memory.
