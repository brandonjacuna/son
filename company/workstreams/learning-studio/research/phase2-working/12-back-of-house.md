Company non-negotiables: no exploitation, no toxicity, no disposable people (BG 01).

**What research adds.** The window or pass is the place where a plate is accepted or rejected, and fine kitchens coordinate there with very short closed-loop calls (Gomez, Bouty, Drucker-Godard, verified-primary). Competency frameworks treat food safety as pass-or-fail regardless of other scores (ACF, verified-primary) and assess mastery by professional discussion with techniques chosen by the assessor on the day (UK chef de partie standard, verified-primary). Both fit Brandon's gates. The front and back of a restaurant often work side by side without knowing each other's names (Wilson 2018, verified-primary for the abstract), which makes cross-house relationship a trunk module, not a nicety.

**Interview guide shape (section 8).** Critical Decision Method, in four sweeps per incident (pick the night, build the timeline, deepen on cues and choices, then what-ifs and the novice contrast), across ten incident prompts. It never asks what a house did; it asks what Brandon saw, did and would hold Sŏn to. Output is Brandon's account, kept as founder source, reviewed by the executive chef, never written as a named house's practice.

## 2. How elite kitchens and culinary programs structure back-of-house training (external)

### 2.1 The brigade and its modern variants

- **The classical brigade.** Codified by Escoffier across the Savoy and the Ritz, openly military in inspiration, each cook with one specialization and one line of report (Britannica, Escoffier school blog: verified-secondary via search summaries; lead-only for the detail). Useful as vocabulary only. Sŏn's structure is the WP's strands, not the classical tiers.
- **What a fine kitchen looks like in practice.** Gomez, Bouty and Drucker-Godard studied a French three-star kitchen (verified-primary, paper read):
  - The window "is where plates are assembled, and controlled, and from where they are expedited to the dining room." It "is also a sanction place. This is where a plate is accepted or rejected". The head chef usually stands there and "controls and does not cook"; only the second-in-chief replaces him.
  - Coordination runs on very short calls: the second-in-chief announces a dish and asks "running?"; cooks answer "yes"; if one does not, he asks directly. The domain chef calls the time left ("the sole, two minutes") and every concerned cook answers "yes, two minutes."
  - The chef writes technical cards that "are not recipes"; they "draw a framework", and the cooks "do what they want but the framework is precise". The result is tasted by the chef and adjusted with the team.
  - Cooking "is a 'learning by doing' profession. Gestures, smells, cooking, consistencies are to be felt in the first place." "The rules of Grande Cuisine are not written in books"; they are absorbed by practicing inside the environment.
  - Synchronization in service "is possible because of an important preparation at other times."
- **Sŏn's variant, from its own canon.** Two execution strands (line and prep), station owners, a steward who holds continuity, fewer title tiers because the data layer carries coordination (WP pp.11, 22). The intake adds the tournant (moves across stations) and the executive chef's left-hand and right-hand pair (ops and production; service and R&D).
- **Reading for Sŏn.** Gomez's framework-not-recipe matches BG 03 Playground Philosophy: legible structure, room to act. The window as a place of acceptance is the pass standard in KIT-040. Short closed-loop calls are drilled as macro movements (KIT-002, KIT-016).

### 2.2 Station progressions and competency frameworks

- **UK chef de partie standard (ST0227, Skills England; verified-primary).** Occupation: runs "a section of the kitchen". Duties include maximizing yield and quality in "time-bound environments", overseeing section staff, and coaching team members. Skills include "Prioritise tasks, directing others as appropriate" (S5) and "Brief, coach and motivate others" (S8). Behaviours include "Remain calm under pressure" (B4) and "Advocate the importance of working safely and legally" (B6). End-point assessment includes a professional discussion in which the assessor selects techniques and "Apprentices are not informed beforehand which techniques will be assessed." This is Brandon's conversational knowledge gate (intake group 4), independently used at scale.
- **UK commis chef standard (Skills England).** Purpose: preparing, cooking and finishing "whilst rotating around each section of the kitchen"; behaviours include "a food safety approach at all times" (lead-only, search snippet; page not opened). Rotation across sections is the structural analog of the tournant path.
- **American Culinary Federation practical exam candidate guide (verified-primary, PDF read).**
  - Four areas: food safety and sanitation; organization; cooking skills and culinary techniques; taste and presentation.
  - Food safety and sanitation is "acceptable or unacceptable. An unacceptable score in this area by two or more evaluators is considered an overall failure regardless of the total points achieved". This matches D11: safety elements pass in every attempt.
  - Organization: "Table is kept clear of nonessentials", "working systematically on one job at a time", "The candidate is not using the dish area as a storage dump. The candidate has an organized withdrawal from the kitchen, leaving it as clean as it was found."
  - Work flow: "smooth transitions from one job to another" and "proper timing of the menu items."
  - Taste: "the duck broth tastes like duck." "Hot Food Hot! and Cold Food Cold!"
  - ACF credential levels run from fundamentals cook through master chef (verified-secondary via search summary and the ACF site listing). Referenced, never re-taught (standing rules, "Scope").
- **Reading for Sŏn.** The UK standard and the ACF rubric give observable anchors for kitchen gate specs (D18). Neither decides Sŏn's standard; the chef does.

### 2.3 Mise en place as discipline

- Dan Charnas, *Work Clean* (Rodale, 2016; publisher page read, verified-secondary): mise en place "signifies an entire lifestyle of readiness and engagement." Ten principles: planning is prime; arranging spaces and perfecting movements; cleaning as you go; making first moves; finishing actions; slowing down to speed up; call and callback; open ears and eyes; inspect and correct; total utilization. Drawn from interviews with working chefs.
- **Reading for Sŏn.** Charnas splits mise into movement ("arranging spaces and perfecting movements") and judgment ("making first moves", "inspect and correct"). That is Brandon's split (intake group 3). The principles become a shared house vocabulary across kitchen and bar (BEV-003 station and body). The station map itself is `chef.*`.

### 2.4 Tasting and seasoning

- Gomez et al. (verified-primary): the chef tastes the result and adjusts it with the team; the palate is the chef's and is transmitted by tasting together.
- ACF (verified-primary): taste is scored against the ingredient's own identity ("tastes like duck") and doneness, not against a judge's preference alone.
- Graf, "Taste knowledge: couscous and the cook's six senses", *JRAI* 2022 (lead-only; page refused): taste knowledge as embodied, multi-sensory and learned through practice.
- An ethnography of restaurant R&D kitchens in *International Journal of Gastronomy and Food Science* 2024 reports tasting sessions where chefs see how each other perceive saltiness or acidity and give feedback (lead-only; page refused).
- Leschziner, *At the Chef's Table* (Stanford UP, 2015; verified-secondary, publisher and review records): elite chefs balance originality against tradition; one chapter treats "Cognitive Patterns and Work Processes in Cooking".
- **Reading for Sŏn.** No study found tests a method for calibrating a line's palate to a chef's. The module design is inference from perceptual-learning evidence already in `03-external-evidence.md` (interleaved near-miss discriminations, many short trials with feedback) plus the tasting-with-recall modality (`framework/modality-library.md`, "Know it"). Status: unverified as a kitchen method.

### 2.5 The pass and kitchen-floor communication

- Gomez et al. (verified-primary), above: the window, the call, the callback.
- Charnas (verified-secondary): "call and callback" is one of the ten principles.
- AHRQ TeamSTEPPS closed-loop communication: call-outs, check-backs ("repeat-back") and teach-back verify that a message was received (lead-only; AHRQ pages refused the fetch, definitions seen in search results).
- William Foote Whyte, *Human Relations in the Restaurant Industry* (McGraw-Hill, 1948): waitresses and cooks at odds; cooks protecting a work rhythm treated floor requests and errors as disruption (verified-secondary, via Wilson 2018 and search summaries).
- Eli R. Wilson, "Bridging the Service Divide: Dual Labor Niches and Embedded Opportunities in Restaurant Work", *RSF Journal*, 2018 (verified-primary for the article page read): front and back labor niches split by race, class and language; "thick social networks ... severely curtail the prospect of workers within different labor niches switching from back-of-the-house to front-of-the-house jobs"; front-back tension intensified by social distance, with many workers not knowing each other's names; bilingual workers bridge the niches.
- Gary Alan Fine, *Kitchens: The Culture of Restaurant Work* (UC Press, 1996; verified-secondary via publisher record, table of contents and reviews): the relationships among cooks, servers, dishwashers, managers and customers; time pressure ("Cooks' Time: Temporal Demands and the Experience of Work"); how organization shapes the food.
- **Reading for Sŏn.** The kitchen-floor seam is a known, persistent failure point with a social cause as well as a procedural one. Sŏn's answer has three parts: a drilled closed-loop call at the pass (KIT-002); a trunk module on what each side needs from the other (KIT-003); and a relationship module in the trunk so cross-level critique (CUL-002, D9, D22) has names and trust under it (CUL-005). Language access is a design requirement, not an accommodation (WP p.17 language choice).

### 2.6 Sanitation and the porter's role as essential

- WP p.11 (verified-primary): the Steward "is not a support role."
- ACF (verified-primary): the candidate "is not using the dish area as a storage dump" and leaves the kitchen "as clean as it was found". Respect for the dish station is scored.
- In 2017 René Redzepi made Noma's long-serving dishwasher, Ali Sonko, a partner and called him "Noma's soul" (The Staff Canteen, 2017-03-01, verified-secondary; CNN Money and others lead-only). Not lineage; cited as a public example that the dish station can carry a house's culture, not as a practice to copy.
- Fine (verified-secondary) includes dishwashers among the kitchen's core relationships.
- **Reading for Sŏn.** The porter's module (KIT-010) is a craft with its own mastery: flow, prioritization, equipment, chemical safety, reading the whole kitchen's load. Whether every cook works porter rotations is a chef call (section 9). The porter's path points in several directions (prep, line, floor), never only "up from the pit".

### 2.7 Food safety culture

- FDA, "Advancing Food Safety Culture as Science Not a Slogan: A Systematic Literature Review" (2022-12-12; verified-primary): the most cited definition is "the aggregation of the prevailing, relatively constant, learned, shared attitudes, values and beliefs contributing to the hygiene behaviors"; building it means addressing "the beliefs, attitudes, and the behaviors of people and the actions of organizations."
- FDA New Era of Smarter Food Safety, Core Element 4, food safety culture (lead-only; FAQ and blueprint pages seen in search, not opened).
- FDA Food Code 2-102.11, demonstration of knowledge by the person in charge (lead-only; state reproductions seen in search). This is certified or regulated content: referenced and linked (SAF-002, D17), never re-taught.
- **Reading for Sŏn.** Certification proves knowledge; culture is what happens unobserved. The durable module (SAF-003) teaches behavior and speaking up; the certificate is linked. Sensor logging (WP p.21) is a `tool.*` binding.

### 2.8 Kitchen culture, abuse, and who gets to stay

- Burrow, Smith and Yakinthou, "'Yes Chef': life at the vanguard of culinary excellence", *Work, Employment and Society* 29(4), 2015 (verified-secondary via York repository and SAGE record): elite kitchen life as thrilling and rewarding but also "mundane, degrading and dehumanizing"; workers "socialized into accepting, adopting and propagating extreme workplace behaviour."
- Bloisi and Hoel, "Abusive work practices and bullying among chefs: A review of the literature", *International Journal of Hospitality Management* 27(4), 2008 (lead-only, abstract in search): personal characteristics, culture and socialization, and the transient industry explain why abuse is tolerated.
- Harris and Giuffre, *Taking the Heat: Women Chefs and Gender Inequality in the Professional Kitchen* (Rutgers UP, 2015; verified-secondary, carried from `05-reading-list.md`).
- Erica Catubig's posts (intake group 8; `05-reading-list.md`) name an era that normalized abuse in celebrated kitchens.
- **Reading for Sŏn.** BG 01 is Tier 2 authority and overrides any inherited norm. Intensity and precision are kept; degradation is not. A D4 module teaches kitchen leaders to hold the standard without harm, and the trunk culture modules hold the floor to the same rule. Voices of women, immigrant and multilingual cooks, and labor enter here because they earn their place (intake group 8), with any kitchen-side use chef-gated.

### 2.9 Kitchen teaching methods and research on kitchen learning

- **Cognitive apprenticeship** (Collins, Brown and Holum, *American Educator*, 1991; verified-primary): modeling, coaching, scaffolding, fading, then articulation, reflection and exploration; traditional apprenticeship works because the work is visible, and thinking must be made visible the same way.
- **Situated learning** (Lave and Wenger, *Situated Learning: Legitimate Peripheral Participation*, Cambridge UP, 1991; verified-secondary): newcomers learn by legitimate participation at the edge of real work, moving toward the center; cases include tailors and butchers.
- **Gomez et al.** (verified-primary): the rules are learned by practicing inside the environment; the difference between a great chef and a good one is "a hard-to-pin-down mix of confidence, concentration and ability to rise the occasion".
- **Mac Con Iomaire**, oral history of Dublin restaurant kitchens (TU Dublin; lead-only): grand hotel kitchens as "nurseries" for young chefs.
- **Bocuse d'Or** (Wikipedia, verified-secondary): each team is a lead chef plus a young commis, with a coach outside the kitchen area; one jury scores organization, teamwork, cleanliness and lack of waste in the kitchen, and a separate jury tastes. Ment'or recruits and trains the US team over a long program supervised by senior chefs (Ment'or site and press, lead-only). Reading: the kitchen process and the plate are judged separately, and a coach stands outside the work. Both translate: the station gate records process and product separately, and the angel-shift trainer coaches without cooking for the learner.
- **Reading for Sŏn.** The documented kitchen tradition is apprenticeship: watch, do under correction, get corrected at the window. Its weaknesses are also documented: tacit rules never written down, correction delivered as humiliation, and learning that depends on one chef's mood. Sŏn keeps the apprenticeship and fixes its weaknesses with written gate specs, drilled movements, spaced recall, a taught critique protocol and private incident review.

### 2.10 The paid practical

- A US Department of Labor investigation found unpaid "working interviews" violated the FLSA's minimum wage requirements (HR Dive, 2018-12-18, verified-secondary). State and local rules can be stricter (lead-only).
- **Reading for Sŏn.** The paid practical is already canon (WP pp.17 to 18). Its design, length and pay are `founder.*`, `workflow.*` and `fact.*`, and the HR seats confirm compliance. Selection evidence stays separate from gate evidence (starting structure §1.6).

## 5. Candidate module list

**Column keys.** Job: know, see, decide, do, hold, find, teach (`framework/modality-library.md`). Unlock: time, mastery, or both (D2); any period is a binding. Gate link: G1 spoken check, G2 small-menu practical, G3 angel shift, G4 eyes-off window (starting structure §1.7), or "conversation" for a proven conversation. Chef: **C** chef-gated content inside; **C!** the whole module waits on the chef. IDs are candidates until `/identify`.

**Teaching approach, applied to every row.** Physical macro movements are drilled on their own, short, spaced and interleaved, at varied tempo, before they are combined with decisions (intake groups 3 and 5). Decisions are practiced separately in scenarios and expert comparison. Mastery is proven by conversation and practical, never by a screen quiz alone (intake groups 2 and 4). Each critical-path unit carries a recall schedule tied to the learner's next shifts (D15). Learners self-direct: finishing a module sends a booking link to a talent block (D13). Take-home practice is invited, optional, unpaid and never required (D7); knife and heat work is never take-home.

## 6. Role required sets and competency lists

Titles are `people.*`. A role is a required set of stars at stated depths plus its gates (D28); job descriptions render from it (starting structure, "Job descriptions generated from the path"). Competencies are written as "on their own, a person can..." so each traces to a gate. Every set includes the D0 trunk and the shared core. Station names are `chef.*`.

### Porter (WP: Steward)

- **Required set.** D0 all; KIT-010, SAF-004, KIT-011, KIT-015, KIT-016 at D1; KIT-025 at D2; the continuity strand's D3 star (a full night's continuity, released on G4).
- **Competencies.** Keeps wares, equipment and space flowing so no station waits. Reads the whole kitchen's load and chooses what to clear first. Uses chemicals and machines safely and correctly. Receives, stores and rotates product to standard. Moves and calls safely through the kitchen. Asks before acting on an unfamiliar call. Names every teammate front and back.
- **Adjacent unlocks.** Prep (KIT-020, KIT-012), the floor (runner, via the service discipline), the on deck reserve on any released star. No direction is framed as "up".
- **Chef calls.** Whether the role carries the title steward or porter (INT C17); whether every cook rotates through it.

### Prep cook (WP: Chef on prep)

- **Required set.** D0; D1 all except KIT-060; KIT-020, KIT-021, KIT-022, KIT-025, SAF-005 at D2; KIT-033 at D3 on the prep strand; D3 release on production.
- **Competencies.** Produces the day's prep to list, par and sequence. Cuts and handles product to the house standard. Tastes and seasons to the house reference. Follows specs and can say why each step exists. Prevents cross-contact. Uses yield and trim fully. Keeps the station clean as it goes.
- **Adjacent unlocks.** Line stations, tournant path, left-hand production work at D4.

### Line cook or chef de partie (WP: Chef on the line; Station Lead as domain owner)

- **Required set.** D0; D1 (KIT-010 by chef call); D2 including the station's KIT-023.x, KIT-024; D3 KIT-030, KIT-032, KIT-034 on each owned station.

- **Adjacent unlocks.** Tournant (more stations), R&D elective (KIT-042), D5 teaching ring, a floor shift (KIT-061).

- **Required set.** Line cook set on a first station, plus KIT-023.x and G4 on each additional station, plus KIT-031.

## 8. Interview guide for Brandon: the standards you learned in the best kitchens you worked in

**Purpose.** Draw out, in Brandon's own words, the back-of-house standards he holds from his years in some of the country's best restaurants, so the draft discipline can be framed by them (D30) and the executive chef can redevelop it. Also feeds the decision interviews behind ORI-003 and the reference panel (D14).

**Ground rules (read aloud at the start).**
- We are asking what you saw, did and would hold Sŏn to, not what any house does. You never need to name a house. If one comes up, we record your account as your standard, not as that house's practice, and flag it.
- Much of your time in top rooms was on the floor and in operations. Answer from where you stood. "What I saw from the pass" is exactly the right altitude.
- No figures. If a number comes up, we note that one exists and leave it unbound until a source is chosen (Airtable retired).
- Recording: consent for the recording and where it is stored (Box) is confirmed first (`workflow.recording_consent`).
- One incident at a time, specific nights, not general principles. Principles come out of the incidents.

**Method.** Critical Decision Method (carried from `03-external-evidence.md` §8): four sweeps per incident. Sweep 1, pick a specific night and tell it straight through. Sweep 2, build a timeline and mark the moments where something turned. Sweep 3, deepen at each turn with the probes below. Sweep 4, what-ifs and the novice contrast. Plan for a few incidents per session, sessions spread over weeks, inside Brandon's calendar.

**Standard probes at each turning point (sweep 3).**
- What did you notice first? What did you see, hear, smell?
- What did you expect to happen next? What told you it would or would not?
- What options did you have? Why that one?
- Who else saw it? Did they read it the same way?
- What would a new person on that night have missed? What would they have done instead?
- What would you want a cook at Sŏn to do the first time they meet this?

**Sweep 4 probes.**
- If the kitchen had been at half the staff, what would have changed?
- If the person had been new, what would you have wanted them to have been taught before that night?
- Where was the standard written down, and where did it live only in people?

**Incident prompts (choose several; each opens one full four-sweep interview).**

1. **A night the kitchen was at its best.** Walk a specific service where the kitchen and room felt like one team. What made it work? What did you notice at the pass?
2. **A night the kitchen broke.** Walk a night that went wrong in the back. Where did it start? When did you first see it coming? What did the people who recovered it do?
3. **The pass.** Think of a moment a plate was stopped before it went out. Who stopped it, what did they see, and what was said, to whom, and how loudly?
4. **The dish station or the steward.** Think of a time the kitchen's environment fell behind, or someone at the dish station held the night together. What did you see? How did the rest of the kitchen treat that person?
5. **Tasting.** Think of a time someone's palate was corrected or calibrated. How did the correction happen? What did the person do differently afterward?
6. **Teaching a new cook.** Think of one new person you watched learn a station. What were they shown first? When did support fall away? What did the good teachers do that the others did not?
7. **A correction that worked, and one that did not.** One time a mistake was corrected and the person got better; one time a correction hurt the person or the team. What was different?
8. **The seam.** Think of a specific friction between floor and kitchen. What caused it? What would have prevented it? What did the floor not understand about the kitchen, and the reverse?
9. **A standard nobody wrote down.** Think of something every good kitchen you worked in held to that no document said. When did you first notice it? How did people learn it?
10. **What you will not carry.** Think of something you saw in a top kitchen that you do not want at Sŏn. Walk the night you decided that.

**Closing questions per session.**
- Of what you told me today, what is non-negotiable for Sŏn's kitchen, and what is your preference that the executive chef may overrule?
- What should the executive chef hear from you directly rather than read here?

**What happens to the output.**
- Transcript and notes live in Box (Brandon's source rule). The studio summarizes each incident into cues, choices and novice contrasts, attributed to Brandon, with any house reference flagged and removed from module text.
- Standards Brandon marks non-negotiable become `founder.*` bindings until written into modules; preferences are handed to the executive chef as starting positions (D30).
- Cases feed the reference panel's kitchen item bank, kept separate from practice and gate banks (starting structure §1.7).
- Tracked in ClickUp under the founder decision interviews.
