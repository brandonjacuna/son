## A. Onboarding sequences and role paths in documented houses

**A1. Ritz-Carlton: orientation, then a coach, check-in days, a daily line-up, and an annual recommitment**
- **What it is:**
  - Culture-and-standards orientation comes before the person starts the job.
  - A learning coach is assigned.
  - A named check-in day a few weeks in brings a second day of orientation.
  - Operational certification comes later in the first period.
  - Every shift, company-wide, starts with a daily line-up carrying one shared featured topic, a value and a brand component.
  - The anniversary is treated as an "emotional rehire": the person is asked whether they are still committed.
- **Source:** NIST Baldrige blog, Dawn Bailey, 2017-07-27 ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/blogrige/customer-really-always-right-hotel-company-invests-its-employees-first)). An ICMR case excerpt and Quizlet cards put the certification exam on the check-in day itself, which contradicts NIST.
- **Status:** verified-secondary (NIST). ICMR and Quizlet are lead-only. The Ritz-Carlton Leadership Center course page describes none of this and is marketing. The whole account is company self-report, relayed through Baldrige.
- **Implies for Sŏn:**
  - The line-up is a documented precedent for Brandon's "pre-shift is always a teaching slot" (group 3). What transfers is one shared featured topic across the house each day, not a format. Format and timing stay team-gated.
  - The anniversary recommitment is a low-cost check against complacency (group 7).
  - Check-in days map to `workflow.*` bindings, never to fixed dates.

**A2. Union Square Hospitality Group: documented hiring philosophy, undocumented internal path**
- **What it is:**
  - USHG hires on a stated split that weights hospitality quotient above technical skill.
  - Hospitality Quotient is its external training arm, now with a self-paced digital platform.
  - No public source this pass documents USHG's internal onboarding, certification or role path.
- **Source:** Boston Hospitality Review interview with Patti Simpson, USHG Chief Administrative Officer, 2023-03-01 ([bu.edu](https://www.bu.edu/bhr/2023/03/01/shake-shack/)). The HQ+ launch is from FSR (not read).
- **Status:** verified-secondary, self-report. HQ+ is lead-only.
- **Implies for Sŏn:** USHG backs Brandon's group 7 premise (hire capability, own the skill set) but gives no model for gates. Do not cite USHG as a certification-ladder precedent.

**A3. Eleven Madison Park (Guidara): Dream Weaver role**
- **What it is:** a floor role whose only job was to help teammates bring customer-delight ideas to life.
- **Source:** trade press and Guidara's book, which Brandon cites in the intake. Not read at the source this pass.
- **Status:** lead-only (founder self-report).
- **Implies for Sŏn:** this is an organizational enabler, not a training path. It fits Brandon's "master unblocker" framing (group 7) and an elective strand at most.

**A4. Cheesecake Factory: certification with a retake limit, recertification, and a paid certified-trainer rung**
- **What it is:**
  - New servers go through a mentored certification period, then written exams with a high pass bar and a limited number of attempts. Missing the bar means no hire.
  - Follow-up classes after hire, periodic menu training, and annual recertification.
  - Top servers are cross-trained into a paid certified-trainer level, and each unit keeps a floor on how many trainers it has.
- **Source:** Workforce Management, 2006-05-03, interviewing the company's VP of Performance and Development ([workforce.com](https://www.workforce.com/news/cheesecake-factory-cooks-up-a-rigorous-employee-training-program)).
- **Status:** verified-secondary. The source is dated and self-reported, so current practice is unknown.
- **Implies for Sŏn:**
  - This is the clearest chain-restaurant precedent for a fail limit (team-gated for Sŏn) and for a paid teaching rung, which matches the white paper's paid-teacher commitment (Part II, "Training infrastructure").
  - It also shows recertification as routine, which bears on Brandon's periodic-practical idea (group 4).
  - Its gate is written; Brandon's is verbal. Take the gate structure, not the format.

**A5. Starbucks Coffee Master (black apron)**
- **What it is:**
  - A three-level Coffee Academy covering origin, roast and brew. It is open to anyone.
  - The black apron requires finishing all levels, a minimum tenure, and selection by the store manager.
- **Source:** Starbucks Global Academy ([starbucksglobalacademy.com](https://starbucksglobalacademy.com/coffee-academy/about/)).
- **Status:** verified-primary (the company's own documentation).
- **Implies for Sŏn:** a visible, named mastery marker that people pursue. But the final step is a manager gatekeeping, which is exactly what the invisible advocate removes (white paper Part I). Keep the visible marker and replace manager selection with an auto-scheduled, proven conversation (group 2).

**A6. Hotel and restaurant new-employee training, field study (pre-opening; see E1)**
- **What it is:**
  - Most firms teach strategic content (culture, brand) first, then technical and interpersonal skills.
  - Brand and culture material is corporate-designed; technical material is property-driven.
  - Mixed teams of managers and line staff sit together for the culture sessions.
  - Many firms rely heavily on passive methods even for interpersonal skills, which the authors call surprising.
- **Source:** Tracey, Hinkin et al., "A Field Study of New Employee Training Programs," *Cornell Hospitality Quarterly* 56(4), 2015, doi:10.1177/1938965514554211. Fifteen hotel and sixteen restaurant firms, surveyed with telephone follow-up.
- **Status:** verified-primary. Firm self-report, small sample; the authors themselves urge caution.
- **Implies for Sŏn:** independent support for "why before how" (catalog ORI-001; white paper Part II, "Onboarding"). It also flags the common failure Brandon wants to avoid: teaching interpersonal work at a screen (group 5).

## B. Readiness gates: staged first shifts and sign-off

**B1. Aviation: supervised operating experience before counting as crew**
- **What it is:**
  - A new flight attendant must work for a minimum period under a qualified supervisor who personally observes the work.
  - While doing so, they may not be assigned as a required crewmember.
  - Prior experience on the same aircraft group waives the requirement.
  - Time in an approved full-scale cabin trainer can replace part of the requirement.
- **Source:** 14 CFR 121.434(e), read from the govinfo CFR PDF.
- **Status:** verified-primary.
- **Implies for Sŏn:** the best external model for the angel shift (group 4). The transferable rules:
  1. The supervisor personally observes.
  2. The trainee is extra, not part of the scheduled staffing, so the trainer's attention is not traded against coverage.
  3. Simulation (the small-menu practical) can carry part of the load.
  4. Proven prior experience can shorten the path.
  - Staffing is a `workflow.*` binding; nothing here is written as fact.

**B2. Entrustable professional activities: graded supervision and trust**
- **What it is:**
  - Medicine assesses readiness by activity: "Can we trust the trainee to execute X without supervision?"
  - A five-step scale runs from observe only, to direct proactive supervision, to indirect reactive supervision (help on request), to distant oversight, to supervising others.
  - There are two kinds of decision: ad hoc entrustment made daily during work, and summative entrustment made formally.
  - Trust rests on four conditions: ability, integrity, reliability and humility (knowing one's limits and asking for help).
  - Decisions should draw on multiple sources, including short observations, multisource feedback and tests.
- **Source:** ten Cate, "Entrustment as Assessment," *J Grad Med Educ* 8(2), 2016 ([PMC4857524](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4857524)).
- **Status:** verified-primary.
- **Implies for Sŏn:** this maps almost exactly onto Brandon's gate.
  - The angel shift is direct proactive supervision.
  - The eyes-off shift is indirect reactive supervision, with support available on request.
  - The top step, supervising others, is the positional lead who may run practicals alone (group 4).
  - Humility is Brandon's "using the support system effectively." It becomes something to assess, not a hope.
  - Multisource feedback is his "prove it to the rest of your team."
  - Gates should be defined per activity (for example, a two-top alone, a bar well alone), not per job title. That fits a skill tree.

**B3. Peer assessment reliability**
- **What it is:** a meta-analysis of peer-versus-teacher marks. Peer marks resemble expert marks more when peers make global judgments against well-understood criteria than when they score several separate dimensions. Agreement is weaker for professional practice than for academic products.
- **Source:** Falchikov and Goldfinch, *Review of Educational Research* 70(3), 2000 (ERIC EJ630369 abstract).
- **Status:** verified-primary (abstract).
- **Implies for Sŏn:**
  - For the whole-team feedback shift, give critics one global question against criteria everyone has been taught, not a multi-part rubric.
  - Because agreement is weaker for practice, keep a trained assessor alongside the peer until leads are certified. That is what Brandon already specified: peer plus manager until the lead has completed teaching training.

**B4. Trained preceptors are the active ingredient in nursing transition**
- **What it is:** NCSBN ran a randomized, multisite national study of new-nurse transition programs. Structured programs gave better competence, fewer self-reported errors, better safety practice, lower stress and better retention when they included:
  - institutional support
  - preceptors trained for the role
  - time for feedback and reflection
  - specialty-specific content
  - a sustained program length
- **Source:** [ncsbn.org/study-results](https://www.ncsbn.org/study-results).
- **Status:** verified-primary (NCSBN's own results page). Evidence is strong in hospitals and weaker outside them.
- **Implies for Sŏn:**
  - The person running the angel shift and signing a practical needs their own training. This supports catalog LEA-001 "Teaching at Sŏn" as a prerequisite for peer sign-off.
  - Reflection time after the eyes-off shift is an ingredient, not a nicety.
  - The white paper's peer mentor chosen as a "cultural steward" (Part II, "Onboarding") fits this.

**B5. Nursing competency validation with peer review (Wright model)**
- **What it is:** a competency-validation model that offers many verification methods beyond checklists, including peer review, return demonstration, case studies, exemplars and discussion groups.
- **Source:** PubMed-indexed implementation papers and practitioner summaries.
- **Status:** lead-only (not read at the primary source).
- **Implies for Sŏn:** precedent for letting a "proven conversation" count as validation (group 2), and for choosing the validation method per competency rather than one test for everything.

**B6. Restaurant shadow, reverse shadow, then sign-off**
- **What it is:** common restaurant practice has three stages:
  1. The trainee follows a veteran.
  2. The trainee works their own small section while the trainer watches ("reverse shadow").
  3. A final assessment is signed by both trainer and manager.
- **Source:** 7shifts, Homebase, RestaurantOwner and similar.
- **Status:** lead-only (vendor and blog content).
- **Implies for Sŏn:**
  - The industry norm puts the test after customer contact. Brandon puts the verbal check and small-menu practical before it (group 4); that is his "test before impact" principle.
  - The term "angel shift" did not appear in documented sources this pass. It may be Brandon's own term; confirm.

**B7. The white paper's hiring practical**
- The white paper already commits to a paid practical in a real environment in place of the unpaid stage (Part II, hiring passage, pp. 17 to 18).
- The readiness gate and the hiring practical should be designed together, so one does not duplicate the other.

## C. Comparable high-skill progressions

**C1. Court of Master Sommeliers, Certified level**
- **What it is:**
  - The Introductory level is a prerequisite, and industry experience is strongly suggested.
  - The exam has three sections in one day: deductive tasting, theory, and a hospitality and service practical that approximates a real restaurant (opening wine, recommending, pairing, tableside service).
  - A minimum score is required on every section in the same sitting.
- **Source:** [mastersommeliers.org](https://www.mastersommeliers.org/certification/certification-levels/certified-sommelier-examination/).
- **Status:** verified-primary.
- **Implies for Sŏn:**
  - The service practical is the closest external analog to Brandon's "stand at a table and talk" practical.
  - "Pass every part in one sitting" is a design choice for the fail-limit conversation (team-gated).
  - Wine is a natural elective strand for bartenders and baristas (group 2). An external credential can be linked rather than written, as the intake already does for legal certifications.

**C2. USBG Master Accreditation**
- **What it is:**
  - Spirits Professional is a written exam with no prerequisite.
  - Advanced Bartender requires Spirits Professional and adds a live practical covering tasting, creativity, presentation, efficiency and technique, and bar management with customer service.
- **Source:** [usbg.org](https://www.usbg.org/education/master-accreditation).
- **Status:** verified-primary.
- **Implies for Sŏn:** knowledge unlocks the practical, and the practical includes the customer-facing part. Barback to bartender (group 2) can follow the same shape.
- **Related:** BarSmarts (a Pernod Ricard program, online prerequisite before a live advanced level) and WSET spirits levels are lead-only this pass.

**C3. SCA Coffee Skills Program**
- **What it is:**
  - Barista Skills runs at Foundation, Intermediate and Professional levels.
  - Each level pairs a written exam with a practical: grinder calibration, extraction, milk texturing, latte art, drink building.
  - Levels earn points toward a diploma.
- **Source:** the SCA education page returned an error at fetch; the description comes from authorized-trainer pages.
- **Status:** lead-only until re-read at the source.
- **Implies for Sŏn:**
  - The practicals isolate macro movements (milk, pour) from judgment. That matches Brandon's split between muscle memory and decisions (group 3) and his water-pour drill (group 5).
  - Points toward a diploma is an existing precedent for "stacks" in a skill tree.

**C4. German dual system**
- **What it is:**
  - Most of an apprenticeship happens in the company.
  - In-company trainers must pass a trainer aptitude exam.
  - The employer turns a national training regulation into a company-specific training plan.
  - The apprentice keeps a regular written training record, signed by both parties, which is required for exam admission.
  - A final exam before the competent body leads to a certificate.
- **Source:** BIBB ([bibb.de/en/147094.php](https://www.bibb.de/en/147094.php), [146850](https://www.bibb.de/en/146850.php)).
- **Status:** verified-primary. The makeup of examination boards was not confirmed this pass.
- **Implies for Sŏn:** the three transferable pieces are:
  1. **Trainers are certified to train.** Same conclusion as B4.
  2. **A house-specific plan hangs off a stable role standard.** This mirrors durable content plus bindings.
  3. **A learner-kept record of practice, co-signed.** This is a low-tech, platform-neutral progression log.

**C5. "See one, do one, teach one" critique**
- **What it is:**
  - Critics say the model risks patient safety, gives too few repetitions, and is squeezed by time pressure.
  - The authors keep the shape but add deliberate practice, simulation and adult-learning cycles.
  - They note that plain repetition plateaus below maximal performance.
  - They cite a meta-analysis in which simulation with deliberate practice beat traditional clinical education.
- **Source:** Kotsis and Chung, *Plast Reconstr Surg* 131, 2013 (PMC full text); the meta-analysis they cite is McGaghie et al. 2011.
- **Status:** Kotsis and Chung verified-primary. McGaghie verified-secondary (read only as cited).
- **Implies for Sŏn:**
  - One observed rep is not readiness. Gates should require repeated deliberate practice to a standard; Brandon's drills are that.
  - "Teach one" is powerful but should come after mastery, which is how Brandon has already sequenced peer sign-off.

**C6. Training Within Industry, Job Instruction**
- **What it is:**
  - A four-step supervisor method: prepare the worker, present, test (learner performs and explains the key points), then follow up with coaching that tapers off.
  - A related tool is the training timetable, or versatility matrix: a visible grid of who can do what, used to plan cross-training.
- **Source:** NIST MEP ([nist.gov/mep/training-within-industry-twi](https://www.nist.gov/mep/training-within-industry-twi)); the matrix detail comes from practitioner sites.
- **Status:** method verified-secondary; matrix lead-only.
- **Implies for Sŏn:**
  - Coaching that tapers off is the angel-shift to eyes-off logic, already standard in industry.
  - The versatility matrix is the plain ancestor of Brandon's skill tree and the on deck reserve roster.

## D. Cross-training and floating labor pools

**D1. Chaining: limited, linked cross-training captures most of the flexibility**
- **What it is:** process-flexibility theory. Limited flexibility, configured as a chain linking each unit to its neighbors, yields close to the benefit of full flexibility. Skill chaining (overlapping zones between adjacent workers) is a robust and efficient way to get workforce agility.
- **Source:**
  - Jordan and Graves, *Management Science* 41(4), 1995 (abstract read)
  - Hopp, Tekin and Van Oyen, "Benefits of Skill Chaining," *Management Science* 50(1), 2004 (abstract read)
- **Status:** verified-primary (abstracts). The evidence is manufacturing and queueing models; it is not tested in restaurants.
- **Implies for Sŏn:**
  - The on deck reserve and cross-training need not mean everyone learns everything. Each person cross-trained into an adjacent role, in a linked chain, gives most of the coverage:
    - service chain: food runner, back waiter, front server
    - beverage chain: barback, bartender, barista
    - the host as the bridge between the two chains, as Brandon described (group 2)
  - The kitchen chef de tournant is a chaining role. Its station specifics are chef-gated.

**D2. Float pool failure modes**
- **What it is:** a qualitative study of float-pool staff. Failure modes:
  - uncertainty on arrival
  - inconsistent reception by host teams
  - role ambiguity
  - poor induction
  - repeated "outsider" status
  - gaps in unit-specific knowledge
  
  What protected staff: unit-specific orientation, a designated contact or mentor, clear role definition, inclusive leadership, and protected debrief time.
- **Source:** Suarez et al., *Journal of Nursing Management*, 2026 ([PMC13382358](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13382358/)).
- **Status:** verified-primary (small qualitative study). Consistent with nursing position statements seen in search (lead-only).
- **Implies for Sŏn:**
  - Brandon wants cross-training as "a planned design system, not something we fall back on" (group 2).
  - To get there, each reserve member needs a home team, an orientation for every station they may cover, a named contact on shift, and a way to be received rather than tolerated.
  - Brandon's all-level critique culture (group 4) must include people who are floating in.

**D3. Hospitality cross-training practice, benefits, and a counter-view**
- **What it is:**
  - About half the firms in the Tracey study use cross-training for new hires, mostly within a department (bellstaff to front desk, servers to bussers). They cite flexibility and job enrichment for high performers.
  - Non-users object strongly: "we want them to be the best at their assigned job."
  - Kalloch, Silver and Ton (HBR, 2023) argue that cross-training both improves operations and stabilizes the workforce.
  - A claim that skills decay unless people rotate often enough appears in secondary sources.
- **Source:** Tracey et al. 2015 (verified-primary); HBR (paywalled, only the summary read); skill decay (lead-only).
- **Implies for Sŏn:**
  - Brandon's rule that cross-training unlocks after mastery in one role (group 2) answers the "best at assigned job" objection.
  - Rotation frequency to prevent decay is a `workflow.*` binding.
  - Cross-training into the kitchen (the white paper's example of a server learning the kitchen, Part II, "Training infrastructure") is chef-gated.

## F. Visible progression: skill trees, badges, matrices

**F1. Badges raise activity volume, not demonstrated quality**
- **What it is:** a field experiment on a trading service. Users in the badge condition were more active across trading, commenting and general use. Quality was not measured.
- **Source:** Hamari, *Computers in Human Behavior* 71, 2017 (the research group's own summary).
- **Status:** verified-secondary.

**F2. Visible metrics steer behavior, including in unwanted directions**
- **What it is:** when GitHub removed its visible streak counter, long streaks were abandoned and weekend activity fell. The authors warn that gamification can steer behavior "in unexpected and unwanted directions."
- **Source:** Moldon, Strohmaier and Wachs, ICSE 2021 ([arXiv 2006.02371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02371)).
- **Status:** verified-primary (abstract).

**F3. Expected, contingent rewards and intrinsic motivation**
- **What it is:** a meta-analysis of experiments. Expected tangible rewards contingent on engagement, completion or performance undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation. The finding is contested (Cameron and Pierce comment).
- **Source:** Deci, Koestner and Ryan, *Psychological Bulletin* 125(6), 1999.
- **Status:** verified-secondary (not read in full). Mostly lab tasks.

**F4. Badge standards can carry criteria and evidence**
- **What it is:** Open Badges 3.0 is a W3C Verifiable Credential. It can embed the criteria assessed, evidence of the skill, the issuer, and alignment to a skill framework.
- **Source:** 1EdTech's own spec pages.
- **Status:** verified-secondary (the standards body describing its own standard; spec text not read in full).

**F5. Hospitality skills passport**
- **What it is:** a portable digital credential after a pre-employment program, built by UKHospitality with government partners.
- **Status:** lead-only (outcome claims are self-reported).
- **Implies for Sŏn:** entry-level only. It is a precedent for portability, not for progression.

**F6. Evidence gap on self-directed learning**
- No rigorous workplace study was found showing that visible competency matrices drive self-directed learning.
- The claims found are vendor marketing (lead-only).
- Treat "visibility drives self-direction" as a hypothesis to test, not a fact.

**Implies for Sŏn (F1 to F6):**
- Brandon wants a visible skill tree (group 2) and distrusts complacency and "participation" (group 7).
- The evidence says visible counters raise activity and can pull behavior toward whatever is counted.
- So the tree should light up on evidence (a passed practical, a proven conversation), not on completions. Open Badges' criteria and evidence fields fit that.
- The white paper's "more modules completed, more earned" (Part II, "Training infrastructure") pays for completion, which is the reward type the meta-analysis links to undermining. Flag for Brandon. One option is to pay for demonstrated mastery and for teaching, not for completion. Compensation design is Brandon's.

## Most load-bearing findings for Sŏn's program map

1. **Define gates by activity and supervision level, not by job title (B2, B1).** Entrustment scales and aviation operating-experience rules already match Brandon's structure:
   - verbal check and practical, then
   - the angel shift (personally observed, trainee extra to scheduled staffing), then
   - the eyes-off shift (help on request), then
   - lead status (may supervise and sign others).
   
   Humility, meaning using support well, is an assessable trust condition. Status: verified-primary.
2. **Train and certify the assessors (B4, C4).** Randomized nursing evidence and German law both treat preceptor or trainer training as the active ingredient. This supports Brandon's peer-plus-manager rule until leads complete the teaching path, and makes "Teaching at Sŏn" (LEA-001) a prerequisite on the map. Whether hourly leads can do this is HR-gated. Status: verified-primary.
3. **Peer and whole-team feedback works best as one global judgment on taught criteria (B3).** The "prove it to the team" shift should ask everyone one shared question rather than scoring a rubric. A trained assessor still makes the formal decision. Status: verified-primary.
4. **Cross-train in chains, not everyone-in-everything (D1).** Adjacent-role chains give most of the flexibility benefit:
   - service: runner, back waiter, front server
   - beverage: barback, bartender, barista
   - host as the bridge between the two
   
   This gives the on deck reserve its shape. Status: verified-primary theory, untested in restaurants.
5. **A floating pool fails without a home team, station orientation and a named contact (D2).** Design these into the reserve from day one, or reserve members become outsiders. Status: verified-primary (qualitative).
6. **Light the skill tree on demonstrated evidence, not completions (F1 to F3).** Visible counters raise activity and steer behavior toward what is counted, and completion-contingent rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation. This bears directly on the white paper's pay-per-module line and Brandon's distrust of participation. Flag for Brandon. Status: mixed; the Deci meta-analysis is contested.
7. **Every external ladder pairs knowledge with a realistic practical, and the knowledge unlocks the practical (C1 to C3, A4).** Sommelier, bartender guild and coffee ladders all work this way, and some require passing all parts together. Recertification is routine (Cheesecake Factory, 2006, self-report). This supports Brandon's verbal-plus-practical gate and his periodic-practical idea. External credentials are candidate linked electives, not Sŏn-written content. Status: verified-primary, except SCA (lead-only).
8. **One observed repetition is not readiness (C5, C6).** "See one, do one, teach one" survives only with deliberate practice to a standard and coaching that tapers off. Brandon's macro-movement drills are the deliberate-practice layer, and "teach one" belongs after mastery. Status: verified-primary.
