## Part 1. Service: shared customer-facing ground

### ORI-006 Two layers: the fixed standard and reading the customer
- Area: service
- Positions and timing: every CF position; readiness, first week, before release (it may follow the first supervised service; only the safety items and the calls must precede it). K takes a knowledge-only version (30 minutes inside KIT-101, writer 4).
- What it teaches:
  - The service has two layers, and the skill is knowing which one a moment belongs to.
    - The fixed layer is exact and identical for every customer: the place setting, glass and silver handling, the pour level, the set-down, seat order, the allergy route, the read-back, the call and callback. Exactness here is the care (BG 02 Mahk; BG 03 "This is not omotenashi").
    - The read layer changes with the customer: how much to say, when to approach, how warm or how spare, whether to offer more, the pace of the meal, whether an occasion is acknowledged aloud.
  - Three tests for sorting a moment: would a different customer change the right move? Does the house standards book (D46) name it? Is safety or another person's work at stake? If the book names it or safety is involved, it is fixed.
  - Paired cases: the same moment shown done to the standard and done by reading. Set-down (fixed: seat order, quiet; read: whether to name the dish or let a conversation run). Water (fixed: level and pour; read: whether to top up during a toast). Description (fixed: honest, no overselling; read: length).
  - The personas as calibration, never as boxes to sort people into (BG 05 Mia, Marcus, Clara, David): the expert who does not want her food explained; the regular who wants to be known without being announced; the first-timer; the customer whose kitchen visit is set before service.
  - The two failure modes: improvising the fixed layer ("I do it my way") and scripting the read layer (the same speech for every table).
- What the person can do afterward: sort ten service moments into fixed or read and give the reason for each; for one fixed moment, state the standard exactly; for one read moment, name two cues that would change the move.
- How it is proven: a spoken sorting check with a lead (ten moments, two explained aloud). Test-out: an experienced hire takes the check on day one; a pass skips the remote lesson. Unlearning another house's habits is ORI-033, not this module.
- Time and place: 1.5 h (0.75 remote, 0.75 on site).
- Waits on: the standards book, floor half (D46); BG 02, 03, 05 re-read in Box.
- Absorbs: ORI-006, ORI-019 (persona cases), the principle half of ORI-029.

### ORI-020 The register for each daypart you work
- Area: service
- Positions and timing: every CF position, for each daypart worked; readiness for the first daypart, then before working each new daypart. The late-night part is readiness for BT and for anyone working late night only once late night runs (`workflow.daypart_schedule`, D24: content for every daypart is built now, and a daypart not yet running is parked; SVC-070 is parked with it). Until then it counts in no window.
- What it teaches:
  - One house, one standard, several registers. The register is how the house sounds and moves at a given hour: pace of speech, how much is said, how close the team stands, the energy of the room. Dayparts are named by plain descriptors only (morning, dinner, late night); internal daypart names never appear on any surface.
  - For each daypart: what the customer is usually there for, the pace, the sound and light (BG 14, cited), what the six constants look like in it (BG 06 "Master register · the six constants"), and the moment the room changes into it (`workflow.daypart_schedule`).
  - The late-night part: what loosens (the register, the music, the length of conversation, the pace of the check) and what never moves (allergy route, pour level, glass standard, responsible service, the read-back, the call and callback). "Loosened" is never permission to drop precision.
  - How to recognize the turn: the cues before it is called, who calls it (`people.late_night_trigger`, see SVC-070, parked), and what each position changes at the call.
- What the person can do afterward: describe how the same moment (arrival, a drink order, the check) differs between two dayparts they work, and name what stays fixed.
- How it is proven: a spoken check per daypart with a lead; the late-night part also observed on a supervised late shift. A spoken check does not release a daypart on its own: release needs a supervised service in that daypart. Test-out: the spoken check alone, prove-first.
- Time and place: 1 h per daypart (0.5 remote, 0.5 on site); the late-night part counts only once late night runs.
- Waits on: `workflow.daypart_schedule`; which dayparts run at opening (D24); BG 06 and BG 14 re-read; `people.late_night_trigger`.
- Absorbs: ORI-020, SVC-043.

### SVC-009 When a customer crosses a line
- Area: service
- Positions and timing: every CF position, readiness, before the first supervised service (kept ahead of it with the safety items: a new person can meet this on their first night, and the first move protects them; 0.75 h); the manager part for MD and OM, readiness. No host is released before the policy exists (spine 2.2).
- What it teaches:
  - What "crossing a line" covers, as the policy defines it: abuse or threats to a team member, harassment (sexual, racial or other), repeated degrading treatment, behavior that endangers others (`founder.mistreatment_policy`).
  - The first move for the person on the receiving end: step back from the table, do not argue, do not handle it alone, and get the person holding the room (the MD, or the lead by delegation: `people.room_holder`). One short line is enough ("I'll get someone to help with this").
  - For a teammate who sees it: take over the table's immediate needs, and give the person a way out of the space.
  - The person mistreated comes first: they are checked on before the table is dealt with, and they may leave the section for the rest of the night.
  - The report afterward: facts only, written at close (ADM-109 standard), to the route the policy names; harassment goes to the HR route (CMP-103), never only to the incident review.
  - Manager part: the conversation with the customer (private, short, the house's position stated once), the options the policy allows (finish the meal elsewhere, end the visit, no further service), never negotiating the team member's treatment against the customer's spend, and the follow-up with the team member.
- What the person can do afterward: in three scenarios, name the first move, who they go to, and what they write afterward. Managers: run the customer conversation in role-play.
- How it is proven: a spoken check of three scenarios; manager part by role-play with Brandon. No test-out: house policy, and it touches safety.
- Time and place: 0.75 h (0.25 remote, 0.5 on site); manager part 1 h on site.
- Waits on: `founder.mistreatment_policy` (D50; must exist before opening); TBRI review; `people.room_holder`; Brandon interview (a night a customer crossed a line, from his own account).
- Absorbs: SVC-009, LEA-031 (floor side).

## Part 2. Service: floor block (H, LH, FR, BW, FS, LFR, LS, MD)

### SVC-013 Calls and callbacks on the floor and at the service bar
- Area: service
- Positions and timing: Floor, readiness, first on-site days, before the first supervised service; the service-bar part for BW, readiness on the move from FR; a service-well part for FR, readiness, only if runners carry drinks (`workflow.runners_carry_drinks`; if they do not, BEV-016 says so). Built on SAF-007 (movement calls) and shared with KIT-002 (the pass).
- What it teaches:
  - The closed loop: name the receiver, say the message, hear it repeated, confirm. A message not called back was not received. Critical calls are always closed: an allergy, an 86, a refire, a change of seat, a table that has slowed.
  - The floor call list (`workflow.floor_calls`): what each call means, who may make it, who answers it. Kitchen words come from `chef.pass_calls` and are not changed by the floor.
  - Volume: the call is pitched for the receiver, not the room; nothing from the floor or the pass is heard at the tables (BG 13 beat 8, threshold volume).
  - Where calls are never made: at the table, across the room, in the customer's sightline when a signal would do.
  - The service-bar part (BW): how drinks are called and picked up at the service well; checking each drink against the ticket for seat, garnish, glass and allergy before lifting; what to do when a drink is wrong or missing (call it back to the bartender, never fix it yourself); the service well's lanes and who yields (shared with BEV-016's service-well part, written by writer 3).
  - The service-well part (FR, conditional): picking up a called drink for a run, the same check against the ticket before lifting, carrying stemware on a tray to the seat (SVC-003), and calling a wrong drink back rather than fixing it.
- What the person can do afterward: run a sequence of floor calls at service tempo with every critical call closed; at the service well, pick up and check a set of drinks with one planted fault and catch it.
- How it is proven: a practical drill with the pass and the bar together, then observed on supervised services; recheck in the warm-up (D44). Test-out: one run at tempo for an experienced hire. The allergy call never tests out and passes in every attempt.
- Time and place: 1 h on site (+1 h on site for BW, the service-bar part; +0.5 h on site for FR, the service-well part, if runners carry drinks).
- Waits on: `workflow.floor_calls`, `chef.pass_calls`, `workflow.service_bar`, `team.call_vocabulary` (team-gated); the space (D58).
- Absorbs: SVC-013, SVC-014 and SVC-022 (floor side; the pass itself is KIT-002).

### SVC-015 Acting on a protocol flag
- Area: service
- Positions and timing: Floor, readiness, first week, before release (it may follow the first supervised service, where a lead holds any flagged table).
- What it teaches:
  - What a flag is: a decision made before service about how a particular customer or table is served (for example a pre-set kitchen visit, a request not to be recognized aloud, a mobility arrangement, a known allergy at high severity). Flags are system decisions, never floor calls (BG 15 "Key protocols").
  - Where flags live and how they reach each position: the reservation record, the pre-service view, the POS ticket (`tool.reservations.flags`, `tool.pos.ticket_notes`; the reading skill is SYS-111).
  - The rule: act on the flag exactly; do not improvise around it, override it on instinct, or mention it to the customer. If the flag no longer fits what is in front of you, take it to the person holding the room before acting.
  - Cases: David's kitchen visit set before service (BG 05 David); a customer flagged not to be greeted by name; a flag that conflicts with what the customer now asks for.
- What the person can do afterward: given four flagged tables, state the move each flag requires and what they would never say; name who they go to when a flag and the customer disagree.
- How it is proven: a spoken check of four cases. Test-out: the same check, cold.
- Time and place: 0.5 h (0.25 remote, 0.25 on site).
- Waits on: `founder.protocol_criteria` (D50); `tool.reservations.flags`; BG 15 re-read.
- Absorbs: SVC-015 (the tool side is SYS-111 and SYS-116).

### SVC-079 Calling your own load early
- Area: service
- Positions and timing: Floor, readiness, taught before the first supervised service and practiced on it.
- What it teaches:
  - What never drops on your route, however the night builds: the allergy call, the callback, the seat check, the plate or drink check before lifting.
  - What may wait, in order, when you are behind (`workflow.floor_priorities`, set with the MD and OM): for example a polish or a restock waits; a waiting table's first contact does not.
  - The point of no return: the moment when asking for help no longer saves the table. Ask before it, in one line, to the person named for your position (`people.floor_help`).
  - How to ask: what you need, for which table, by when ("I need someone on 12's water and 14's clear, now").
  - Asking early is a skill the house expects, never a sign of weakness.
- What the person can do afterward: in a loaded scenario, name what they keep, what they let wait, and the exact line they say to ask for help, before the scenario reaches its point of no return.
- How it is proven: a scenario walk-through with a lead; then observed on the hardest supervised service (D40). Test-out: prove-first; an experienced hire reads the priorities and takes the walk-through cold. The observation on the hardest service is always taken.
- Time and place: 0.5 h on site.
- Waits on: `workflow.floor_priorities`; `people.floor_help`; Brandon interview (a night the floor went under, and where it could have been called).
- Absorbs: SVC-079, the floor part of ORI-004.

### KIT-003 What the kitchen needs from the floor, and why
- Area: food and menu (taught by the chef; content owner writer 4, floor framing writer 2)
- Positions and timing: FR, BW, FS, LFR, LS, MD; readiness, first week. Not the host: the host's slice (pacing and the cost of a stacked wave) is taught inside SVC-011. Taught by the service and development chef or the executive chef.
- What it teaches:
  - How an order becomes a plate at Sŏn, from the floor's side: the ticket, the fire, the station, the pass, the run (`chef.service_flow`).
  - Honest times: what the floor tells the pass when a table slows, leaves the table, or is not ready, and why a table that is not ready makes a cooked plate wait or die.
  - Early 86s: why the floor stops selling at the call, and how the floor hears about one (SVC-013).
  - Modifications: which ones the kitchen can make, which ones it cannot (`chef.modifier_list`), and what a modification costs mid-fire (a station stopping, other tables waiting).
  - Fire when the table is ready, never by the clock (the floor half of SVC-035).
  - Kitchen visits and chef introductions are set before service, never improvised at the table (BG 05 David; `workflow.kitchen_visit`).
  - Allergies: the floor's links in the chain from booking to plate (SAF-001, SAF-004, SAF-013).
- What the person can do afterward: explain to the chef why a fire is held or sent, what a modification will cost at that moment, and what they do the instant they hear an 86.
- How it is proven: a spoken check with the chef. Test-out: prove-first; an experienced hire reads the service flow and modifier list and takes the spoken check cold. The allergy links are always proven.
- Time and place: 1.5 h on site.
- Waits on: chef (service flow, modifier list, kitchen visits, who expos); `workflow.kitchen_visit`.
- Absorbs: KIT-003, SVC-014 (the floor's half).

## Part 3. Service: the food runner set (FR; BW and FS inherit it)

### SVC-003 Carrying plates, trays and glass racks (drills)
- Area: service
- Positions and timing: FR readiness, first week; BW and FS hold it from FR. BB takes the glass rack bank inside its bar block.
- What it teaches:
  - Drills, each one movement at a time, slow, fast, then slow to fast (ORI-016 method):
    - two plates in one hand and one in the other; three plates; hot plates with a cloth (`chef.plate_temperature_handling`);
    - a stemware tray, empty then loaded, held flat and balanced from the center, at the height set in the standards book;
    - a glass rack, lifted with the legs and carried through traffic (SAF-007).
  - Eyes up, not on the load: the carry is automatic so attention is free for the room and the table.
  - Carrying while talking: a drill set at three tempos while answering a question.
  - The set-down: quiet, in seat order (SVC-019), plate turned to its presentation mark (`chef.plate_orientation`), never reaching across a customer. The side of service is `workflow.service_side` (D48).
  - What to do when a plate starts to tip, a tray shifts or a path closes: stop, set down at the nearest safe surface, call it.
- What the person can do afterward: carry two and three plates, hot plates and a loaded stemware tray at three tempos while talking, and set down in seat order without sound.
- How it is proven: a banked drill run-through scored against the standards book (D46), then the carry inside the runner practical; recheck in the warm-up (D44). Test-out: the banked run-through at entry for an experienced hire (D39). Safety element, demonstrated on every attempt and never tested out: hot plates (the cloth between hand and plate, never a bare grip, a hot plate named aloud to the customer and set down before any word), the call before a blind corner or door (SAF-007), and the stop-and-set-down when a load shifts.
- Time and place: 4 h on site, split across days.
- Waits on: the standards book (D46); `workflow.service_side`; `chef.plate_orientation`, `chef.plate_temperature_handling`; tableware (BG 17 held items); the space (D58).
- Absorbs: SVC-003, SVC-020, the carrying half of SVC-084.

### SVC-071 Glass, silver and linen: handling, polishing, inspection
- Area: service (bar part written by writer 3)
- Positions and timing: FR and BW, floor part, readiness; BB, BA, BT, bar part, readiness. FS holds the floor part from FR.
- What it teaches:
  - Floor part:
    - Handling: stemware by the stem only, tumblers by the base, silver by the handle, never touching a rim, bowl or blade; plates by the edge.
    - Polishing to one method from the standards book (D46): steam or not, cloth type, the order of the glass, how it is held, where polishing happens (never in the customer's sight).
    - Inspection under light: spots, lipstick, chips, cracks, bent tines, water marks; a flawed piece is pulled, never "good enough".
    - Linen: folding, placing and replacing to the book; a stained or damp piece never returns to the table.
    - Breakage: stop, clear the area, call it, where broken glass goes (`workflow.breakage`); glass near ice is BEV-014's call at the bar.
  - Bar part (writer 3): every house glass and the drink it serves (`brand.glassware`); chilling and pre-warming; rack loading; bar breakage and the ice well (BEV-014).
- What the person can do afterward: set and inspect a station of glass and silver to the book with zero defects found by a second person.
- How it is proven: a practical with a zero-defect table check by a second person; recheck in the warm-up. Test-out: technique by banked run-through; the inspection standard is house-specific and is always checked.
- Time and place: floor part 1.5 h on site; bar part 2 h on site.
- Waits on: the standards book (D46); `brand.glassware`; `workflow.breakage`; tableware makers (BG 17 held items).
- Absorbs: SVC-071, the glass bank of SVC-003, BEV-104 (beverage draft, glass), the handling half of MNU-006.

### SVC-004 Moving through the room and walking a customer in
- Area: service
- Positions and timing: FR, runner part, readiness; H, host part, readiness. MD proves both by run-through.
- What it teaches:

  - "We walk, we never point" is in BG-md 06; confirm against the PDF before citing.

### SVC-018 Scanning on every trip
- Area: service
- Positions and timing: FR readiness; H and BW part readiness. Reinforced in ongoing education for everyone on the floor.
- What it teaches:
  - The habit: look up and across the room on every trip out and every trip back, before you need to (scanning research in sport, Jordet, verified-primary carried, correlational).
  - What to look for, in order: a customer looking for someone; a hand raised or a glance held; an empty glass or a finished plate; a hazard on the floor; a table that has slowed or stopped eating; a teammate in trouble.
  - What to do with what you see: act if it is yours and quick; tell the owner in one line if it is not; never walk past a customer looking for help.
  - Host part: scanning the threshold and the room while greeting; BW part: scanning sections for the table's state (SVC-006).
  - BW ongoing part, the room's senses (after release): what a customer feels but may not name, and the fix for each:
    - a draft from the door or a vent: offer a seat change through the FS, or tell the MD if the door is the cause;
    - glare from a low sun or a lamp: lower a blind where the house allows, or offer to move the customer's chair;
    - a sound spike near the bar or the pass: tell the MD (sound is SVC-069), never adjust it yourself;
    - a smell from the kitchen or the restroom: tell the MD and the pass in one line;
    - a seat too warm or too cold (sun, a vent, a radiator): offer a seat change through the FS.
    - The rule: the BW fixes what is within reach (a blind, a chair offered); sound, light and the room's systems go to the MD.
- What the person can do afterward: after a trip through a staged room, name what changed since the last trip and what they did or told. BW ongoing: notice and act on two sense problems in a live service.
- How it is proven: a habit drill in the set room with planted changes, recalled aloud; observed on supervised services. BW ongoing part: observed by the LS or MD on live services; not a gate. Test-out: the drill run cold.
- Time and place: FR 1 h on site; H and BW 0.5 h on site; BW ongoing part 1 h on site.
- Waits on: Brandon interview (what he looks at when he crosses a room); the space and its controls (D58, `workflow.room_controls`).
- Absorbs: SVC-018, SVC-041 (folded in as the BW ongoing part).

### SVC-019 Seeing the table as positions
- Area: service
- Positions and timing: FR, BW, FS, BT (counter seats); readiness, first week.
- What it teaches:
  - The house numbering: where position one is at each table shape, which way the count runs, how counter seats are numbered, how a joined table is counted (`workflow.position_numbering`; `tool.pos.seat_position`).
  - Reading the seat from the ticket and the plate marker, and holding a table's positions in your head without asking the table "who had the...".
  - Why it matters: every plate to the right seat, every modified plate to the right seat every time (SAF-004).
  - Encoding method: picture the person in the seat, not the number (seat encoding research, Stevens 1993, verified-secondary carried).

- How it is proven: a practical at a staged table with one seat change mid-meal. Ongoing part: a conversation with a lead; not a gate. Test-out: the same practical, cold. The modified-plate seat never tests out.
- Time and place: 1 h on site; ongoing part 1 h on site.

### SVC-008 Describing honestly, at the right length
- Area: service (bar part written by writer 3; wine language shared with the wine track)
- Positions and timing: by part, readiness; the ongoing part for anyone after release.
  - FR: the set-down line.
  - BW: the second question.
  - FS: full description without instructing.
  - BA and BT: the bar part.
- What it teaches:
  - The honesty rule for every part: say what is in the dish or drink and what it is like; never oversell ("the best", "amazing"), never claim what the chef has not said, never shape the customer's taste before they taste. "Let me find out" when unsure.
  - Set-down line (FR): the dish name and, at most, one plain line chosen for this table; skipped or shortened when a conversation is running (read layer, ORI-006). Built from MNU-001 runner depth.
  - The second question (BW): answering the follow-up in one or two sentences (what is in it, how it is made, where it comes from) and handing deeper questions to the front server.
  - Full description (FS): the dish at the depth the customer asks, technique and origin and the chef's intent, without lecturing; never explaining a food to a customer who grew up with it (BG 05 Mia); reading whether the customer wants more.
  - Pronunciation: house names and Korean terms said correctly and simply, never performed (`founder.glossary`).
  - Bar part (writer 3): a drink at two lengths; never explaining to a customer who knows more.
  - Ongoing: telling a story once, well, at the table; the voice at conversation distance in a loud room (BG 06 register).
- What the person can do afterward: describe any item at their depth at two lengths and adjust to a customer's cue; say "let me find out" instead of guessing.
- How it is proven: a role-play with a lead playing three customers (the hurried, the curious, the expert); recheck on menu change through MNU-002. Test-out: prove-first; an experienced hire studies MNU-001 at their depth and takes the role-play cold (for an external FS, inside the FS mock service, see SVC-078). The honesty rule and "let me find out" are proven on every attempt.
- Time and place: FR 0.5 h, BW 0.5 h, FS 1 h, bar part 1 h; all on site.

### SVC-002 Recovering a miss within your range
- Area: service
- Positions and timing: by part, readiness: FR, BW, FS (full range), LS (lead part), MD (widest range).
- What it teaches:
  - The method for every part:
    1. Notice the miss, or hear it, without defending.
    2. Name it to the customer plainly and own it for the house ("That's not right; I'll fix it now").
    3. Match the make-good to the kind of loss: time lost is answered with speed and attention; a wrong item with the right one, fast; a lost moment (an occasion missed, a bad pour at a toast) with a gesture that restores the moment; a safety miss (an allergen reached the table) with the allergy and emergency route first (SAF-001, SAF-006), and nothing else until it is handled.
    4. Act inside your range; hand up beyond it.
    5. Close the loop: check back, tell the pair and the lead, and record it at close (ADM-109).
  - Ranges: what each position may give without asking, and the triggers that send it up (`fact.recovery_range.*`, holders `people.*`, D25). Amounts are never recalled.
  - FR part: repairing a miss between the pass and the table without showing the customer the coordination failure (hold the plate, reroute through the server, call it back to the pass).
  - BW part: the clear, pour or reset that went wrong, and who to tell.
  - FS part: the full range for the table; when a miss belongs to the MD.
  - Lead part (LS): stepping in on another server's recovery without taking the table (SVC-067).
  - Widest-range part (MD): the gestures only the room holder gives; when a miss goes to Brandon; a pre-authorized gesture with no one free to carry it out (`workflow.recovery_owner`).
- What the person can do afterward: in four scenarios at their level, choose a make-good that fits the loss, stay inside range, and close the loop.
- How it is proven: a spoken check of four scenarios, then a recovery case inside the position's practical. Test-out: prove-first; an experienced hire reads the ranges and triggers and takes the four scenarios cold (for an external FS, banked inside the FS mock service, see SVC-078). The safety-miss scenario (an allergen reached the table) is in every attempt and never tests out.
- Time and place: FR 0.5 h, BW 0.5 h, FS 2 h, LS 1 h, MD 1.5 h; on site.
- Waits on: `fact.recovery_range.*` (unbound until a source is chosen (Airtable retired)); `people.*` holders; `workflow.recovery_owner`; Brandon interview (how he chooses a recovery).
- Absorbs: SVC-002, SVC-048.

### ORI-005 How flavor works: what we say changes what they taste
- Area: food and menu
- Positions and timing: CF, ongoing, after release. Parked until each source is verified: the effects below are named now so the module can be populated, but nothing is taught as fact before verification.

### MNU-101 Product feedback: from the customer to the maker
- Area: food and menu (shared with beverage; track examples from writer 3)
- Positions and timing: All after release; LS, LB and LH collect. Ongoing, standing.
- What it teaches:
  - The note: what was served, when, to whom; what the customer said or left; what you tasted yourself; who it suits; keep or change.
  - Separating taste, fault and fit.
  - Trusted once the tasting check for that track is passed (BEV-008, BEV-019).
  - How notes reach the chef and HB, and how they answer back (`workflow.product_feedback`, `tool.feedback_log`).
  - Reading returned plates without comment at the table (FR's first act of feedback).
  - Asking a customer for a reaction without fishing: one open question at a natural moment, never at the first bite ("How is it landing for you?" rather than "Isn't it amazing?"); accept "fine" without pressing; never ask a table that is mid-conversation; never ask twice.
  - At the table, a customer's taste versus a product fault: a dislike (too spicy for them, not what they expected) is met with an alternative within range (SVC-002) and noted as fit; a fault (an off taste, a wrong temperature, a corked wine, a flat drink) is taken back at once, called to the pass or the bar, and noted as a fault. When unsure, treat it as a fault at the table and sort it out at the pass.
- What the person can do afterward: give a short, specific note a maker can act on, and tell at the table whether a returned item is a fault or a fit.
- How it is proven: three logged notes reviewed by a lead in the first month.
- Time and place: 1 h on site, then standing.
- Waits on: `workflow.product_feedback`, `tool.feedback_log`; chef and HB agreement.
- Absorbs: SVC-050, FLV-006 (reporting), BEV-105 and BEV-110 (draft product feedback).
