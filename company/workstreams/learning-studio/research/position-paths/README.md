## 1. What this is and how to read the folder

This folder is the content-first rebuild of Sŏn's training program (D52 to D61). It starts from what each position must know, do and decide, and builds modules that say specifically what they teach. The 274 loose candidate modules from the discovery pass are raw material: each one has been placed, merged or dropped with a reason, and none was copied as-is.

Six files, read in this order:

1. **`spine.md`.** The program in one place: the positions and how they connect (section 1); the readiness window for every position and entry route, with hours (section 2, including a day-by-day reference sequence in 2.6); ongoing education and the promotion gates (section 3); the knowledge tracks (section 4); the full module list by area (section 5); who writes what (6); what is open for Brandon (7); and a crosswalk of every discovery and draft ID (8).
2. **`modules-house-compliance-systems.md`.** The house (ORI, CUL, SAF), systems and technology (SYS, including the SevenRooms track) and compliance (CMP). 69 entries.
3. **`modules-service-food-menu.md`.** Service (SVC) and food and menu (MNU). 57 entries.
4. **`modules-beverage.md`.** The bar team's core, the drink list, the barista and bartender sets, and every knowledge track: wine, coffee, tea, spirits, beer, Korean drinks, non-alcoholic. 47 entries.
5. **`modules-leadership-admin-culinary.md`.** Culinary (KIT, a draft for the executive chef), leadership and teaching (LEA) and administration (ADM). 80 entries.
6. **`placement-map.md`.** One row per discovery module (274 rows): where it went and why.

Every module entry uses one format: area; positions and timing (readiness, meaning before working solo, or ongoing); what it teaches, as the actual facts, steps, distinctions, standards and scenarios; what the person can do afterward; how it is proven; time and place; what it waits on; and which discovery modules it absorbs. Each module file ends with a hours table, a list of what could not be made specific yet and who supplies it, and a "Spine changes needed" list. Those lists have been applied to the spine, so the spine and the modules now agree.

Three terms are used throughout. **Readiness** is the training completed before working solo to the standard; it fits in two to three weeks at 40 paid hours a week, two preferred (D55). **Ongoing education** is everything after release; it is paid, scheduled, and may run much longer. The **angel shift** is Brandon's name (pending his confirmation) for the last supervised shift, where a trainer oversees the person the whole night; the unsupervised shifts after sign-off follow it, with support on hand and nobody watching.

Mastery is proven in conversation and practice, never by clicking through: a spoken check for knowledge, a practical for skill, supervised services including at least one full night, and a standing recheck after release. Physical movements are drilled apart from decisions, on site and paid (D41). Experienced hires may attempt any non-safety module's proof cold before taking the instruction ("prove-first", D56); a failed cold attempt is not a recorded miss. Safety elements are demonstrated on every attempt and never test out.

## 3. The knowledge tracks

Every track builds two things (D54): customer advocacy, which is knowing what this customer wants and would enjoy, and business advocacy, which is being trusted to give product feedback the chef or the head of beverage can act on. Every level of every track names its customer element and the structured note it must produce; one module, product feedback (MNU-101), carries the method for all of them. Outside courses count only if delivered and tracked inside Trainual (D27); none of the providers was found to do so, so until that is answered each serves as a content benchmark and as evidence toward a test-out, never as the gate.

**Wine gates hardest.** Level 1 (about 10 hours, the first part of WSET Level 2) is the food runner's hard gate to back waiter: how wine is made in plain words, structure in customer words, the principal grapes, the by-the-glass list and who on the floor would order each glass. Level 2 (about 36 hours, WSET Level 2 scope, plus opening and pouring still and sparkling) and level 3 (about 70 hours: Sommelier 1-level knowledge on Sŏn's list in dated sections, plus recommending wine by role-play against set customer profiles) are the back waiter's hard gate to front server; a front server holds level 3 as a condition of the role. Level 4 is an elective toward a wine seat if one is created; the head of beverage holds above level 3 at hire. The design rules: full depth of knowledge; translating for the customer and reading the customer are separate elements that are always proven; expert vocabulary is a team tool and is graded absent at the table; selling and recommending practice sits inside the track. A certificate counts toward theory test-outs only.

**Coffee.** The house program at readiness (what is poured, where it is pulled, hold times, which coffee suits which customer and course), with a new module for whoever pulls the batch espresso in the back. Level 2, coffee craft (about 40 hours, SCA intermediate and Barista Hustle scope), in the barista's first months. Level 3, owning the coffee program (80 to 120 hours, SCA professional or Barista Hustle advanced scope; the Q Grader is not needed unless Sŏn buys green coffee) for the coffee program owner, a soft gate to that role. Hand espresso is an elective, conditional on equipment, because there is no espresso machine on the front bar (WP, to confirm in Box).

**Spirits.** Recognition at readiness for barbacks and baristas; every back-bar bottle in full for bartenders, a hard gate from barback. Part one (about 20 hours, WSET Spirits Level 2 scope) is a soft gate for back waiters and the proposed hard gate to lead bartender. Full depth (about 70 hours) for leads and the head of beverage. The customer element is who would enjoy a bottle neat or in which drink; the note is on a back-bar bottle.

**Beer.** Only if beer is listed. Serving at readiness for bartenders and barbacks; styles (Cicerone Certified Beer Server syllabus) open; an owner step only if beer becomes a program.

**Tea.** Every tea and the founder's matcha method at readiness for baristas and bartenders, with which tea suits which customer and course; tea craft (about 30 hours) in the first months, and a floor level (about 6 hours) as a soft gate for back waiters; owning the tea program is parked until a tea owner exists.

**Food and menu gates less hard.** Every dish at the position's depth (host 3 hours, runner 5, back waiter 8, front server 12, counter 3, Maître d' 12 with prove-first), re-proven on every menu change; Korean food culture behind the menu (a short readiness part, then about 10 hours ongoing); producers and where the food comes from, a soft gate from front server to lead server. No certificate exists; it is house knowledge proven in conversation. The customer element is who each dish suits and helping a customer choose; the note is on a dish or a producer, and the module teaches how to ask a customer for a reaction without fishing and how to tell a customer's taste from a product fault at the table.

**Korean drinks and non-alcoholic.** Korean drinks at a short level for bartenders if listed and at floor level for back waiters and front servers, with sources to be founder-sourced and verified first. The customer not drinking alcohol is readiness for the bar and front servers, with equal ceremony.

## 4. The sommelier findings

**What the introductory certification covers, and its time and format.** The Court of Master Sommeliers Introductory course and examination covers the major wine regions of the world with their classic appellations, classifications and label law; grape growing and winemaking; sparkling methods and fortified wines; beer, sake and spirits at an introductory level; pairing principles; and service standards. It runs as two days in person, or online over up to 180 days, and ends in a 70-question multiple-choice examination on theory only, with no tasting and no service practical; it does not expire (mastersommeliers.org, verified-primary). The pass mark and the fee are bindings: `fact.cert.cms_intro.pass_mark`, `fact.fee.cms.intro`. The next level, Certified, examines deductive tasting, theory and a hospitality and service practical in one sitting, with a minimum score on each (verified-primary; `fact.fee.cms.certified`). The nearest alternatives are WSET Level 2 (the knowledge benchmark for Sŏn's level 2; verified-primary; `fact.fee.wset.level2`) and WSET Level 3, which sets a minimum of 84 study hours (verified-primary; `fact.fee.wset.level3`). Whether Sŏn pays for any of them is `policy.wine_cert_sponsorship`.

**What the evidence says certification does and does not produce on the floor.** One peer-reviewed study (Orlowski 2022, International Hospitality Review; verified-primary) put servers through WSET Level 2 and found that knowledge rose but wine sales did not move against control units, and that the trainees themselves named the missing piece as selling practice. Research on wine language finds that experts and novices describe wine in different vocabularies, and that expert descriptions are less useful to people outside the field (Croijmans and Majid 2016; Food Quality and Preference 2021; verified-secondary). The Court's own 2022 member survey had members rating belonging lowest among the measures asked (CMS Americas; verified-primary, self-report). No evidence was found that the certificate itself raises customer satisfaction or the quality of a recommendation; no evidence was found that it lowers them either. The content of the introductory curriculum is sound and is the right knowledge benchmark for a front server.

**How Brandon's view holds up.** Brandon's view, held without having taken the course, is that certification culture builds exclusionary language and rewards flexing knowledge over communicating it to the customer. The evidence supports the half about communication: the certificate examines theory only, and the one controlled study found that knowledge without selling practice changed nothing at the table. The evidence is thinner on exclusion as a cause: the vocabulary gap is real, and belonging scores are low inside the Court, but neither shows the certificate making people worse with customers. The honest reading is that the certificate produces knowledge and nothing else by itself, and that what Sŏn wants (plain translation, reading the customer, a recommendation the customer keeps control of) has to be built and proven separately whichever route is chosen.

**The recommendation: both.** Replace the gate and link the certificate. The gate is Sŏn's internal program (wine levels 1 to 3 with recommending wine), which teaches the introductory curriculum to full depth on Sŏn's own list, in dated sections a back waiter can reach while working full time, and adds the two elements the certificate omits: translation for the customer and reading the customer, both always proven in conversation and role-play and never tested out. The certificate is linked, not written: a CMS Introductory, Certified or WSET holder tests out of the theory hours by passing the section tests and the spoken check, and the certificate is uploaded and tracked in Trainual as evidence. No provider was found to deliver inside Trainual (unverified; ask each), so no outside course can be the gate under D27. Whether Sŏn funds the outside exam for people who want the credential is a policy call that does not change the gate.

## 7. What the red teams changed

Two red teams read the first draft: one for specificity and justification, one for time fit and consistency. Their findings were applied to the four module files by their writers and then to the spine. In brief:

- **Specificity.** Topic stubs were given content or removed: the master server bar is drafted for Brandon to confirm; the unblock conversation names its four outcomes; the watchlist names its signals; the inspection walk names its stops; "present without performing" names four moments with a cue and a house move each; "care as craft" names the warning signs and the routes; three white-paper measures (employee NPS, the cultural labor score, the customer recognition rate) are defined in one plain sentence each.
