## 1. The house

### ORI-001 Why Sŏn exists and the standard it holds
- Area: the house
- Positions and timing: All, kitchen included (the kitchen version is approved by the executive chef). Readiness, day one, before any other module.
- What it teaches:
  - Why the house exists and what it is for, told by Brandon on screen in his own words (`founder.why_story`). The studio writes no version of it.
  - What "one of the best restaurants in the country" means at Sŏn: consistency on every night, for every customer, not one great night. The public criteria the field is judged by are cited as context and never adopted as house standards: Michelin's consistency between visits (verified-secondary), the James Beard Foundation's "consistent excellence" and values clause (verified-primary), Forbes Travel Guide's anonymous inspection (verified-secondary). The house never plays to a guide's checklist.
  - The standard lives in the smallest task. One unseen detail is shown, not told: a polished glass beside an unpolished one, a set place beside a near-miss. The person names what is different.
  - The non-negotiables, cited by label from BG 01 "Company non-negotiables"; never restated in the module.
  - The house value: learning is work, and it is paid, on the clock and never cut to make a number (WP Part II "Labor as asset, not cost", cited). The legal rule behind it is taught and cited in CMP-105, not here. Any pay detail waits for the transparency sheet (`founder.transparency_sheet`).
  - Struggle during readiness is normal and temporary. The bar is something the house builds with the person, not a screen they are measured against.
- What the person can do afterward: explain in their own words why one house rule exists, and give one example from their own role of what consistency over time looks like.
- How it is proven: a spoken check of about 10 minutes with the mentor (D21). No test-out and no prove-first: the module is Brandon's own telling, so the material and the instruction are the same thing and cannot be skipped. Experienced hires take it unchanged.
- Time and place: 2 h (1.5 remote and paid, 0.5 on site).
- Waits on: Brandon interview (the why story, his own bar, told in his own account; nothing reconstructed from his lineage houses); `founder.why_story`; `founder.transparency_sheet`; the recording or presenter production route (`tool.video`; SYS-144 if authoring opens).
- Absorbs: ORI-025, ORI-029 (the principle; the exact-standard drills stay in SVC-071 and SVC-072), ORI-030, the why half of ORI-022.

### ORI-015 How learning, proof and promotion work here
- Area: the house
- Positions and timing: All. Readiness, day one, with ORI-001.
- What it teaches:
  - Readiness and ongoing education. Readiness is what is completed before working solo: two to three weeks, two preferred, at 40 paid hours a week, part remote, never overtime (D55). Everything after release is ongoing education, some of it long.
  - How readiness is proven, in order: knowledge at the screen; understanding in a spoken check; skill in a practical; the angel shift, Brandon's name for the gate shift where a trainer, the mentor, oversees the person the whole night (D21; term pending his confirmation, `founder.term.angel_shift`); then unsupervised shifts after sign-off, where support is on hand but nobody is watching and the team gives notes as on any night (D9), until one more would not change the call (D10). Nobody is signed off by clicking through.
  - Release covers only what was seen: at least one supervised service is a full night (`workflow.full_night`), and a person is released only for the dayparts in which they were supervised (D10). A walk-through of a daypart (ORI-020) does not release it.
  - "Not yet" is a changed practice plan and a required interval, never a verdict (D11). A not-yet extends the paid readiness window; that is normal, planned for, and says nothing about ability. The fail limit and the override path go to Brandon (`team.fail_limit`).
  - After sign-off: the warm-up inside pre-shift; a re-proof on every menu or list change (D40); and the standing recheck at every rank, founders included (D44). What a recheck is: on a set cadence (`workflow.recheck_cadence`), safety items always, then a sample of the person's practicals and spoken checks, run in booked check slots on a leader's schedule (LEA-101) or inside the warm-up. A recheck miss means a short practice plan and a second check, never a demotion by itself.
  - Test-outs and prove-first (D56). Every module that is not safety allows prove-first for an experienced hire: read the material, then attempt the module's own proof (spoken check, role-play, simulation, practical) cold, skipping the instruction and drills. A failed cold attempt is not a miss: it routes the person into the instruction, with no interval and nothing recorded under D11. Safety modules keep both the instruction and the proof, every time, and any drill with a physical hazard carries a named safety element shown on every attempt.
  - Ongoing education is paid and scheduled, never homework: each position has set weekly hours for it (`policy.ongoing_education_hours.<position>`), placed in the schedule (SYS-134), and the expected time in seat before each promotion is published.
  - The three entry routes: green hire, experienced hire, internal move, and what each skips. An internal mover never repeats a signed-off module; they re-prove only currency.
  - The paths: service (food runner, back waiter, front server, lead server; food runner to lead food runner); beverage (barback, bartender, lead bartender; barista and bartender are one team); the door (host, lead host); host into either team; the kitchen ladder as the executive chef sets it. Leads are designations, paid, with no authority over discipline or pay (D5).
  - Knowledge tracks stack by position and can gate promotion. Hard gates must be held before the next window opens; soft gates allow promotion with a dated plan (`policy.soft_gate_window`). Wine gates hardest. Every track builds customer advocacy and business advocacy (D54).
  - Asking for a direction: how to say you want to learn something or move into a role, and the short conversation that unblocks it (D13). Interest in the food, a producer or a study placement is raised the same way; placements follow `policy.study_placement` if Brandon adopts one.
  - Learning beside an expert while trailing: ask for the cue and the check; name one thing noticed each shift; keep a trailing log (cognitive apprenticeship, Collins, Brown and Newman, verified-secondary).
- What the person can do afterward: name their own path to working solo and what proves each step; explain what "not yet" means and does not mean; ask for a direction correctly.
- How it is proven: a spoken check of about 10 minutes with the mentor. No prove-first: this module is where the prove-first rule itself is taught, and it is short.
- Time and place: 1.5 h (1 remote and paid, 0.5 on site).
- Waits on: the gate specs per position (Assessment and Competency Designer); `workflow.recheck_cadence`; `workflow.full_night`; `team.fail_limit`; `founder.term.angel_shift`; `policy.soft_gate_window`; `policy.ongoing_education_hours.<position>`; `policy.study_placement`; Brandon's confirmation of the hard gates (spine section 7, item 6).
- Absorbs: ORI-017, ORI-034, SVC-001 (the shared part), the progression half of ORI-002, CUL-019 and CUL-020 (what a recheck is, from the dropped CUL-023).

### ORI-003 How decisions get made here, and your range
- Area: the house
- Positions and timing: All. Readiness, first week, after the first supervised service and before the angel shift (the calls a person needs on that first service are SVC-013 and SVC-079). Managers go further in LEA-027.
- What it teaches:
  - Intent over script: when the script no longer fits, act on what the house is trying to achieve, stated in one line (commander's intent, ADP 6-0, verified-secondary).
  - The house's order of priorities when goods conflict (`founder.decision_order`), drawn from Brandon's own decisions by interview and never invented by the studio. Safety comes before speed in every version; the rest of the order is his.
  - Your range: the calls your position makes alone, the calls you hand up, the escalation triggers, and to whom. Holders are `people.*`; any amount is `fact.recovery_range.<position>` (D25).
  - Judge the call, not the result: the process is rated apart from the outcome (the "resulting" idea, Duke, verified-secondary).
  - Ask and unblock: asking for help is part of the system; a stuck process is raised, not waited out, and the person knows who to raise it to.
  - Three to five casebook cases, each with the cue, the usual move and the check (`founder.casebook.*`).
- What the person can do afterward: given a case, say whether the call is theirs, make it, give the house reason, and say when and to whom they would hand it up.
- How it is proven: a spoken conversation through three casebook cases with a lead or manager. Practice items ask for "the most effective move". Prove-first (ORI-015): an experienced hire reads the ranges and the casebook, then takes the three-case conversation cold; a pass skips the on-site session.
- Time and place: 2 h (1 remote and paid, 1 on site).
- Waits on: Brandon's decision interviews (D45; first in the queue); `founder.decision_order`; `founder.casebook.*`; `people.*` ranges per position; `fact.recovery_range.*`.
- Absorbs: ORI-004, ORI-011, ORI-012, ORI-014, CUL-005.

### CUL-002 Giving and taking a note at every level
- Area: the house
- Positions and timing: All, kitchen included. Readiness, after the first supervised service and before the first unsupervised shift, where the whole team gives notes as on any night (D9). Only the one-line allergy rule below is needed before the first supervised service, and it is carried by SAF-001.
- What it teaches:
  - Why notes run across levels here: a food runner can name a bartender's miss. Critiquing a founder or manager is invited, never required (D42).
  - During service: one short line, only about safety or the immediate task, never at the table or in a customer's earshot. On an allergy, speaking up is required, whatever the other person's rank.
  - After service: aim the note at the task, not the person. Shape: what I saw, its effect, what would work (Lerman's Critical Response Process, verified-primary).
  - Taking a note: listen, ask one question to understand, thank the person, decide later. No defending in the moment. Founders are held to the same.
  - The one-line shift review at close: what went right, what went wrong, one change for next time (TC 25-20 after-action review, verified-secondary).
  - Two rituals kept apart: open everyday critique, and the private incident review, where the participant explains the process and nobody is blamed (just culture, Reason and Dekker, verified-secondary; WP Part II "Blame the process, fix the process", cited). Running a review is LEA-021; writing the note is CUL-010.
- What the person can do afterward: give a teammate in another role a task-focused note; take a note without defending; run their own one-line review at close.
- How it is proven: role-play, the first attempt private and the second with peers, with a stop rule. Prove-first (ORI-015): an experienced hire reads the protocol and takes the peer role-play cold.
- Time and place: 2 h (0.5 remote and paid, 1.5 on site).
- Waits on: TBRI and culture seats review; `founder.incident_policy` (for the boundary with CUL-010).
- Absorbs: CUL-006, CUL-007, CUL-008, CUL-009, CUL-012, part of CUL-018.

- How it is proven: a spoken walk-through at the tabletop. No test-out: Sŏn's flow is specific to Sŏn.

### ORI-016 How we drill: movement apart from decisions
- Area: the house
- Positions and timing: every position with physical work (All, kitchen where the chef agrees). Readiness, taught inside the person's first drill.
- What it teaches:
  - Brandon's method: isolate the movement and run it at many tempos (slow, fast, slow to fast) until it is automatic, so attention is free for the decision. Brandon tells the water-pitcher pour drill himself.
  - Find the error, isolate it, change the tempo, repeat until right (Duke, Simmons and Cash 2009, verified-secondary).
  - Vary the conditions so the skill transfers, then return to the whole task.
  - Self-review: compare against an example clip or a peer check, and name one error.
  - Composure at speed: under pressure, attention on an automatic skill disrupts it; use a reset routine (Beilock and Carr 2001, verified-secondary). Never framed as a personality trait.
- What the person can do afterward: run one drill alone at three tempos and name their own error.
- How it is proven: the drill on site, recorded as evidence toward the person's practical. Test-out: an experienced hire shows the method on one drill in place of the explanation.
- Time and place: 0.5 h on site, inside the first drill.
- Waits on: Brandon recording the drill story; the movement library per position (SVC-003, BEV-002, KIT-012).
- Absorbs: ORI-026 (self-review), ORI-028, ORI-032.

### SAF-001 Allergies and dietary needs: knowing, taking and routing
- Area: the house (house safety)
- Positions and timing: part 1, All, kitchen included, readiness before any customer or food contact. Part 2, CF, readiness after part 1 and before any customer contact.
- What it teaches:
  - Part 1, knowing:
    - Four kinds of need: allergy, intolerance, preference, religious or ethical need. All travel the same route; nobody below the kitchen decides which is which.
    - The rule: the person taking it never judges severity, never promises a substitution, never says "it should be fine". The kitchen decides what can be made (`chef.allergen_matrix`).
    - The major allergens in US law: milk, egg, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soy and sesame (FASTER Act; lead-only, confirm at the FDA before drafting).
    - Hidden sources on both sides of the house, drinks included: nut syrups and orgeat, dairy in finishes, egg white in sours, wheat in some spirits and beers, sesame and soy in fermented pastes. The bar's cases come from the head of beverage; the kitchen's from the chef (`chef.allergen_hidden_sources`).
    - Cross-contact: why a clean knife, board, tong, shaker or bar spoon matters.
    - Signs of a reaction: hives, swelling of lips, tongue or throat, coughing or wheeze, vomiting, dizziness, collapse; it can start within minutes (lead-only; medical content linked from a provider, never written by the studio). The first minute is SAF-006.
    - The route: who is told, how it is marked, how it is confirmed at the pass and the table (`workflow.allergy_route`, `chef.allergen_marking`). The allergy goes in the reservation's allergy field (SYS-110) and the POS allergy flag (SYS-130). Any doubt at any point stops the plate.
  - Part 2, taking:
    - The words at the table, the door and the counter: ask once, plainly; repeat it back in the customer's words; never ask why; never perform alarm.
    - Confirming back after the kitchen answers: what can be made, exactly, and nothing promised beyond it.
    - Versions: door (at booking and arrival, routed on the closed loop); table (on the order, read back with the order, SVC-032); counter (drinks and counter food, with the bar's hidden sources).
- What the person can do afterward: part 1, sort a customer's statement, route it correctly and say why nothing was promised; part 2, take, confirm and route an allergy at a mock table, door or counter.
- How it is proven: part 1, a spoken check of five cases built around the moment of doubt, passed on every case (non-compensatory). Part 2, a practical at a mock table, required on every attempt and a required element of every later practical. No test-out on either part: safety.
- Time and place: part 1, 1.5 h (1 remote and paid, 0.5 on site); part 2, 1.5 h on site.
- Waits on: Brandon interview (the allergy route, first in the D45 queue); `chef.allergen_matrix`; `chef.allergen_marking`; `chef.allergen_hidden_sources`; `workflow.allergy_route`; `founder.epinephrine_policy`; drink cases from writer 3 and table cases from writer 2.
- Absorbs: SAF-008; the host arrival bank; the everyone part of SAF-013 (production stays with SAF-013, chef-gated).

### SAF-004 Delivering a modified plate
- Area: the house (house safety)
- Positions and timing: FR, BW, FS, LFR. Readiness, before the first run of food.

- How it is proven: a practical on paired runs with planted modified plates; a safety element that passes on every attempt. No test-out: safety.
- Time and place: 1.5 h on site.
- Waits on: `chef.allergen_marking`; `workflow.allergy_delivery`; `workflow.position_numbering`; KIT-002 (writer 4).
- Absorbs: none (SAF-004 continued).

- How it is proven: the walk-through in week one with a spoken check of roles; then a drill for every event type over time, rechecked on the cadence. No test-out: safety.
- Time and place: 1.5 h on site in readiness; drills on cadence after.
- Waits on: `workflow.emergency_plan` (a policy that must exist first, D50); the space (D58); Brandon interview (first-minute roles); `workflow.emergency_drill_cadence`.

### SAF-009 Noticing intoxication, and slowing or stopping service

### CUL-013 Present without performing
- Area: the house
- Positions and timing: CF. Ongoing, after release.
- What it teaches:
  - The principle: status at the table is adjusted to put the customer at ease, lowered or held steady as the moment needs (Johnstone status work, verified-secondary); warmth without performance (BG 03 "This is not omotenashi", cited).
  - Four moments, each with the cue, the over-performing move and the house move (house moves confirmed by Brandon):
    - Presenting a dish to an expert (a chef, a critic, a regular who knows the food). Cue: they lean in, name a technique, or ask a precise question. Over-performing: a longer speech to prove knowledge. House move: the short description, then answer exactly what they asked and let them lead; "let me ask the kitchen" if past your knowledge.
    - A celebrating table. Cue: an occasion in the record (SYS-111), raised glasses, a gift on the table. Over-performing: inserting yourself into the celebration, a scripted announcement. House move: mark it once at the right moment, warmly and briefly, then give the table its own time.
    - A complaint. Cue: a plate pushed aside, a raised hand held up, a flat answer to a check-in. Over-performing: elaborate apology, defending the dish, visible distress. House move: lower your status, listen to the end, repeat it back, state the one thing you will do, and do it (recovery range in ORI-003).
    - A silent table. Cue: no conversation, eyes down or on each other, short answers. Over-performing: filling the silence with chat or extra description. House move: the shortest service that is still complete, and the held pause: say less and leave room.
- What the person can do afterward: in each of the four moments, read the cue and make the house move without performing.
- How it is proven: a role-play with a lead on all four moments, each run once; the lead names the cue the person responded to. Prove-first (ORI-015): an experienced server takes the role-play cold.
- Time and place: 1.5 h on site.
- Waits on: Brandon interview on what performing looks like at a table, and his house move for each of the four moments.
- Absorbs: CUL-015.

## 2. Systems and technology

Every tool name is a candidate from Sŏn's archived SaaS catalog (lead-only), except SevenRooms, which the white paper names as the customer identity spine (WP Part II, cited). Every menu path, field label, status name and permission level is a `tool.*` binding. SevenRooms' screens use the industry's other word for the customer; these modules say customer and name each screen by what it does: the reservation list, the floor view, the timeline view, the customer profile, the waitlist, the pre-service view, the availability settings.

**The SevenRooms track (D60).** It stacks like a knowledge track:
- Basics, All: SYS-110 (booking), SYS-111 (reading the record), SYS-112 (writing to it).

SYS-115 to SYS-117 are the hard gate from host to lead host. - Managers: SYS-119 (configuration), SYS-120 (data, tags, permissions), SYS-121 (reading the book as the business).
- Every position that keeps customers: SYS-122 (regulars and product feedback).

**What a master knows that a host does not.** A host runs the book. The lead host knows why the book says what it says (shifts, access rules, pacing ceilings, durations, combinations, blocks), changes it on the day inside the rules, sets it before service, keeps the record clean, and teaches it. The MD and OM configure it, govern its data and read it as the business.
