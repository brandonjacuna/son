### 5.10.3 Decide the ways employment ends at Sŏn, as a closed list

**The question:** what is the complete list of ways someone stops working at Sŏn, written so a candidate can read it at the first interview and nothing else can happen to them?

**Why it matters now:** the candidate sheet promises "no surprises and nothing withheld" (WP p. 18). Every later decision in this chunk assumes an item on this list. The white paper never says the house ends anyone's employment, so this is the first time it is written down.

**Openers:**
- A cook asks at their first interview, plainly: "How do people get fired here?" Answer in one breath.
- Sideways: the white paper says "no one is ghosted. Every candidate receives a clear answer, including the no" (WP p. 18). Say the same sentence about a departure. What has to be true for it to be honest?
- The book's best outcome is that the manager and the person reach the conclusion together (p. 455). Has that happened to you, on either side? What made it possible?
- Name a way someone left a room you worked in that you never want to happen here. Which item on the list rules it out?

**Narrowing questions:**
- The person's choice, with what notice asked and what if none?
- The early reversal (3.4): confirm it is its own item.
- The partners' decision on 5.8's package; the partners' decision on a confirmed breach: two items or one?
- The reduction's last step: confirm it is on the list and nowhere else.
- The book's mutual managing-out: its own path, or folded into the person's choice?
- "And no other way": is he willing to write that sentence?

**What the book says:** two paths, managing out and firing; plus gross misconduct and layoffs; no hiring process is perfect, so there will be people who do not work out; fire fast but fire well (pp. 454 to 455, 460 to 461).

**White-paper default:** silent on the house ending employment; a period may come "out of the plan entirely" (WP p. 36); "speed is respect" (WP p. 18).

**How others have handled it:** General practice, not Sŏn-specific. Most employers list grounds for termination in a handbook and keep the list open-ended; a closed list is unusual and is the earlier work's central proposal.

**Options:** (a) the closed list, the mutual path declined as separate. Commits Sŏn to routing every case into an item. (b) The closed list plus the mutual path as the person's choice. Commits Sŏn to a rule for when a gap conversation becomes a choice. (c) The book's categories as written. Commits Sŏn to managing out as a manager's act, which 5.8.14 refused. Depth: cons. 1.

**Watch for:** an item that is really "the partners decide, for reasons"; a list that omits the early reversal or the reduction and so leaves them unruled.

**A finished answer:** the list in plain words, the "no other way" sentence, and where a candidate reads it.

**Needs agreement from:** both seated founders; counsel reviews.

### 5.10.5 Decide what may never cause or be cited in a separation, and where the reason must already be

**The question:** what can never be the reason, and must every reason already be on a record the person has read?

**Why it matters now:** 5.8.5 and 4.2.11 have already ruled things out at earlier stages; this closes the list at the last stage. The earlier work's "citation only" rule shapes the conversation (5.10.11), so it has to be decided first.

**Openers:**
- A regular you value tells you a server was rude to them. Where does that go, and what can it never become?
- Sideways: the white paper's rule is "if something is wrong, the person knows before the review" (WP p. 19). Push it one step: if something is the reason someone is leaving, when did they first read it?
- The earlier work said the deliverer says only the citation and nothing more. Say that conversation out loud. Does it sound like respect or like a form?

**Narrowing questions:**
- The never list: one opinion, a lead's or founder's request, a customer complaint or rating, a review, a recognition tally, a scorecard, a diagnostic line, a designation count, a schedule state, an attendance count outside a window, a health or personal situation, anything not on a record the person has read. Add or remove?
- Where the reason must already be: only the package or the flag record; or the book's assembled file?
- May the deciders hear anything outside the package? Never, and it goes back to its own process; or counsel's lawfulness read only?

**What the book says:** assemble all written feedback and prior plans before the conversation; state the reasons and the process; the decision is not up for negotiation (pp. 455 to 457).

**White-paper default:** blame the process first (WP p. 17); no surprises (WP p. 19).

**How others have handled it:** General practice, not Sŏn-specific. Employers commonly document reasons in a file the person has not seen; the earlier work's rule that only a record the person has read may be cited is the no-surprises rule taken to its end.

**Options:** (a) the closed never list, the package or flag record as the only source, nothing outside it heard. Commits Sŏn to the package being complete. (b) The same list, with the book's assembled file as the source. Commits Sŏn to a file the person may not have read. Depth: cons. 3.

**Watch for:** a never list that quietly excludes the customer's account from the incident capture too (it belongs there, as process, not here as cause).

**A finished answer:** the list, the source rule, the "nothing outside it" rule.

**Needs agreement from:** both seated founders.

### 5.10.6 Decide whether one act alone may end employment, and how long a confirmed conduct record limits eligibility

**Openers:**
