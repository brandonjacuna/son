# 5.10 Managing out, firing, and layoffs: considerations

What the white paper holds, what the earlier work argued, the options and what each commits Sŏn to, and where Sŏn may diverge from the book. The book's guidance is in `book.md`; the work is in `tasks.md`. "WP p. N" is the white paper, the only Sŏn context. Nothing here is legal advice; section 13 lists what counsel is asked.

## 0. The gap the white paper leaves

The book's section assumes a manager who can decide a departure, an HR partner, employment counsel, a severance matrix, and a layoff run as company restructuring. The white paper has none of those as designed: HR is "removed entirely" into software (WP p. 22), no counsel is named, and every departure it describes is the person's choice or a key person leaving (WP pp. 14, 25). It says what a departure costs ("a tension re-establishment problem," WP p. 9; "bounded, temporary, calculable," WP p. 14) and what the house learns from it ("exit interviews read for what could have been better," WP p. 23). It never says how the house ends anyone's employment. That is designed here from scratch, on the white paper's principles: blame the process first (WP p. 17), no surprises (WP p. 19), nothing withheld at the first interview (WP p. 18), and "speed is respect" (WP p. 18).

Upstream has already built the edges. 5.8.14 hands this chunk a closed package when a performance window is not held and no move is chosen. 5.2.2 defines the serious breach, names who is told and how fast, and permits one three-person conversation. 3.4 holds the early reversal of a hire. 4.3 holds the departure read, what the team hears, and a lead's handoff. 4.8.14 holds the exit conversation and what the house confirms outside. 5.7.7 and 5.7.13 hold whose hours are reduced and its publication. 4.1.12 says a reduction's reason is never a person and a held seat is never retired as a retirement. This chunk builds the exit itself.

## 1. The ways employment ends (5.10.3)

**Book.** Managing out, where manager and report reach the conclusion together; firing, the formal conversation; gross misconduct; layoffs (pp. 454, 460 to 461).

**White paper.** Silent, except that a service period may come "out of the plan entirely" (WP p. 36), which is the one restructuring it names, and that every candidate gets "a clear answer, including the no" (WP p. 18), which the earlier work extended by analogy to how a departure is told.

**Earlier work.** A closed list of three: the person's choice; the partners' decision on a closed record; a reduction that becomes a departure only at its last step. The reasoning was that a short closed list, with everything else ruled out by name, is what prevents an ad hoc firing dressed up afterwards.

**Options.** (a) The closed list, with the book's mutual managing-out declined as a separate path because the partners decide either way. Commits Sŏn to a candidate-readable list and to routing every case into one of its items. (b) The closed list plus the book's mutual path for the case where the person raises leaving first, handled as the person's choice. Commits Sŏn to a rule for when a conversation about a gap becomes the person's own choice. (c) The book's four categories as written. Commits Sŏn to "managing out" as a manager's act, which 5.8.14 has already refused.

## 2. Who decides, and the default (5.10.4)

**Book.** The manager decides, with their own manager and HR informed and a practice conversation held (pp. 454 to 455).

**White paper.** The leads are accountable but neither is above the other (WP p. 10); the only decision reserved to all three partners is whether lunch is built (WP p. 36). 2.1.13 decides the reserved classes and the two-partner interim.

**Earlier work.** A separation is a reserved decision: both partners, counsel present, staying as the default, a lead never. The reasoning was that firing authority held by a lead is the load-bearing act that recreates the manager-fires-employee structure; a lead who announced one "would have become the manager who fires."

**Considerations.**
- Both-or-neither with staying as the default makes a separation procedurally hard. That is the point and also the cost: a person whom one partner believes must go, and the other does not, stays. 2.1.13's deadlock rule may already answer this; if it says "domain owner decides after consultation," the two collide and one has to give.
- The chef partner is a partner. Whether a kitchen separation needs all three, or the two seated founders with the chef partner as second reader, is 2.1.10's standing question applied here.
- Counsel's view (5.8.2) on whether "the lead never decides" creates exposure should be in hand before this is written on the sheet.

**Options.** (a) Both seated partners, counsel present, staying by default, as a reserved class. Commits Sŏn to the collision with 2.1.13 being resolved in this chunk's favor. (b) The partner whose domain holds the seat decides, the other as second reader, counsel present. Commits Sŏn to a single decider and to 2.1.13's ordinary rule. (c) The book's manager with HR. Commits Sŏn to the lead as decider, which 5.8.14 refused.

## 3. What may never be cited, and where the reason must already be (5.10.5)

**Book.** Assemble all written feedback and prior plans; state the reasons and the process; the decision is not up for negotiation (pp. 455 to 457).

**White paper.** No surprises (WP p. 19); blame the process (WP p. 17).

**Earlier work.** Nothing outside the closed package or the flag record may be cited: not one person's opinion, a customer complaint, a review, a score, a diagnostic finding, or anything not on a record the person has read. And the reason said at the conversation is only the citation, because a spoken justification not already on a record is an informal case file said aloud. Upstream has since made this consistent: 5.4.5 decides whether a review feeds a separation record; 4.2.11 says the diagnostic never hands to managing out; 5.8.5 lists what never opens the track.

**Consideration.** "Citation only" is the strictest form of no surprises. Its cost is a conversation that can sound cold. The book's alternative, summarizing the plan and what was agreed, is also no-surprises if the plan was the person's to read. The difference is whether the deliverer speaks in their own words about why. 5.10.11 decides that; this decision fixes only what may be cited.

## 4. One act alone, and the conduct record's life (5.10.6)

**Book.** Gross misconduct: one conversation, terms of departure, same-day departure; an investigation may take a day; if cleared or inconclusive, a feedback conversation about the perception (pp. 460 to 461).

**White paper.** Silent on conduct. Mentors are "proven cultural stewards" (WP p. 18), which implies a record of some kind bears on eligibility.

**Earlier work.** Left open: how long a confirmed conduct record limits eligibility (the mentor, assessor, and ready-now pools), and whether one act alone can be the whole package. The eligibility effect is a real consequence in a house with no pay lever, and counsel's view on a standing limit is needed.

**Options.** (a) One confirmed act from 5.2.2's list can be the whole package; nothing ends employment before the partners decide; a confirmed act that does not end employment carries a stated pool limit. Commits Sŏn to the hold (section 6) covering the gap, and to a reset parameter for the limit. (b) The book's same-day departure for the gravest acts, both partners deciding within a day. Commits Sŏn to a fast reserved decision and to the conversation being the book's one-sitting form. (c) Never one act, always a pattern. Commits Sŏn to keeping a person in the building after a confirmed act against another person.

## 5. The flag read and its record (5.10.7, 5.10.8)

**Book.** Notify the person of the accusation and the process; interview those involved; conclude within about 24 hours; a code of conduct must exist (pp. 460 to 461). If "retaliation" or "discrimination" is said, stop and continue with the right people (pp. 459 to 460). A harassment report may carry a legal duty to report; "off the record" may not be possible (p. 464).

**White paper.** Post-mortems are private (WP p. 17). The camera feed carries a weapon-detection layer and compliance is logged (WP p. 21). Nothing on investigation.

**Upstream and what is missing.** 5.2.2 defines the breach, names the destination, who is told, how fast, and the three-person conversation; 5.2.3 places it in the conduct policy. 5.8 routed the same-day review and the closed incident record to 5.2.3. 5.2.3's done-when does not cover a review that sorts a report, or a record; so this chunk serves them (5.10.7), and 5.2 is told.

**Earlier work.** Both leads read a report the same day and sort it three ways: a violation confirmed (which becomes a package), a process failure misrouted as a person's act (back to the incident capture and the process update), or unclear. The incident record is closed in form, kept apart from the person page with one line stating it exists. A lead as subject is read by the other lead and the partner who does not hold the subject lead, and hears it through their own partner with a reader present, before anything is written. A founder as subject is read by both leads together, with counsel where required and no partner; hears it from the other partner; the line is written on the partners' page because a founder has no person page; and what follows is the operating agreement's and counsel's, with the house's part ending at the record.

**Considerations.**
- The book's "unclear" outcome is a feedback conversation about the perception. At Sŏn that is 5.2's hard conversation, held by the person's lead. It is worth saying so, because "unclear" otherwise becomes a shadow record.
- Two leads reading a report about a person in one lead's domain means the other lead reads across the seam. That is the one place the flat structure asks a lead to read the other's domain directly, and it should be named as an exception to 5.9.10.
- Counsel's answers on who must investigate what, on what timeline, and what each party is told (section 10, tier one) shape this more than any option here.

## 6. The paid hold (5.10.9)

**Book.** Same-day departure for severe violations; an investigation of about a day (pp. 460 to 461). No suspension is described.

**White paper.** Labor is an asset (WP p. 16); pay is daily on a point system weighted by days worked (WP p. 20). Silent on a suspension.

**Earlier work.** Where the act's object is a person in the house, the subject is scheduled off from the flag read to the partners' decision, paid at base for every scheduled shift, with those days counting for the pool as if worked, written as a schedule state with no reason field. Where the act's object is not a person, the subject works. The alternative, working until the review, was set aside as worse for the other people in the room. Counsel is asked whether the hold is lawful, how unworked scheduled hours are paid, and whether counting the day as worked for the pool raises a separate wage question; 5.5.30 holds the register row.

**Options.** (a) The earlier work's hold as written. Commits Sŏn to paying for shifts not worked and to a pool rule counsel must accept. (b) The book's same-day departure for the gravest acts, and the hold for the rest. Commits Sŏn to a fast reserved decision (section 4's option b). (c) An unpaid suspension, if counsel and the founders accept it. Commits Sŏn to placing a consequence before a decision without pay, which the white paper's labor stance sits badly with.

## 7. The partners' review, the conversation, and the day (5.10.10 to 5.10.12)

**Book.** Pre-work; the junior conversation in one or two sittings (summarize the plan, ask their view, state the decision, move to the timeline, meet the two responses, let people control what they still can); the senior conversation over sessions, framed on the gap; the pauses (a legal threat, new information); logistics (pp. 455 to 460, 481 to 482).

**White paper.** "Management absorbs blame from staff" (WP p. 17); "speed is respect" (WP p. 18); uniforms and lockers are the house's (WP pp. 19 to 20); a server's regulars may follow them and the customer data is the system's (WP pp. 25, 27).

**Earlier work.** The partners' review runs a checklist in a fixed order: the house's own failure modes first, with the item returned to the leads' review on an unmade fix; the package whole, the person's words last; counsel's read on lawfulness only; the decision, staying by default; a postponement of at most one review with a written reason. The conversation is on the person's next scheduled shift or the one after, off the floor, on paid time, in the person's language, delivered by the partner who decided with the person's check-in holder present, in a fixed order: the decision, the last day, the final pay shown, the exit conversation offered, the hand-back. Same day: the record, the departure logged, the coverage note at the next brief, final pay calculated and shown, nothing said in any other setting. The person's own words about their experience are theirs; the house adds no narrative.

**Considerations.**
- The book's "let people control what they still can" and the earlier work's fixed order are compatible: the order is the deliverer's, the choices inside it (the last day within a range, how the team is told within 4.3.9) are the person's.
- The book's two-sitting form exists because the first conversation is a shock. If the package was the person's to read, and 5.8.8's conversation already said "this is a pattern," one sitting may be honest. If not, two is kinder.
- A lead's departure conversation is the book's senior case: longer, framed on the gap, over sessions. 5.9.9 decides whether a lead's process follows that path; this chunk's conversation follows what 5.9.9 chose.
- The book's pause words carry legal weight. The card (5.10.22) should hold them verbatim as counsel writes them.

## 8. Separation pay (5.10.13)

**Book.** Exit packages may be negotiated; the senior case may involve severance, insurance, or vesting timing; the backstop is a severance matrix (pp. 457 to 459).

**White paper.** Silent. Pay is the wage plus the daily share; benefits through Rippling on "a fast path" (WP p. 20).

**Earlier work.** A pay system with "no raise to withhold, no bonus to deny, no rating to lower" cannot support negotiated severance either; final pay is only what was earned through the last day. If anything is ever paid, it is a fixed rule by kind, identical for everyone in that kind, or nothing.

**Options.** (a) Nothing beyond what was earned, for every kind. Commits Sŏn to that being the answer for a lead as well as a line cook, and to counsel confirming no release is needed. (b) A fixed rule by kind, published. Commits Sŏn to a value Dominic sets and to a release counsel drafts, if one is asked for. (c) The book's matrix or case-by-case negotiation. Commits Sŏn to an individually negotiated outcome, which 5.5 was built to avoid. No figure belongs in this manual; financials are not a source for this work.

## 9. The reduction that is not a departure (5.10.14, 5.10.15)

**Book.** A layoff is role elimination due to restructuring and must meet that standard; it needs a communication and change plan, tightly managed (p. 461).

**White paper.** "Sŏn fights for every hour" (WP p. 33); the opening is phased, dinner first, other periods added when dinner is steady, and lunch may come "out of the plan entirely" as "an equal decision among all three partners" (WP p. 36). Hours are pay (WP p. 20). So the shape of the day, and the hours in it, is an operating fact the white paper expects to move.

**Upstream.** 5.7.7 decides whose hours are reduced (stated preference, then rotation by eligibility, skill level as a fact only), published in advance, never a read of a person. 5.7.13 publishes it and the seasonal pattern. 4.1.12 makes a reduction's reason structural, never a name, and says a held seat is never retired as a retirement. 4.3.4 decides the order of telling; 4.3.15 the change guide.

**Earlier work.** A reduction is a scheduling change until a seat is eliminated with its holder in it and no other open seat is accepted; every open seat is offered first; a retired seat may not be recreated for someone else inside a window; a performance window in progress finishes before a reduction touches that person; the seasonal shape is stated before anyone is hired; a departed person gets priority reconsideration if the seat reopens inside a window. The central hazard named throughout: a schedule used quietly to reduce someone's hours to nothing, the soft way of pushing someone out.

**Considerations.**
- Counsel's tier-three questions decide more than the options: whether weeks at zero scheduled hours is itself a separation, what it does to benefits and unemployment eligibility, and what may be promised about rehire.
- Closing a whole period is the book's layoff. The white paper already makes it a three-partner decision (WP p. 36); the telling order and the plan are 4.3's. This chunk only applies the boundary to each affected person and names what counsel must be asked first.
- The guards cost flexibility: a seat retired in a slow season cannot be quietly reopened for a favorite in the busy one. That is their purpose.

## 10. What the house does when something bad happens (5.10.16, 5.10.17)

**Book.** Three kinds: a one-time event (empathy, ask what they need, no timeline questions at once); an ongoing hardship (observe, share an observation rather than pry, balance empathy with coverage); legal ramifications (know your duties; "off the record" may not be possible). Across all: do not compartmentalize if something feels off; keep it confidential; the missing employee found through a wellness check (pp. 461 to 465).

**White paper.** Burnout is a system failure (WP p. 17); "dignity is a precondition for presence" (WP p. 20); benefits from the start (WP p. 20). Silent on leave, absence, or crisis.

**Earlier work.** No lead "observes" or "tracks" a hardship; the schedule shows a "not available" state with no reason, changed because the person asked; a report of harassment or discrimination goes to both leads the same day with counsel's exact confidentiality sentence said first, and its account is kept apart from the person's file; a person who stops showing up is reached by the means given at hire, the emergency contact only after a stated count of missed shifts set with counsel; a health situation is never by itself grounds.

**Considerations.**
- The earlier work and the book differ on the ongoing hardship. The book says notice and name a change, once, kindly; the earlier work said do not observe. 5.1.5's hypothesis form is a middle path: an observation shared once at the check-in, in the coaching words, never pried further. The risk the earlier work named is real: a lead's notes on a person's life. The risk the book names is also real: the worst mistake is failing to act.
- Leave is law before it is policy. What the house must provide, what it may ask, and how a no-reason schedule state interacts with legally required leave categories are counsel's (tier four) and must be answered before the page (5.10.20) says anything.
- The missing person is the case where "do nothing" is the harm. The rhythm of contact and the count before the emergency contact need numbers, set with counsel and kept as reset parameters.

## 11. The exit conversation's reader (5.10.18)

**White paper, stated outright.** "Exit interviews read for what could have been better rather than filed and forgotten" and "how people grow beyond the building" is "the longest measure of whether the environment did its job" (WP p. 23).

**Upstream.** 4.8.14 decides whether the conversation is offered, who holds it, what it asks, that it is read at the house review as process, what is confirmed outside, and the yearly opt-in. 4.2.6 and 4.3 parked the reader here.

**Earlier work.** Never usable in any decision about the person or anyone else by name; aggregated by theme; the yearly answers aggregate only. The one open seam: a conversation in which a departing person reports a serious breach. That is a flag report by another route and needs the person's consent stated.

## 12. Where Sŏn may diverge from the book

- No manager decides a departure; a reserved decision with a default of staying (or a single-decider variant), rather than the manager with HR informed.
- A closed list of ways employment ends, readable by a candidate, rather than the book's four categories held by the manager.
- Citation-only reasons at the conversation, or the book's plain summary; either way nothing the person has not already read.
- No negotiated severance, or a fixed rule by kind, rather than a matrix.
- A paid hold rather than same-day departure, or the book's same-day for the gravest acts.
- A reduction with published formula and guards, rather than a layoff plan directed by HR.
- A "not available" schedule state with no reason, rather than the book's observation and tracking of a hardship, or a middle path.
- The exit conversation never feeds a decision about a person.

## 13. Counsel

Nothing in this chunk should be written on the team home before counsel has answered its tier. The questions, condensed from the earlier work and grouped by when the answer is needed. The register (5.5.32) holds them; 5.10.2 files them; only counsel writes answers.

**Before the first paid shift.** The default legal form of employment in Texas and whether any house document (the transparency sheet, the seat sheet, the ninety-day plan, the departure page) changes it. Whether the house may state a notice expectation, in what wording, and whether any consequence may attach. What must be paid to a departing person and by when, for each kind of departure, including the pool share earned on the last day. Whether the built-in compensation is legally a wage, a tip, a bonus, or something else for final pay (5.5.30 asks the same for other purposes). How long each employee record is kept, who lawfully holds it with no HR seat, and whether a departed person is owed copies. What the incident record may or must contain; what the house must do on a report of harassment, discrimination, or retaliation, who investigates, on what timeline, with what documentation, and what each party is told. What a lead may promise about confidentiality, and the exact sentence to say before a person continues.

**Before the first flag read.** Whether a paid hold as section 6 describes is lawful, how unworked scheduled hours are paid, and whether counting the day as worked for the pool raises a wage question. Whether the decision process (two partners and counsel on a closed record) and the delivery meet what Texas requires of an employer, including what must or must not be documented. Whether a reason must be given, in writing or aloud; whether a bare citation is lawful and sufficient; whether the person may see more of the record than their own page. Whether a standing limit on pool eligibility after a confirmed act is lawful, and for how long.

**Before the first slow-season cut.** Whether weeks at zero scheduled hours while on staff is a layoff or separation of any kind, what it does to benefits and unemployment eligibility, and what the person must be told. Whether closing a period, retiring a seat, or cutting hours triggers any notice, filing, or payment duty. Whether priority reconsideration for a reopened seat may be promised, and for how long. Whether the published whose-hours formula (5.7.7) is defensible or omits something the law requires.

**Before the wider opening gate.** Whether any separation pay may or must be offered, in what form, and whether a release may be asked for and what it may cover. What must be given in writing and in what language. When benefits end under each scenario and what continuation must be offered or notified. What may be said to a prospective employer and to a customer who asks (4.8.14 holds the policy). Whether the house may keep in touch with a former employee with consent, in what form, and how long the contact may be kept. Whether an anonymous reporting route is required or advisable. What the house must do when a person stops showing up, when an emergency contact may be reached, and what form a wellness check may take. Which leave the house must provide, what it may ask, and how a no-reason schedule state interacts with legally required leave. Whether an exit conversation that asks only "what could have been better" and stores the answer as described is lawful. Whether a written concern about a lead may limit what the lead is assigned, and for how long. Whether a founder holding a lead's conversation or covering a seat changes the founders' classification (2.2.5 logged it). Whether any house document may state anything about the law directly, or every legal statement must be counsel's wording.
