## 1. Orientation

**What this chunk covers.** The book's checklist of management basics (pp. 30 to 32): the things she assumes are in place before the book starts. At Sŏn most of the rows are built by later chunks. This chunk holds three things nothing else does: the legal list from counsel, the decision about how the checklist itself is used, and the decision about whether the two partners hold the basics for each other, since they have no manager above them.

**What it depends on upstream.** Nothing. This is the first chunk, and it is short by design. Half an hour is enough.

**Who else has to agree.** 0.3 binds Dominic, so it is marked "pending agreement from Dominic" until he has agreed. 0.1 is counsel's to answer, not the founders'.

**The frame that runs through every chunk after this.** She wrote for a company going from hundreds to thousands. Sŏn opens near full size and stays there (WP p. 22). What compounds is what the system knows, the customer relationship, and the brand (WP p. 06), not headcount (cons. 1). Anything in the book that presupposes growth is suspect by default. It is worth saying out loud once here so it does not have to be re-argued in every later session.

**The book's frame in one line.** Structure comes first, and you need it sooner than you realize (p. 14); but only a few frameworks should apply to everyone (pp. 20 to 21).

## 3. Briefs

### 0.1 Get counsel's list of the founders' and leads' legal responsibilities

**The question:** who asks employment counsel for the list, and by when, so it exists before the first lead is hired?

**Why it matters now:** it is the first row of her checklist and the one this manual cannot answer. The leads will manage people within weeks of being hired; anything they must be trained on legally has to be known before 3.3 writes their onboarding. The compensation model's labor-law exposure (WP p. 20) is a second reason to have counsel engaged early, and 5.5 depends on it.

**Openers:**
- Which of you has already talked to an employment lawyer, about what, and what did they say you would need before the first payroll?
- Sideways: at a room you worked in, what did a manager get wrong legally that nobody had told them about?

**Narrowing questions:**
- Who owns the ask: Brandon, Dominic, or both with counsel in one meeting?
- Two lists or one: the founders as employers, and the leads as managers?
- What does counsel need from the manual: the compensation model as described in WP p. 20, the two-lead structure, the paid practical (WP p. 18)?

**What the book says:** you are aware of your legal responsibilities as a manager in the jurisdiction in which you operate (p. 31).

**White-paper default:** none; compliance logging runs through the stack (WP pp. 10, 21), which is a different thing.

**How others have handled it:** General practice, not Sŏn-specific. Restaurants that open well usually get an employer checklist from counsel before the first hire (postings, classifications, scheduling and pay rules, harassment training) and make the manager portion part of every lead's onboarding. Nothing more specific is useful here without counsel.

**Options:** none to choose; this is an ask and a hand-off.

**Watch for:** answering the legal question from memory or from this manual; treating the stack's compliance logging as the answer to the founders' own obligations.

**A finished answer:** an owner, a date, and the two hand-offs named (3.3 for lead training; 5.5 for the compensation exposure).

**Needs agreement from:** both seated founders on the owner; counsel supplies the substance.

### 0.2 Decide how the checklist is used at Sŏn

**The question:** is the checklist run once here as a gap list and retired, kept as a recurring check for the leads, or both?

**Why it matters now:** if it is a scorecard, Sŏn fails five rows by design (cons. 1 and 2) and someone will eventually try to fix the failures by adding a manager above the founders or a ladder under the leads. If it is a gap list, each row goes to the chunk that builds Sŏn's version and the list is done. The routing table in cons. 2 is the run; this decision is whether Brandon accepts it.

**Openers:**
- Read down the rows. Which ones would you be embarrassed to find missing in year two? Which ones would you be embarrassed to find present?
- Sideways: tell me about a manager you worked for who ticked every box on a list like this and was still bad at the job. What was the list not measuring?
- The two leads will each manage a dozen or more people. Which rows do you want to be able to check on them, and how would you check without a desk?

**Narrowing questions:**
- Option A, B, or C (cons. 3)?
- Row by row in cons. 2: does the routing hold? Anything that should go elsewhere, or that Sŏn holds already and the table missed?
- If the leads keep the translated rows: who translates them (2.3 for the rhythm, 5.9 for how a lead is reviewed)?

**What the book says:** the checklist is what she assumes before the book starts (p. 24); few frameworks should apply to everyone (pp. 20 to 21).

**White-paper default:** none stated. Five rows are replaced by design (WP pp. 10 to 11, 13 to 14, 19), which is the argument for the gap-list reading; that argument is inferred.

**How others have handled it:** General practice, not Sŏn-specific. Companies that adopt a management-basics checklist for managers (Google's manager research produced one such list) use it to review managers, not to score the company. Nothing further is useful.

**Options:** cons. 3 A, B, or C, with what each commits Sŏn to.

**Watch for:** keeping the checklist because it is tidy; dropping rows that Sŏn does need (the legal row, the job description row) along with the rows it replaces.

**A finished answer:** the option named; the routing table confirmed or amended; 2.2 and 2.3 told whether their readiness definition replaces the checklist.

**Needs agreement from:** Brandon; Dominic is informed.

### 0.3 Decide whether the partners hold the checklist's basics for each other

**The question:** do the two founders stand in as each other's manager for goals agreed each quarter, a standing partner conversation, and priorities shared in writing?

**Why it matters now:** the "You" section of the checklist has no one to require it at Sŏn. During the build the founders coordinate by proximity. The day the building opens, one is on the floor at dinner and the other is in the model, and the coordination that was free becomes a rhythm or disappears. 2.3 designs the founder rhythm; this decision is whether there is one to design.

**Openers:**
- When did you and Dominic last agree, in writing, what each of you would get done this quarter? How did you find out whether it happened?
- Picture the third week after opening. You are in the room every night. Where is Dominic, and when do the two of you actually talk?
- Sideways: what does the pollster's question to her, "who wrote that memo," look like between the two of you? Who writes the memo, and who reads it?
- The content engine already has a daily, weekly, and monthly read (WP p. 31). Is that the founders' rhythm in disguise, or is it a brand rhythm with nothing about the partnership in it?

**Narrowing questions:**
- Option (a), (b), or (c) (cons. 4)?
- If (a): the three basics, goals, a standing conversation, shared priorities; all three or fewer?
- What is the sentence a third partner reads on day one about how the partners hold each other to this?
- Does this need the operating agreement (2.1.1) to speak, or is it below it?

**What the book says:** quarterly goals agreed with your own manager; 1:1s on a regular cadence with your own manager; regular context on priorities from above (p. 32).

**White-paper default:** none; silent on how the founders hold each other to anything. Equal principals (WP p. 05); one stated joint decision (WP p. 36).

**How others have handled it:** General practice, not Sŏn-specific. Two-founder companies that last usually keep a standing weekly conversation between the founders that is not a status meeting, and many write quarterly goals for each founder that the other reads; the practice is widely described in founder accounts. Nothing else is verifiable enough to add.

**Options:** cons. 4 (a), (b), or (c).

**Watch for:** "we talk all the time" as the answer; a rhythm designed for the build that assumes both founders are free at the same hour once the house is open.

**A finished answer:** yes or no on each of the three basics; the one-sentence rule; the hand-off to 2.3 for the rhythm and to 2.2 for the goals cycle.

**Needs agreement from:** both seated founders.
