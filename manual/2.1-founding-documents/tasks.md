# 2.1 Founding documents: the work

Every decision, action, and deliverable this section of the book calls for, in the order they would naturally be done. Reasoning and options are in `considerations.md`; the book's guidance is in `book.md`. "WP p. N" is the white paper.

### 2.1.1 Read the operating agreement and note what it already settles
- Type: Action
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 71 to 72 (founding documents give every system a common purpose; they cannot contradict the legal instrument beneath them)
- Default assumption: None; the white paper is silent on the operating agreement
- Depends on: None
- Done when: a short note exists listing what the agreement holds on votes, deadlock, admission of a new principal, a principal's departure or incapacity, and ownership of domains, so the founding documents can defer to it by reference rather than restate or contradict it
- Replaces old items: 86akh3t62

### 2.1.2 Decide Sŏn's mission and the entity it belongs to
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 73 to 76 (state why you exist; uniquely specific, never fully achieved; every layer ladders up to it)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 34, 36) the mission describes the company, whose asset is the connective layer, not the restaurant alone; Sŏn is the only brand the customer meets and the parent stays in the background. No default for the sentence itself; the white paper holds a thesis, not a mission
- Depends on: None
- Done when: one sentence exists that passes her two tests and the white paper's two (never assert the unproven, WP p. 36; not the restaurant alone, WP p. 34), with the entity stated and the cuisine question answered either way; agreed by both seated founders
- Replaces old items: 86akh3tdr

### 2.1.3 Choose the long-term goals
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 76 to 77 (a short list of ambitions over years, stable, the parent of every quarterly and annual goal)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 06, 33, 35, 38) the three compounding engines; a company built to run without its founders; one restaurant coherent across the day with dinner protected; proof that the layer moves a customer across the day; the horizon of further concepts in category terms only, gated on that proof
- Depends on: 2.1.2
- Done when: a list short enough for one page exists, without figures or lagging indicators (WP p. 22), and every metric chunk 2.2 later sets can name its parent here
- Replaces old items: None

### 2.1.4 Write the principles, version one
- Type: Deliverable
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 77 to 79 and 145 to 147 (relevant, believable, enduring, deliverable; drafted from moments, reviewed by the founder, circulated for comment; expressed in how the company hires, rewards, leads)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 07 to 08, 11 to 14, 16 to 21, 25 to 26, 33, 36) version one is drawn from the white paper's stated tenets, listed in `considerations.md` section 4, because Sŏn has decisions and no moments. The white paper names "no hierarchy of importance" as an operating principle in its own words (WP p. 20). People and compensation commitments (labor as asset, pay for what matters, compensation built into price and shared with the kitchen, benefits from the start, WP pp. 16, 19 to 20) are stated here as commitments; every mechanism belongs to 5.5
- Depends on: 2.1.2, 2.1.5
- Done when: a short set exists, each item traceable to a decision in the record or one the founders make here, each marked as a principle (an observable behavior), a commitment, or an operating rule; version one marked pre-opening; the chef partner invited to add from the culinary domain once seated; Dominic has seen the commitment list before 5.5 builds against it
- Replaces old items: 86akh2qbc, 86akh3t9p, 17tn048qck3

### 2.1.5 Decide whether "bandwidth, then joy, then retention, then results" is a rule or a description
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 77 to 78 (principles must be deliverable and connected to how the company actually operates)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 12, 16 to 17) it is a rule: "This is mechanical, not aspirational." As a rule it decides trade-offs on a real night: bandwidth wins over results when they conflict
- Depends on: None
- Done when: the founders have stated which reading holds, with the operating consequences of the rule reading (reduce covers rather than thin the floor; restructure a period that consumes bandwidth before defending it on results) accepted or declined; agreed by both seated founders
- Replaces old items: None

### 2.1.6 Write the founding seat descriptions
- Type: Deliverable
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 75 to 76, 83 (every layer of the stack has a mission; clarity about who is responsible for what, and to what end)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 03, 05, 10) Brandon owns the concept, the operating systems, and the experience, and is the public voice; Dominic owns the capital, the financial architecture, the data architecture, and the technology build; the chef partner owns the culinary vision. The two leads serve the founders' vision as parallel co-leads
- Depends on: 2.1.1
- Done when: each seated founder's description exists with domain owned, outcomes accountable to (no figures), decisions held alone, decisions shared, what the seat does not own, seams, the relationship to the two leads, and an absence rule; the OS surface seam (WP pp. 03, 05, 21 to 22) is resolved or marked open; each founder has written their own; no strengths, working style, or failure modes (those are 6.x)
- Replaces old items: 86ajgnhp7, 86akh3x9m

### 2.1.7 Define the chef partner's seat
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 75 to 76, 83 (the seat's mission ladders to the company's; what others can expect from it)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 03, 05, 36, 37) the seat is an equal founding partner who owns the culinary vision, with an equal vote on building or removing a service period; the business does not rest on the survival of any one person in it
- Depends on: 2.1.1, 2.1.6
- Done when: the seat's description exists in the same form as the other two, with who holds culinary direction overall stated (WP p. 10 assigns execution during service and leaves direction open), the seat's standing in joint decisions before and after it is filled stated, and the parts held for the chef partner to shape listed; agreed by both seated founders, and by the chef partner once seated
- Replaces old items: 86akh3tw9, 86akh2qnt

### 2.1.8 Decide how the partners make joint decisions and break deadlocks
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: p. 76 (Eric Yuan: write down the principles on which delegation depends)
- Default assumption: (WP p. 36) building or removing a service period is an equal decision among all three partners. Otherwise none; the white paper is silent on reserved classes, deadlock, and the two-partner interim
- Depends on: 2.1.1, 2.1.7
- Done when: the classes of decision reserved to all partners are listed (candidates in `considerations.md` section 6); the deadlock rule is chosen from majority, unanimity for a named class, or domain-owner-decides after consultation, with the status quo as default; the interim rule for two partners is stated; the whole defers to the operating agreement where it speaks; agreed by both seated founders
- Replaces old items: 86akh2qt2, 86akh3ty7, 86akh5u8m

### 2.1.9 Decide which units get a charter
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: p. 83 (a page per team stating why it exists, its long-term goals, what others can expect, metrics, risks, dependencies)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 33 to 36) each service period needs "its own reason to exist and its own operating logic" before it opens, which is a charter in her sense at the service-period unit. The white paper is silent on charters for the domains (kitchen, floor, beverage, operations)
- Depends on: 2.1.2
- Done when: the founders have chosen per domain, per service period, or both, and stated that no service period opens without its charter if periods are a unit
- Replaces old items: None

### 2.1.10 Write dinner's charter, then one for each service period before it opens
- Type: Deliverable
- Phase: Before opening
- Book: pp. 83, 148 to 150 (her template: mission, vision, customers, metrics, strategic importance, major risks, provided and dependent interfaces)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 05, 33 to 36) three core periods, early morning coffee, dinner, late night; dinner opens first and runs alone for at least a quarter; the morning is the lowest-friction door and enrolls the customer on the first visit; late night is dinner's later register; lunch and brunch are in reserve, not revisited before a full year, and removable from the plan
- Depends on: 2.1.9, 2.1.3
- Done when: dinner's charter exists on her template as mapped in `considerations.md` section 7 (reason to exist, what it becomes, first customer, leading-indicator measures, what it gives the layer, what would make it a worse dinner, what it hands on, what it draws from, operating logic, what is held for the chef partner or filled by the leads); each later period's charter exists before that period opens; a lunch charter, if drafted, is the document the three partners rule on; no charter carries a revenue target or a figure
- Replaces old items: 86akh3u0k, 86akh67a1, 86akh3uxu, 86akh5urn

### 2.1.11 Assemble the founding document
- Type: Deliverable
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 71 to 73 (founding documents state why and how the company exists and operates; the foundation the operating system is built on)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 02, 15, 22) it carries no figures and it keeps its open items visible with who closes each, because "a settled-looking answer to an open question is the tell" of a document that has not been pressure-tested

- Done when: one short document exists, named by Brandon, holding the mission, the long-term goals, the principles, the founding seats, how the partners decide, and the open items with their closers; it names commitments and owners rather than tools or document addresses; it cites the operating agreement rather than restating it; it is readable by a new hire before a first shift; agreed by both seated founders
- Replaces old items: 86akh3tm1, 86akh3u60, 86akh3xe0

### 2.1.12 Set who may change the founding documents and when they are revisited
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: p. 79 (revisit every year or two; what matters is that they are expressed in how the company hires, rewards, and leads)
- Default assumption: None; the white paper is silent on versioning authority. Its phased opening offers natural revision points: after dinner's first quarter alone and after the first full year (WP p. 36)
- Depends on: 2.1.8
- Done when: the versioning authority is named, including the interim before the chef partner is seated and the chef partner's voice once seated; each change carries a version, date, and author; the first two revisions of the principles are fixed to the opening gates and an annual revision after that, with placement on the calendar handed to 2.3
- Replaces old items: 86akh3t3y, 86akh3trj, 86akh3unw
