# 3.2 Hiring: the work

Every decision, action, and deliverable this section of the book calls for, in the order they would naturally be done. Reasoning and options are in `considerations.md`; the book's guidance is in `book.md`. "WP p. N" is the white paper. A "read" here means one thing an interview is trying to learn about a candidate; a "packet" is the set of scorecards and notes a hire decision is made on. A deliverable marked "Repeatable" is one others will later produce for their own seats; building it with the founders is also how the process gets built for everyone else, and each such deliverable has a paired kit task.

### 3.2.3 Decide how a candidate's outside experience counts
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: p. 195 (overweighting experience relative to trajectory and aptitude to learn is a common interviewer mistake; Eric Yuan's line that wanting to learn matters more than background); p. 215 (experienced candidates are practiced interviewees; probe for what they specifically did and whether it worked)
- Default assumption (WP p. 9, p. 11): new people always enter at the outside edge, and every position has documented competency requirements, so entry is against a competency bar, not a résumé. The white paper is silent on whether experience earns anything at entry
- Depends on: 3.2.2; 4.1.7 (whether Sŏn has levels)
- Done when: one sentence states whether a seat description may carry an experience or credential requirement, whether an interviewer may weigh experience for or against a candidate at any read, and how prior experience in fine dining specifically is treated (as evidence of capacity, as neutral, or as something to probe), and the anchors in 3.2.27 are written to that rule

### 3.2.4 Decide the stages each seat class runs, and who runs each
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 193 to 194 (cap at eight interviewers, four or five for well-understood roles; a narrowing first round; process weight flexes with volume); pp. 196 to 197 (a consistent small panel for infrequent roles, a wider pool for scaled ones, plus a cross-section from beyond the immediate team)
- Default assumption (WP p. 17 to 18): a short phone screen for high-volume roles such as line cook and porter; a first interview for everyone at which the transparency sheet is handed over; a paid practical as the second interview for roles with a physical skill component; a dinner in the room as the final round for leadership and salaried roles; self-scheduling with the candidate choosing language and format
- Depends on: 3.2.2; 3.1 (whether the phone screen exists and what it may read); 4.1.6 (which seats are leadership lines); 4.1.9 (the test for a salaried seat)
- Done when: a table lists each seat class, its stages in order, who runs each stage (by seat, never by rank), which reads each stage carries, the length the house commits to for each stage, and the floor rule for how many independent reads a packet must hold before a decision may be made on it
