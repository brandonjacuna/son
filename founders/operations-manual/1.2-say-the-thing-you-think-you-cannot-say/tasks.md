# 1.2 Say the thing you think you cannot say: the work

Every decision, action, and deliverable this section of the book calls for, in the order they would naturally be done. Reasoning and options are in `considerations.md`; the book's guidance is in `book.md`. "WP p. N" is the white paper. The feedback system for the team (how a lead gives hard feedback, the culture of informal feedback) is 5.2 and 5.3; this chunk holds only the founders' own rules.

### 1.2.1 Decide the partners' rule for raising hard things with each other
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 52 to 55 (over-filtering; the it, the we, and the I; share feelings, be measured, separate the person from the task; the payoff is trust)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 07 to 09, 11) authority lives in the domain, so a founder brings a tension to the other's domain rather than a decision; "the absence of a clear path is where politics begin." The white paper is silent on the form: where a disagreement is said, in what medium, and what the domain owner owes in return
- Depends on: None (2.1.1, the operating agreement note, if it exists; the rule must not contradict the agreement)
- Done when: the founders have chosen from the options in `considerations.md` section 2; the rule states where a disagreement is said (in the room where the decision is made, or not), how a founder raises a problem inside the other's domain, and what the domain owner owes in return; it is written so the chef partner reads it before agreeing to it; it is handed to 2.1.17 to sit beside how the partners decide (2.1.13); each founder's own preferences for hearing they are wrong stay in their working-with-me document (1.1.7, 1.1.8); agreed by both seated founders
- Replaces old items: None (the S1 page's Principle 3 gap and prompt E)

### 1.2.2 Decide what the team hears before a decision is made, and what it hears after
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 38 to 40, 54 (Eli: honest but unmeasured; be measured); p. 52 (do not over-filter)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 15, 18, 22, 29) open items are visible to an investor and the build's decisions to an audience; the candidate sheet withholds nothing; orientation puts why before how. Silent on whether the team hears that a question is open, and on how the founders speak about the house's condition in front of the people who work in it
- Depends on: 1.2.1
- Done when: the founders have chosen from the options in `considerations.md` section 3 for each of three classes (decisions made; decisions not yet made; the house's condition), stated so a lead can repeat it; the rule is handed to 2.1.20 (how the founding documents reach the team) and 2.3 (the rhythm on which decisions are told); the public build's content rules (WP p. 31) are noted as a separate channel with its own approval framework; agreed by both seated founders
- Replaces old items: None

### 1.2.3 Decide whether a founder's own domain failure gets the house's post-mortem, and who reads it
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 54 to 55 (separate the person from the task; stand next to someone and look at the same thing); p. 55 (practice, and saying it badly the first time)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 14, 17, 19) the first question is what allowed it, not who failed; post-mortems are private and process-focused; management absorbs blame from staff; a review never contains new information. The white paper's rule reaches the founders by its own logic and does not say so
- Depends on: 1.2.1
- Done when: the founders have chosen from the options in `considerations.md` section 4; the rule states that a founder's domain failure is written up in the same form as a floor failure or is not, who reads it (the other partners; the leads), and that the no-surprises rule applies upward (a lead never learns in a review that a founder has held a concern for a month); the form of the post-mortem itself is 2.2's to build with the process loop; agreed by both seated founders
- Replaces old items: None (the S1 page's Principle 4)
