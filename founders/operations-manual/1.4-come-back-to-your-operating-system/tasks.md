Reasoning and options are in `considerations.md`; the book's guidance is in `book.md`. "WP p. N" is the white paper. ### 1.4.1 Decide what "operating system" means at Sŏn and what the software is called
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 61 to 62 (a repeatable operating system: missions, goals, metrics, meeting structures, cadences; a foundation of consistent practices)
- Default assumption: None stated. The white paper uses the phrase for both the human systems Brandon owns (WP p. 03) and the software's top layer, "the OS surface" (WP pp. 21 to 22), and calls the stack "the nervous system" (WP pp. 12, 21). The old synthesis fixed a convention: "operating system" is the human system; the software is "the stack" or "the OS surface"
- Depends on: None
- Done when: the founders have chosen from the options in `considerations.md` section 1; the convention is written in one sentence with the two words for the two objects; every document this manual produces from here on uses it; 2.1.7 (who owns the OS surface) is told the words; agreed by both seated founders, since one word is Dominic's domain
- Replaces old items: None (the S1 page's standing correction 2, which was a binding convention in the context ledger)

### 1.4.2 Decide which frameworks apply to everyone at Sŏn and which vary by unit
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 20 to 21 (few company-wide frameworks; uniformity must outweigh variability; four areas plus a limited set of cross-team activities); p. 61 (the same components on every team, a common user interface)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 11, 13 to 14, 19, 22, 33 to 34) the candidates the white paper already holds for every unit: a reason to exist, leading-indicator metrics, the shift brief and shift close, a review rhythm, a feedback channel. Goals per unit, a rhythm above the shift, and a planning cycle are not in the white paper. The units are the domains and the service periods, and each period has "its own operating logic" (WP p. 34), which argues for a short uniform set
- Depends on: 1.4.1
- Done when: a short list exists of the components every domain and every service period must have in the same form (candidates in `considerations.md` section 2), with everything else explicitly free to vary; each listed component names the chunk that builds it (2.1.14 and 2.1.15 for the reason to exist, 2.2 for goals and metrics, 2.3 for rhythms and meetings, 5.3 and 5.4 for feedback and review); agreed by both seated founders
- Replaces old items: None

### 1.4.3 Decide which tempos the founders set deliberately fast and which the hour and the table set
- Type: Decision
- Phase: Before the first hire
- Book: pp. 17 to 19 (break through the plateau by practicing faster than is comfortable; a growing company sets that pace); p. 62 (routine first; break the rhythm only because you have one)
- Default assumption: (WP pp. 04 to 05, 11, 13, 18, 22, 26, 30 to 31, 33, 36, 38) service tempo is a property of the hour and the table, with speed bought upstream by moving work away from the point of service; the build sequence is gated on proof and deliberately slow; the build tempo inside it is fast by system, sized so it runs "without burning the founders out"; learning is self-paced. The old synthesis argued this as two metronomes and it is now option (a) in `considerations.md` section 4
- Depends on: None
- Done when: the founders have stated, for each of service tempo, the build sequence, the build tempo, and a founder's presence on the floor, whether the founders set it fast, protect it, or leave it to the hour and the leads; the consequence for dinner's first quarter (a founder in the room runs on the room's tempo, or does not) is accepted or declined by name; 2.3 and 4.5 inherit the ruling; the founders' own learning tempo is handed to 6.1; agreed by both seated founders
- Replaces old items: 86akh2qdd
