## 3. What was dropped and why

Three kinds of material left the working manual. First, everything that rested on the brand guidelines: the canon citations for service tempo (the step-back architecture, the master register, the table walk, the transition rules, the framework names), and the brand row in the operating-system inventory. The service-tempo reading itself survives, because the white paper states dinner's composure, late night's quieter register, the morning's speed, and the upstream engineering of speed in its own words. Second, program machinery: the binding-convention status of the vocabulary rule (it is now a decision), the ledger routing, session numbers as destinations, and Session 16's extension tying the founders' learning tempo to "the gates and the cadence." Third, private vocabulary: "the record," "canon," "code-bearing" as an unexplained term (where it is used it is defined in plain words), and "daypart" is replaced by "service period" except where the white paper is quoted. Nothing that survives without brand support was removed.
