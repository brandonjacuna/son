## Six decisions that matter

### 1. Allowance timing can create a Month 1 cash shortfall

Model 06 Capital!D37:D41 and 03 Monthly!C26; disclosure Section 4.

Before reimbursement, $99,442.50 remains after opening uses. Subtract Our Plan Month 1 operating cash use of $103,218.74: cash is negative $3,776.24 at Month 1 end if no allowance or other funding arrives. This conditional calculation assumes the full raise is received, all modeled opening uses are paid and sales follow Our Plan. Actual payment dates can change the timing and peak deficit; a monthly model cannot establish intramonth need.

Put that consequence beside the cash headline. Obtain executed reimbursement mechanics, a dated construction payment schedule and an actual funding response. The disclosed $1,291,711 bridge preserves the full six-month no-sales reserve; it is different from the minimum amount needed to avoid a deficit. No bridge facility is established by this review.

### 2. Downside depends on an executable payroll reduction

Downside uses a 0.913 wage multiplier, an 8.7% reduction. It saves $178,802 of Year 1 payroll and employer costs relative to Our Plan. Disclosure Section 5.2 already conditions this on a workable staffing roster.

An internal sensitivity retaining Our Plan payroll while holding Downside demand and other costs constant produces approximately negative $218,457 Year 1 EBITDA, $743,737 minimum Year 1 cash and $980,986 year-end cash. These still assume allowance receipt and exclude unpriced cash uses. This is an internal calculation, not a new official scenario.

If staffing cannot be reduced, recommend unchanged staffing in the official Downside. If it can, substantiate the changed roles/hours. Do not call the current Downside a floor or worst case. Do not assume tipped or service-charge economics have been validated.

### 3. Resolve schedule overlap and annual staffing coverage

Early morning and lunch both include Monday/Tuesday 11 a.m.–2 p.m. activity. Disclosure Section 5.2 flags the overlap; confirm whether it is independent demand and capacity or double counting before changing affected revenue and labor.

Revenue uses 52 weeks while hourly payroll uses 50. Confirm the staffed calendar, leave/replacement coverage, overtime and benefits. Confirm employer-cost treatment within the $300,000 operator allocation. These are unresolved operating inputs, not formatting defects.

### 4. Quantify relevant cash uses before treating cash as distributable

Taxes, maintenance/other capital outlays, financing/distributions, working-capital movements and additional employee benefits are not quantified. Zero means excluded, not proven unnecessary. The package discloses these limits. Budget relevant items before relying on cash for reserve sufficiency or distributions; do not invent amounts to fill cells. EBITDA and investor cash are different measures.

### 5. Support demand and outdoor-seat exposure

74 of 126 planned seats, or 58.7%, are outdoors. No empirical weather discount is applied. A general downside haircut does not prove that exposure is measured. Provide dated paid-event covers, spend, repeat behavior and reservation conversion where available, and confirm capacity and patio operating constraints. The absence of this evidence in the pair does not prove it does not exist elsewhere. Historical competitive claims in profiles were not adopted as current facts.

### 6. Complete ownership and closing terms

Disclosure Sections 1.2 and 6 identify pricing references and definitive-document limitations. The $6m/$7m/$8m pre-money references do not by themselves establish final investor ownership. Obtain the closing cap table, unit basis, service equity, preferences and aggregate dilution, plus actual voting/distribution/transfer/capital-call and closing terms.

Counsel and the CPA must establish applicable offering and tax requirements from actual facts and documents. Do not invent IRR, payback or preferred returns. The SEC's [private-placement guidance](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/private) supports diligence into offering terms, financial information, use of proceeds and illiquidity. Specific requirements depend on the offering route and investors; this review does not select an exemption.
