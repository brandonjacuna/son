## 1\. Her ask against the record, and how much is already decided

### 1.1 What she asks

Her formal review process asks for five things a company must have once it has a few dozen people: a talent strategy, which "could be as simple as 'pay for performance'"; rubrics tied to job levels and ladders; agreement on how to assess results against them; "a program to capture performance feedback and promotion nominations via written and numeric assessments"; a calibration process "to equalize treatment of people at the same level"; and "a compensation philosophy and a means for the performance and promotion processes to feed into any compensation outcomes" (Book pp. 399 to 400). The review itself is a written document the manager drafts after reading three to five peers' feedback and the person's self-assessment, carrying a promotion nomination and a performance designation on a five-point or three-point scale, submitted into calibration, adjusted there, formalized in an HR tool, and delivered in a conversation that spends little time on the past and most on the next half (Book pp. 402 to 407). Calibration is a roll-up of managers by level, facilitated by a division leader, checking outliers, distributions, and bias, and ending in a spreadsheet of changed designations and promotions checked against compensation budgets (Book pp. 407 to 413). Compensation is a philosophy, a fixed and variable split, job levels, market data from an outside source, salary bands and equity targets, and an annual refresh; the compensation conversation is a manager explaining the system, sharing a raise or a bonus or the absence of one, handling comparisons and disappointment, and pointing a disappointed person at the job ladder (Book pp. 413 to 419). Her closing sentence is the one this page answers: employees are "assessed and rewarded fairly, meaning appropriately for their contributions at their level, within the system you've built" (Book p.419, context line).

The workbook adds two instruments: a Performance Review Template (name, current level, proposed designation, proposed promotion, why not higher, why not lower, the one-sentence takeaway, three impact areas with metrics, two strengths and two development areas each with an approximate designation, and a promotion proposal with its timing) and a Compensation Conversations Preparation and Guide (the timeline and resources, preparation at the individual level including the size of the last increase, owning the message, and an outline that reminds the person of their current pay, shares base salary and target bonus for the coming year, and opens for discussion).

Every piece assumes a salaried knowledge worker, a manager who assigns, reviews, and rates from above, an annual or semiannual cycle with a review season, a calibration committee, levels and a ladder, salary bands and an equity refresh, a raise or a bonus as the reward the review produces, and a written narrative about the person in the manager's voice. None of that is Sŏn, and the session task says so. The task's own premise is tested in 1.5.

### 1.5 The session task's premise, tested, and the task text's figure

The task says almost none of her compensation machinery transfers, and that what transfers is the principle: compensation must be legible, defensible, and connected to a review people trust. Tested piece by piece, the premise is right about the form and wrong about one of its three principles.

What transfers as function once the form is removed, and is built here: a published account of how pay works that every person can explain in their own words (her philosophy, section 13); a stated mechanism from which a person can compute their own pay (her bands and market data have no equivalent, but her "instill trust in your systems" does: the pay page, section 13); a calendar (her review cycle becomes the person's own clock, section 2); a conversation guide (her compensation conversation becomes the offer conversation, the mechanics module, and the destinations of a pay question, section 14); and a rule for what a lead may and may not say about pay (section 14). What the record already decided and this page does not re-derive: built into menu price, never a service charge and never presented as one, no tips, the whole team including the culinary team, a point system weighted by days, calculated daily, paid on a reasonable rhythm, benefits from the start.

The third principle does not transfer. At Sŏn compensation is not "connected to a review process people trust." It is disconnected from the review by design, and the disconnection is what makes the review trustworthy and the pay legible (section 12). Her pay is connected to her review because her review produces a rating and her rating produces a raise; the record's review produces "development, growth, and the path forward rather than a verdict," the check-in refuses pay, and no feedback act touches pay (`17tn048qcjg`). What the task's principle is reaching for, that a person trusts the system that pays them, is carried at Sŏn by a system with no judgment in it: the seat, the horizon, the markers, and the days, each on a record the person can read.

The task text also carries a specific percentage and names a data source that is on the program's exclusion list. The record itself says the built-in compensation is "set at a defined percentage that is still being finalized" (WP p.20) and that "the remaining open piece is the final percentage" (WP p.22). The task text and the record therefore disagree about whether the figure exists, and the team did not use the figure, reason from it, or let it shape a sentence; financials are not a context source for this program. That is a finding about the task text, not a position, and it is stated once.

**P1 (Recommended).** Her review and compensation material lands on a record that settled the pool's principle and form and the review's focus, interval, and governing rule, and on eleven sessions that built everything a review would read and fenced everything it may not. What this page adds is the review as a designed mechanism (when, where, who, on what, writing what, refusing what) and the compensation architecture as a mechanism with no figure in it. Her forms are refused wherever they assume a rating, a level, a manager's narrative, or a raise; her functions survive as the pay page, the mechanics page, the person's own clock, and the rules for what a lead may say.

**P2 (Recommended).** The session task's premise is answered in two parts: her compensation machinery does not transfer as form, and two of its three underlying principles transfer as function (legible, defensible), while the third (connected to a review) is refused by design, because at Sŏn the review's trustworthiness and the pay's legibility both depend on their separation. The task text's figure and source were not used, and the record's own statement that the percentage is unfinalized is the one this page follows.

## 2\. The review, placed: interval, calendar, clock, room, holder, language

### 2.1 The interval, inside "three to six months"

The record states a range and no owner (S4 finding, line 41). Session 4 argued the check-in to monthly as "the longest interval at which the review every three to six months can be true to its own rule" (S4 4.5). This page picks the review's interval inside the range from the other side: the longest interval at which the review still does work the check-in does not, and the shortest the Maitre d's windows can carry beside every floor check-in.

Six months. Three arguments. First, the check-in already carries the running conversation monthly, so a review at three months is the fourth check-in wearing a longer form: it would re-read three lines the lead has already read and the path line the career conversation just wrote. At six months the review reads five check-in lines, a quarter's captures twice over, at least one unlock window on most seats, and a fluency window's currency, which is enough record for the dimensions to say something the last check-in did not. Second, the Realist's arithmetic (S4 4.5) governs: the Maitre d holds every floor check-in monthly, and a review is a longer block on the same windows; at six months the review adds one block per person twice a year, at three months it adds four, and Session 12 has already added placed and extended check-ins to what the seat spends (S12 P37). Third, the record's word is "every three to six months," and the six-month end is the one that keeps the review formal: a review that runs as often as the check-in stops being the moment the record wrote it as. The interval is a reset parameter with a floor: the reset may shorten it toward three months and never below, on one read only, whether reviews changed a path line or a supplied line the check-ins had not touched, which would mean the check-ins are not carrying the path. Reviews that contained something new are not a reason to shorten the interval; they are a candor failure on the lead's entry (section 4).

### 2.2 The calendar: the person's own clock, never a season

Her cycle is a company calendar: everyone reviewed in the same weeks, calibration after, delivery in a two-week window (Book pp. 408, 411). At Sŏn the review runs on the person's own clock, placed by the stack: the first review six months after the person's first paid hour, and every review six months after the last. There is no review season, because a season is a load spike on one seat's windows in one month, because it makes the review a house event rather than a person's, and because the only reason her season exists, calibration across everyone at once, is refused (section 6.4). The clock is the same clock the check-in runs on (S4 P15), and the stack places both.

Against the other rhythms. The ninety-day plan runs six check-ins every two weeks and closes as a state (S7 P25 to P29); the review never falls inside it. The career conversation is held once, "in the month after the plan closes and before the first review" (S9 P43); at six months from the first paid hour the first review falls about two months after it, which keeps the record's order and gives the path line time to be re-read at a check-in. The leads' review is weekly and the house review is quarterly, and neither reads a person (S4 P22, P26; S3 P10.4); the review's block is placed so that it never displaces the brief's slot or the readiness block, which are the two things a pre-service window already owes (S10 P20; S12 P37). The mechanism reset and dinner's first-quarter freeze: inside the freeze the room's cadence mechanisms do not change (S4 2.7; S9 P21), and the review is a person mechanism the stack places on the person's clock, so the freeze does not stop a review from being held; in practice none is held in dinner's first quarter, because the cohort is inside its plans and the leads' first reviews wait on `86akhb2jg`. The reset reads the review's parameters (the interval, the block's length, the load) like every other mechanism's.

### 2.3 On the clock, in a block, and where

The review is a scheduled block, paid as shift, placed by the stack as the career conversation is placed (S9 15.2): in a pre-service window with slack or at a closed hour, never mid-service, never at the close, never off the clock, ending before the brief. Its length is thirty minutes to start, a reset parameter, the same as the career conversation's and twice the check-in's, because it reads a period whole and carries an upward half longer than the fourth question. The lead may extend it when the window has the slack, at the lead's own read, and may move it to the person's next shift the lead also works when the window is short-crewed, by the check-in's own range (S4 P20); a moved review keeps its place and is never cancelled. "Nothing is required of a person before their first paid hour" and nothing is owed off the clock (S7 P15): a self-review written at home is refused, and the person's own half of the review is written in the block (section 5).

Where: not on the floor, not in a room the roster is in, and never with a third person present except an interpreter designation under S11 P20. Canon's rule that the environment names no person in a room (S10 P14) and the record's "post-mortems are private" (WP p.17) hold; the review is between two people and its content reaches no one beyond them (S11 P32, P33).

### 2.4 Who holds it

The lead who holds the person's check-in: the Maitre d for every floor seat on both strands (S8 P21, P24), the Head of Beverage for beverage seats, and, for the kitchen, whoever the chef partner rules holds the kitchen's check-in (S4 P16, chef-gated). The holder does not change for the review, because the review opens on the check-in's lines and a second reader would be reading a record written in a relationship they were not in. A lead's own review waits on `86akhb2jg`, with the team's reading that its holder is whoever holds the lead's check-in (section 8.5). The mentor is never in the room and never consulted (S7 P42; S10 P32). A founder holds no review except by the default Session 12 named for the first leads (`17tn048qcjz`, to Session 16).

### 2.5 In which language

The person's chosen language, as the check-in (S4 4.3; S11 P17), with the review record written in it and canon's terms standing untranslated (S11 P19). Where the lead and the person share no language the interpreter designation's rule for a check-in applies unchanged and is founder-gated with it (S11 P20; S12 I9).

### 3.2 The four dimensions at review, read as states and never as marks

Session 6 fixed the dimensions and the terms, and said "the marks Session 13 uses at review are Session 13's to set, and the record constrains them already: a review is 'development, growth, and the path forward rather than a verdict' (WP p.19), so the review's marks carry no verdict either" (S6 6.2). This page sets them as states read from records, in the record's own vocabulary, with no scale.

**Dimension 1, competency for the seat.** Read from the progression state: the horizon the person holds on the seat sheet's field 6 and the date of the next scheduled unlock. There is nothing to mark. A person in a seat holds its entry horizon by construction, because the readiness form read ready before the first solo (S7 P23), and holds each later horizon because an assessor read the form ready and the lead recorded it (S8 P37). A lead who "rated" competency at review would be judging an unlock, which no lead may do. So the review's dimension 1 is a sentence the stack writes: the horizon held, since when, and what is scheduled next. Her "does not meet" has no place here, because a person who did not hold a horizon would be a plan not held (Session 14's, S7 P29) or a readiness read that reads not yet (S12 P21), and neither is a review.

**Dimension 2, self-directed mastery.** Read from the invisible advocate's own record, "not from a lead's impression, because 'No one's advancement waits on a manager's attention or judgment'" (S6 6.2): unlocks opened and taken, modules completed, modules authored and approved, connections added, and the fluency markers' currency, as a list with dates. The person's part in teaching is here: the modules they authored, the plans they mentored (section 11). Nothing is scored; the list is read aloud and the person says what they want next.

**Dimension 3, conduct under the non-negotiables and inside the range.** Two halves. ## 4\. "No surprises" as a mechanism

### 4.1 The rule, and why a wish is not enough

"The governing rule is no surprises: continuous communication means a formal review never contains new information" (WP p.19). Her version is the manager's diligence: "If you've managed well, there are no surprises" (Book p.401). The record's is stronger and Session 4 built the first half of it: the check-in is placed by the stack rather than by a lead's memory, so the conversation the review summarizes cannot silently stop (S4 P15). Session 12 built the second half: the only record of coaching is the person's own line, so there is no lead's file for a review to open (S12 P10, P48). This page builds the third half, the review's own: the review is bounded by the page, it stops when it would exceed the page, and the record proves which it did.

### 4.2 Bounded: the stack assembles, the lead adds nothing

Before the block, the stack assembles the review from the records in 3.1 into the review record's fields (I1): the lines with dates, the states, the counts, the connections, the infrastructure items. The lead's pre-read is that assembly and the records it cites, and the lead writes nothing (S12 4.1). The lead's card (I3) has no field for the lead's observation, summary, or view. So the review's content is fixed before anyone speaks, and it is the same content the person can open on their phone the day before.

### 4.3 Stops: what happens when a review would contain something new

A lead who arrives at a review holding something about the person's work that is on no record the person can read has a hypothesis, and the hypothesis has one destination, the check-in (S12 P5, P6). At the review the lead does not say it. The review proceeds on the page. After the block the lead asks the stack to place a check-in (S12 P16), and the hypothesis is said there, once, as an observation of behavior and impact, after the person's own account. The review never becomes the placed check-in and the placed check-in is never held inside the review's block; the two are different mechanisms with different refusals, and a review that absorbed a hypothesis would have become her manager's review with the writing removed.

Three cases, each with its path. Something the lead saw and the person did not write: a hypothesis, to the check-in, as above. Something a record shows that the person has not read: the lead reads the record with the person at the review, because the record is the person's to read and the review is where the page is read whole; a capture the person wrote and forgot is not new information. Something excellent the lead has not said: the record's rule runs both ways, "If something is excellent, they know before it too" (WP p.19), and a strength the lead has not named at a check-in is named at the next one; at the review the lead may read aloud what the record shows, which is section 5.3's formal half of "you really have talent," and may not add a judgment the record does not carry.

### 4.4 Proves: the read-back and the candor line

At the end of the block the lead reads the review record back and asks one question in the person's language: was anything said in this review that was not on your page or on a record you could read. The person's answer is a mark on the record, nothing new or something new, in the person's words if they choose to say what. The mark is the mechanism's proof and its enforcement. A review marked "something new" is a candor line on the lead's entry at the leads' review, read as a failure of the check-in to carry what it should have, "read on the lead's entry and not a diligence lapse" (`17tn048qcjg`), with the new item placed at a check-in by the stack and nothing about it written on the person page. The count of reviews marked "something new" per lead per period is on the leads' review record as a count on the mechanism, never per person, and at the reset it is read beside the check-in's interval: reviews that contain new information are the check-in failing, and the fix is on the check-in and the lead's entry, never on the review's interval and never on the person.

The form enforces it a second way: every item on the review record carries the date of the line or the state it cites, and an item with no citation cannot be entered. What cannot be cited cannot be reviewed.
