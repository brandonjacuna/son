## 5. What the review writes, who writes it, and what the person writes

### 5.1 The review record, in two halves and no narrative

The review writes one record on the person page, the review record (I1), and it has two halves. The first half is assembled by the stack and confirmed at the review: the four dimensions as states (section 3.2), each item with its citation date. The second half is the person's, in the person's words, written in the block: what they hold, in their account of the period; what they want next, the path line re-read and re-written if they choose and the one track (S9 P43); what the building must supply for it, which becomes the infrastructure line; their read on the lead's holding of the lead's range and on the house, written only if they want it written (section 6.5); and the "nothing new" mark. The person writes it, or the lead writes the person's words and reads them back, by the check-in's own rule (S4 P15; S12 P10). The lead writes nothing in the lead's voice: no summary, no assessment, no strength or development area in the lead's words, no rating, no designation, no nomination, no recommendation. A review record is the person's words and the stack's states, and nothing else is on it.

### 5.2 Ratings, tested and decided

Her scales, five designations or three (Book pp. 404 to 405), are refused, and the refusal is not a preference. The record says the review focuses on the path forward "rather than a verdict," and a designation is a verdict with a name (WP p.19). The person page refuses ratings (S7 P25). Session 6 fixed that the review's marks carry no verdict (S6 6.2). The three-seat advancement rule means the lead may not judge competency, which removes the one dimension a rating could honestly rate (S8 P37). "There is no hierarchy of importance" (WP p.20), and "greatly exceeds" is a rank among people whatever it is called. And the only reason her designation exists, that it "largely depends on how much you want to differentiate rewards based on performance" (Book p.405), does not exist at Sŏn, because no review output reaches pay (section 12). Her further argument, that designations over time identify top talent (Book p.405), is carried by the record's own mechanism: the advocate's record, connections, and the ready-now pool identify who is furthest along, on documented competencies "anyone can see" (WP p.11, p.14), and never on a lead's mark.

What replaces a rating is nothing, deliberately. The states in 3.2 are facts (the horizon held, the list of unlocks, the count's own words, supplied or not), and a fact is not a mark. A person leaves the review knowing what the record shows and what they said they want next, and knowing that no one wrote a word about them.

### 5.3 "You really have talent," the formal half

Session 12 placed the informal half: the check-in's first question, what is working, asked every time and heard as the person's account, and the recognition a peer writes about an act (S12 section 2). The formal half is the review's, and it is the reading of the record. At the review the lead reads aloud, in canon's register, what the period's records show: the connections added and their dates, the unlocks taken, the modules authored, the captures in which a coordination failure was absorbed with no recovery needed (the record's "held at once" moment, S8 P35), the range used well on a named service. That is what "as part of a formal process" (Book p.384) means at Sŏn: a formal reading of what the person did, from the record, in specific acts on specific services, with no adjective performing warmth (Canon 1.0, section 09). "Saturday the fourteenth, the re-seat when the pyeong-sang ran late, in your own capture" is the form. The lead never says the person has talent; the lead reads what the person did, and the person hears it read.

### 5.4 The review's form beside the one line and the plan block

Three records now live on the person page in three forms, and the forms do not blur. The check-in line is one line, monthly, in the person's words, about anything (S4 P15; S12 P10). The plan block is states and milestones with dates for ninety days (S7 I2). The review record is the four states with citations and the person's own half, twice a year. The check-in never writes a state; the plan block never holds a line; the review never writes a hypothesis, a milestone, or a plan state. The stack writes what the stack writes and the person writes the rest.

**P11 (Recommended).** The review writes one record in two halves: the four dimensions as states with citation dates, assembled by the stack and confirmed at the review, and the person's own half in their words (what they hold, what they want next as the path line and the track, what the building must supply, their read of the lead and the house if they want it written, and the "nothing new" mark). The lead writes nothing in the lead's voice, and the record carries no rating, designation, nomination, summary, or assessment.

**P12 (Recommended).** Her performance designations on any scale are refused, and nothing replaces them: the record's "rather than a verdict," the person page's refusal of ratings, the three-seat advancement rule, "no hierarchy of importance," and the separation of review from pay each close the door. Top talent is identified by the advocate's record and the pool, never by a mark.

**P13 (Recommended).** "You really have talent" as part of a formal process is the review's reading of the record: connections, unlocks, authored modules, the captures where a failure was absorbed unseen, and the range used well, read aloud as specific acts on specific services in canon's register; the lead reads what the person did and never says what the person is.

## 6. Her self-review, peer input, manager assessment, calibration, and the upward half, each tested

### 6.1 The self-assessment

Absorbed as the person's own half, with two changes. It is written in the block, on the clock, and never before it or at home, because nothing is owed off the clock (S7 P15) and a self-review written at the kitchen table is unpaid work; the person may of course think about it, and the page is theirs to open any time. And her three prompts are translated: "a short summary of your top contributions and accomplishments" (Book p.404) becomes what you held this period, in the person's account against their own captures and connections; "one capability from the job ladder that you excel at" is refused as a ladder item and returns as what is working, which the check-in already asks; "one area of development from the job ladder and ideas on how you might improve. Include thoughts on how your manager could support you" becomes what you want next (the track) and what the building must supply (dimension 4). The person's half is never scored, never compared to the lead's view (there is none), and never a document a lead reads before the block and forms a view from.

### 6.2 Peer reviews

Refused as a review input. Her prompts ask a peer for a capability the person is strong in, one to improve, and which operating principle they most embody (Book p.403). At Sŏn a peer's account of a peer is a refused source (S12 P4), because the environment names no person and a lead who asks one peer about another has built the informal bus the record names as failure (WP p.13; S10 P14). A peer's positive feedback already has its whole mechanism, the recognition, which is never a review input (S11 P11). A principle is never a rating scale (S11 6.7; S12 P34). What does the function's work: the recognition read at the brief in the moment; the captures written at the edge by the people who felt a failure absorbed (S8 P35), which are the record's own peer evidence and are read at the review only when the person wrote them; and the mentor, who "gives no verdict on the person to anyone" (S7 P42).

### 6.3 The manager review

Refused as a written narrative in the manager's voice, for the reason Session 12 refused the recap: a lead's summary is free text about the person in the lead's voice, which the person page refuses (S7 P25) and which the lead may not write outside a check-in and may not write there either (S4 P20; S12 P10). Her promotion nomination is refused because advancement has three seats and the lead records a form the assessor scored and "may not override it in either direction" (S8 P37); the review may read that an unlock is scheduled and may hear the person name a track, and it nominates no one. Her designation is refused in 5.2. What the manager review did that survives: the lead reads the record aloud (5.3), and the lead hears the person's half and the upward half.

### 6.4 Calibration

Refused as a committee, a roll-up by level, an outlier check on designations, an expected distribution, a promotion-percentage check, and a check against compensation budgets, and each refusal has a reason the record already holds. There are no designations to compare, no levels to roll up by, and no distribution to expect, because the review writes states and not marks (5.2), the house has no levels (S8 P30), and "high n counts" (Book p.411) is a headcount premise the record's single house does not meet (`86ajgnh8n`). On the floor there is one holder of every review, the Maitre d, so there is no second manager to calibrate against; across domains the review's states are facts from records assembled by the stack identically for every person, so calibration would be calibrating arithmetic. And the compensation-budget check is refused because no review output reaches pay (section 12).

What carries calibration's three functions. Fairness across people at the same seat: the review record has one form, the stack assembles it the same way for everyone, the assessor pool scores every unlock on the practical's rows (S6 I4; S7 I7), and the ninety-day calibration read already tests the hiring bar and the readiness bar against each other (S6 7.6, P41). The check on a lead who is too lenient or too harsh: at Sŏn a lead cannot be either, because the lead marks nothing; what a lead can be is silent, and the candor line and the count of reviews marked "something new" per lead read that (P10), at the leads' review and never by person. Her bias check, "fewer women than men promoted in a particular function" (Book p.411): the review has no promotions to count; the outputs pay actually runs on (unlock rate, marker rate, days scheduled) can be read by the reach read's tags with no name, no figure, and no act on a person, which is the parity read section 13.4 designs and marks founder-gated with counsel (`17tn048qc2c`). Her calibration leader's "would you enthusiastically rehire this person" (Book p.412) is refused as a question about a person asked in a room; where a hire does not hold is Session 14's (`86akh9t9q`).

### 6.5 The upward half: management effectiveness, both directions

"Team members weigh in on management effectiveness, operational improvements, and culture" (WP p.19). Session 11 placed the running version on the channel and the check-in's fourth question, and noted that "the house has no manager," so what a person weighs in on is the house, a mechanism, a tool, a space, a process, and "their lead's holding of the lead's range" (S11 12.2). The review's upward half is the fourth question at length: the person's read of the lead's holding of the range, in the vocabulary of the lead's working-with-me document, which is "the vocabulary a domain may use upward about the lead" (S10 P30); the person's read of the house's mechanisms as items for the channel, which the lead writes on the channel with the person's knowledge (S4 4.4); and dimension 4 as the review of the building. The lead asks it in her form, as a request for something to change (S12 4.5), repeats it back, and does not explain. It is written only if the person wants it written, in the person's words.

What the upward half about the lead cannot yet reach. A concern about the lead who holds the review has no destination the lead does not hold until `86akhb2jg` closes (S10 P15; S12 9.2), and this page does not fill it; the review makes the gap sharper, because a review is the one formal moment the record promises for feedback about management and the person's read of their lead goes, at present, only to their lead. The team's reading, held: once a lead's review has a holder, that holder reads the upward halves of the lead's domain's reviews aggregated by mechanism and never by person, as the lead's own review's dimension 4 from the other side. That is Session 15's to design (managing managers) and the founders' to close.

**P14 (Recommended).** Her self-assessment is absorbed as the person's own half of the review, written in the block on the clock and never before it, with her three prompts translated to what you held, what you want next, and what the building must supply, and never scored or compared. Her peer reviews are refused as an input, the recognition and the person's own captures carrying the function. Her manager review is refused as a narrative in the lead's voice and as a nomination, the reading of the record carrying what survives.

**P15 (Recommended).** Calibration is refused as a committee, a roll-up, an outlier check, a distribution, a promotion percentage, and a budget check, because there is nothing to calibrate: the review writes states from records assembled identically for everyone, the house has no levels, the floor has one review holder, and no review output reaches pay. Its functions are carried by the one form, the assessor pool, the ninety-day calibration read, the candor line, and the parity read.

**P16 (Recommended).** The review's upward half is the fourth question at length: the person's read of the lead's holding of the range in the working-with-me vocabulary, the house's mechanisms as channel items, and dimension 4 on the building, asked as a request for something to change, repeated back, not explained, and written only if the person wants it. A concern about the review's holder stays undestined until `86akhb2jg` closes, and the reading that a lead's review holder reads the domain's upward halves by mechanism is held for Session 15.

## 7. What the review may never become

Stated as refusals, each with the rule that makes it a refusal and not a hope.

A verdict. The record's "rather than a verdict" (WP p.19); the review record has no field for one (I1).

A rating, a designation, or a rank. Section 5.2; the person page refuses ratings (S7 P25); "no hierarchy of importance" (WP p.20).

A pay input, by any door. No review record has a pay field; no pay page cites a review; the point system's inputs are the seat's horizon, the markers, and the days, and nothing a review writes is one of them (section 12). The "nothing new" mark, the states, the person's half, and the upward half are read by no pay mechanism, and the stack's configuration refuses a field that would carry any of them across (row 159). A raise, a bonus, and a withheld increase do not exist as events.

A promotion nomination or an advancement input. Advancement has three seats and the review is not one of them (S8 P37); the review may read a scheduled unlock and may not schedule, hasten, delay, or condition one.

A managing-out file. No review record may be cited in a managing-out decision, as no evidence line, scorecard, or reset record may (S9 P42) and no coaching record may (S12 P7). A review carries the person's words and the stack's states, and a document made of a person's own words and dated facts about horizons and ranges is not a case; what Session 14 designs for a person whose work is not holding is not a review and does not wear its form (`17tn048qcjt`), and Session 15's managing out cites the review nowhere. Her "if you're managing someone out or providing a lot of critical feedback, compensation conversations can be a helpful tool to make the situation feel concrete" (Book p.418) is refused whole: there is no compensation conversation to use, and pay is not a lever anyone holds.

A read of Nunchi or Jeong. Neither is scored at hiring and neither is developed by being graded; a lead may read an act at a table from a capture and may never say a person has, lacks, or is developing either (S12 P39; Canon 1.0, section 01).

A room. Two people and, where needed, an interpreter designation; never a third person, never the floor, never a room the roster is in (S10 P14); its content reaches no one beyond the person and their lead (S11 P32, P33).

A coaching record, a hypothesis's page, or a written recap. The review holds no lead's observation and the hypothesis still has no page (S12 P5, P13).

A probation label, a plan state, or a milestone. Those are the plan block's, and the plan is closed before the first review (S7 P28).

A promise: of pay, of a seat, of a date beyond the sheet's unlock period, of a designation. The career conversation refuses them (S9 P44) and the review inherits every refusal.

A calibration input, a talent-review input, or a distribution's data point. Section 6.4; note 71's nine-box is refused with it.

**P17 (Recommended).** The review may never become a verdict, a rating or rank, a pay input by any door, a promotion nomination or advancement input, a managing-out file, a read of Nunchi or Jeong, a room, a coaching record or recap, a probation label or plan state, a promise of pay or a seat, or a calibration or talent-review input. Each refusal is a rule already in the record or the ledger, and the review record's form, with no free field and no pay field, is what holds them.

## 8. The review in each context the carryovers name

### 8.1 The first review after the plan

Six months after the first paid hour, about three months after the plan closed as a state and about two months after the career conversation. It opens on the plan's close state, the fourth-read baseline the candidate gave at the first interview (S7 I2) read against what the building supplied since, the career conversation block's path line and track, and the lines since the plan closed. Its dimension 1 is the entry horizon held and the first unlock's scheduled date; its dimension 2 is what the advocate's record shows since the readiness read; its dimension 4 is the first full read of whether the building kept the transparency sheet's word. Nothing from the plan's six reads is re-read as a row, and the readiness form is not opened (S7 I7).

### 8.2 A person on two strands, or two periods

The review reads the connections and their currency as facts: two strand connections in one domain with their dates and the window (S8 P35), or two period connections (S8 P36). It does not score fluency, because fluency is a marker priced in pay (section 10.2) and never a dimension. It reads the "held at once" captures the person wrote as part of dimension 3's range half and as section 5.3's reading of the record. A person whose window has lapsed is told so by the stack on the progression state, not by the lead at the review, and what re-establishes it (a seat held on the other strand on a scheduled service) is the schedule's, not the review's.

### 8.3 A designation's holder

The review reads nothing about designations held: no count, no mark beyond the brief's record, no pay line (S10 P33; `86akht376`). The person may write that holding the room was what worked, in their own half; the lead may not cite the count, name the rotation, or treat the designation as evidence of anything. A designation held every service is a reset question about the seat's hours and never a read of the holder (S9 P32).

### 8.4 The Maitre d's review load

The Maitre d holds every floor review, on the same windows that hold every floor check-in and every placed check-in. The load is read as counts and no new read: reviews held, moved, and extended per period, added to the reset's first read of the seat (S9 P36) beside Session 12's three counts (S12 P37). Its permitted carriers are the stack (placing reviews at closed hours and across the seat's windows; the person's own clock spreading them through the year, which is the second reason there is no review season) and the procedure (the stack's assembly, the thirty minutes, the lead's card); the refused carriers stay refused (a second floor seat, a floor manager under any name, an assistant, a permanent room holder, a service manager), and a designation cannot carry it because a designation holds no check-in and no review (S8 8.5). The review is never thinned to fit and never delegated to a mentor or a senior server; a load finding is answered at the seat and the schedule, as Session 12 answered the coaching load.
