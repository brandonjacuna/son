## 9. The compensation architecture, designed without a figure

### 9.1 What the record fixed, and what it left

Fixed, and not re-derived: the team's compensation is built into the menu price on the backend; it is not a service charge, is never presented as one, and is not a tip, so no tip-aggregation tool exists (WP p.20, p.22); it is distributed to the whole team, the culinary team included; on a point system weighted by the days each person works; calculated daily rather than quarterly; paid on a reasonable rhythm; benefits are planned from the start; cross-strand fluency is recognized in compensation; teaching is paid; the percentage is unfinalized (WP p.11, p.19, p.20, p.22). Left, and designed here: what the pool is a share of; who is in it, seat by seat; how a person's points are set and what may never set them; what a day is; how the daily calculation is done and shown; the payment rhythm's owner; how base pay and the pool relate; how a salaried seat sits on a day-weighted system; and every parameter's owner.

The architecture has two layers, and the record describes only the second. Base pay is the hourly wage of an edge seat or the salary of a salaried seat, which the record never names and everywhere assumes: "a paid practical" (WP p.18), "the training plan with pay dates" (WP p.18), and every mechanism this program placed "as shift." The pool is the built-in share the record describes. Every person on the roster holds both. No figure for either appears in this program; the wage and salary values, and wage and hour law, are the founders' with counsel (finding 19.1).

### 9.2 The pool, as a mechanism

The pool is the day's built-in share: a defined proportion, the percentage the record calls unfinalized, applied to a defined base, computed by the stack at the close of each day from the point-of-sale record. Three things define it and each is a parameter with an owner:

The percentage. The record's open item (WP p.22). Owner: the founders, set with Dominic's domain against the financial model. Financials are not a context source for this program; this page states no value and no range.

The base: which revenue counts. The record says where the compensation is collected, in the menu price, and not what the pool is a share of. The base is a list of revenue classes, in or out, and the team's constraint on the list is one sentence: the base must not shrink when a server uses the generosity range, because a comp that reduces the pool makes the team pay for its own generosity, and the record wants the range "fully used, not minimized" (WP p.26). The team recommends that a comped item counts in the base at its menu price and that recovery spend does not reduce the day's pool; which other classes are in (the morning window, events, retail, a private tasting) is the founders' with Dominic's domain, versioned on the parameters register (I5).

One pool per day, for the whole house. The record's picture is dinner-shaped, "the nights they help fill" (WP p.20), and the record's thesis is one restaurant "met at different times of day" (WP p.33). The team recommends one pool per calendar day across every open period, shared by everyone who worked that day in any period, and not a pool per period. Three reasons. A per-period pool makes the periods compete for the same people's days and pays the morning window's team out of the morning window alone, which is the daypart bolted on that the record's first page names as the structural failure (WP p.2). Cross-period fluency is a marker the record needs (S8 P36), and a person who holds the window at seven and the floor at seven is one person on one day. And "no hierarchy of importance" (WP p.20) applied to periods means the person who set the room for dinner at ten in the morning shares dinner. The alternative, a per-period pool, is stated so the founders can refuse the recommendation: it would pay each period's team from its own revenue and would need a rule for the shared seats, and the team found no sentence in the record that asks for it.

### 9.3 Who is in it

Every seat on the roster that worked the day, in every domain and both strands: servers, hosts, runners, the beverage seats, the kitchen's Station Leads, Chefs on the line and on prep, the Steward, and the salaried seats on the rule in 10.1. A person in training is in it from their first paid hour (11.4). A candidate on a paid practical is not, because a candidate is not on the roster and the practical's employment form is counsel's (`86akh7qwd`). The overnight cleaning crew is contracted labor outside the web (S8 finding 21.10) and is outside the pool by that fact; finding 19.13 states what that costs the record's "whole team." The founders are not the team; whether the partners hold points is Session 16's and founder-gated (`86akh3x9m`). Under branch A the chef partner is a founder; under branch B the chef seat is a salaried seat in the pool (10.1).

### 9.4 A person's points: the inputs, and what may never be one

A person's points on a day are set by four things and nothing else, each on a record the person can read:

1. The seat held, at the horizon held. Every seat sheet carries, at each of field 6's horizons, the seat's point weight (I6). The weight is a seat property, the same for every person holding that seat at that horizon, and it is published on the seat sheet and on the transparency sheet's item 6. Its value is the founders' with Dominic's domain. This is the unlock-tied progression Session 8 named as this page's (S8 P30): an unlock recorded by the lead moves the person to the next horizon's weight on the date of the record, which is the record's "direct line between learning and earning" (WP p.19) made mechanical.
2. The markers. Cross-strand fluency and cross-period fluency, each an on-or-off addition while current inside Session 8's window (S8 P35, P36), and the teaching marker while a mentored plan is open (11.1). A marker's value is a parameter; a marker is never graded.
3. The days. The weight the record fixed: the person's points count on each day they worked, and a day is defined in 9.5.
4. The seat's day-weight rule for a salaried seat (10.1), which is the one place a seat's days are counted by rule rather than by shift.

What may never set or move a person's points, each with its rule: a review's states, mark, or the person's half (section 12); a check-in line or anything said at one (S4 P15); a rating, which does not exist (P12); a designation held or withheld (S10 P33); a recognition given or received (S11 P13); a lead's judgment of any kind, because "No one's advancement waits on a manager's attention or judgment" (WP p.11) and pay is advancement's downstream; a lagging indicator as a driver beyond the pool's own base (`86akh5ux8`), so a person's points never rise or fall with food cost, labor percentage, or a period's revenue; a customer's rating, a tip, or a per-server sales attribution, because the relationship is the server's book and the pool is shared (WP p.25); hours worked, because the record chose days; a period worked, because one pool per day already carries it; the person's history before the house, tenure as such, or a negotiation at hire, because "one rule for everyone by construction" is the whole defense of the system (`17tn048qc2c`). The points a person holds are a function of records, and no person in the house, the founders included, moves them by hand.

### 9.5 A day, defined

The record says "weighted by the days each person works" and never says what a day is (finding 19.3). A day is a calendar day on which the person worked a scheduled shift of any length in any period, counted once: a person who works the morning window and dinner on one day has worked one day, which is why cross-period fluency is a marker and not a second day (10.2). A check-in, a review, a career conversation, a training block, a paired service, a module hour on shift, and the orientation block are scheduled shift and count, because they are paid as shift (S4 P15; S7 P15; S9 P43) and the record does not shift costs to employees (WP p.19). A scheduled day not worked does not count. A day the house closes after the schedule posted, and what the scheduled people are paid for it, is a wage question for the founders with counsel and a parameter on the register, with the team's reading that the day's pool is zero and the base pay is the founders' call. The definition of a day is on the mechanics page in every house language.

### 9.6 The daily calculation, and who can see it

At the close of each day the stack computes the day's pool from the base and the percentage, sums the points of every person who worked that day (each person's seat weight at their horizon plus their current markers, and the salaried rule's days), and writes each person's share as their points over the day's total points, times the pool. The person's share, their points and how they are composed, and the day counted are on the person's pay page (I4) by the next morning, in the person's language, the way the thank-you note follows the night (WP p.26). The share accrues to the pay date. No lead computes anything, no lead sees another person's page, and the calculation runs on the point-of-sale record and the schedule, two systems the record names as unsettled and settled respectively (finding 19.8). Until the OS surface carries it, the calculation is a build on the backbone and the partners' page records it as manual, by Session 7's rule for every mesh gap (S7 P31).

What the person can see of the arithmetic is section 13.2's question and it is founder-gated at the pool's total.

### 9.7 The payment rhythm

"Calculated daily rather than quarterly and paid on a reasonable rhythm" (WP p.20). The rhythm's owner is the record's silence (finding 19.4). The team recommends: the pool share is paid on the same pay date as base pay, in the same payment, never later than base pay and never on a slower cycle, because a share that arrives a month after the nights it was earned is not the "upside of a full night" (WP p.20) as anyone feels it; the cycle's length is bounded by pay-date law, which is counsel's, and by what the payroll platform carries, which is to confirm; the Realist's read is that an hourly team in this labor market is paid weekly or every two weeks in practice and that a rhythm slower than the market's makes "reasonable" false at the first pay date. The rhythm is a parameter on the register, founder-gated with counsel, and it is a duration and not a figure.

### 9.8 Base pay against the pool: the record's silence, stated

The record describes the pool and never describes base pay (finding 19.1). The two relate as two layers on one pay date: base pay is the seat's hourly wage or salary and is what the training plan's "pay dates" (WP p.18) and the paid practical pay; the pool is the share. The team states three constraints and no value: base pay for an edge seat is an hourly wage set per seat, the same for every person in the seat, and published on the sheet like the weight (one rule for everyone); the pool is never a substitute for base pay and no offer states a total that depends on the pool, because the pool is a share of nights not yet filled and "never assert the unproven" (WP p.36) applies to a candidate before it applies to anyone; and every wage, overtime, tip-law, and pay-transparency question the two layers raise is counsel's, including whether a share built into menu price is or is not a tip in law, which the parked carryover already holds outside the program (`86ajgnhzk`). The wage values are the founders' with Dominic's domain and counsel.

The company runs on Rippling, chosen for what it delivers, including access to cost-effective benefits for frontline employees and a fast path to full benefits as the business supports them" (WP p.20). The platform's features are to confirm and are not asserted; the tool is named because the record names it. What the architecture states: which benefits exist at hire is on the transparency sheet's item 3 as a fact; the "fast path to full benefits" is a gate, not a date, and its condition, "as the business supports them," is a financial condition the founders write on the partners' page as a readiness-row-like statement (which benefit opens on which condition) that the house can read without the figure; and no candidate is told a benefit's date the house has not earned, because "never assert the unproven" reaches a candidate (finding 19.11). The employee area, the lockers, and the uniform are benefits the record already pays in kind and the charter's commitments carry them (S2 P10.1).
