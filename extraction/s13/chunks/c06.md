## 14. The compensation conversation, and where a pay question lands

### 14.1 No pay event, so no conversation about one

Her compensation conversation follows a review and delivers a change or its absence (Book p.416). At Sŏn there is no change to deliver: a weight moves on a form's date and the pay page shows it; a marker turns on when the window's conditions are met and the page shows it; the pool rises and falls with the nights and the page shows it daily. There is no raise, no bonus, no refresh, no merit cycle, and no moment at which a lead tells a person what they will earn next year. What exists instead is three things her guide is reaching for, each already a mechanism: the once-per-person explanation, the daily statement, and the destinations of a question.

### 14.2 The once-per-person explanation: the offer conversation and the mechanics module

Her "educate yourself" (Book p.414) is a requirement on every lead and every founder who hands item 6: they can explain the mechanics in their own words and answer a candidate's question about them without a figure beyond the sheet (S6 7.1, module 6). The compensation mechanics module (I8) is the form: completed on scheduled shift by every person in onboarding as part of the training plan's paid time, and by every lead and the founders before the first interview they run, teaching the mechanics page and the pay page and nothing about anyone's pay. The offer conversation (S6, `86akh9te2`) is where a person first hears their own numbers, from the sheet, in the correspondence register, with item 6's mechanism already in their hands from the first interview; it is the one time a lead speaks a figure, and the figure is the sheet's.

### 14.3 The daily statement

Her "instill trust in your systems" (Book p.415) is the pay page (13.1). Her "Am I being treated fairly?" is answered by the arithmetic every morning and her "Am I being recognized for my performance?" by the weight moves and the markers on the same page (Book p.414). No conversation is scheduled to say either.

### 14.4 Where a pay question lands

The check-in refuses pay (S4 P15), and this page reads that refusal exactly: the check-in refuses a pay negotiation, a pay promise, and a pay figure spoken by the lead. It does not refuse the path, and a question about the path (what unlocks next, what a marker needs, which seat after this one) is the third question's and the career conversation's, answered from the sheet and the fluency record with no figure spoken, because the page shows the figure. Every other pay question has a destination that is not the check-in, on the destinations page (S10 I3):

A question about the arithmetic of one's own share (why was Tuesday's share what it was): the pay page first, which shows the composition; then the correction path (16.6) if a record is wrong.

An error (a day not counted, an unlock not moved, a marker not on): the pay correction path, an item to the Operations Lead's domain, because "Cash, payroll, HR, compliance... in software" is that domain's (WP p.22), with the channel's window, acknowledged with an owner within seven days and corrected or declined with a written reason within thirty (S4 P30), and the correction paid on the next pay date. An error is a capture about a mechanism and never a question about a person.

A question about the rule (why is the weight what it is, why one pool per day): the mechanics page first; then the channel, as an item about a mechanism with an owner and a window (S11 6.8), which the leads' review reads and the reset may change by versioned decision.

A question about what another person earns: no destination, because a pay page is the person's; the rule is the answer (12.4).

A question about what one could earn: the path, at the check-in or the career conversation, as above.

### 14.5 What a lead may and may not say about pay

May say: the mechanics in their own words; what the sheet says the seat's wage and weights are, at the offer; that an unlock was recorded and the weight moves on that date, at the check-in where the form is read (S8 P37); where a question goes; that they do not know and will find out, which is her own "if you don't know the answer to a question, say that" (Workbook p.111) kept whole. May not say: a figure not on the person's own page or the sheet; another person's pay or share; a promise or a prediction of the pool, a marker, or an unlock's date; any sentence linking the review, a check-in, a designation, or a recognition to pay; "you'd earn more if" beyond the path the sheet shows; and any sentence from her disappointed-person script, "the system will not provide additional rewards if the contribution hasn't been high enough" (Book p.418), because the system has no such lever and a lead who implies one has invented a rating.

### 14.6 Her "managing disappointment," answered

A person disappointed by their pay at Sŏn has three destinations and no conversation to be managed in: the arithmetic (an error, to the correction path), the path (an unlock or a marker, at the check-in from the sheet), and the rule (an item on the channel). Her reframe, "How can we work together to make sure that six months from now, you feel that your compensation and perceived impact... are more closely aligned?" (Book p.418), survives as the career conversation's track and the check-in's path read, with the pay half removed: what would move the weight is on the sheet, and the lead names what the building must supply for the person to reach it, which is dimension 4. Her example line that compares a person's increase to a share of the company is refused with its number (Book p.418, md 295). Her "it may be that they really want to talk about getting promoted" (Book p.418) is the seat after this one, on the person's own path line, and the ready-now pool's order, which the person can see.

**P41 (Recommended).** There is no compensation conversation because there is no pay event; her guide's functions are carried by the offer conversation, the compensation mechanics module completed on shift by every person and by every lead and founder before their first interview, and the pay page. The check-in refuses a pay negotiation, promise, and figure, and answers the path from the sheet with no figure spoken.

**P42 (Recommended).** Every pay question has a destination on the destinations page: the pay page for the arithmetic, the correction path to the Operations Lead's domain with the channel's window for an error, the mechanics page and the channel for the rule, the path at the check-in or the career conversation for what one could earn, and no destination for another person's pay. ## 16. Decision rights for review and compensation

Every entry in the ledger's form: owner; range as may and may not; triggers, each with a named destination. An entry the record cannot furnish is marked incomplete and never filled by analogy.

### 16.1 Holding a review

Owner. The lead who holds the person's check-in: the Maitre d for the floor's seats, the Head of Beverage for beverage seats, the kitchen chef-gated. A lead's review: incomplete until `86akhb2jg` closes.

Range. May read the stack's assembly and the records it cites; may read the record aloud; may ask the four dimensions' states as facts; may ask the upward question and repeat it back; may extend the block when the window has slack; may move the block to the next shared shift when the window is short-crewed. May not write in the lead's own voice; may not rate, designate, nominate, or recommend; may not say anything not on a record the person can read; may not discuss a non-negotiable line; may not cite a coaching record, a count, a recognition, a readiness row, or any refused source; may not speak a pay figure or link anything said to pay; may not cancel a review; may not hold one mid-service, at the close, off the clock, or in a room the roster is in.

Triggers. A review marked "something new" goes to the leads' review as a candor line on the lead's entry and the item to a placed check-in. A review moved twice goes to the leads' review as a load reading (S4 P20's rule). A review that surfaces a non-negotiable goes to both leads that day (S4 P20). A review in which every dimension 4 item reads "not supplied" goes to the leads' review as an infrastructure line and to the diagnostic. A review that would discuss a breach ends as a review with the flag routed. Complete for the floor; the kitchen chef-gated; a lead's review incomplete.

### 16.2 Placing a review

Owner. The stack, on the person's own clock.

Range. May place the first review six months after the first paid hour and each review six months after the last; may place it in a pre-service window with slack or at a closed hour as a paid block; may re-place a moved review. May not place a review inside a ninety-day plan, before the career conversation, mid-service, at the close, or off the clock; may not place two reviews in one interval; may not place a review for a person whose check-in has no holder.

Triggers. A review that cannot be placed within the interval because the holder's windows are full goes to the leads' review as a load reading on the seat and the interval stretches, reported and never absorbed (S4 P19). Complete.

### 16.3 Writing the review record

Owner. The stack for the states; the person for their own half; no one for anything else.

Range. The stack may write the four dimensions' states with citation dates and nothing without a citation. The person may write anything in their own half in their own words, including nothing. The lead may write the person's words at the person's asking and read them back. No one may write a rating, a designation, a nomination, a summary, a hypothesis, a plan state, or a pay line.

Triggers. A record with an item lacking a citation is refused by the form. A record found with text in the lead's voice goes to the leads' review as a candor line and is re-written to the person's words. Complete.
