## 4. What the house protects by design, and what it never depends on a founder for

### 4.1 The protections already built, collected

The earlier sessions built the founders' protections one at a time, each for its own reason. Collected, they are one rule: the house protects a founder's attention by never needing it at the wrong moment, and never by asking a founder to guard it.

- **The absence rule.** "The founders can be absent for a full service period and no decision is deferred to their return" (S3 row 14, checked by both founders). The founding seat description's eighth field is the absence rule for each domain (S2 P7.1). A failed row 14 is node overload at the top of the web (S3 12.3).
- **Post-service destinations only.** "The structure's own escalation paths terminate at seats the structure says it must run without, which is consistent only if those paths are reached after service and never during it" (S8 finding 21.11). Every founder destination is post-service; a call deferred to a founder on the floor is a node-overload entry at the close (S8 18.8).
- **No founder as a default actor.** Every automated alert has a named actor "who is not a founder by default" (S3 row 11).
- **Hand-over dates.** The hiring partner's load hands to each lead six weeks before dinner's gate, dated on the first-run record (S9 P22; row 116). The why block hands to the internal voice's holder at the first reset (S7 P17). The interim holder's line has a closing condition (S15 P19).
- **A founder's impression is never a source, a trigger, or an override.** Row 118; S14's rule fixed as configuration (`17tn048qfp3`); a founder's no at the founder stage names a read or is not a no (S5 P53); a founder may not make a yes the lead declined (S5 12.4).
- **The seam protection.** No build meeting in the building during service hours; the founders' floor presence on the service metronome; the room's mechanisms frozen for dinner's first quarter (S4 2.7). "The hazard being protected against is the build habit of speed reaching the room" (ledger, S4).
- **The reserved classes at the status quo.** Nothing in Class A changes until both partners agree; a disagreement is a hold and not a deadlock (S3 4.4). A founder at zero bandwidth cannot be pressured into a reserved-class change by the other, because the default is that nothing changes.
- **The knowledge-transfer register.** Prompt B moved to the partners' page and read monthly (S4 9.2), so that what a founder knows is a dated to-do for the system and not a dependency.
- **The senior person speaks last.** For a founder in any room, and as the domain read's own form (S10 P26). A founder attending the leads' review by invitation speaks last; a founder's question on a domain page comes after the page.

**P14 (Recommended).** The protections above are collected as one rule on the mechanism map: the house protects a founder's attention by never needing it at the wrong moment, through the absence rule, post-service destinations, no founder as default actor, dated hand-overs, the impression rule, the seam protection, the status quo in Class A, the knowledge-transfer register, and the senior-speaks-last rule, and never by asking a founder to guard their own attention.

### 4.2 What the house never depends on a founder for

Stated as refusals, because each is a dependency an earlier session found the house about to acquire by default.

- A founder's memory. Anything the house needs that lives only in a founder is a prompt B item with a date (S1 Principle 1).
- A founder's presence on the floor. The room is held by the Maitre d or a room designation on every service (S8 P4, P25); the founders' presence runs on the service metronome and holds no range on the floor (section 4.3).
- A founder's approval mid-service. No destination during service is a founder (S8 21.11).
- A founder's reading of a person. Never a source, a trigger, or an override (row 118; S14).
- A founder's hours. No mechanism on the map lists a founder as its holder in service except the partners' cadence at closed hours; a mechanism that depends on a founder's hours the schedule cannot see is a node-overload line (section 8.7).
- A founder's voice in the room after the first quarter. The why block hands over; the concept's voice internally is a founder decision (`86akh2qq0`) and until it closes the founder continues and the reset records the load.
- A founder as the first everything past its date. The hiring partner, the interviewers, the translators, the module authors, the leads' check-in holders: each by default, each dated.
- The content engine's volume by heroic effort. The record sizes it by multiplication (WP p.30); a volume held by a founder's hours is cooling failure on the engine, read by the ratio.
- A founder's judgment of another founder. The domain read is questions on a page; the read on the center is a load line; neither is a judgment.

**P15 (Recommended).** The house never depends on a founder for their memory, their presence on the floor, their approval mid-service, their reading of a person, their hours, their voice in the room after the first quarter, their being the first holder of anything past its date, the content engine's volume by effort, or their judgment of another founder; each is a refusal on the mechanism map with the mechanism that carries it named.

### 4.3 A founder's external load, and a founder on the floor

**The external load, generically.** Every founder has commitments outside the house, and the house does not know their shape and does not ask. Reasoned structurally: a founder whose attention is split with commitments outside the house is a seat whose presence by hour is partial, and the record's answer to a partial seat is already written: the seat's shift shape is a field on its sheet (S8 2.5, field 11), the room at any hour is held by whoever is present, and a load finding is carried by a designation, the stack, or a procedure. So the founders' version is: each founder states, in prompt C of their working-with-me document, their presence by service period and by week as a fact, in the same form a seat's field 11 takes, including the consequences of commitments outside the house without naming them; the house reads that statement as a fact about availability, exactly as it reads field 11; no mechanism on the map may depend on a founder's presence outside the statement; and the read on the center reads the consequences on records (a partners' review moved, a live-risk state missed, a why block scheduled outside stated presence) and never the commitment. What the house must be able to do regardless: run every service, every brief and close, the leads' review, the check-ins, the stack's placements, and every in-service mechanism with every founder unreachable for a full service period (row 14) and, this page adds, for a full week of the in-service cadence with the partners' review held as a read of the record by whichever partner is present and every reserved class at the status quo (row 205). What the house must never depend on: a founder's hours outside the statement, a founder's reachability during service, or a founder's presence as a condition of any mechanism that is not the partners' own cadence. The same treatment applies to Dominic's and the chef partner's commitments outside the house: the page does not know or guess them, and prompt C is where each states the consequences.

**A founder on the floor.** Session 12 routed here whether a founder on the floor is bound by the two-kinds rule and the live-speech rule (S12 P15; `17tn048qcjz`). The answer is yes, and more tightly than a lead. The two-kinds rule: during a live service an operating instruction is the range's holder's to say to anyone doing that work, and feedback about a person is never said on the floor, at the pass, or in any room the roster is in. A founder on the floor holds no range on the floor: the server owns the table, the Maitre d owns the room (Canon 1.0, section 08), the designations hold the door, the sequence, and the room on absent services (S8 P25), and the founders' presence "runs on the service metronome" (ledger). So a founder on the floor is in the same mode as a lead rolling up their sleeves (S9; `86akhpvub`): a person doing the work inside a range someone else holds. A founder may make a live call only inside a range the founder holds, and the only way a founder holds a floor range during a service is to be named at that service's brief for a designation, which the designation's rotation floor and eligibility govern like anyone's (S10 P33). Otherwise a founder on the floor says nothing operating that is not the range holder's to say, corrects no one, sends no message about a person, and carries any hypothesis about a person to the lead who holds that person's check-in, never to the person (S12; `17tn048qcjz`). A founder who takes a call on the floor that a range holder should have taken is the deferred call at the close (S8 18.8), a node-overload entry on the founder's seat, and the founder's own Principle 4 run on the mechanism that let the call reach them. The Realist's read: the moment this bites is dinner's first quarter, when the build engine idles and the founders are in the room; a founder who has run opening service at a top room will read the floor faster than the cohort and will want to say so, and the rule exists for exactly that founder, because the cohort must learn to hold the room from the Maitre d and the designations, not from a founder's eye.

**How Brandon's seat holds the operating system without becoming its integration layer** (`86akh5uy9`). The owner of the operating system owns its record and its versions, not its running. The leads chair the leads' review; the stack places every check-in and review; the Maitre d and the designations hold the room; the reset corrects; the partners' review reads records the leads produced (S4 P58: the partners "do not attend the leads' review to learn what happened; they read it"). Row 15 reads whether the stack holds the integration work, row 16 whether every operating document has a version, owner, and last change, and the owner of the operating system is the checker of row 16 and not of row 15. The line between owning and integrating is the line between versioning a spine and running a meeting: a founder who chairs a leads' review, places a check-in by hand, or holds a lead's people has crossed it, and the load line reads the crossing.

**The cultural labor score.** Its definition is Brandon's to supply: the question it is built from, the population, and the rhythm (`86akh5u9x`). This page names the gap and supplies nothing. It notes one consequence for the read on the center: whatever the score reads about the team, it does not read the founders, and the read on the center does not wait for it.

**P16 (Recommended).** A founder on the floor is bound by the two-kinds rule and the live-speech rule more tightly than a lead: the founder holds no floor range unless named at the brief for a designation, makes a live call only inside a range they hold, corrects no one and messages no one about a person, carries any hypothesis to the person's check-in holder, and a call deferred to a founder is a node-overload entry on the founder's seat. A founder on the floor is a person doing the work inside a range someone else holds.

**P17 (Recommended).** A founder's commitments outside the house are a fact stated by the founder in prompt C as presence by period and by week in a field's form, read by the house as availability and never as a commitment, with no mechanism depending on presence outside the statement, and with the read on the center reading only the consequences on records. The house runs every in-service mechanism with every founder unreachable for a full service period and a full week of the in-service cadence, with every reserved class at the status quo. Brandon's seat holds the operating system as its record and versions and never as its running.

### 5.3 The proposed protection order, argued from the record

The record's order is the argument: "Bandwidth, then joy, then retention, then results" (WP p.17), and Principle 6, "bandwidth before results, founders included" (S1). Applied to one founder's time, the order says: protect first what keeps the seat's bandwidth above zero, because everything else the seat produces is produced from bandwidth; then what only the seat can do and that gates a date; then what only the seat can do and that can wait; then hand over or refuse the rest. The proposal follows that order and names what falls into each tier from the record and the ledger. It is a proposal about a seat's time. It is not a description of Brandon, and it is his to confirm, reorder, or rewrite.

**First: the seat's bandwidth itself, which at Sŏn means the record's own sizing and the dates.** Two things protect it and neither is will. The content engine runs on multiplication or it does not run at that volume (WP p.30); the proposal is that the engine's volume is read by the ratio and never held by hours, and that a week in which the ratio falls is a week in which the volume falls, not a week in which the founder works later. And the hand-over dates hold: the hiring partner's load to the leads six weeks before the gate, the why block to the internal voice at the reset, the leads' check-ins to whatever `86akhb2jg` rules. A founder who keeps a by-default load past its date to save the house the disruption of handing it over has spent bandwidth the record says comes first.

**Fourth: what his seat hands over or refuses.** Being the integration layer of the operating system (4.3); being a destination mid-service; holding the leads' check-ins past the ruling; being an alert's default actor; holding any of canon's specialist seats by default past the point at which a seat, a contract, or a session owner is named; the translations past the point at which translators are named and paid; the founder stage for edge seats past the reset.
