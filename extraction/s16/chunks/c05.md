## 8. The founders read as the house reads everyone, to the ceiling

Every session from 12 to 15 recorded nothing about a founder's own performance, pay, or departure and routed it here with one instruction: the operating agreement governs first, and the program never reads or writes it. This section designs the house's part to the ceiling one founder can reach and names what is the operating agreement's and counsel's at every step. The governing rule is the ledger's: nothing is assigned, reviewed, or escalated by a person standing above another, and the founders are the only seats above which there is no one. So the founders are read as the house reads everyone, minus every mechanism that needs a holder above, which leaves the domain read, the load line, the flag, the departure read, and the reserved classes.

### 8.1 The founders' own review, in whatever form survives

The person's review is six months on the person's own clock, held by the check-in holder, reading the four dimensions as states (S13 P3 to P11). A founder has no check-in holder, no seat sheet with an entry horizon, no competency rows, and no one who could hold a review without standing above. So the founders have no review, and the page refuses to build one under another name.

What survives, and reads the founders on three of the four dimensions in a form the house already runs. The domain read (S4 P53) reads each founder's domain monthly, in writing, against the readiness rows pre-opening and the goal pages in service, which is dimension 1 read on the domain and never on the person: the operating system's owner is read by rows 12 and 16 to 19 holding, and the stack's owner by row 15. The load line (3.3) reads dimension 4, what the building supplies, on the founding seat: what the house has failed to hand over, name, or carry, which is the building's failure and not the founder's. And dimension 3, conduct under the non-negotiables and inside the range, is read of a founder exactly as of anyone by the flag (8.2) and by the range: a founder deciding inside another founder's domain, taking a reserved-class decision alone, deferring a call to themselves mid-service, or speaking feedback about a person on the floor is a range breach, written on the partners' page by the other partner as a question, said at the next partners' review, and nothing in any room. None of this is a review. There is no interval, no block, no state written, no "something new" line, and no rating, and it reaches no pay, because the founders' pay is the operating agreement's and no review output reaches pay for anyone (S13 P23). The record's "feedback runs both directions" (WP p.19) reaches the founders through the leads' read on the center and the partners' lines on each other's pages, and stops there by design.

### 8.3 The absorber above the founders

"Management absorbs blame from staff" (WP p.17); Session 15 read the center as absorbing a lead's failure at the partners' review (S15 P11); and the founders' own failure has no absorber above them (S12 finding 18.5; S15 finding 13.7). The answer is that there is none, by design, and that the design still has to say where a founder's failure lands so that it lands on a mechanism and not on a person.

Three things absorb it, and none is a person above. First, Principle 4 run on the founders' own mechanisms: "the founders apply this to their own domains before anyone else's" (S1), which is the failure-mode reference run at the partners' review on the range the founders set, the carrier the reset named, the manual item left manual, the version not published, with an evidence line on the partners' page naming no person (S9 P3), exactly as S15 P11 runs it for a lead, and written by the other partner. Second, the record itself: the process update from the capture, the versioned decision, the read-back at the next scorecard (S9 P24 to P26); a founder's failure that is a mechanism's failure becomes a version, and the version is the absorption. Third, the other partners, in the reserved classes at the status quo: a founder's failure that is a Class A matter is held, not overruled, because "both or neither" means no partner can make the other's failure into a change the other did not agree to (S3 4.4).

What does not absorb it: the leads, who absorb blame from staff and never from a founder, because a lead absorbing a founder's failure is the load-bearing model with the weight reversed; the team; the public, through the content engine's transparency-first crisis rule (WP p.31), which is the brand's absorption of the house's failures toward the customer and not a founder's toward the house; and the operating agreement, which governs the partnership's consequences and is not the house's mechanism. The record's one founder post-mortem is public: "Sŏn has done it too, which is how the team knows the trap" (WP p.17), written in a diligence document while every other post-mortem is "private and process-focused" (WP p.17). The team reads that asymmetry as the absorber's shape: the founders' failures are absorbed by being written into the record, and the record is the only thing above the founders that the house holds (finding 15.7).

**P36 (Recommended).** There is no absorber above the founders by design. A founder's failure is absorbed by Principle 4 run on the founders' own mechanisms at the partners' review with an evidence line written by the other partner naming no person; by the record, through the process update, the version, and the read-back; and by the reserved classes held at the status quo. Never by a lead, the team, the public, or a person above.

### 8.7 The founders' version of "nothing before the first paid hour," and the founders' unpaid work

"Nothing is required of a person before their first paid hour, and nothing is owed off the clock" (ledger, S7 P15).
