## 7. Three times, three channels, made operational

### 7.1 The rule, and its proof

Session 3: important information travels spoken at the brief of every open period, written on the team home, and carried by the domain lead or mentor, and "A change that reaches one period's brief and not another's has not been communicated" (S3 P11.4). Session 8 fixed a structure change's four channels in order: the affected person's check-in, the sheet's version, the brief, the team home (S8 P49). This section makes both provable, because a rule about reach that no record can check is a good intention.

Every change to a range, trigger, goal, designation, mechanism, seat, line, or period carries a change-reach record (I5), written by the stack from records that already exist and by no one's hand: the entry's version date; the date of each affected person's check-in at which it was read, from the person page; the sheet's version date where a seat changed; the date and period of every brief whose record marks the change line spoken; and the team home's version date. A change has reached the house when the brief record of every period open since the version date marks it spoken and the team home shows it. A change has reached a person when their check-in line dates after the version, or, for a change that touches no one's seat, when the brief of a service they worked marks it. The third channel, the lead or mentor carrying it, is proven by the check-in date and by nothing else, because a conversation with no record is the record's tribal knowledge (WP p.13).

### 7.2 The order

The order is fixed and it is a rule about who hears first. For a structure change: the person, then the sheet, then the roster, then anyone (S8 P49). For a change that touches no person's seat: the entry, then every open period's next brief, then the team home, with the brief first because the roster reads the team home after it works and the brief is before. For a departure: the tension-loss row and the leads' review line, then the brief of every open period as coverage, then the inventory (S9 P30). For the one thing: the leads' review, then every brief that week, the same sentence. The stack places a structure change's check-in before the version publishes, so that the order is produced by the schedule and not by a lead remembering it; where the affected person's next shift is after the version must publish, the version waits, and a version that cannot wait is read as a load on the change's clock and not as a reason to skip the person.
