## 8. Language as an architecture constraint

### 8.1 The house's languages

The record's one language rule is at hiring: "The applicant system lets candidates... choose the interview language," and "a candidate whose first language is not English can interview with someone who speaks it" (WP p.17). Sessions 5 to 7 carried it forward: the seat sheet's field 14 names the languages the seat works in and the languages the process is available in (S5 I2), every posting states which languages the process runs in (S5 I3), the transparency sheet is handed in the candidate's interview language (S5), every stage runs in the language the candidate chose (S6 P23), every seat's module set exists in every language field 14 names before the seat's first start date (S7 P31, I11), the why block runs in the cohort's languages with an interpreter where needed (S7 P17), and a module missing in a person's language is a building fault on the training-infrastructure line (S7). The team home is read "in the languages the team speaks" (S3 P11.8) and the cadence page exists in them (S4 W9).

What none of those pages fixed is the list. "The languages the team speaks" is a fact about who is on the roster, and it changes when the roster does. This page names it: the house's language list is the union of field 14 across every open seat sheet, held on the team home's definitions page as a versioned entry, owned by the Operations Lead for the record and revised when a sheet's field 14 changes or a person's chosen language enters the roster. Pre-opening the list is the cohort's languages from the seat sheets and the founders' own; it is team-filled once the roster exists. A language enters the list when a seat works in it or a person on the roster chose it at hiring, and it never leaves the list while a person who chose it is in the house. The list is the constraint every surface below is tested against. Whether a language on the list beyond the ones a seat works in is a language the house selects for is answered in section 15: never. A person's chosen language is a fact the house serves and not a fact about the person it records anywhere but field 14 and the person's own page.

### 8.2 Every written surface, in every house language

Every page on the team home, the destinations page, the architecture page, the cadence page, every seat sheet, every module, the goal pages, the register, the definitions, the change entries' reason lines, and the feedback channel's acknowledgements exist in every language on the list, under one version number, before the page publishes. A page that exists in one language when the list has two has not been published; the change-reach record reads it as not reached (section 7.4). The person page, the check-in line, the capture, a recognition, and a channel item are written in the writer's own language and are not translated for the house, because they are a person's own words and the house does not rewrite them; the lead who cannot read a person's check-in line in the person's language has a check-in they cannot hold, and that is a language on the lead seat's field 14 or a language pairing rule for check-ins, section 8.5.

### 8.3 The spoken surfaces: the brief, the slot, the gathering

The brief runs in the languages of tonight's roster (S4 P10). The rule that makes it hold at tempo: the room's owner speaks the languages the room's owner holds; the readiness block is on the OS surface in every roster language, so the block is read by anyone in their own language whether or not it was spoken in it (S4 P10, section 6.1); and where tonight's roster holds a language the room's owner does not, one roster member who holds both languages speaks the block and the change line in it, on the clock, as a designation for the service named at the brief like the room, the door, the sequence, and the pass (S8 P25), with the designation's rotation floor (S10 P33). The interpreter designation is a floor act and never a customer-facing one; the person holding it is not the house's translator and writes nothing. The room's owner confirms on the brief's record that the block and the change line ran in every roster language, and a service on which they did not is sequence corruption on the window, not on the interpreter. The Realist's condition: on a compressed window, the thirty-second block is spoken in the owner's language and read on the surface in the others, and that counts as the block having run, because the record on the surface is the equalizer and the spoken word is its reading. The slot's items and a recognition are read in the language the room's owner holds, and a recognition written in a language the owner does not hold is read by the interpreter designation. The gathering, if approved, runs under the same rule, with the center speaking and the interpreter designation for each roster language present.

The check-in runs in the person's language (S4 P15). Where the lead does not hold it, section 8.5.

### 8.4 Translation: who writes, who approves, and canon's terms

The house writes every page's source in one language and translates it. Who writes the translation: a team member who holds the language, paid for the work on the module authorship model (WP p.19; S7 P33), which is Session 13's to price and this page names as a paid act and never an unpaid favor asked of a bilingual server. Who approves: the page's owner approves that the translation says what the source says, by reading a back-translation or by a second holder of the language reading both; a second holder of the language approves that it reads; and the version number is one across languages, so a source change re-opens every language version and no language version is ever a version behind. A translation drafted by software is a draft only, published after a holder of the language has read it against the source and marked the page with the disclosure line canon uses for AI-assisted work (Canon 1.0, section 10), which is the team's reading of a canon rule written for imagery applied to text, and section 20 files it. Until the OS surface, the translation record (I2) is a page on the team home listing every page, its language versions, the translator, the approver, and the version, a build. Pre-opening the founders and the first leads are the translators of the founding pages, which puts the languages the founders and leads hold on the critical path of the cohort's first shift, and the hiring calendar reads it (S6 3.9).

Canon's Korean terminology rule: "Korean terminology stands without explanation in all brand contexts from day one. No parenthetical translations, no footnotes, no italicized definitions" (Canon 1.0, section 04; Deck 3.0, p.45), and "Translation never appears" (Canon 1.0, section 04). Canon's terms stand untranslated in every language version of every internal page: Jeong, Nunchi, and the rest of canon's list are the same word in every version, in the Latin form canon gives, with Hangul where canon's rules place it. What an internal page may do is what the module standard does (S7 I11): say what a term asks of a seat without defining the term, in every language, as the team's reading held on canon's owner under the deferred Korean Terminology Deployment Rules (Canon 1.0, What Remains; Deck 3.0, p.91; `86akh7r2t`; S7 finding 19.6). This page adds one thing to the hold: whether an internal page is "a brand context" at all is the same question S5 marked for a candidate's document (S5 P22), asked now for a team member's page, and the two are one question for canon's owner. A person who does not hold Korean meets a term they cannot look up on the house's own pages; the house's answer until the ruling is that the term is taught in person, at the why block and by the mentor, and never glossed in writing, which is the record's "felt knowledge, not recited description" (Canon 1.0, section 01) applied to the vocabulary. The two canon versions list different term sets (section 20), and the internal pages use Canon 1.0's longer list until reconciled, because the longer list is the one that cannot be wrong by omission.

## 9. Canon on internal surfaces

### 9.1 The register the team reads, held for canon's owner

Canon governs every word under this name, applies the language constant to "any internal communication" (Canon 1.0, section 01; Deck 3.0, p.5), and applies the lexicon "in all contexts, no exceptions" (Canon 1.0, section 09; Deck 3.0, p.57). Its register matrix names six surfaces, the dinner menu, the lunch menu, short-form digital, longer-form digital, in-space copy, and correspondence (Canon 1.0, section 09; Deck 3.0, p.55), and none is internal. Session 5 read a register for candidate surfaces and marked it held (S5 P22, finding 15.5). This page reads one for employee-facing surfaces and marks it held on the same question, for canon's owner (`86akh7r2t`), as one ruling with the candidate's.

The team's reading, surface by surface. A house-written internal page (the team home, a sheet, a goal page, the register, the destinations page, a change entry's reason line, a channel acknowledgement) is correspondence's cousin: it "names what it knows specifically. Confirms without reselling. No hospitality-speak" (Canon 1.0, section 09), with "we" as the house's voice and "you" to the person, because the alternative is a third-person page to a team member and canon's "You" rule is written for the customer and does not forbid the person (S5 P22's reading, extended). Every house-written sentence passes the six binary tests (Canon 1.0, section 09; Deck 3.0, p.54), and test five, whether the sentence could appear unchanged in another house's handbook, is the one that does the work on internal pages, because almost every sentence of an industry handbook could. The brief is speech read from a record and speaks the record's words; canon's one rule on speech, that "welcome" and "enjoy" are "acceptable as in-person service language only" (Canon 1.0, section 09), is the whole of canon's spoken register, and the brief inherits the written rules for everything read from a record and canon's in-person allowance for nothing, because the brief speaks to the team and not to a customer. The check-in is a conversation and canon does not script conversations; its one written line is the person's.

Canon's voice rules bind the house's internal writing the way Session 3 said: "the forbidden lexicon and the six binary tests are what keep internal writing short enough to be read between services" (S3 P11.5). Rule 1, copy never names its own effect, removes "a check-in that helps you grow." Rule 2 removes "our dedicated team." Rule 3 ends "the brief runs every service without exception" at "service." Rule 4 means a page does not explain why the check-in is paid; it says the check-in is paid. Rule 5 means "we take feedback seriously" is out and "an item is acknowledged with an owner in seven days" is in. The standing rules of this program, no performed conviction and no em dash, are canon's own on the internal page.

### 9.2 A peer's own words, and finding 20.10

Session 10 found that canon's lexicon applies in all contexts and the record's peer recognition is written by the team "in the moment," and read the language constant as applying and the lexicon as not, held (S10 finding 20.10). This page holds the same reading and extends it to every surface a person writes in their own words: the recognition, the check-in line, the capture's free field, and a channel item. The reasoning: canon's lexicon is a rule about "a brand performing itself" (Deck 3.0, p.57), and a server thanking a runner is not the brand performing; the language constant is a rule about what the customer is called, and it reaches every mouth in the house because the record's own reason for it is that "Language shapes culture" (WP p.25). So a person's own words carry the customer word and nothing else of canon's, and a recognition that says a colleague was "amazing" is read at the brief as written. Exclamation points and emoji in a person's own words fall under the same hold: canon forbids them "in any written brand voice context" (Canon 1.0, section 09) and a peer's words are not brand voice. What is house-written on the same surfaces, the platform's labels and prompts, the channel's states and the acknowledgement's text, the brief's record, carries the lexicon in full, no exclamation, no emoji, sentence case.

Two things stay canon's owner's, and this page states its reading on each: whether a person's own words on a house surface are a brand surface at all (the team reads no); and whether a room's owner reading a peer's words aloud at the brief makes them the house's speech (the team reads no, because the owner is a reader, and section 5.1's refusals govern what is read).

### 9.3 Sentence case, code names, and the customer word, inward

Sentence case always, on every internal page, label, heading, and record field (Canon 1.0, section 01; Deck 3.0, p.28); uppercase for a single-line eyebrow only, which on an internal page is a section label at most. No daypart code name on any internal surface, because the internal names canon lists are brand names for a spin-out and one of them is "Never public-facing" (Canon 1.0, section 01; Deck 3.0, p.6), and a name that is never public-facing on a page the whole house reads on personal phones is a name that will be public. Service periods are named by time of day on every internal page and at every brief. The customer is the customer on every internal surface and in every mouth, and this page repeats the record's reason so it is on the architecture page in the team's languages: "Customer keeps the transaction visible... The accountability is clear and it runs one direction, toward them. This matters for belonging, because belonging requires honesty" (WP p.25).

**P23 (Recommended).** The team reads canon's register for employee-facing surfaces as correspondence's cousin for every house-written page, with "we" as the house and "you" to the person, every sentence under the six binary tests with the competitor test run on each; the brief as speech reading a record's words; the check-in as a conversation canon does not script.
