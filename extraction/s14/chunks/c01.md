## 1. Her ask against the record, and how much is decided

### 1.1 What she asks

Her thirty pages are three playbooks. For high performers: know whether a person is a pusher or a puller and manage each type; anticipate when their work will bore them and "say the thing you think you cannot say"; find them opportunity inside the organization, restructure teams to make room, consider giving them your job, and if none of that works "help them find it elsewhere"; spend most of a manager's time on the highest long-term return; hold pre-exit interviews with the top share of talent once a year and extensive retrospectives when one leaves; keep them uncomfortable; and, when they are not ready to be promoted, "own it" and name what is missing (Book pp. 419 to 431). For the steady middle: accept that "given enough time" a company shows "the entire normal curve of performance," treat the team "as a portfolio of talent" in which "each person... should be assessed and either supported, coached to better performance, or moved," recognize that some teams "must deliver similar work, consistently well," allow a "terminal" level, give medium performers development plans, seek their feedback, and celebrate them with "verbal recognition and special assignments" rather than compensation (Book pp. 431 to 432). For low performers: notice when one-off feedback "has become a pattern"; determine skill, will, or wrong job; involve HR and be aware of legal protections; give no surprises; document every conversation in writing to the person and to HR; be compassionate by being direct; move deliberately inside a stated number of months; form a hypothesis on the outcome before starting; get aligned in one to three conversations by stating the pattern and that the person is not meeting the role's expectations, handling denial with more examples and relief with three questions; agree next steps by the third conversation whether or not the person agrees; write a performance improvement plan with skills to change, an end date, and a measure, tuned to whether the fix is operational, a skills fit, or behavioral; and end in one of three outcomes, the person improves, moves, or leaves, with leaving deferred to managing out (Book pp. 432 to 449). The workbook adds a plan template that is "a signed, standalone document" carrying role expectations, areas for improvement with dated examples, goals and deliverables, milestones, resources, weekly progress checks, an evaluation date, a clause that the manager may end the plan at any time, an at-will clause, a warning that failure "may result in termination," a "must maintain this level" clause, and two signatures (Workbook pp. 118 to 122), and three letters in the manager's voice: a progress update, a getting-back-on-track letter, and an insufficient-progress letter (Workbook pp. 114 to 116).

Every page assumes a manager who assigns work, rates people, retains the best by pay, promotion, and visibility, manages the middle by expectation-setting, and moves the bottom through a documented case in the manager's own voice toward recovery or exit, with an HR team beside them. The session task tests one of those assumptions by name, "three tiers," and section 1.5 decides it.

### 1.6 The reweighting, stated and argued

Her proportions are refused. She gives the top a third of the chapter, the bottom half, and the middle one page, and the proportion is a fact about her industry: in a company where the top decile of engineers or dealmakers produces the outsize result, the manager's return on attention is at the two tails, and the middle is where attention is not needed. The session task and carryover `86ajgmmpg` say the proportion does not hold on a floor, and the record says why, in five places that are not about performance at all.

Consistency is the product. The record's founding argument for its own kitchen technique is that "the tenth cup is not the first" and that the method's whole value is to "raise the floor and hold it" (WP p.4). Canon makes the same thing a cultural commitment and names inconsistency as the thing that breaks memory (Canon 1.0, section 01). A customer's fifth visit depends on the same room, the same service, the same plate as the first four (WP p.25), and that sameness is produced by whoever holds the seat on the night, which on most nights is a person holding a horizon and not moving past it.

The web has no altitude and no hierarchy of importance. The record's structural claim is that "every position matters because the sequence is the mechanism" (WP p.8) and that distributed anchors beat concentrated strength (WP p.9). A house that reads its own people through her proportions has re-introduced the concentrated anchor by another name: the top performer as the load-bearing column. The record's whole Part I is the argument against that column.

The horizon held is a full seat. Session 13 fixed that a person who holds a horizon and never unlocks earns the horizon's weight and the pool's growth and is not "partially meets" (S13 12.3). That is a pay decision and a structural one: the seat's horizon is what the house asked for, what the person was hired and validated on, and what the schedule relies on; holding it is the job done, not the job half done.

The tempo test. On a Friday with a full book, a short station, and a green runner, the room is held by the people who hold their seats at the horizon on their mise alone, and the person moving past their horizon is the one the schedule can least afford to have coaching a peer mid-service. The Realist's line is that reliability holds a line and brilliance does not: a rotating hourly crew is the baseline, and a design that survives it is a design whose middle is deep, not whose top is bright.

The labor market. "Austin's restaurant labor market is tight" (WP p.16), and the record's answer is not to win the top of it but to build the environment "a 22-year-old server six months into the industry" can succeed in (WP p.17). That server, in their second year, holding the seat at its first unlock and not chasing the second, is the person the record was written for and never describes.

So the page's weight is section 2. What follows for the two tails is short by design: the person moving past a horizon is carried by mechanisms nine sessions already built (section 3 names them and adds two rules), and the person whose work is not holding is carried by one new mechanism with a narrow trigger, a bounded window, and a stated end (section 5), because the house has already done, before anyone's name is spoken, the work that in her company the performance plan does.

**P1 (Recommended).** Her high, middle, and low performer material lands on a record that treats every seat as essential, reads failure at the process first, advances people by a system rather than a manager, pays by the seat's horizon and the days, and describes no act by which the house ends anyone's employment; and on thirteen sessions that fenced every surface a tier, a label, a list, or a case could live on. What this page adds is the account of what the house owes a person who holds a seat, the two rules that keep the path from becoming a favor, and the horizon window as the one designed destination for work that is not holding after the building's fixes.
