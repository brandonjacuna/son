## 8. Moves, not verdicts

### 8.1 What a move is

A move is a change of seat, strand, period, or horizon that a person makes, from what is open, with the lead who holds their check-in, on the internal loop and the domain and period plans that already exist (S6 P69; S7 P30). It is not an outcome a lead assigns, not a consequence a window imposes, and not a demotion, because the web has no altitude to fall from (WP p.9). Her "move the employee to another team" (Book p.438) and her "use hypothesis-based coaching to help identify what roles are a better fit... and how to seek them out" (Book p.448) survive as the person's reading of the inventory, the tracks, and the pool, with the lead stating what is open and nothing about fit.

### 8.2 The four moves

A seat change inside the domain. A server to a host seat, a runner to a server seat where the readiness form reads ready at the receiving seat's entry horizon, on the internal loop: the pool read, the receiving seat's paid practical in a real service, and the dimension-4 conversation with the decision on the form (S6 P69). The check-in holder does not change on the floor, because the Maitre d holds both strands (S8 P21).

A strand or period change. The other strand's readiness form under the same domain (S8 P35), or a seat in another open period under the period plan (S7 P30), where the sheet's shift shape or register fits the person better than the one they hold. The record's own account of the two strands is that "the best outward-facing operators may be mediocre in the inward-facing role, and the reverse" (WP p.11), which is the record saying, in structure, that a person whose seat is not holding may be holding the wrong strand. Her example of the customer-facing person who prefers "the analysis, process, and strategic aspects" (Book p.449) is the inward strand's track.

A domain change. The domain plan on the receiving domain's sequence with its lead and mentor (S7 P30), kitchen chef-gated, a beverage seat under the Head of Beverage, the receiving domain's assessors scoring (S8 P39).

A return to an earlier horizon. Where a person holds a seat at its first unlock and the window's form reads not yet at that horizon but the rows at the entry horizon hold, the person may return to the entry horizon on the same seat: the weight moves on the form's date as any unlock moves it (S13 P29), which is a pay move on a record and never a pay lever; the next unlock opens again on the sheet's period; and nothing about the return is a mark. The team recommends the return as a move the person chooses and never as a lead's act, and marks it founder-gated as a pay question the architecture did not anticipate (12.4).

### 8.3 What a move is bounded by, and what it is never

Bounded by what is open: a move needs an open seat, a strand with a form, or a period with a charter, and the house holds no seat open for a move, because a hole is held only against the bar (S5 9.1). Bounded by a count: the person chooses inside a stated count of services after a not-held close, a reset parameter, because her "it should be time-bound" (Book p.449) is right and a choice left open is the loose edge. Bounded by the practical: a person moving to a critical-path seat takes its practical in a real service like anyone from the pool (S5 P61). Never: a move to a seat that does not exist, invented to keep a person, because a seat is a structure change with a reason line that is never a person (S8 17; S9 P26); a move a lead makes for the person; a move the window's block records as a verdict; a "less demanding role" as a manager's response to a personal hardship (Book p.433), because the house asks about no one's life (S9 P44) and a shift shape or period change is the person's to ask for at any check-in as a schedule need (S4 4.3), on the clock and with nothing written about why.

### 8.4 Her skill-will "change team members," decided

Session 9 refused the matrix as a read of anyone and routed its "change team members" quadrant here and to Session 15 (S9 P40; `86akhpvqx`). Decided: refused as a diagnostic outcome, a lead's act, and a category. What survives of its function is the move as the person's choice after a not-held close, and the partners' decision under Session 15's design where no move is open. "Change team members" as a manager's prescription for "low skill, low will" does not exist, because "will" is read by no one (S12 P5), and a seat that does not hold is a seat question and a horizon question before it is a person question.

### 8.5 Retiring a seat is never a departure, and a designation is not performance

Carryover `86akhcz7b`: the partners may retire a seat whose row has held no person for a stated interval and whose tension the adjacent rows have re-established, and may not retire a seat whose holder is in it (S8 18.2).
