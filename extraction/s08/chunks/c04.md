## 11. Cross-strand fluency defined, and cross-period fluency placed

### 11.1 What a strand is

A strand is the set of seats in one domain that share one primary output and one intelligence (WP p.10 to 11). The floor has two: outward-facing (server, host; output the experience at the table; intelligence relational, expressive, attuned under load) and inward-facing (runner; output the operational sequence; intelligence architectural, coordinated, timing-precise). Beverage has the same two (WP p.11). The kitchen's strands are chef-gated. A strand is not a level, and "an outward-facing server is not a more senior inward-facing server" (WP p.11).

### 11.2 Cross-strand fluency, defined so it can be demonstrated

The record's definition is a capacity: "the ability to hold both at once," "the rare person who can manage a table's emotional temperature while absorbing a coordination failure invisibly" (WP p.11). A capacity is asserted; a marker must be demonstrated. The team's definition, in three parts, all recorded and none scored:

1. *Certified on both strands.* The person holds a readiness form reading ready (S7 I7) for a seat on each strand in the same domain, each scored by that strand's assessor in a real service, so that both strands' entry-horizon competencies are validated by the strand that holds them. Two forms, two dates.
2. *Exercised on both.* The person has held a seat on each strand on scheduled real services inside a stated window, the quarter recommended, so that the fluency is current and not archival. The stack reads it from the schedule.
3. *Held at once.* The record's sentence names a moment, not a test, and the team declines to build a test for it, because a staged moment reads a performance and the shift close already captures the real ones. What the record's moment looks like on the page is a shift-close capture in which a coordination failure was absorbed without a recovery, written at the edge by the people who felt it (S3 shift close), on a service where the person held a seat. It is read, not scored, and it is the qualitative side's own source (WP p.23).

Fluency is recorded on the person page as connections: two strand connections in one domain, with their dates and the window's currency. It is not a dimension, not a rank, not a rating, and not a title, and it is the marker Session 13 prices (`86akh3wjw`). It is demonstrated through parts 1 and 2 and evidenced by 3; a person with 1 and 2 holds the marker.

**P35 (Recommended).** Cross-strand fluency is defined as two readiness forms, one per strand in the same domain, each scored by that strand's assessor in a real service, plus a seat held on each strand on scheduled services inside a stated window. It is recorded as connections on the person page, never as a dimension or rank. The record's "held at once" is read from shift-close captures, not staged. Session 13 prices it.

### 11.3 Cross-period fluency, placed

Session 5 named cross-period fluency as a marker parallel to cross-strand fluency and routed its placement here (S5 P62, `86akh7qxy`). It has the same shape one step over: a readiness form under the period plan (S7 I15) for a seat in a second period, and a seat held in each period on scheduled services inside the window. **P36 (Recommended).** Cross-period fluency is placed as period connections on the person page, demonstrated by a period-plan readiness form and a seat held in each period inside the window, sitting on the inventory's period tags and the sheet's field 4.
