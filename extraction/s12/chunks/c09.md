## 19. The session task's answer

**The coaching model, on one page.** A lead at Sŏn coaches by hypothesis, and a hypothesis is a sentence of one shape: a behavior in a service and its impact on the work. The lead forms it from records the person can already read, their own check-in lines, the plan block, the readiness rows, the captures they wrote for their own tables, the range-used count, the domain's dated fixes, the schedule, and from the lead's own unwritten observation, which is the weakest source because the person cannot read it; never from a recognition, a channel item, the diagnostic, a scorecard, a mentor, a peer, or a note. The hypothesis has no page. It lives in the lead's head between the records that prompted it and the check-in at which it is said, and if the lead cannot hold it until the next shift the two share, the lead asks the stack to place the check-in there. At the check-in, after the person's own account of what is working and what is in the way, the lead asks an open-ended question about a specific service, and then, if needed, says the observation once: the situation, the behavior, the impact, owned as the lead's perception, in the person's language, and asks what the person sees. Solutions come after the shared problem. What is written is one line in the person's words, read back: what they will do differently, what the building must supply, or nothing about the hypothesis at all. A wrong hypothesis writes nothing. A hypothesis the person declines is said again only against a record that has changed. It may never become a verdict, a word on the page in the lead's voice, a dimension or evidence at the review, a diagnostic read, a task, a pay line, a designation withheld, a reason in a change, a cause on a capture, or a citation in any decision about the person's staying, because nothing exists to cite. A hypothesis said at consecutive check-ins with the records unchanged after the building's fix and no line from the person naming it is the pattern Session 9 defined, and it goes to Session 14 with the person's lines and the records they were read against. The lead never says a trait, a quadrant, a motivation question, "disengaged," or "feedback is a gift." A not-yet row is read in the assessor's words with nothing added. A designation holder is coached about the range. A failure is typed at the capture review as basic, complex, or intelligent, on the process and never on a person, and only a basic failure recurring on one seat after its placement is fixed reaches a check-in. A non-negotiable breach is never coaching. Most check-ins carry no hypothesis. The kitchen's is the chef's to ratify, recommended unchanged.

**The feedback culture, in one paragraph.** Candor at Sŏn moves in every direction and lands on a record every time: lead to person at the check-in and in the live call about the work; person to lead at the fourth question in the person's words and on the channel with an owner and a window, with a concern about the check-in's holder undestined until the founders name one; peer to peer as a recognition of an act in the peer's words read at the brief, and as a spoken word between services about the work that the house neither builds a surface for, asks for, nor records; and to and from a designation's holder on the holder's check-in and the capture. During service the only speech about a person's work is the range's call on the object, complete when the act is done; feedback about the person waits for a check-in the lead can bring forward to the next shared shift, and the person's own account is on the record that night before any lead's hypothesis has formed. Praise publicly is a peer's recognition; criticize privately is the check-in; team feedback in public is the goal page's state and the one thing. The house asks for feedback at every check-in so that no lead has to model asking, and asks no one to give it. No room hears a correction of a person, no page holds a word about a person the person did not say, and no lead performs candor or warmth; the culture is the property of those mechanisms and not a disposition the house requests.

**The task's premise, answered.** The task predicted direct transfer, that hard feedback fast and in the moment is native to a kitchen and a floor. It is half right, and the page keeps the right half whole. What a floor and a pass are native to is the live call about the work, and the house keeps it under one rule: its subject is the object of the work and its verb is a present act. What a kitchen is also native to, the correction of a person in the line's hearing, is not fast feedback but blame in the moment the system should have absorbed, and the non-negotiables refuse it before this page does. Her hard-feedback conversation transfers nearly whole as a way of talking and is placed at the check-in, brought forward by the stack when it cannot wait, so that "fast" at Sŏn is a sequence: the work corrected in the second, the process that night, the person's own account that night, the conversation at the next shared shift. The one thing the ledger and the premise could not both hold, Session 4's "said in person between check-ins" against Session 10's "the check-in's," is surfaced for the founders with the team's narrowing recommended and the alternative stated. What she gets right, that the hypothesis is said soon, said as a perception, and said before it is written, the house keeps, and strengthens the last: it is written nowhere at all.

**P48 (Recommended).** The session task's answer as stated: the coaching model as a hypothesis with no page, tested at the check-in in the person's language and written only in the person's words; the feedback culture as candor with a record in every direction and no room; and the direct-transfer premise answered as two transfers, the live call whole and the conversation placed.
