### 10.3 The deliverability test, specified, and the candidates run through it

Her test is that a principle be "truly expressed in how we hire, reward, lead, and conduct ourselves" (Book, via `86akh3wxv`). For coaching the test is narrower and this page states it: a principle can be a feedback reference only if it names a behavior a service produces, observable from the room's owner's coverage position or on a record the person can read, in one service, without inferring a state of the person. A principle that names a house commitment, an operating rule, or a founder's stance fails as a feedback reference and is not wrong as a principle; it goes back to the charter to be marked as a commitment or a rule rather than dropped.

Run on the seven candidates. Authority lives in the domain: observable, as a range used or an escalation made on a trigger; a feedback reference. The first question is what allowed this, not who failed: observable, as a capture that names a process and not a person; a feedback reference for the writer of a capture. Management absorbs blame: observable only on a lead's record (the process fix's owner); a feedback reference for a lead and not for an hourly seat, which is Session 15's and `86akhb2jg`'s. No hierarchy of importance: observable, in the table alignment, the phone, the station's state; a feedback reference. Never assert the unproven: observable at the table, in a server speaking of a dish with felt knowledge or not (Canon 1.0, section 01, Mahk); a feedback reference. Pay for what matters: a house commitment; not observable in any person's behavior; fails as a feedback reference and goes back to the charter as a commitment. Advancement requires preparation, not permission: an operating rule of the system; not a person's behavior; fails as a feedback reference and goes back as a rule. Two of seven fail her test as feedback references and are flagged, and both are the charter's to re-mark rather than remove.

The test runs again at ratification and at the revision cadence (`86akh3trj`), and any principle Brandon adds that names no service behavior is flagged the same way. Canon's positioning words, "Korean restraint, Texas warmth, polished but playful" (Canon 1.0, section 01; Deck 3.0, p.3), are not principles and are never a feedback reference about a person, because "Texas warmth lives in the service, the room, and the voice" (Deck 3.0, p.3) and a lead who tells a server they are not warm enough has named a quality and not an act.

**P34 (Recommended).** Feedback references the range card, the seat sheet, the readiness rows and load read, the non-negotiables as the flag's, canon's service model as behavior, and the charter's principles once version one exists; nothing referenced in coaching is a fifth dimension. A principle is a feedback reference only if it names a behavior a service produces, observable from the coverage position or a record, without inferring a state of the person.

**P35 (Recommended).** Of Session 2's seven candidates, five pass the coaching test (authority lives in the domain; what allowed this, not who failed; management absorbs blame, for a lead only; no hierarchy of importance; never assert the unproven) and two fail it and go back to the charter to be marked as a commitment and a rule (pay for what matters; advancement requires preparation, not permission). ## 11. The non-negotiable breach: what this page closes

Session 10 found that "Blame absorption runs one way, and the one act canon treats as a person's has no path in the record," and routed it to the founders with Sessions 12, 14, and 15 (S10 finding 20.5; `86akht39y`). The flag is fixed: raised by anyone, to both leads, the same day (S7 P45; S4 P20); never said at the brief, the close, or any room (S11 5). What follows the flag is Sessions 14 and 15's and the founders'. This page closes what coaching can close, which is where the breach is not.

A non-negotiable breach is never a check-in item. The check-in refuses discipline (S4 P15), and a breach is the one act the record treats as a person's, so a lead who brings it to the check-in has put discipline in the one conversation built to exclude it. A breach is never a hypothesis: it is not a behavior and its impact but a line crossed, and a lead who says "I have a theory that you may have been" has softened a flag into a coaching act, which is the ruinous form. It is never typed at the capture review (section 8.2). It is never a placed check-in's reason (section 6.4). It is never on the person page as a line the lead writes, because nothing is written there by a lead but at a check-in in the person's words (S7 P25; S4 P20); what the record holds is "the leads' record" (S11 5) and a non-negotiable line on the person page whose existence Session 8 already relies on for eligibility (S8 P25, "no non-negotiable line on their page") and whose writer and form this page does not fill, because that is the destination question. The lead who receives the flag holds no check-in about it with the person; whatever conversation follows a flag is not a check-in and is not designed here. Where a check-in itself surfaces a breach, S4's trigger runs: to both leads that day (S4 P20), and the check-in ends as a check-in with the person's line about whatever else was said and nothing about the flag.

What stays open, stated: the destination after the flag and what the leads do with it (Sessions 14 and 15, the founders); who writes the non-negotiable line on the person page and in what form, which Session 8's eligibility rule needs (`86akht39y`, extended); a breach by a lead, which has no destination until `86akhb2jg` closes; and the definition of toxicity, which canon names and nowhere defines, so that the line between hard feedback and the thing the non-negotiable forbids is undrawn (finding 18.9).

**P36 (Recommended).** A non-negotiable breach is never a check-in item, a hypothesis, a typed failure, a placed check-in's reason, or a lead's line on the person page; the flag goes to both leads the same day and what follows is not coaching. A check-in that surfaces one ends as a check-in with the flag routed. The destination after the flag, the writer and form of the non-negotiable line, a breach by a lead, and the definition of toxicity stay with the founders and Sessions 14 and 15 (`86akht39y`, `86akhb2jg`, extended and not filed twice).

## 12. The load: the Maitre d's seat holds every floor check-in

The floor is one domain and the Maitre d is its lead for both strands, holding the check-ins of servers, hosts, and runners (S8 P21, P24); the floor manager and the Maitre d are one seat by Brandon's ruling (S8 section 2); a designation "holds nothing else the Maitre d's seat holds: no check-in" (S8 8.5). So every hypothesis on the floor is the Maitre d's to form and say, every hard row is the Maitre d's to read, every placed check-in is the Maitre d's window, every disagreement about a person on the floor arrives at the Maitre d twice, and every recognition decline is the Maitre d's conversation on the services the Maitre d works. The Realist's arithmetic from Session 4 governs: "The monthly rotation fits only if the number of people whose check-ins the Maitre d holds is not larger than the number of pre-service windows the Maitre d works in a month" (S4 4.5), and this page adds placed check-ins and extended check-ins to the windows the seat spends.

What it costs, without figures. Each placed check-in is a window the standing rotation did not need. Each extended check-in is minutes of a window that the brief's slot or a meal (S10 P12, founder-gated) would spend. Each hard conversation is the same fifteen minutes as an easy one on the schedule and is not on the schedule as harder, so the load is read only as counts.

What the bandwidth ledger reads. Session 9 placed the reset's first read at the Maitre d's seat from nine inputs (S9 P36). This page adds three counts to those inputs and no new read: placed check-ins per month; check-ins extended past fifteen minutes per month, as the lead's own read on the check-in record; and check-ins moved (S4's two-move and three-move triggers already run, S4 P20). Session 10's rule that a culture mechanism met at a bandwidth cost is cooling failure on the mechanism (S10 P34) applies: a coaching load that displaces the slot or the block is cooling failure on the check-in, read on the window's count, not a Maitre d who coaches too much.

Which carriers are permitted. Session 9's list under Brandon's ruling: "A load finding is carried by a designation, the stack, or a procedure; a second floor seat, a floor manager under any name, an assistant of any title, a permanent room holder, and a service manager are refused carriers" (S9 P36). For the coaching load specifically, a designation cannot carry it, because a designation holds no check-in (S8 8.5) and the check-in is the coaching surface. So two carriers remain. The stack: placement that spreads check-ins across the Maitre d's windows, the interval as a parameter, and the placed check-in fitting into the next shared shift rather than the next window. The procedure: the fifteen minutes, one hypothesis per check-in, the pre-read from records rather than from memory, and the rule that most check-ins carry no hypothesis. If those two do not carry it, the interval stretches and the stretch is reported "as a load reading on the lead, not absorbed" (S4 P19), and the reset reads the seat (S9 P36). The refused carriers stay refused: no assistant holds a check-in, no senior server holds a check-in under a designation's name, and no lead's coaching is delegated to a mentor (S10 P32). The kitchen's load is the chef's (S8 P12, the Station Lead count).

The one thing this page will not do is thin the check-in to fit. Fifteen minutes, monthly, on the clock, with the fourth question, is the floor of the mechanism (ledger, S4), and a load finding is answered at the seat and the schedule, never by making the conversation shorter than it is.

**P37 (Recommended).** The coaching load on the Maitre d's seat is read as three counts added to the reset's first read and no new read: placed check-ins, extended check-ins, and moved check-ins per month; a coaching load that displaces the slot or the block is cooling failure on the check-in. Its permitted carriers are the stack and the procedure only, because a designation holds no check-in; the refused carriers stay refused; the check-in is never thinned to fit.

**P49 (Team-filled).** The coaching load baseline: the placed, extended, and moved check-in counts per month from dinner's first quarter, written at the first house review beside Session 10's safety baseline (S10 P16), against which the reset's parameter for placed check-ins is set.

## 13. Language and canon in coaching

### 13.1 The person's chosen language

The check-in runs in the person's language (S4 4.3), which is the language chosen at hiring (WP p.17; S11 P17). A hypothesis is said in it, the records cited exist in it (S11 P17), and the line is written in it. Where the lead does not hold it, section 7.7 and S11 P20 apply. A lead seat's field 14 carries the domain roster's languages as a developed competency (S11 P20), which means the Maitre d's own path includes the floor's languages, and that is a training-infrastructure line on the Maitre d's seat and never a reason a person is coached less.

### 13.2 Canon's register for what a lead says and writes, held

Canon "does not script conversations; its one written line is the person's" (S11 9.1), so the check-in's speech is outside canon's register matrix and this page adds no register for it. What canon does reach: the language constant, "customer" in every mouth (Canon 1.0, section 01; Deck 3.0, p.5), which a lead uses in every hypothesis and a person's line carries (S11 P24); the master register's spirit, "present without performing," which this page reads as the rule against a lead's performed warmth or performed candor in a check-in ("I'm going to be really honest with you") and marks as the team's reading, held with the internal register for canon's owner (`86akh7r2t`; S11 P23); and the six binary tests on anything house-written about coaching, the feedback culture page (I1), the coaching module (I5), and any card a lead reads from, none of which may say "a conversation that helps you grow." What a lead writes: nothing, so canon's written rules reach the check-in only through the person's own line, which carries the language constant and nothing else of canon's (S11 P24).

### 13.3 Jeong and Nunchi: developed, never graded

Recruiting never selects for them (S5 3.4). Coaching may develop them and may never grade them. What that means at the check-in: a lead may say an observation about a service in which a table's need was read late or read wrong, as a specific act ("the water on nine came after they asked"), and may never say that a person lacks Nunchi, has good Nunchi, or is developing it, because Nunchi is "Not diagnostic inquiry" (Canon 1.0, section 01; Deck 3.0, p.15) and a lead diagnosing a person's Nunchi has done the thing canon says Nunchi is not. Jeong "cannot be scripted" and "requires time, repetition, and memory" (Deck 3.0, p.15), so nothing a lead says at a check-in can produce it and no hypothesis may be about it; a regular's return is the platform's and the book's record, not a person's mark. The felt-knowledge standard, "service speaks to a dish with felt knowledge, not recited description" (Canon 1.0, section 01), is the one canon standard coaching references directly, because it is observable at the table: a server describing a dish they have not tasted is an act, and the felt-knowledge block is the record (S7 I-felt-knowledge).

### 13.4 No performed conviction, and the Korean terms

No lead says "I believe in you," "we're invested in your success," or "feedback is a gift." The observation ends at its last load-bearing word. Canon's terms stand untranslated in a check-in as on every surface (S11 P19); a lead may say what a term asks of a seat in a service and may not define it (S11 8.4), and the person's line may use the term or not as the person chooses.

**P38 (Recommended).** Coaching runs in the person's chosen language; a lead seat's languages are a training-infrastructure line on the lead's seat and never a reason a person is coached less. Canon's language constant reaches every hypothesis; canon's register does not script the conversation, and the team's reading that "present without performing" refuses a lead's performed warmth or performed candor is held for canon's owner with the internal register.

**P39 (Recommended).** Jeong and Nunchi are developed and never graded: a lead may observe a specific act at a table and may never say a person has, lacks, or is developing either; felt knowledge is the one canon standard coaching references directly, because it is an act at the table with a record.

**P40 (Founder-gated).** The team's reading of canon's register inside a check-in (held with `86akh7r2t` and S11 P23, extended and not filed twice), for canon's owner.
