### 8.3 What a module is, under canon

A module is the unit the platform teaches, and canon governs every word in it because a new team member is taught in the house's voice and will speak in it. The standard (I11) is a specification and not a template.

A module must contain: the procedure it teaches, as a sequence of what is done, in what order, with what, and what it looks like when done; the seat and the field 6 row it serves; the one thing the procedure is for, said as a decision and not an outcome, in the way canon's menu copy "names what is present, how it is present, and the role of its presence" (Canon v1.0, section 09); a Loom recording of a real person doing it, where the procedure is physical; the range that touches it, if any, with a trigger named; the language it is in, the author, the approver, and the version; and how mastery of it is demonstrated, which is the readiness row it feeds.

A module must do: exist in every language the seat's field 14 names before the seat's first start date; take no longer than the check-in's unit of time to complete on shift; be readable on a phone; be revised by versioned decision when the floor contradicts it (the week-four line, section 7.4); carry the language constant in every sentence; pass the six binary tests sentence by sentence (Canon v1.0, section 09; Canon v3.0, p.54), because a module that could appear unchanged in another house's training is not teaching this house.

A module must refuse: a script for a customer interaction, because canon prohibits "service delivered by script rather than felt knowledge" (Canon v1.0, section 01) and "service protocols built entirely on scripted delivery" (Canon v1.0, section 01), so a module teaches the arrival's one line, "We have you" (Canon v1.0, section 08), as the house's line and teaches nothing else as lines; a procedure with no decision inside it where the seat holds one, which is "training systems that measure procedure execution without building toward judgment" (Canon v1.0, section 01); a description of nunchi or Jeong as a skill to execute, since neither is executed; a translation, gloss, footnote, or italic definition of a Korean term (Canon v1.0, section 04), which is the finding in 19.6 and is held; canon's forbidden word for the customer; a daypart code name; a figure or a rate; a forbidden-lexicon word (Canon v1.0, section 09); "welcome" or "enjoy" in the written module, which are "acceptable as in-person service language only" (Canon v1.0, section 09), so a module may say that the Maitre d at the steps says "We have you" and may not write "welcome the customer"; a synthesized presenter (P32); a recording with a customer in it; a quiz that scores a person, since the readiness read is the read; and a module that teaches a competency the seat sheet's field 7 says the house develops as if it were required at entry.

Authorship and approval. "Any qualified team member can create training modules" (WP p.19), and "qualified" is defined here as holding the competency the module teaches, validated by the platform's own record or the readiness form, in the seat the module serves. The domain lead approves a module for their domain against the standard; the chef partner for the kitchen; canon's owner for any module that carries brand facts, the arrival line, the register, or the uniform, until the concept's voice holder is named (`86akh2qq0`); and the Operations Lead for any module carrying a compliance or food-safety procedure, with counsel where the law draws a line. Approval is against the standard and not against taste, and a module refused names the standard's item. Payment for authorship is the record's commitment and Session 13's mechanism; this page states no figure and files the coupling (section 19, finding 19.1, and the carryover).

**P33 (Recommended).** A module is specified as in I11: what it must contain, do, and refuse, with canon on every word, no script beyond the house's one arrival line, no synthesized presenter, no scoring quiz, and no competency taught as required that field 7 says is developed. "Qualified" to author means holding the validated competency; approval is the domain lead's against the standard, the chef's for the kitchen, canon's owner's for brand-carrying content, and the Operations Lead's with counsel for compliance. Authorship pay is Session 13's.

Second, the authors of the first set are the people who hold the competencies: the leads for their domains' floor and beverage seats, the chef partner or the kitchen's domain owners for the kitchen, both founders for the why block's fourth part and the system modules (the stack's surfaces, the person page, the feedback channel, the range card's form), and Brandon's seat for every module that carries canon until the voice holder exists. ## 9. Felt knowledge and the menu: the training sequence against the chef chain

Canon requires that "service speaks to a dish with felt knowledge, not recited description" and that "the Executive Chef can articulate what each dish is reaching for, not just what it tastes like" (Canon v1.0, section 01). The strand definition does not carry felt knowledge (S6 18.5), the hiring read scored only the capacity for it (S6 P18), and the menu is chef-gated and does not exist (S5 P44). Carryover `86akh9tap` gives this page the training plan's sequence and asks it to state the interval the sequence needs as a gate condition without inventing a figure the record does not hold.

What felt knowledge is, operationally. Not a description memorized; canon forbids exactly that. It is a person who has eaten the dish, knows what decision produced it in the chef's own account, and can say one true thing about it in their own words when a customer asks and nothing when a customer does not. Mia "cannot receive unsolicited instruction" and "To explain what she already knows is an insult" (Canon v3.0, p.22), so felt knowledge includes knowing when to say nothing, which is a judgment the readiness read cannot score and the plan does not try to. The readiness read's felt-knowledge row scores three things only: the person has tasted every dish on the current menu; the person can state, for a dish named at random, what it is and the decision that produced it, in the chef's terms and their own words, with no forbidden word and no gloss of a Korean term; and the person does not describe a dish in a category word ("savory," "rich," "complex" are named placeholders, Canon v1.0, section 01).

The sequence, against the chain. The chain runs ruling, chef seated, canon's Session 1B, menu, kitchen seat sheets, kitchen practicals, kitchen hires, their training, dinner's gate (S6 P15). Felt knowledge for the floor hangs off the fourth link and needs, after it and before the gate: the chef's per-dish account written, one entry per dish, what it is reaching for and the decision that produced it, in the chef's words, which is the felt-knowledge block's source and the menu module's content (chef-gated); the dishes cooked and tasted by the whole floor cohort in training services, each dish at least once by every person who will serve it, with the chef or a station lead present to say the account aloud; the felt-knowledge block built from the account on Trainual, in the floor's languages; and the readiness reads' felt-knowledge rows passed, which cannot happen before the tastings. The kitchen's own felt knowledge is the kitchen's practicals and training and is chef-gated whole.

The gate condition, stated without a figure. No floor readiness form may read ready on its felt-knowledge row before the cohort has tasted every dish on the fixed menu in a training service after the menu was fixed, with the chef's account given. The interval the sequence needs is therefore measured in training services, not weeks: at least as many training services after the menu is fixed as it takes to cook every dish for the cohort at least once and to run every floor cohort member's readiness read after that, with the paired-service count the sheets require. How many training services that is depends on the menu's length, the kitchen's readiness to cook it at cohort volume, and the cohort's size, none of which this page holds, and it is chef-gated with the founders holding the calendar. A menu that changes between the tastings and dinner's gate re-opens the row for the changed dishes and is a chef-gated calendar event. A menu fixed so late that the training services cannot carry the tastings is the record's own sequence failing, and dinner's gate does not open around it, because a floor without felt knowledge is a floor "delivered by script," which canon forbids at Tier 1.

The steady state. Every menu change, by versioned decision, adds the changed dishes to the tasting record: each server tastes each new dish, with the account, before serving it, in the pre-service window on shift, and the stack records it against the person's page; a server who has not tasted a dish does not carry a table that orders it alone, which is the seat holder's or the mentor's until they have. That is the record's "It is a continuation of things" (Book p.216, Crenn) as a mechanism, and its bandwidth cost is a tasting in a window already funded.

**P37 (Recommended).** The felt-knowledge row on the readiness form scores that every current dish has been tasted, that a dish named at random can be stated in the chef's terms and the person's words with no placeholder and no gloss, and nothing else. The sequence hangs off the menu link of the chain: the chef's per-dish account, the cohort's tastings in training services after the menu is fixed, the block built from the account, then the rows. In service, every menu change adds tastings before the dish is served alone.

**P38 (Chef-gated).** The per-dish account in the chef's own words, the number of training services needed to cook every dish for the cohort at least once after the menu is fixed, the kitchen's own felt knowledge and training, and any menu change between the tastings and dinner's gate. The gate condition stands without a figure: no floor form reads ready on its felt-knowledge row before every dish has been tasted with the account, and dinner's gate does not open around a floor that has not.

## 10. Cross-training and the first unlock

### 10.1 What the record holds, and the two clocks

Two clocks run on a new person from the first day. The plan's clock, ninety days, is this page's. The invisible advocate's clock, "After a set period in any role, the next skill set unlocks automatically" (WP p.11), is the seat sheet's, fixed on field 6 before anyone was hired (S5 P60), and it runs regardless of the plan. The record does not say which is longer, and this page does not need it to: the plan reads the entry horizon and the unlock reads the second horizon, and a person on both clocks sees both on their person page from the first day (S5 row 57).

Cross-training is the record's development system: "Cross-training is career development, not only operational insurance. A server who learns the kitchen becomes more valuable everywhere" (WP p.19), and progression "develops outward, deeper into their domain and across into adjacent ones" (WP p.11). The profile's anchor agrees: in a flat house with no rungs, development has to be the retention system, and cross-training is its mechanism (profile, people systems designer). What the record does not say is when a new person may start on another track, and who signs a competency held across two domains (S6 18.8).

### 10.2 When the tracks open

Every track is visible from the first day: "Every position has documented competency requirements visible to anyone" (WP p.11), and Session 5 made every inventory seat's requirements exist before the cohort arrives (S5 P60). A new person may open any module on any track from the first day, self-paced, on shift, and the platform records it; nothing about visibility or reading waits on the plan. What waits is the practical assessment in another domain. The team's position: no cross-domain practical assessment is scheduled for a person before their own plan has closed with its goal held, because the plan's goal is the seat held solo and a person assessed for a second domain before holding their first has been onboarded against two things and holds neither, which is the Realist's read of what a green person on two tracks looks like on a Friday. The first unlock in the person's own seat is not gated on the plan; the sheet's period governs it and the invisible advocate schedules it (WP p.11). Where the sheet's period is shorter than ninety days, the unlock's practical may fall inside the plan, and it runs; the plan is not a hold on the record's own advocate.

Cross-period fluency is the same shape one step over: a person moves to a second period through the period plan (P30), and the platform's period module set is visible from the day the period's charter is ratified.

**P39 (Recommended).** Every track is visible and every module openable from the first day; a cross-domain practical assessment is not scheduled before the person's own plan has closed with its goal held; the person's own first unlock runs on the sheet's period regardless of the plan, and the plan is never a hold on the invisible advocate.

### 10.3 Who signs a cross-domain competency

Finding 18.8 stands and is not closed here. The record has the practical assessment auto-scheduling "with the appropriate domain lead" (WP p.11) and the sign-off in "a competency conversation with the appropriate lead" (WP p.19), and for a competency in a second domain "appropriate" has two candidates. The team's reading, already used by the internal loop (S6 P69), is that the receiving domain's lead signs, because the receiving domain holds the information about whether the competency is held there, and the person's own domain lead is informed through the person page and the leads' review. For the kitchen, the receiving lead is chef-gated. Session 8 holds the structure, and the founders and the chef close it; onboarding depends on it only for the domain plan (P30), whose readiness read this page says is scored on the receiving domain's rows by that domain's assessor and read by that domain's lead, as the team's reading.

**P40 (Founder-gated).** Who signs a competency held across two domains. The team recommends the receiving domain's lead, with the person's own lead informed through the person page and the leads' review, and the kitchen chef-gated. Session 8, the founders, and the chef close it; the domain plan runs on the team's reading until then and says so.

## 11. The mentor

### 11.1 What the record says, and what it leaves

"Every new person is paired with a mentor, a proven cultural steward rather than simply the strongest performer, someone who answers questions and makes the early experience reflect the standard" (WP p.18). Her equivalents are the "spin-up buddy" and the "guide," one for day-to-day questions and one for "a safe space for any 'Why do we do things this way?'" (Book p.219; p.258). The record's one sentence is more specific than her two roles: it names who the mentor is (a cultural steward, proven), who they are not (the strongest performer as such), and what they do (answer questions; make the early experience reflect the standard). It leaves who chooses, how, from whom, how the mentor is paid, what the mentor carries beyond questions, and what the mentor refuses.

### 11.2 Who, chosen how

The pool. A person is eligible to mentor when: they have closed their own ninety-day plan with its goal held, or, pre-opening, hold a readiness form reading ready from a training service (P8); their person page carries no non-negotiable line; they have completed the mentor module (I6) on the platform; they hold the seat, or a seat on the same strand in the same domain, as the person they will mentor; and they have said yes. The pool is a list on the stack per strand per domain, readable by the domain leads and the Maitre d, like the ready-now pool (S6 P72), and a person's presence on it is on their own person page. "Proven cultural steward" is read as those five conditions and nothing softer: a steward is proven by the record, not by a lead's sense of them. "Rather than simply the strongest performer" is read as the record's own refusal of the industry's default, and the pool does not rank.

Who names. The domain lead, at the offer, from the pool, so that recruiting can write the name on the person page (S5 P16) and connect the mentor before arrival (P13). The lead may not name a person not on the pool, may not name the person who will run the readiness read, may not name themselves, and may not name a person already mentoring someone whose plan is open, because a mentor with two open plans is node overload in the strand (WP p.14). Where the pool holds no eligible person for a seat, the start date cannot be set, and that is the training-infrastructure load S5 12.6 already routes to the leads' review, and pre-opening the two-wave rule (P8) is the answer.
