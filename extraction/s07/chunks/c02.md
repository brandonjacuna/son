### 2.3 What onboarding refuses to conclude

A verdict on the person. The review is not a verdict (WP p.19) and the check-in refuses one (S4 P15); the plan cannot be a verdict on the way to a review that is not.

A level, a placement, or a pay outcome. Everyone enters at the seat's entry point (S6 P62) and the architecture is Session 13's; the plan's close changes nothing about pay and says nothing about it.

Nunchi or Jeong. Neither is a ninety-day expectation (ledger; S5 P6). Nunchi is "Developed through mastery, exercised through judgment" (Canon v3.0, p.15), and ninety days is the beginning of mastery; Jeong "requires time, repetition, and memory" (Canon v1.0, section 01) and the plan is too short to have accumulated any. A plan that named either as a milestone would be scoring at ninety days what recruiting refused to score at entry.

Month-six performance, cross-strand fluency, or a person's ceiling. The plan reads the entry horizon of field 6 and nothing past it.

Whether a hire was a mistake. The plan reads states; where a hire that does not hold is read, and what is concluded from it, is Session 14's (S6 18.6; section 13). The plan may say a milestone did not reach its state and by which link. It may not say what that means about the person.

Whether the person "fits." There is no such dimension at hiring (S6 6.2) and none at onboarding.

Anything about the person that the person has not heard. The person page is written in the room and read back (S4 P18), and the plan block is part of the page.

**P4 (Recommended).** Onboarding's intended behavior is the six behaviors of 2.1 at the close of the ninety-day plan. It may conclude service-readiness per seat with a readiness form, the state and date of each plan milestone, what the building supplied against what the person said they needed, and what in the training infrastructure did not hold. It refuses to conclude a verdict, a level or pay outcome, nunchi or Jeong, month-six performance or a ceiling, whether a hire was a mistake, fit, or anything the person has not heard.

### 2.4 Who runs it, and the departure from her manager

Her onboarding has one owner, the manager, who understands the curriculum, adds team content, holds the first 1:1, sets expectations, and later runs the career conversation (Book p.216 to 219). At Sŏn nothing is assigned, reviewed, or escalated by a person standing above another person (ledger), so her manager's functions are distributed across five runners, each with a stated range, and no runner holds all of them.

The domain lead, by seat. Holds the person's check-in and reads and writes the person page there, and only there (S4 P20). Names the mentor from the pool (section 11). Places the paired services and the first solo on the schedule, the first solo only on a passed readiness form (section 6.5). Reads the training-infrastructure line to the leads' review. Does not teach the modules, does not run the readiness read, does not write a verdict, does not assign tasks, does not hold advancement (S5 P33). For floor seats on the outward-facing strand the domain lead is the Maitre d, who owns "Team development, training, and the invisible advocate function" (WP p.10); for the inward-facing floor strand, and for runner and host seats, the lead is held (S5 P64; `86akh7r10`) and every entry on this page that names the domain lead for those seats is incomplete until it closes; for beverage the Head of Beverage; for the kitchen, chef-gated.

The mentor. A proven cultural steward from the person's strand, named by the lead from the pool, paid as shift, beside the person at the live session and the paired services, answering questions, carrying the house's ways and the standard, and writing nothing on the person page (section 11).

The assessor. A trained assessor from the seat's strand who is not the mentor and does not decide, scoring the readiness read on the practical's rows (S6 I4; S5 P41). The same separation hiring uses: the person who taught is not the person who reads.

The founder, for the why. Pre-opening and through dinner's first quarter, a founder delivers the why block of orientation in person (section 5.4). After that, the holder of the concept's voice internally, which is an open question the ledger carries (`86akh2qq0`; spec `86akh3u2y`).

The stack. Creates the accounts at acceptance, delivers what arrives in the person's hands, places the check-ins, records module completion and paired services, refuses a start date without its conditions and a solo without a form, and writes the milestone dates it can see. Onboarding runs without a lead's memory, or it is a node-overload failure by the ledger's rule.

**P5 (Recommended).** Her manager's onboarding functions are distributed across five runners with stated ranges: the domain lead (check-in, mentor named, paired and solo placed on a passed form, the infrastructure line), the mentor (beside the person, teaching the house's ways, writing nothing), the assessor (the readiness read, not the mentor, not the decider), the founder and later the voice-holder (the why block), and the stack (accounts, delivery, placement, refusals, records). No runner holds all of them, and no mechanism depends on a lead remembering.

## 3. The pre-opening cohort and the steady-state hire, mechanism by mechanism

Her onboarding is written for a person joining a running house: a curriculum exists, teams exist to absorb them, a manager has context to translate, and "internal resources to self-educate" (Book p.217) already exist to be found. Sŏn's first cohort joins a building that is not open, has no menu until the chef chain reaches it (S6 P15), has no team to absorb them because they are the team, and has no learning platform content until someone builds it. The record's commitments are written in the steady state and do not say which of them assume a running house. This section says so, and writes the pre-opening form of each beside the steady-state form, so that neither is quietly used for the other.

Three positions fall out of the table.

The cohort in two waves. The record's mentor is "a proven cultural steward" (WP p.18), and on the cohort's first day no one below the leads has proven anything. The industry's answer is to let the strongest performer mentor, which the record refuses in the same sentence. The team's answer is sequencing: the cohort arrives in two waves, the first wave small enough for the leads and the chef's kitchen owners to mentor directly, trained through the training services, and the second wave mentored by the first once the training-service record shows, per person, the procedures reproduced on the readiness form and no non-negotiable line on the person page. The first wave's mentor mark is provisional and converts at the mechanism reset (S4 P39) against the record. This is a hiring-calendar choice and it interacts with the pre-opening hiring burst, which is founder-gated (`86akh7qnb`; S5 P49), so it is marked founder-gated with the team's recommendation stated. What the team does not recommend: a single wave mentored by the leads alone, which is node overload by construction (WP p.14) at the moment the leads are also standing up their domains; or hiring an outside "trainer" seat, which the record's leadership count has no room for (WP p.22) and which would teach the industry's floor.

Who builds the first modules. Section 8.4 holds the mechanism. The position here is only that the first module set exists before any cohort start date is committed, that its authors are the leads for the floor, the chef partner or the kitchen's domain owners for the kitchen, and the founders for the why and the system modules, and that until the first cohort completes them there is no completion record on the platform and the record's "constantly expanding" (WP p.19) has not started. A cohort start date committed before the seat's first modules exist is a refusal the stack already holds (S5 12.6), and pre-opening the refusal is read against the partners' page, where the module set's state is a readiness row (row 78).

The first solo, pre-opening. The ledger forbids a first shift on a new period's first service. It does not say whether a cohort member's first solo, meaning their first service holding a section or station without a mentor beside them, may be dinner's opening night. The team reads the record's "no one touches a table until they are actually ready" (WP p.19) with the Realist's rule that "opening night is not month six" (profile) as forbidding it: the training services are where the cohort's readiness forms are passed and where the first solos happen, so that on dinner's first service every section and station is held by a person who has already held it alone in at least one training service. The training-service count that makes this possible is a hiring-calendar item (S6 I14) and is not a figure this page sets.

**P7 (Recommended).** The pre-opening form of every onboarding mechanism is as tabled beside its steady-state form, and no mechanism written for a running house is used on the cohort without its pre-opening form: the training services stand in for real services and read procedure without load, the first four dinner weeks are the load read, the felt-knowledge block waits on the menu, and the onboarding read first runs at the mechanism reset.

**P8 (Founder-gated).** The cohort arrives in two waves, the first mentored by the leads and the kitchen's domain owners through the training services and the second by the first wave once the training-service record shows procedures reproduced and no non-negotiable line, with the first wave's mentor mark provisional until the mechanism reset. The founders close it with the pre-opening hiring burst (`86akh7qnb`), because it is a calendar and a count.

**P9 (Recommended).** The first module set for every dinner seat exists before any cohort start date is committed, authored by the leads for the floor, the kitchen's domain owners for the kitchen, and the founders for the why and the system modules; the stack's refusal of a start date without the seat's first modules (S5 12.6) is read pre-opening against the module set's state on the partners' page.

**P10 (Recommended).** No cohort member's first solo is dinner's first service. Readiness forms are passed and first solos are held in training services, so that every section and station on dinner's first service is held by a person who has already held it alone at least once. The training-service count is the hiring calendar's and is not set here.

## 4. Before the first shift

### 4.1 The seam: acceptance

Session 6 fixed that the written offer's acceptance "is the seam to Rippling and the person page" (S6 P58). At that moment three things happen and the stack does all three: the person page is created with recruiting's ten entries (S5 P16); Rippling's onboarding opens, carrying the legal and payroll content the Operations Lead sets with counsel, which this page does not write; and the person's accounts are created by single sign-on through Rippling into Google Workspace, Trainual, and the team home, in the language the person chose at the fourth read (S6 P23). Whether Rippling's onboarding task list can carry the house's own items (the uniform fitting, the locker, the arrival note's acknowledgement) beside the legal ones is to confirm; if it cannot, the house's items live on the person page and the legal ones in Rippling, and nothing is duplicated.

Between acceptance and the first shift the person is in Greenhouse's accepted state and on Rippling's pre-start record, and they are not yet on the clock. Everything the house does in this window is the house's work, not the person's. That rule is the one the record's paragraph implies and does not state: "Everything needed to succeed is waiting" (WP p.18) describes what the house has done, and nothing in it describes what the person must have done, read, or completed before their first clock-in. This page makes it a rule (4.6).

### 4.2 The person page's ten entries, and the plan block added

Carryover `86akh7qn7` holds the ten entries whole: the seat and sheet version; the strand; the domain lead by seat; the decision-rights range as accepted; the service periods; the start date; the mentor, connected before arrival; the ninety-day plan's start; the first unlock the sheet names; and what the candidate said they need from the building, in their own words. No scorecard travels to the page (S6 row 71). This page changes none of the ten and adds one thing beneath them: the plan block (section 7.2), which is the record's "ninety-day plan while they are on one" (S4 P18) given its form. The plan block is written by the stack at acceptance from the sheet, and its milestones are empty states until they are reached.

Two things the ten entries need from this page that recruiting could not supply. First, the mentor entry is a person's name, and section 11 says who may be named and who may not, so recruiting's "mentor connected" now has a pool to name from. Second, "the ninety-day plan's start" is the start date, not the acceptance date and not the first solo, because the record's plan "sets expectations and goals with regular check-ins" (WP p.18) and the first check-in is two weeks after the start date (S5 4.5); a plan that started at acceptance would run its first two weeks off the clock.

**P11 (Recommended).** The person page's ten entries are held whole and unchanged; the plan block of section 7.2 is added beneath them, written by the stack at acceptance from the seat sheet with its milestones as empty states; the ninety-day plan starts on the start date, not at acceptance and not at the first solo.

### 4.4 The mentor connected

"The peer mentor is connected" before arrival (WP p.18) is a verb the record does not define. This page defines it. Connected means: the mentor is named on the person page from the pool (section 11); the stack sends the person, in their language, the mentor's name, seat, and a way to reach them through the team home; the mentor sends one message, on shift, in their own words, saying who they are and that they will be beside the person at the live session on the first shift; and the mentor's shifts for the person's first two weeks are placed on the schedule to overlap the person's. Nothing is required of the person in return. A conversation before the first shift, if the person wants one, is at the person's initiative, is answered by the mentor on the clock, and is not a condition of anything.

What connected does not mean: a checklist the mentor works through by phone; a pre-arrival "buddy" call the person is expected to make; the mentor delivering paperwork, the schedule, or the range; or the mentor learning anything about the person from the packet, which stays in Greenhouse and which the mentor never sees (S5 P16). The mentor meets the person as the house does, from the seat sheet and the person page's ten entries, and not from an interviewer's evidence lines.

**P13 (Recommended).** "Mentor connected" means named from the pool on the person page, introduced by the stack in the person's language, one message from the mentor on shift, and the mentor's first two weeks of shifts placed to overlap the person's. Nothing is required of the person before the first shift; the mentor never sees the packet.
