## 5. The team that forms and dissolves every service

### 5.1 Her stages, and the three objects they would have to describe

Tuckman's forming, storming, norming, performing (Book pp. 306 to 308, Table 7) describes a fixed group of people meeting repeatedly over time, with a manager who is directive in forming and consultative by norming. Her guidance follows the stage: build safety and norms while forming, frame conflict as healthy while storming, celebrate and hone while norming, raise the rate of challenge while performing, and "know that this phase is not permanent" (Book p.307).

Sŏn has one team in her sense, the house, and her word means three things (S8 P6). Her stages have to be tested against each, and each gives a different answer.

**The service roster.** The people the brief named for one service, across every domain; it exists for one service and dissolves at the close (S8 3.2). It cannot form, storm, or norm as a group, because it does not meet again as that group. The Realist's read is exact: a roster has to perform on its first service, so every stage before performing has to be done before the roster exists, by the structure the roster instantiates. Forming is the brief: the designations named (who holds the room, the door, the sequence, the pass), the readiness block, the period's goal, the one thing, and the roster is formed in the time it takes to say them (S4 P10; S8 section 8). Storming is refused as a stage the roster is allowed: its conflicts are resolved by the register before the service (the pass owner and the sequence designation's rule for a station down, S8 P23; the live cross-domain call, S4 P61), and a conflict the register did not anticipate is a capture at the close and a hold at the leads' review, not a stage the roster works through. Norming happened once, for the house, and the roster inherits it. Performing is the record's own picture of a great night, "every element in relationship with every other, information moving laterally without routing through a chain of command" (WP p.8), and it is produced by the structure or not at all.

**The house.** The one team forms once, pre-opening, through the cohort in two waves and the training services (S7 P8, P10). It storms, if the word is kept, in dinner's first quarter, which is exactly why the freeze exists: the record's own quarter "so the heart is solid before anything is added to it" (WP p.36) is her storming phase with a rule that the norms do not move while it runs. It norms at the first mechanism reset, where every mechanism is continued, changed, or ended on the quarter's record (S4 P39; S9 6.1), which is the one time the house's rules are re-set on evidence rather than assertion. Its performing has no stage; the record's health state, "an active positive condition where every system functions and the whole operates with bandwidth" (WP p.12), is what the diagnostic reads for, quarterly, on five modes, and "this phase is not permanent" is the reset's own premise.

**The cohort.** Her forming guidance is the training sequence's own shape and needs no translation: "be more directive," "provide extra support, direction, and modeling in activities," "actively work to build psychological safety by setting up collaborative, supportive team norms" (Table 7) are the modules, the live session, the observed and paired services with the mentor, the why block delivering the non-negotiables first, and the readiness read (S7 P10; S6 P38). The cohort forms in training services and its first solos are held there, so that no one's first solo is dinner's first service (S7 P10). Her suggestion that forming is "a great time to conduct one of the work style and personality preference exercises" (Book p.308) is refused; the grid does not enter onboarding or the cohort (S7 P53; section 13).

**The domain.** A domain is not a team (S8 P6) and does not form; its people meet their lead one at a time at the check-in and each other on the roster.

### 5.2 Task against team, her Table 8

Her offsite balances task-focused activities (metrics, goals and DRIs, retrospectives, long-term strategy) against team-focused ones (work style activity, a meal together, defining norms, discussing roles and accountability) (Book pp. 308 to 309, Table 8). At Sŏn every task-focused item is already on the mechanism map at a fixed rhythm: metrics at the leads' review weekly and the house review quarterly; goals at the house review; retrospectives as the reflection paragraph and the reset; long-term strategy as the gate review and the partners' domain read (S4 10.2). Her team-focused items land as follows. Defining team norms: refused as an act a team performs; the norms are the non-negotiables, the register, and the spines, written once and versioned, never agreed per group (section 8). Discussing roles and accountability: the designation block at the brief, nightly, in one line each. The work style activity: refused (section 13). Team building as an activity: refused as a manufactured activity under Ma (Canon 1.0, section 01). A meal together: the one item on her list the record and canon are silent on, and the silence is the environment's most consequential (5.3).

### 5.3 The staff meal, a silence the brief did not anticipate

Her table's "team breakfast, lunch, dinner activity" is, in a restaurant, the staff meal before service: the one team mechanism the industry already runs daily, at no meeting cost, in the paid pre-service window, and the one place a roster eats together before it serves together. Neither the white paper nor either canon artifact mentions it. The record allocates the pre-service window to "prepare for the customer" (WP p.26) and the brief, and canon's pre-service scene is a lighting and sonic program "30 to 45 minute... before first seating" (Canon 1.0, section 06). Whether the window carries a meal, who cooks it, and what it costs the kitchen's mise are not in the record.

The team does not invent Sŏn's intent here and does not recommend a meal as a culture mechanism from scratch. It records the silence as a founder decision with a chef-gated half, because the meal is the one team-focused item on her list that passes the entry test on its face: it costs no meeting, it buys the roster ten minutes together that "time not taken" already funds, its runner is the kitchen, and its record is none. The Realist's constraint is stated with it: a meal that sits inside the mise window on a Friday is a station's cost, and the chef confirms whether the kitchen can carry it; a meal that displaces the brief is refused. Whether Sŏn holds one is the founders' with the chef.

**P10 (Recommended).** Her development stages are refused as a read of any team and absorbed as three shapes: the service roster is formed by the brief in the time it takes to name the designations and performs or does not on its first service; the house forms once through the cohort, holds its rules still through dinner's first quarter, and re-sets them at the first reset; the cohort forms through the training sequence, whose shape is her forming guidance already. A domain does not form.

**P11 (Recommended).** Her task-focused activities are the mechanism map's fixed rhythms. Her team-focused activities are refused (norms agreed per team, the work style activity, team building as an activity) or placed (roles as the designation block at the brief), except the meal, which the record does not hold.

**P12 (Founder-gated).** Whether the pre-service window carries a staff meal, as the one team mechanism on her list the record is silent on and the one that passes the entry test on its face. The team states the silence and the Realist's constraint, and recommends nothing from scratch.

**P13 (Chef-gated).** The kitchen's cost of a staff meal inside the mise window, if the founders open the question.

## 6. Psychological safety, the environment's half

### 6.1 Her definition, placed act by act

Her definition, from Project Aristotle, is "the ability to take interpersonal risks like asking questions, offering a dissenting opinion, discussing a failure, or expressing vulnerability without fear of negative repercussions," which she calls "a central requirement for teams to progress from one phase to the next" (Book p.306). Session 9 placed the signal (the capture rate, the error-reporting rate) as a source the diagnostic reads and handed the system that produces candor to Session 12 (S9 3.3). This page places the environment's half: what makes each of her four acts safe by construction, before any coaching or feedback conversation exists.

**Asking a question.** The mentor, who "answers questions" (WP p.18) and is "the person a new hire asks before they ask the lead" (S7 P42); the check-in's "what is in the way"; the feedback channel. Safe because none of the three writes anything about the asker: the mentor writes nothing on the person page, the check-in's line is the person's own, and the channel's item names a friction.

**Offering a dissenting opinion.** The joint half-page as the form of a hold, with a lead's refusal to hear a unilateral case (S8 P50); the check-in's upward question; the dissent line in the hiring packet (S6 section 19). Safe because a dissent at Sŏn is a written entry on a record with a named destination, not a thing said in a room to a person who decides; and because the decision-rights rule means the dissenter is never asking someone above them to change their mind, only asking the owner to consult, which the owner must do before the event under Class B (S3 4.3).

**Discussing a failure.** The capture and blame the process (section 3.4). Safe because the post-mortem is "private and process-focused" (WP p.17) and "Management absorbs blame from staff" (WP p.17), and because the close carries nothing about a person.

**Expressing vulnerability.** Refused as a mechanism. The house does not ask, and no opener, prompt, or ritual invites it: a person brings what they bring to the check-in and it is written only if they say it and only in their words. Canon's register is "present without performing" (Canon 1.0, section 01), her personal check-in example (a parent's surgery, apologies for distraction, Book p.309) is precisely a performed vulnerability, and the non-negotiables' refusal of intrusion (S9 P44, on the career conversation's origin questions) applies. What the house offers instead is the thing the record names: "dignity is a precondition for presence" (WP p.20), which is a locker and a break space, not a prompt.

### 6.2 What safety is made of here

Six things, all mechanisms already on the map: blame the process (WP p.17); the capture at the close (WP p.13; S4 P12); private, process-focused post-mortems off the floor (WP p.17; S4 P12); the check-in's refusals of verdicts, task assignment, pay, and discipline (S4 P15); nothing on a person page but states and the person's own words (S7 P25 to P29); and the non-negotiables above the philosophy (section 2.1). A seventh is the range: a pre-authorized act is not an interpersonal risk, "pre-authorizing it signals trust to staff" (WP p.26), so the range itself is a safety mechanism, and the size of the range is the size of what a person can do without fear.

What the environment does not supply, and states as dependencies. The response by type of failure (basic, complex, intelligent), which decides whether a capture is met with a process fix or something else, is S12's. What happens when a non-negotiable is breached by a person, the one failure the record treats as a person's, is S14's and S15's; the environment's rule is only that the flag goes to both leads the same day (S7 P45) and that it is never said at the brief or the close.

### 6.3 The measure, and a finding

The record commits "engagement results across psychological safety and values alignment" as a cultural measure (WP p.23). That is a survey-shaped measure, and Session 9 refused the survey as a form (S9 P5, I10). The record's own measure of safety therefore has no instrument the program accepts. What stands in: the capture rate and the process-update rate, on item 1 of the leads' review every week (S4 P26), and the error-reporting rate the designer's seat instruments (S9 3.3); the pulse's one question; and check-in lines aggregated by friction. A baseline for all three is team-filled from the first quarter. Section 20 records the finding.

### 6.4 "No topics are undiscussable," and correcting bad meeting behavior

Her norm is that a team agrees to bring lurking issues to the table because it will smell them eventually, and she calls it the meeting form of "Say the thing you think you cannot say" (Book pp. 317 to 318). At Sŏn the rule is inverted and stronger: every topic has a named destination, and the undiscussable topic is the one with no destination, which is the trigger with no destination the ledger forbids (S3 4.1, rule 1). A friction goes to the check-in or the channel; a dissent to the hold; a failure to the capture; a non-negotiable to both leads; a lead's own concern to the leads' review as a Class B or C entry; a founder's to the partners' page. Her "stinky fish" as a periodic reminder is the leads' review's item 5, items open past the window, read every week whether or not anyone remembers them (S4 P26). What the environment cannot yet route is a concern about a lead that the lead does not hold (section 3.5), and that is the one undiscussable topic left in the house until `86akhb2jg` closes.

Her "correct bad meeting behavior" has two halves. Inside the room: her rule that it is fair to call out a violated norm in a nonjudgmental way (Book p.327). At Sŏn the correction inside a meeting-shaped mechanism is the spine, not a person: the chair holds the clock and declines an item off the agenda (S4 P27); the owner of the room runs the brief's spine and takes no item about a person (S4 13.1); a Class B case argued unilaterally is refused until written jointly (S8 P50). No one is corrected in front of the roster, ever, because the brief and the close carry nothing about a person. Outside the room: her rule that behavior is corrected privately and quickly (Book p.328) is the check-in's own placement, and its content is S12's. Her example of naming someone's work style in a leadership meeting ("acting a bit 'red'") is refused whole: the grid is never spoken about another person (section 13). Her "praise publicly" is the peer recognition read aloud at the brief, "not editorialized" (S4 P10); "criticize privately" is the check-in and nowhere else.

**P14 (Recommended).** Psychological safety's environment half is made of blame the process, the capture, private process-focused post-mortems, the check-in's refusals, a person page with no verdict, the non-negotiables above the philosophy, and the range as a pre-authorized act. Her four acts are placed: a question to the mentor, the check-in, or the channel; a dissent to the hold's joint half-page or the upward question; a failure to the capture; vulnerability refused as a mechanism. The response by failure type is S12's; a non-negotiable breach by a person is S14's and S15's.

**P15 (Recommended).** "No topics are undiscussable" is the rule that every topic has a named destination, and the one undiscussable topic left is a concern about a lead that the lead holds, until `86akhb2jg` closes. Bad behavior in a meeting-shaped mechanism is corrected by the spine and the chair's clock, never by naming a person in the room; the private correction is the check-in's and S12's; the work style call-out is refused.

**P16 (Team-filled).** The safety baseline from the first quarter: the capture rate, the process-update rate, and the pulse composite, written at the first house review.

Her offsite has three objectives: "Evolve a set of people from a work group into a team. Evaluate progress and determine near-term priorities and goals. Engender long-term strategic thinking" (Book p.305). Each is tested for the three groups an offsite could gather.

**The house.** Refused. Her first objective is met for the house by the cohort and the training services (section 5); her second by the house review; her third is the partners' and not the house's.

**The leads.** Refused as a separate event. **The partners.** The reset and the principles revision are the two annual points where the partners do exactly what her offsite does, and both sit at a closed hour outside service hours by the seam rule (S4 P9, P58). The team recommends that the two be held as one closed-hour block per year, and that whether the partners hold it away from the building is theirs and costs the room nothing. It is paid, it is in the building, it forms the group, and it carries the mission and the non-negotiables. It is not called an offsite and is not one; it is named here so that no downstream cluster adds one beside it.
