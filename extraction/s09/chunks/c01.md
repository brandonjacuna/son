## 1. Her ask against the record, and how much is already decided

### 1.1 Her ask

Her diagnosing section asks a manager to "take stock" of a team, especially a new one, against three things: "whether it has the structures, plan, and people it needs to deliver excellent results" (Book p.283). Her method is a survey of the group before a meeting or offsite, read against Lencioni's five dysfunctions, then a person-by-person placement on the skill-will matrix with a prescribed response per quadrant (Book pp. 283 to 286). Her restructuring section adds an effort-impact matrix for ordering the work, two legitimate triggers for a reorganization (structure not matching strategy, a talent issue) and one misused one (an underperforming team), three rules of timing, and a three-phase method with its own clock: a month, one to two weeks, one to two days (Book pp. 287 to 294). Her rebuilding section says the bedrock of a team is its people, and asks the manager to understand each one through a career conversation, then to delegate to them by a two-axis framework, with a DRI for anything that needs a single accountable name (Book pp. 294 to 303).

Read as one, her three sections assume a manager who inherits or builds a team, reads its people, and changes its shape from above. The reading is the manager's, the placement is the manager's, and the change is announced.

### 1.2 What the record already holds

The record holds a diagnostic, and it sits at the end of Part I rather than in a people section: "Use this as a diagnostic against any operational problem. Before intervening at the people level, identify which failure mode is actually present" (WP p.14). It names five modes and a fix for each. Tension slack: "a role is occupied but the person is disengaged, the thread present but not carrying load, and other nodes are compensating without visibility. The fix is not replacing the person. It is diagnosing which adjacent nodes are overloaded and re-establishing tension in that domain." Node overload: "one domain owner is absorbing load that belongs to adjacent domains, usually a manager doing too much operational work because the parallel structure has blurred. The fix is restoring the Operations Lead and Maitre d separation." Sequence corruption: "information is not flowing to where it is needed before it is needed... The fix is system maintenance, not people management." Skill invisibility: "employees do not know what advancement requires because the competencies are not documented or accessible, and politics fills the vacuum. The fix is documentation and system access." Cooling failure: "bandwidth is at zero, the team has no capacity beyond surviving the shift, joy is absent, and retention is suffering. This is a bandwidth problem, not a morale problem. The fix is removing friction from the work" (WP p.14).

Three of the five fixes say in words that they are not a person-level move: not replacing the person, not people management, not a morale problem. The other two are a structural restoration and a documentation task. None of the five names a person as the thing to change. That is the record's whole diagnostic posture, and it is stated as a rule of order: the mode is identified before anyone intervenes at the person.

Behind the reference sit the machine's four failures, which are its source: CPU failure (expensive cognition throttled by the architecture around it), memory failure (knowledge living in people and leaving with them), bus failure (information that moves only by verbal handoff and relationship), and cooling failure (no way to measure thermal load) (WP p.13). And behind those sit the structural claims the diagnostic reads against: tension loss rather than slot vacancy on a departure (WP p.8 to 9), the two parallel leads (WP p.10), the inverted web and the invisible advocate (WP p.11), bandwidth as the metabolic rate and leading indicators as the nervous system's signal (WP p.12), and the code-bearing version of a key person leaving, "a bounded, temporary, calculable" cost because the knowledge stayed in the system (WP p.14).

Canon adds two things the record's reference does not carry. The three company non-negotiables, "No Exploitation Ever. No Toxicity Ever. No Disposable People," sit above every executional system (Canon 1.0, section 01; ranked at tier 2 in Deck 3.0, p.32, and unranked in Canon 1.0's hierarchy, a difference the ledger holds open under `86akh3rx4`). And the Playground Philosophy requires "training architecture that progresses from procedure mastery to autonomous judgment" and prohibits "training systems that measure procedure execution without building toward judgment" (Canon 1.0, section 01), which is a test on the skill-invisibility fix that the record's "documentation and system access" does not state.

**P1 (Recommended).** Sŏn's diagnostic is the record's failure mode reference (WP p.14), assembled into one instrument from what Sessions 3 to 8 built around it. **P2 (Recommended).** This page specifies the diagnostic and runs no diagnosis. Every line it describes is a form; no line on this page is evidence about Sŏn's team, which does not yet exist.

## 2. What a diagnosis reads at Sŏn

### 2.1 The five objects, and which are teams

Session 8 settled that Sŏn has one team in her sense, the house, and that her word translates three ways: the house for the collective, the domain for a manager's reports, and the service roster for who is on tonight (S8 P6; carryover `86akhcz6u`). A domain, a strand, and a service period are not teams. A diagnosis at Sŏn reads five kinds of object, and the two that are not among her three are read as often as the three are:

| Object | What it is | Is it a team | What the diagnostic reads of it |
|---|---|---|---|
| The house | The whole company as one integrated entity, "distributed intelligence rather than coordinated direction" (WP p.8) | Yes, the only one | The five modes' quarterly scorecard; the leading indicators as the spine; the readiness test's failures; the variety pass's classes resolving to nothing or to a founder |
| The domain | A set of seats with one owner, the domain lead (S5 P33) | No | The check-in load the lead's seat holds and its moves and stretch; the domain's open seats and their age; the training-infrastructure lines naming the domain; the captures by domain; the domain's goal page state; the tension-loss rows in the domain |
| The service roster | The people the brief named for one service across every domain; exists for one service and dissolves at the close (S8 3.2) | No, and it is the closest thing to her "boat" | The brief's designation block and whether every function was held; the close's incidents with their range status; the range used across sections; the designations named and onto whom |
| The seat | A row on the inventory, the only way a seat exists (S8 P26) | No | The row's fields, including field 15 (why the seat is open, S5 P12); the seat's age open; the designations the row carries and how often each was named onto the seat's holders; the sheet's version history |
| The service period | A period of one restaurant, opened by a ratified charter with non-empty dependent interfaces (S8 P7) | No | The charter's provided and dependent interfaces and the weekly interface read (S8 I10); the period's goal page; the period's incidents mapped to disturbance classes; the period plan's records |

The person is a sixth object, and it is the one the diagnostic reaches last and reaches only by the path in section 14. A person is never the first object of a read. The record's order, "before intervening at the people level, identify which failure mode is actually present" (WP p.14), is enforced by the form of the line: every evidence line names one of the five objects above, and none carries a person's name (section 4.1).

### 2.2 The order of every read

Every read the diagnostic makes, at any rhythm, runs in the same order, and the order is what keeps the record's rule mechanical rather than a matter of a lead's restraint:

1. Name the object read: house, domain, service roster, seat, or service period.
2. Name the failure mode present, from evidence on a mechanism's record: a capture, a close, a brief's record, a check-in line in the person's own words, a schedule fact, an inventory row, a readiness row, a variety pass entry. A mode with no source on the record is not named.
3. Name the mechanism the record's fix touches: a range, a trigger, an interval, the brief, the close, the stack's surfacing, a sheet, a designation's rule, the inventory, a seat's count under a charter, the schedule.
4. Only where the mode is tension slack, and only after the domain's fix in step 3 has been made and read back, may the read reach a person, and then only as section 14 states, and it hands off at once.

A read that arrives at a person without passing steps 1 to 3 has failed the diagnostic's own form, and the decision-rights entry in section 16.1 sends it back to its writer. This is also how the diagnostic answers her first question, "Are the right people doing the right jobs?" (Book p.283): at Sŏn that question is asked of the sheet, the horizon, the range, and the schedule before it is ever asked of a person, and by the time those have been read the question has usually dissolved into one of the five modes.

**P3 (Recommended).** A diagnosis reads five objects, the house, the domain, the service roster, the seat, and the service period, and every evidence line names which. Two of the five are not teams and are read as often as the three that translate her word. The person is reached last, by one path, and is never an evidence line's object.

**P4 (Recommended).** Every read runs in one order: the object, the mode from a record, the mechanism the fix touches, and only then and only for tension slack, a person. The order is enforced by the line's form and the decision-rights entry, not by a lead's restraint.

## 3. Her diagnostic questions and frameworks, each translated, absorbed, or refused

### 3.1 Her questions

"How is the team doing?" should be answerable "at any time" (Book p.283). At Sŏn it is, because the answer is the leading indicators the stack reads continuously and the leads' review reads weekly (S4 P26), not a manager's impression gathered for the occasion. "Are the right people doing the right jobs?" is section 2.2's order. "How happy, motivated, and fulfilled are team members?" is the employee NPS pulse, monthly, anonymous, on the OS surface (S4 P29), the check-in's upward question in the person's own words (S4 P15), and the cultural labor score once it exists (`86akh5u9x`); no lead answers it on the team's behalf. "Do they work well together?" is the record's description of a great night, information "moving laterally without routing through a chain of command" (WP p.8), read from the close: incidents resolved inside range, recoveries, escalations with destinations. "Are deadlines being met, and are the metrics looking good?" is the goal-page table with its three states and the bandwidth flag (S4 P35). Her final test, whether "every team member and the team overall" is having a positive impact (Book p.283), is refused in its second half: the diagnostic reads the house, and the person is section 14's.

### 3.2 The survey and the offsite

Her method is a survey sent by "the team leader, an HR partner, or an outside facilitator" before a meeting or offsite (Book p.283). Refused as a form. A survey asks for an impression on a date; the record's nervous system is "what allows the organization to feel what a restaurant without it cannot feel, early enough to respond rather than repair" (WP p.12), and Session 7 refused her manager milestone survey on the same ground (S7 P36). What does the survey's work is already running: the pulse, the feedback channel with its loop-closure window (S4 P30), the check-in's upward question, and the close written "at the edge by the people who felt the room" (S3 shift close). What a survey would add is a second, slower, self-reported channel beside four that already read the same population, which fails the four-part entry test on what it buys. The offsite is Session 10's; this page does not place one.

### 3.3 Lencioni's five dysfunctions

Refused as the diagnostic's frame, for two reasons and with a mapping so nothing she reads is lost. First, the dysfunctions are read at the team's interior, and "before intervening at the people level, identify which failure mode is actually present" (WP p.14) is the record's rule against opening there. Second, each dysfunction, where it is present, is a symptom one of the five modes explains, and the mode carries a fix where the dysfunction carries a conversation:

| Her dysfunction | What it looks like on Sŏn's records | The mode the diagnostic reads it as |
|---|---|---|
| Absence of trust | Incidents captured late or not at all; the error-reporting rate the designer's seat instruments as the leading indicator of safety; captures written by a lead on behalf of the floor | Sequence corruption (the capture is not happening) or cooling failure (no bandwidth to report) |
| Fear of conflict | Class B and C holds that never appear on the leads' review record while the interface read shows handoffs breaking | Sequence corruption at the interface; node overload where one lead absorbs the other's side |
| Lack of commitment | Goal pages with no state change all quarter; goals met at a bandwidth cost | Sequence corruption of the goal cycle; cooling failure |
| Avoidance of accountability | Decision-rights entries incomplete; "escalate" with no destination; a founder as an alert's named actor | Node overload at the center; a structural hole the variety pass records |
| Inattention to results | Lagging figures read first; leading indicators unread | Sequence corruption of the review's own order (S4 P26) |

The psychological safety half of her first dysfunction is the designer's seat as org-design theory and is not designed here; the diagnostic reads its signal (the capture rate, the error-reporting rate) and hands the system that produces candor to Session 12.

### 3.4 The skill-will matrix and the effort-impact matrix

The skill-will matrix is section 13's; its team-level half is absorbed there and its person-level half is refused and routed. The effort-impact matrix (Book p.287) is refused as an instrument and absorbed as a habit the reset already has: Session 4 reads every mechanism "against its own cost and buy" and continues, changes, or ends it (S4 P39). Her four quadrants are that read's four outcomes. What the diagnostic adds to ordering is not effort against impact but the record's own causal order among the modes: sequence corruption is fixed first because a degraded nervous system corrupts every other read; node overload second because an overloaded lead cannot run the other fixes; skill invisibility and cooling failure next by the record's fixes; and tension slack last, because it is the one mode whose read can reach a person and the record wants it read only after the adjacent nodes are known.

### 3.5 "Model the new behaviors and hold the team accountable to the plan"

Her manager, after the diagnosis, models the behavior and holds the team to a plan (Book p.284). Refused as a posture, absorbed as a mechanism. At Sŏn a diagnosis becomes a versioned change (section 7), the change reaches the team by four channels and no announcement (S8 P49), and whether it worked is read back on the next scorecard by the same source that raised it. The lead's modeling is the mentor's paired services (S7) and nothing else; a lead who holds the team to a plan by presence is producing stability by hand.

### 3.6 The solid and dotted lines callout

Session 8 refused solid and dotted lines (S8 P15, I12), and this page does not reopen it. What her callout raises that Session 8 did not answer is the "boat": the people a leader needs "no matter whom they report to" (Book p.282). At Sŏn the boat is the service roster, named at the brief across every domain for one service, and it is already the only cross-domain unit the structure has; a lead does not assemble one, because designations are named by each domain lead onto seats that carry the mark (S8 18.9), and nothing else is needed.

**P5 (Recommended).** Her diagnostic questions are answered by mechanisms that already run: the leading indicators, the pulse, the upward question, the close, and the goal-page table. The survey is refused as a form; the offsite is Session 10's.

**P6 (Recommended).** Lencioni's five dysfunctions are refused as the frame and mapped to the five modes as tabled. The effort-impact matrix is refused as an instrument and absorbed as the reset's cost-and-buy read; the diagnostic orders fixes by the record's causal order among the modes: sequence corruption, node overload, skill invisibility and cooling failure, tension slack last.

**P7 (Recommended).** Her post-diagnosis posture (model and hold accountable) is replaced by the versioned change and its read-back. Her boat is the service roster and needs no assembling.

## 4. The diagnostic specified: the evidence line, and the five modes one by one

### 4.1 The form of one evidence line

Carryover `86akh67wy` asks what a failure-mode evidence line looks like. Session 4 fixed where it sits: one per mode at the house review, "the evidence line from the quarter's captures and closes" (S4 P33), with the weekly feeders on the leads' review agenda under load readings and the week's captures (S4 P26, items 2 and 6). This page fixes its form. An evidence line has seven fields and no others:

1. **Mode.** One of the five, in the record's words. Never a sixth, never a blend.
2. **Object.** House, domain (named by seat of its lead), service roster (named by service and date), seat (named by inventory row), or service period (named by charter). Never a person.
3. **Signal source.** The mechanism record the line is read from, named so the reader can open it: the close of a dated service, a capture, a brief's record, a check-in line, a schedule entry, an inventory row and version, a readiness row, a variety pass entry, a pipeline read line, an infrastructure line, the pulse composite, the feedback channel. A line with no source is incomplete and is not read.
4. **Count.** How many times the signal appeared, as a count of services, reviews, check-ins, captures, or weeks. Never money, never a percentage this program generated, never a figure from anywhere the source boundary excludes.
5. **Compensation.** Which adjacent seats, designations, or mechanisms are carrying the load or absorbing the failure, named by seat or by mechanism. This is the record's "which adjacent nodes are overloaded" (WP p.14), and it is the field that makes tension visible where the record says it is invisible.
6. **The fix the record names,** and the mechanism it touches: for tension slack, re-establishing tension in the domain; for node overload, restoring the separation, carried under the ruling by a designation, the stack, or a procedure; for sequence corruption, system maintenance; for skill invisibility, documentation and access; for cooling failure, removing friction, with the allocation consequence marked as founder-gated (`86akh3t00`).
7. **State.** Present; not present; or cannot read yet, with the reason (no baseline, no roster, no closes, the definition open, the kitchen chef-gated).

Three rules on the line. It is written in the record's vocabulary and canon's register: declarative, ending at its last load-bearing word (Canon 1.0, section 09). It names no person, carries no verdict, and adds no dimension to any review, so that it can never become the back door into Session 13's four dimensions (S6 P33). And it is the same line at every rhythm: the weekly feeder is the line with fields 1 to 3 and 5, the quarterly scorecard line adds 4, 6, and 7, and the reset reads four quarterly lines side by side.

**P8 (Recommended).** The failure-mode evidence line has seven fields: mode, object, signal source, count, compensation, the record's fix and the mechanism it touches, and state. It names no person, carries no figure and no verdict, and is the same line at the leads' review, the house review, and the reset.

### 4.2 Who writes and who reads

Each domain lead writes their domain's lines into their memo for the house review (S4 P32), and marks weekly feeders on the leads' review record under items 2 and 6 as the review runs; the chair records them. The Maitre d writes the floor's, both strands, and the Head of Beverage's line for the bar's seats sits under the Maitre d's read as the lead whose outcomes it serves (S8 P9). The kitchen's lines are chef-gated. The center, the partners, integrates the domains' lines into one line per mode for the house, which is what the house review's scorecard section reads (S3 P10.4). The Operations Lead holds the record and writes no line about a floor person, because the Operations Lead holds no floor check-in (S8 P21); the Operations Lead does write the system-side lines, since sequence corruption's evidence is largely the stack's. The reset reads the year's lines; the gate review reads the lines beside the readiness rows.

### 4.3 Tension slack

The record: a role occupied, the thread present but not carrying load, "other nodes are compensating without visibility. The fix is not replacing the person. It is diagnosing which adjacent nodes are overloaded and re-establishing tension in that domain" (WP p.14).

Evidence it reads, and from which record. From the close: incidents on one seat's section resolved by another seat's range, recoveries made by the room's holder on a section whose server did not escalate, a designation's function performed by someone not named to it, all as counts of services. From the check-in: "what is in the way" lines, in the persons' own words, that name an adjacent seat's load (a runner's line that a section's server does not call; a server's line that the sequence never arrives on their side), never a lead's reading of anyone's manner. From the thermal-load lines the close carries (S4 agenda item 6): a rise on the seats adjacent to one seat, read as a week. From the schedule: a section re-drawn by the room's holder mid-service repeatedly to route around one seat. From the pipeline read: a seat the lead holds open rather than fills below the bar, which Session 5 reads as a slack reading on whether the house can absorb the hole (S5 P14), and which this page reads as the same line with the seat unfilled rather than occupied.
