## 5. Signals of the same kind: recurrence, and what a diagnosis does with an object that repeats

### 5.1 Thresholds of recurrence, in counts of reviews and services

The house already carries three recurrence rules: twice for a moved check-in (S4 P20), three consecutive leads' reviews for an open seat (S5 P14), and a quarter of every service for a designation (S8 18.9). This page generalizes them so that any signal on any object has a stated recurrence at which it moves, and the stack surfaces the move rather than a lead remembering it:

| Recurrence | Where the read goes | What it becomes |
|---|---|---|
| One occurrence | The leads' review that week, under items 2 or 6 | A weekly feeder: mode, object, source, compensation |
| The same signal on the same object across three consecutive leads' reviews | The partners' review, that week | A possible node-overload entry or a structure question, in the record's words for three weeks of compensation |
| The same mode on the same object on two consecutive quarterly scorecards, after the record's fix was applied at the first | The mechanism reset's agenda | A mechanism question: the fix named the wrong mechanism or the wrong mode; continue, change, or end |
| A designation held by one person on every service for a quarter | The mechanism reset | A question about a seat, answered in section 9.3 under the ruling |
| A mode present at a gate review on any object | The gate review's record | A failed readiness row's mode; the gate does not open around it |

No threshold is set in money, and none is set in a figure this program generated; each is a count of reviews or services, which is what the house can count on the stack from the first training service.

### 5.2 A domain that repeats

Carryover `86akh7r0c` asks what a diagnosis does with a domain that produces recruiting signals repeatedly. The answer is that the object of the read moves up: a repeating seat signal becomes a domain read, and a domain read asks three questions in order, none of which is whether the lead hires well.

A domain repeats when any of these holds: a seat in it has passed three consecutive reviews open twice inside a year; two of its seats have opened by tension loss inside one quarter; or its lead has held a seat open as a slack reading on two consecutive quarterly scorecards. At that recurrence the partners' review reads the domain, and the questions are:

1. Is the count wrong? The seat count per charter is a field on the charter and a row count on the inventory (S8 I1). A domain that keeps opening seats it cannot fill at the bar may be carrying a count the charter set before a service ran; the fix is a structure change entry on the count, with the lines as its reason (section 7).
2. Is the sheet wrong? Field 6's entry horizon, field 11's shift shape, and field 13's physical demands are the fields a seat stays open on. A sheet whose horizon is set above what the entry pool holds keeps a hole; the fix is a versioned sheet, which re-reads every open candidate (S5 P13).
3. Is the lead's seat overloaded? A domain whose lead is holding the room, the door, and the check-in rotation on the seat's absent services will produce open seats as a symptom, because the pipeline read's action is the lead's (S5 P28). This is section 4.4's line and section 12's read.

Only after those three does anything about the domain's hiring get read, and then by Session 6's calibration read, which reads the packets against the pages and writes to neither (S6 P41), never by the diagnostic. A domain that produces tension-loss departures repeatedly is read as cooling failure first (section 4.7), with the exit interviews as the building's lines; the diagnostic does not conclude that the domain's people leave because of its lead, because the record's rule is that universal problems are design problems (WP p.17) and a domain is not a lead's character.

### 5.3 A seat that repeats, and a service period that repeats

A seat opened by tension loss twice inside a year is a sheet question before it is anything else: the horizon, the shift shape, the physical demands, and the range are the fields that make a seat hard to hold, and each is versioned. The person who left is not the object; the exit interview's building lines are.

A service period that repeats is read through its charter. A period whose incidents cluster in one disturbance class across a quarter has the variety pass re-run for that period (S8 P46). A period whose dependent interfaces field goes empty in practice, which the interface read surfaces (S8 I10), goes to the gate review as a period that has become a separate restaurant, which the ledger says is not opened and, if open, is the record's own "worse version of dinner" (WP p.2). A period that repeatedly consumes the house's bandwidth is, under the bandwidth order, restructured before it is defended on its results (S3's attachment to `86akh3t00`), and the diagnostic states that consequence as founder-gated rather than applying it.

**P17 (Recommended).** Recurrence thresholds as tabled: one occurrence is a weekly feeder; three consecutive leads' reviews on the same object is a partners' entry; two consecutive quarterly lines after a fix is a reset question; a designation held every service for a quarter is a seat question at the reset; a mode at a gate is a failed row's mode. All in counts of reviews and services, surfaced by the stack.

A domain that produces departures repeatedly is read as cooling failure first.

## 6. The rhythm: the weekly read, the quarterly scorecard, the reset, the gate, the freeze, and the first run

### 6.1 The four rhythms

Carryover `86akh67wy` states the two rhythms the diagnostic runs on: "the quarterly scorecard is the read, the reset is the correction." Two more already exist and the diagnostic uses them without adding a meeting, which is the four-part entry test's first demand (S4 2.2).

Weekly, the leads' review. The diagnostic adds no agenda item to Session 4's eight (S4 P26). It is the vocabulary items 2 (the week's captures) and 6 (load readings) are written in: a capture or a load reading that carries a mode carries it as a mark on the line, with the object and the source, and the chair records it. The pipeline read's line sits under item 6 (S5 P28), the training-infrastructure line is a standing item Session 7 placed (S7 I16), and the interface read is item 4 (S8 I10). The weekly read costs the review nothing it was not already spending; what it buys is that the quarterly line is a sum of dated feeders rather than a memory.

Quarterly, the house review. The scorecard section Session 3 set and Session 4 proceduralized (S3 P10.4; S4 P32) is the read: one line per mode for the house, integrated by the center from the domain lines in each lead's memo, read after the leading indicators and before the goal-page table. The section also carries the read-back column of section 7.4 for every change made since the last review, and the list of reads it could not make and why.

At the reset, the correction. The reset runs after dinner's first quarter, after the first full year, and annually at the house review nearest dinner's anniversary (S4 P39). It reads the year's lines side by side, mode by mode, and for every mechanism a line touched it continues, changes by versioned decision, or ends it. Its first read is the Maitre d's seat (S8 P14; section 12). A mode present on two consecutive quarterly lines after a fix is its agenda's second item (section 5.1).

At the gate, the readiness test. Each phase gate runs the readiness test and the variety pass (S3 12.3; S8 P46), and a failed row's mode is the gate's diagnostic line. A gate does not open around a mode; the ledger's rule is that a row depending on a gated item is read as failed until it closes.

### 6.2 Inside dinner's first-quarter freeze

Session 4's seam protection freezes the room's cadence mechanisms for dinner's first quarter except the committed class and shift-close maintenance, and holds first-quarter goals as states and baselines only (S4 2.7). The diagnostic runs inside the freeze, and the freeze decides what it may do:

What it reads in the first quarter: everything. The closes are written, the briefs are recorded, the check-ins run every two weeks inside the cohort's plans, the leads' review runs weekly, the pulse runs monthly, the pipeline read runs, the infrastructure line runs. The weekly feeders accumulate from the first service, and the first house review, at the quarter's end, writes the first five lines. Nothing about reading is frozen, because reading costs the room nothing and the quarter's lines are the baseline the first reset needs.

What it may change in the first quarter: shift-close maintenance, which the record's daily loop already is, "the process that allowed the failure gets updated" (WP p.13), logged as care; a parameter inside a range the leads' review already holds (a response window shortened on a sheet, S5 P29; a check-in moved, S4 P20); a document versioned on the build side (a sheet, a module); a designation named differently at the next brief, which is a lead's act and not a mechanism change (S8 18.9). What it may not change: the check-in's interval, the brief's spine or block, a range's width, a trigger's destination, a designation's rule, the leads' review's day, the close's fields, or anything else on the room's mechanism map. A diagnostic line raised inside the quarter that would change any of those goes to the partners' page marked held for the first reset, with its date, so that the reset opens on a list rather than a recollection.

The one exception the freeze already allows is a seat opened by tension loss, which is recruiting and not the room's cadence (S5 P14); the departure's line (section 9.1) is written in the quarter and the seat opens.
