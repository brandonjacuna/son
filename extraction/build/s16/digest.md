# S16 digest: manage your time and energy, foster relationships, consider your career (book pp. 485-503)

Two rulings govern this material. First, a founder's commitments outside the house are referred to only as "a founder's external load" or "commitments outside the house," reasoned about structurally, never described in nature, for Brandon, Dominic, or the chef partner. Second, every proposal about a founder's time, energy, relationships, and career is developed from the record and from what earlier sessions placed on the founders' seats, argued and offered for confirmation or rewrite. Nothing about any founder's values, interiority, working style, strengths, failure modes, or pay is recorded as a position. The same holds for Dominic and the chef partner; no one has written either's self-description.

## Part 1: what's worth keeping

### The gap: her chapter assumes a manager above; Sŏn's founders have none

Her Conclusion assumes a manager with a manager above, peers beside, and a company that survives if the manager is at zero. At Sŏn the founders have no manager and the leads have no one above them; the three seats above which there is no one are the ones whose overload nothing in the system can feel. That is the shape of the gap this material addresses.

The white paper places the founders concretely: "Two of its founders own everything outside the kitchen... One owns the concept, the operating systems, and the experience. The other owns the capital, the financial architecture, and the technology build... Brandon Acuña-Cardona is the public voice and the origin of the operating concepts. Dominic Thomas owns the financial model and the machine" (WP p.3). The chef partner "owns the culinary vision as an equal founding partner" (WP p.3) and "owns culinary execution during service, which is not the same as culinary direction overall" (WP p.10). "The financial rigor is not a slide prepared for the raise. It is a founder" (WP p.5).

The founding posture is built to remove founder-dependency: "The preparation that answers the objection is not more years. It is a code-bearing organization built so it does not depend on either founder's memory" (WP p.5). "The mark of these founders is a company built to run without them" (WP p.6). The chef-dependency objection gets the same answer: "the business does not rest on the survival of any one person in it" (WP p.37).

The founders are also sized as a load the record itself tracks: the content engine's volume is built "without burning the founders out... multiplication, not heroic effort" (WP p.30); streaming is "hours with the founders directly" (WP p.30); the content approval framework has a founder consultation and an unnamed leadership review (WP p.31).

The founders are also the trap the record names: "Restaurant founders build systems for themselves without realizing it, then blame employees when those systems do not work for anyone else. Sŏn has done it too, which is how the team knows the trap" (WP p.17). "Systems get built for how the founder thinks, learned, and succeeded" (WP p.17).

The founders sit in the operator's old integration-layer seat that the stack is meant to absorb (WP p.21), and the record's leading indicators (employee NPS, cultural labor score, customer recognition rate, WP p.12, p.22) read the team's bandwidth but read the founders nowhere.

What the record does not hold: any founder's values, work style, strengths, failure modes, feedback style, hours, or presence by service period; an operating agreement, a vote, a deadlock, a founder's pay or exit (the record never mentions the operating agreement at all); a read of the center; a founder's place on the web of the organization.

**Option: read "personal, not organizational" as a split.** Reasoning: the founders' seats, loads, absence, read, review, pay place, departure, absorber, and relationships-as-mechanisms are organizational; a founder's hours, protection order, energy, relationships as relationships, and path are personal and proposed only to the ceiling for the founder to confirm or rewrite.

**Option: refuse "founder operating discipline" as the deliverable's name.** Reasoning: the record locates stability in the system, not a person's will ("stability is a property of the system"); a founder whose bandwidth is protected by their own discipline is exactly the founder the house depends on, which is the memory failure and the cooling failure at once. What survives of "discipline" is the founder's own hand-written account of what they look like under load (prompt H of the working-with-me document), which no record can write for them.

**Option: treat "what gets protected" as two layers.** Reasoning: layer one is what the house protects about a founder's attention by design, and what it refuses to let depend on a founder; layer two is a proposed protection order for a founder's own time, argued from the metabolic sequence and offered for confirmation.

The three Chapter 1 exercises (values, work style, strengths) stand as originally specified: founder self-instruments completed alone before writing, still the only place a founder's values, style, and strengths are written, and written by the founder. One addition: the strengths inventory's "does the house depend on this strength in you personally, or has it moved into the system" column now feeds the founder load ledger, so a personally-held strength becomes a ledger row with a hand-over date. No founder's answers to any of the three exercises appear anywhere in this record.

### The founder load ledger

The ledger is the center's first honest inventory: every function placed on a founder's seat by the record or by an earlier decision, with the seat, whether it's held by design or by default, its hand-over date or closing condition, and what reads it at the reset. It is a record of loads on seats, not a read of any founder's capacity, feelings, or performance, and not a to-do list.

Three marks: **by design** (the load sits on the seat permanently or for a stated period because the seat is the closest competent owner), **by default** (the load sits there only because no lead yet exists to hold it, with a stated moment after which continued holding is overload), and **record-native** (the white paper places it there directly).

Loads carried on the ledger (non-brand rows only): the founders' domains themselves (WP p.3, p.5); the chef seat's culinary domain; the operating system as Brandon's seat's responsibility and the stack as Dominic's; the content engine's cadence, the documented build, streaming, and leadership review of content (WP pp.30-31); the capital raise as a load on Dominic's domain with Brandon's seat as public voice in it, never an amount, term, valuation, investor, or date; the chef partner search (gates the kitchen chain); construction; the hiring partner role (opens every seat, runs every pipeline, decides every packet) until leads arrive six weeks before the dinner gate; the founder interview stage as the last scored stage; the dinner-in-the-room read and its pre-opening substitute; interviewer training (founders train each other) and the founders as the only interviewers pre-opening in the languages they hold; reference calls for lead seats; the why block delivered in person through dinner's first quarter; the first wave's mentoring; module authorship and approval; pre-opening translation of founding pages; Class B/C escalations after service; the three disturbance-class resolutions (threshold value, bandwidth order, brand crisis); emergency closures beyond one service period; a live risk's daily written state; the existential-risk register's ownership; the partners' cadence (weekly review, monthly domain read, quarterly gate review, annual reset); the knowledge-transfer register; the leads' monthly check-in/review/career-conversation/window/flag-conversation holding by default; interim holding of an open lead seat's domain; second-reader role in a flag read where a lead is the subject; the decision about a person's staying (with counsel); the departure conversation; counsel engagement and the counsel questions register; five module halves pre-opening (interviewer, coaching, mechanics and review, performance, departure); the parameters register's founder-owned rows; the acoustic consultation "with a founder who holds an educational background in audio engineering" (WP p.21, unnamed which founder).

**Option: table the ledger as one row per load with seat, source, mark, hand-over date or closing condition, and what reads it at reset.** Reasoning: every by-default row needs a date or a held decision, and a by-default load past its date with no overload flag on the record is itself something the reset should catch. The ledger reads loads on seats, nothing about any founder.

**Option: treat three seat assignments in the ledger (the hiring partner as Brandon's seat, construction and the operations lead's pairing as Dominic's, interim versioning authority) as the team's recommendation rather than the record's fact.** Reasoning: the founders can confirm or reassign each; the ledger's rows move if they do.

The chef seat's column depends on which branch is chosen for the chef partner's status: under one branch the chef partner is a founder and the chef's rows read like the other two founders' (domain read, reserved classes, kitchen check-in holder, founder stage, why-block kitchen half, translations); under the other branch the chef seat is a lead's and reads like any lead's rows. Either way the team's view is that the kitchen's loads should sit on the same ledger with the same marks.

### Who reads the center

The record measures the team's bandwidth with leading indicators, names cooling failure (running people at capacity and calling it dedication, WP p.13), sizes the founders' own workload around burnout (WP p.30), but reads the founders nowhere. Because nothing is assigned, reviewed, or escalated by a person standing above another, no manager-style read can be built for the founders. Session findings elsewhere note the founder side of ten conditions for agency is stronger than the employee side on rules, range, and say (because the founders wrote the rules) and weaker on time and a path, because the record was written by the founders about the team.

The read on the center has to (1) read records, never a founder's feelings; (2) be held by no one standing above; (3) exist before opening, since the loads are heaviest pre-opening.

**Option: three readers, all in writing, none above.** Reasoning: (a) each partner reads another partner's domain page monthly and writes a load line about that founding seat, in the same asynchronous, non-hierarchical form already used for the domain read; when a third partner exists, the line rotates so no partner is another's standing reader; (b) the leads write their own "read on the center" on their own pages, naming mechanisms and never a founder by name, aggregated by mechanism (never by person) at the partners' review; (c) at the annual reset, the founder load ledger and the year's load lines become the second read, after the read on the operations-lead-type seat. No meeting is added and no person stands above anyone.

The load line reads only counts on records: node overload (a by-default load past its date with no flag; a call deferred to a founder on the floor; a founder as an alert's default actor; a seat opened/packet decided by the hiring role after a lead exists; an interim-holder line standing past two reviews; an item marked "waiting on founder"); cooling failure (reviews missed or late; domain-read questions not written in a month; the knowledge-transfer register untouched for a month; live risks with missed daily states; the content engine's output-per-session ratio falling below the record's own sizing); sequence corruption (a reserved-class decision taken unilaterally; a required consultation skipped; a version published with no recorded decision; a public statement reaching the public before the internal team); memory failure (dated knowledge-transfer items past their date; an outside relationship with no record; a build reason held only in a founder's head).

**Option: the line concludes only a load and a carrier, never anything about the founder as a person.** Reasoning: the read exists so a load moves, not so a founder is read. It may never become a rating, a case file, a performance record, a read of feelings/energy/mood/health/life/outside commitments, an item at any brief, a trigger for the operating agreement or a vote, a founder's judgment of another founder written as anything but a question, a lead's impression of a founder, a survey, an outside coach's instrument, or a 360.

**Option: a founder's learning tempo is set by dates the house already holds, never by a person.** Reasoning: pre-opening it's set by the interviewer sequence before the first lead's first interview, the five module halves each gated to the act they unlock, and the languages the founders hold as a critical-path fact before the first why block; in service it's set by the domain read and the reset. Prompt K ("what I do not know yet"), dated and revisited each quarter and reset, is the founder's own stated learning agenda, written by the founder and read by the partners.

**Option: prompt H (hand-written account of what a founder looks like under load) is the read on the center's other half.** Reasoning: each founder names in it which of the load-line's sources they expect to show first in their own seat, so the record's read and the founder's self-read can be compared at the reset without either overriding the other.

### What the house protects by design, and what it never depends on a founder for

Protections already in place, collected as one rule (the house protects a founder's attention by never needing it at the wrong moment, never by asking a founder to guard it): the absence rule (founders can be absent a full service period with no decision deferred to their return); every escalation path terminating at a founder does so only post-service, never mid-service; no founder is a default actor on any automated alert; hand-over dates exist for defaulted loads; a founder's impression is never a source, trigger, or override; no build meeting happens during service hours and room mechanisms freeze during the first quarter of dinner service; disagreements in the top reserved decision class are a hold, not a deadlock, so a founder at zero bandwidth can't be pressured into a change; the knowledge-transfer register makes what a founder knows a dated system to-do rather than a standing dependency; the senior person present speaks last.

What the house never depends on a founder for: their memory (anything needed becomes a dated item); their presence on the floor; their approval mid-service; their reading of a person (never a source/trigger/override); their hours (no mechanism depends on a founder's hours except the partners' cadence at closed hours); their voice in the room past the first quarter (hands over once an internal-voice holder is named); being first at anything past its date; the content engine's volume by heroic effort (read by ratio, never hours); their judgment of another founder (never more than a question on a page).

**Option: a founder's external load is a stated fact, never a described commitment.** Reasoning: every founder has commitments outside the house; the house does not know their shape and does not ask. Each founder states, in a working-with-me prompt, their presence by service period and by week as a fact (in the same form used for any seat's shift-shape field), including the consequences of outside commitments in general terms only; the house reads that statement as an availability fact, never a commitment; no mechanism may depend on presence beyond the statement; the read on the center reads only the consequences on records (a review moved, a live-risk state missed), never the commitment itself. The house must be able to run every service, brief, close, leads' review, check-in, and in-service mechanism with every founder unreachable for a full service period and, further, for a full week of the in-service cadence, with any reserved-class change held at the status quo. The same treatment extends to Dominic and the chef partner.

**Option: a founder on the floor is bound by the same live-service rules as anyone, more tightly than a lead.** Reasoning: a founder holds no floor range unless named at that service's brief for a designation; makes a live call only inside a range they hold; corrects no one and sends no message about a person on the floor; carries any hypothesis about a person only to that person's check-in holder, never to the person; a call deferred to a founder on the floor is a node-overload entry and a run of the founder's own accountability principle on the mechanism that let the call reach them. The moment this bites hardest is dinner's first quarter, when a founder who has run opening service before will want to intervene faster than the room is ready to let them, and the rule exists for exactly that founder, because the team must learn to hold the room from its own designations, not from a founder's eye.

**Option: Brandon's seat holds the operating system's record and versions, never its running.** Reasoning: the leads chair their own review, the stack places every check-in and review, room-holding roles hold the room, the reset corrects, and the partners' review reads records the leads produced rather than attending to learn what happened. The line between owning and integrating is the line between versioning a spine and running a meeting; a founder who chairs a leads' review, places a check-in by hand, or holds a lead's people directly has crossed it, and the load line reads the crossing.

The cultural labor score's definition (the question it's built from, the population, the rhythm) is unresolved and named as Brandon's to supply; whatever it reads about the team, it does not read the founders, and the read on the center does not wait for it.

### Manage your time and energy: the protection order (book's ch. on time/energy)

Most of her tactics already have a Sŏn mechanism doing their work without requiring founder will: her good/bad-day calendar marks become the seat's load line plus prompt H; her monthly review of undone tasks becomes the partners' review's asks list (each item with an owner and date) plus the knowledge-transfer register; her "delegate" is refused as a manager's act and replaced by the decision-rights register and designations; her "disappoint people with your time" becomes the loop window (days to acknowledge, longer to close) and the fact that nothing at the center is answered live; her "compartmentalize" and walking 1:1 are absorbed by the seam between build and service hours; her "ask for help" becomes the cross-domain ask on the partners' page, with the overload showing as a record before it's a conversation; her Eisenhower matrix is refused in favor of one goal per unit and one thing per domain per week.

**Option: a four-tier protection order in the order of the metabolic sequence (bandwidth, then joy, then retention, then results).** Reasoning, tier by tier: **first**, protect the seat's bandwidth itself, the content engine's volume is read by its output ratio and never held by hours, and hand-over dates must hold (a founder who keeps a defaulted load past its date to avoid disruption has spent bandwidth the sequence says comes first); **second**, what only the seat can do and that gates a date, named from the record as: the chef partner search/ruling (gates the kitchen chain), the founding charter's ratification (gates the why block and every cohort start date), the parameters register's offer-gating rows (with the capital-side domain and counsel), counsel's urgent questions before the first paid hire, and the two leads' hiring; the capital raise sits here too, as a load whose closing date isn't this program's to set, with only its public-voice component named as Brandon's; **third**, what only the seat can do and can wait for a closed hour, canon-independent versioning work, the mission/principles wording, the existential-risk register's contents, scheduled like the build cadence's closed hours and never done live; **fourth**, what the seat hands over or refuses, being the integration layer, being a mid-service destination, holding leads' check-ins past the point a holder is named, being a default alert actor, holding specialist functions by default past the point someone is named, holding translation past the point paid translators are named, the founder interview stage for non-leadership seats past the first reset.

What the order asks: that dates hold and the ratio gets read; that presence by period be stated as a schedulable fact; that second-tier items get dated on the partners' page before the first cohort start date is committed. What it does not ask: particular hours, a "boundary to guard" (mechanisms guard it), or any statement of what a founder's energy is or where it comes from, those remain the founder's own to write.

**Option (the stated alternative, not recommended): a founder holds every defaulted load personally through the first quarter, on the ground that the team is new and a founder's eye is the best the house has.** Reasoning against: the record's own diagnosis of failed restaurants ("the schedule that does not post, the books that do not reconcile... are caused by architecture," WP p.5) applies in reverse, a house that runs its first quarter on a founder's eye builds an architecture it will have to demolish while open. Stated so the choice is a choice, not a default.

**Option: apply the same four-tier frame to Dominic and the chef partner, populated from each seat's own ledger rows, with none of the answers written.** Reasoning: for Dominic, the stack's own sizing (not his hours) belongs in tier one for the same reason the content engine does; for the chef partner (if a founder), the kitchen's build sequence and hires are tier two. Neither seat's actual answers are the team's to write.

### Foster relationships

Her chapter assumes a hierarchy the founders don't have (manager above, peers beside). Each relationship becomes a mechanism rather than a conversation, or a founder-gated statement where the record leaves it to the person.

**Partners with each other.** Two mechanisms, neither a meeting about the relationship: the working-with-me exchange (a seam document between non-overlapping domains, for founders who rarely watch each other work) and the domain read (monthly written questions on each other's pages, now including the load line). Her "two levels up and down" has no levels to run on; its Sŏn form is "two domains out", each founder's own prompt draws their domain's edges and names the decisions nearest the edge that belong to another seat. Her "never surprise with late bad news" becomes a no-surprises rule running through the domain page's weekly state and the live-risk line's daily state. Her "help your manager be their best" has no manager; its peer form is a prompt naming what each partner needs from the other's domain, feeding the partners' review's asks list.

**Option: a partner's working style changes the weekly review's conduct without changing its fixed, versioned agenda.** Reasoning: four places the agenda doesn't fix, whether a domain page is read before the review or presented live, whether a required consultation happens in writing or in the room, whether a dissent on a top-tier decision is written or said, and who holds the meeting's clock, are set from each partner's own working-with-me prompts, at each gate review, from documents the team has not read and will not write.

**Founders with the leads.** The paired-partner relationship is the only manager-shaped relationship a lead has (the house avoids the word "manager"). Leverage runs both ways: the lead gets unblocking, resource advocacy, and context through named mechanisms rather than favors; the partner gets the lead's own written "read on the center," naming mechanisms and never a person, which is the whole of "managing up" at Sŏn. Her "check the fruit, wait for a founder to be ready" is right in patience, wrong in form: a lead's idea to change a mechanism enters a standing change log and, during the first quarter, is held for the first reset with a date; a lead's idea to add a mechanism must pass a four-part entry test (bandwidth cost, benefit, owner, record), a mechanism that can't answer doesn't enter, which is her "build the lightweight version first" as a rule. What a lead never does is bring an idea to a founder in a room; nothing is assigned or escalated in a room, and the founder speaks last if present at all.

**Founders with the team, and the customer at the table.** Three mechanisms: the why block (spoken in person by a founder to every wave through the first quarter, in the cohort's languages, the founders' one standing relationship with every person, delivering the concept's voice, not a founder's personality); the founders' floor presence, governed by the two-kinds/live-speech rules above; and feedback, which reaches any person only through that person's check-in holder, never directly. One refusal: a founder holds no person's check-in except a lead's by default, reads no page but a lead's, and is never the destination for a concern about a person. The founders' languages are a critical-path fact (not interiority): where no founder holds a cohort's language, the why block runs with the founder present and speaking and a named interpreter role that writes nothing, so the concept is spoken by its author and heard in the person's language.

**Option: a founder's read at the interview stage is a read of the building, not of the candidate.** Reasoning: given no formal interviewer training or anchors, a founder is the one person pre-opening who knows what the building can't yet supply (holding the "unbuilt list"); so a founder's read is limited to whether anything the candidate said touches a stated non-negotiable, and whether the building has, on record, what this person said they need. A founder's "no" must name one of those or it isn't a no. At a hosted meal used as part of interviewing, a partner reads the candidate as the record's own customer archetype would, not on personal taste; scoring the meal on personal enjoyment is scoring the wrong thing.

**Outside relationships.** Four, each a load with a rule and no figure: the capital raise (the white paper is "a diligence document, not an offer," WP p.2; the founders "would rather hand over the machine in full... than describe a room and ask anyone to take it on faith," WP p.39; nothing about amount, terms, investors, or dates is a source here); the public voice (Brandon's seat under the content framework, with an inward-first rule that a public statement must reach the internal team before or at the moment it reaches the public, and that a founder speaking a crisis aloud to the team before it's written is itself a rule breach); creators and partners (an access-over-cash relationship per WP p.31, with the founders hosting access and the house showing the role and process, never a specific person); the chef partner search (the founders search using their own reach, with a final round that is a shared meal). Her "coffee with leaders you admire" and general friendships are the founders' own; the house records none of them.

**Founders' values, for a lead who wants to understand them.** The company's values are not the founders' personal values and aren't derived from them (an explicit refusal from the earlier values exercise). The company's intent lives in the charter (mission, principles, the metabolic sequence, the strategy's trade-offs). A lead never asks a founder about values directly, in a 1:1 or an offsite (both refused); a lead reads the charter and the trade-offs list, and where the charter is silent on a trade-off the house is about to make, that becomes a written question, not a conversation. The founders' own personal values remain a private prompt, exchanged among the partners only, issued to no lead.

### Consider your career

The team's path (career conversations, an internal promotion pool, competencies visible before anyone starts, "advancement requires preparation, not permission," WP p.14) is already built elsewhere and not rebuilt here.

**Option: define a founder's path structurally, without naming where it leads.** Reasoning: in a house built to run without its founders, a founder's path is the sequence of hand-overs by which a seat's domain moves from being held by the founder to being held by the record and the leads (readable directly off the ledger), freeing the founder for the next thing only that seat can do. The record's own accounts of a founder's career, the team's path, and the company's horizon all point outward, "gated on proof, never a promise" (WP p.38); the record's account of Brandon's own career is the same shape at the person scale, "into the best version of each segment... then out, carrying the piece worth keeping" (WP p.5). Where a founder's path actually leads is the founder's own to say; the structural definition doesn't answer it. A founder stuck holding defaulted loads past their dates is a founder whose path has stopped, and the ledger reads that as overload before anyone reads it as a stalled career, one place the house's read and a founder's path genuinely converge.

**Option: keep her private career document and the working-with-me document separate instruments.** Reasoning: her document (reread every six to eighteen months, private, about what you're good at and enjoy) and the working-with-me document (exchanged, dated, versioned, revised at set intervals) do different jobs and shouldn't be merged. Each founder may keep a private career document as their own; the house never holds, reads, or references it. Her six/twelve/eighteen-month rereading rhythm and her advice to "suspend disbelief" about what keeps you going are both refused as house rhythms; the working-with-me document's own dated "what I don't know yet" prompt exists so a founder can write "I don't know yet" instead.

**Option: state what the founders owe the team's path in return, as dated commitments already made elsewhere.** Reasoning: competency requirements and unlock periods exist before the cohort arrives; a pool for internal promotion is read before any outside hire; career conversations happen once per person; the record's succession goal (WP p.19, hiring managers from outside becoming rare) means the functions founders hold by default are exactly the first seats the promotion pool is meant to fill, a founder keeping a defaulted load past its date has closed a path someone in the pool was on.

### The founders read as the house reads everyone, to a ceiling

A governing rule throughout: nothing is assigned, reviewed, or escalated by a person standing above another, and the founders are the only seats above which no one stands. So the founders get every mechanism the house runs for anyone, minus anything that needs a holder above them.

**Founders' own review.** A person's normal review needs a check-in holder, a seat sheet, and competency rows; a founder has none, so there is no review, and none should be built under another name. What partially reads a founder anyway: the domain read covers "what the domain produced" monthly, in writing; the load line covers "what the building failed to supply the seat"; conduct is read exactly as for anyone through a flag or a written range-breach note at the partners' review; self-directed growth has no unlock to measure and isn't read (a dated "what I'm learning" prompt exists but nothing scores it). None of this is a review: no interval, no rating, and it never reaches pay, since a founder's pay is outside this program's design entirely (the operating agreement's).

**Legal/counsel question:** whether the domain read should formally use this four-dimension review form, or stick to readiness rows and goal pages alone with conduct left to the flag, is an open decision for the founders, not something the team resolves.

**A founder as a flag's subject.** The non-negotiables bind a founder exactly as they bind anyone; a house whose founders were exempt would make everyone else disposable at the founders' hands. Path: a flag naming a founder as subject goes the same day to both leads and every non-subject partner; the read is held jointly by the two leads (with counsel where required) and by no partner, since any other partner is the subject's peer in every top-tier decision; the founder hears what was flagged from the other partner, with a reader present, before anything is written; if the finding involves a stated non-negotiable, the two leads write a closed-form record, since a founder has no personal file, and it's read by relevant pools and eligibility checks; everything past that point (the decision about staying, terms) belongs to the operating agreement and counsel, not this system.

**Option (not fully settled, offered as the house's own protection): while a flag read or finding is open, the founder is off the floor and out of any room the flagged concern would otherwise reach, as a rough equivalent of a paid hold for a seat with no schedule or house-set pay.** Reasoning: the same protection anyone else in a similar state would get, extended for the sake of everyone else in the room. Stated as an alternative, not a recommendation, because the act's object here is a person.

**The absorber above the founders.** The record's line that "management absorbs blame from staff" (WP p.17) has no equivalent above the founders; by design, nothing does. What actually absorbs a founder's failure: (1) the same accountability principle run on the founders' own domains first, at the partners' review, with an evidence note written by the other partner naming no person; (2) the record itself, a founder's mechanism failure becomes a process update and a version, and the version is the absorption; (3) the reserved-decision classes held at the status quo, since neither partner can unilaterally turn the other's failure into an unagreed change. What does not absorb it: a lead (that would reverse the load-bearing structure the whole system exists to avoid), the team, or the public. The record's own public admission, "Sŏn has done it too, which is how the team knows the trap" (WP p.17), is read as the model: a founder's failures are absorbed by being written into the record, since the record is the only thing above the founders the house actually holds.

**A founder's departure.** The record never describes one; the operating agreement governs first, and this program doesn't read or interpret it. The house's part is continuity only: the same three-pass departure read used for anyone, applied to the founding seat as the node (what the seat held per the ledger; a memory-failure pass on the knowledge-transfer register); a versioned structure-change record with the departure as its stated reason; the seat's ledger rows moving to the remaining partner as a flagged, dated overload item; a scheduled hand-over conversation running against the records in the final period; and the leads learning of the change through the normal structure-change notification path, which carries coverage information only, never anything about the person. What is explicitly not the house's to design: the reason, vote, terms, pay, equity, notice, non-compete, incapacity/death provisions, or whether founders are employees for any purpose, all the operating agreement's and counsel's.

**Legal/counsel questions, listed as such (verbatim in substance):** whether the operating agreement already holds departure, incapacity, deadlock, and removal provisions this system's continuity design must defer to; whether founders are employees for any purpose; how a founder-as-flag-subject is handled beyond what this record designs; how the founders' place in the pool (in or out) bears on the pool's legal character as compensation; what records and lawful interim holders apply to a founder's departure; whether the founders' own working documents and the load ledger count as records for any legal purpose; and whether a founder's outside commitments raise any duty under the operating agreement or under law. None of these questions are answered here; the mechanisms are designed to run regardless of the answer.

**The partners' place in the incentive pool.** Argued position, not decided: the incentive pool is described as distributed "to the whole team, the culinary team included" (WP p.20); the founders are not employees in that sense, so the argued reading is that the partners sit outside the pool and are compensated under the operating agreement instead, under one branch, the chef partner is included in that same "outside the pool" treatment; under the other branch, the chef seat is a salaried seat inside the pool. The pool's mechanical inputs (four measurable factors) have no analog for a founder seat (no seat weight, no entry horizon), so there's nothing to compute even if a founder were included; putting a founder on the same compensation surface as the team would also put founder pay somewhere the transparency design doesn't intend it to be seen. No figure, percentage, or equity term appears anywhere in this reasoning. Marked as requiring both the founders' agreement and counsel's read on how a partner's working a service shift interacts with the pool's character as a wage.

**Five module halves and default holding of leads' first check-ins.** Both founders complete five training-module halves pre-opening (interviewer, coaching, compensation mechanics and review, performance, departure), each gated to the first act it unlocks, paced by the hiring calendar; no founder holds a first act before completing the half that gates it. Until a permanent holder is named, the founders hold the first leads' check-ins, reviews, career conversations, and flag conversations by default, strictly in the check-in's fixed form (never as an open-ended "how's it going" conversation from a founder).

**The founders' version of "nothing before the first paid hour."** That protection is written for people the house pays by the hour or salary; founders are paid under the operating agreement, so it doesn't extend to them directly. What extends in reverse: nothing the house depends on may rest on a founder's hours that no record can see. Every founder load on the ledger must have a record of its own (a review held, a state written); a load whose only evidence is "a founder worked" doesn't belong on the ledger. This is why unpaid founder translation work, unlike paid lead translation work, is tracked on the ledger as a defaulted load with a hand-over date to named, paid translators, not treated as founder generosity.

### Closing the loop with the earlier founder principles

Six founder-facing principles set earlier in the program are read against everything built since, with each one's status simply noted (not re-litigated here): the principle that the house runs without its founders has hardened into concrete mechanisms (the absence rule, post-service-only escalation, dated hand-overs); the principle that founder cognition isn't the default now has a date attached (it holds as a promise until the first reset, then as a running mechanism); the principle of naming what's open became the register/marks system used throughout, with one internal gap (how the partners disagree with each other in the room) still left to their own working-with-me prompts; the principle of blaming the process rather than a person now has an absorption mechanism at every level, including, newly, the founders'; the principle that authority lives in domains stands, with the deadlock rule and reserved-class list still open, pending the operating agreement; the principle of bandwidth before results is now built out for the founders too, via the load line and prompt H.

The service-side and build-side operating rhythms (metronome) held in every part; the one open piece, the founders' own learning tempo, is answered by the gates-and-cadence rule above. The earlier decision to drop "manager" from the vocabulary entirely stands; the paired-partner relationship remains the one manager-shaped relationship, with no name of its own. The working-with-me document's eleven prompts stand as originally specified, with additions accumulated from every session since (each stated as "a record to feed" or "a rule to answer," never as content), presence by period, languages held, closed-hour availability, dependency notes tying strengths to the ledger, learning agenda, and so on.
