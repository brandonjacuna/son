# Lens: employee-harm (harsh only)

Could any line of the target, applied as written by an ordinary manager on an ordinary bad day, reach an employee as harm?

Harm here means: an unfair gate (hours, pay, or advancement withheld on a check that does not measure the skill), a discipline trigger (a developmental note that becomes a write-up, a log line that becomes a file), a privacy breach (health, immigration, family, or complaint detail where a peer can read it), uneven enforcement (a rule one manager can apply and another cannot), retaliation exposure (a complaint followed by a schedule change), or legal exposure for the person or for Sŏn (wage and hour, classification, leave, discrimination, Texas-specific items in `kb/domains/texas-employment.md`, read the relevant section only).

Check against the people canon, which the target may not weaken:
- Brandon alone decides every personnel action until a general manager exists; seats and systems recommend only.
- Developmental feedback never moves into a discipline file automatically; only Brandon carries a specific dated incident across, and the team member sees it.
- A readiness-gate "not yet" never changes hours or pay; the person stays on paid training hours. No retry cap; a second not-yet audits the gate.
- Where the floor task does not need English, a check may run in the trainee's first language.
- When Brandon is a party, he recuses; Dominic decides after counsel reviews first.
- The culture read is seen in aggregate only.

Trace the path: who applies the line, with what information, and what the employee can do about it. Critical when the harm is hard to undo (a record, a termination, a pay effect, a legal filing). Say what you checked.

For a seat (agent or skill): whether the seat's rules can be read as deciding or recording a personnel action; whether an output lands in a place an employee cannot see or answer.

Zero flags is a valid result. Never soften a critical finding because the author meant well.
