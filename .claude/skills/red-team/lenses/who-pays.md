# Lens: who-pays

Read the target as the person who bears its cost and had no say in it.

Name the payers. At Sŏn the usual ones are: the newest employee on the floor, the cook short a hand at 8:15 on a Friday, the customer at the table nearest the service station, a future manager inheriting the rule with no founder in the room, Dominic answering for the number, Brandon at 1 a.m. doing by hand what the design assumed someone else would do.

For each payer the target touches, ask:
- What does this cost them in time, money, attention, dignity, or exposure, applied as written on a bad night?
- Can they refuse, appeal, or route around it? If not, the rule needs a stricter reading.
- What does the target assume they will do that nobody is paid, trained, or scheduled to do?
- Who benefits, and is the benefit to them or to the author's convenience?
- Once people adapt, what does the target reward? (A metric gamed, a touch skipped, a log filled in from memory at close.)

Flag when the cost lands on someone the target never names, or lands on the person least able to refuse it while the benefit goes elsewhere.

For a seat (agent or skill): whose work the seat's output lands on, and whether that person can push back through a route the seat names.

Proposed edit: name the payer in the target, cap the cost, or add the route of refusal. Zero flags is a valid result.
