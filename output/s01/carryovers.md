## 1. The record holds two accounts of the chef seat

**Target session:** S2 (`86ajgmhfd`), Ch2, founding documents

**Why S2 needs it.** Session 2 writes the founding documents, and the existing carryover on founder job descriptions (`86ajgnhp7`) will describe the chef partner's seat. The record currently describes it two ways. The white paper: the chef partner "owns the culinary vision as an equal founding partner" (WP p.03), freed from carrying the rest of the business (WP p.05), with the business explicitly not resting on any one person (WP p.37). The canon's held items, written under a heading that anticipates an executive chef hire: "The brand owns the culinary identity. The chef executes within it" (canon, what remains). These are not the same statement, and S2 cannot write the chef's founding document without knowing which governs. A founder decision to reconcile them has been filed in the post-extraction work; S2 reads the outcome if it exists, and surfaces the conflict rather than picking a side if it does not. The chef partner's own reading is invited in their working-with-me document (Session 1 page, section 7.4) and is chef-gated.

## 2. The founder decision-rights rule does not exist

**Target sessions:** S2 (`86ajgmhfd`), Ch2, founding documents; S3 (`86ajgmhgx`), Ch2, the operating system

**Why S2 and S3 need it.** The record holds that authority lives in the domain and that the founders own their domains outright. It holds one rule for a decision that crosses domains: lunch is an equal three-partner decision (WP p.36). It holds no general rule for which decisions are domain-owned, which are joint, what happens when a domain decision has consequences in another domain, or how three equal partners break a deadlock. This is distinct from the founder job descriptions (`86ajgnhp7`), which describe what each founder owns; this is the rule for the seams between what they own. S2's founding documents need to state it or state that it is open. S3's accountability material assumes a decision-rights structure and will otherwise import one from the standard apparatus. Each founder's draft of the rule from inside their domain is collected in prompt D of their working-with-me document; the rule itself is a founder decision filed in the post-extraction work.

## 3. The founder cadence does not exist

**Target session:** S4 (`86ajgmhjr`), Ch2, operating cadence and exercises

**Why S4 needs it.** The record holds a floor cadence (shift brief, shift close), a review cadence (every three to six months), an onboarding cadence (the ninety-day plan), and a brand cadence (daily, weekly, monthly). It holds nothing for the center: how the three partners meet, decide, review each other's domains, and read the knowledge-transfer register. This is distinct from the existing S4 item on the 1:1 (`86ajgnhv9`), which concerns the manager-and-report form. The founders have no manager and are not each other's reports. S4 designs the cadence for three equal partners with non-overlapping domains, and for the two parallel leads who likewise have no one above them. The working-with-me documents explicitly do not set this cadence (Session 1 page, prompt I) and are its input.

## 4. The nervous system does not read the center

**Target session:** S16 (`86ajgmjz6`), Conclusion, you

**Why S16 needs it.** The record's metabolic sequence, bandwidth then joy then retention then results, is stated as mechanical and applied to the whole team, with leading indicators (employee NPS, cultural labor score) as the nervous system's read on team bandwidth (WP p.12, p.17, p.22). The cooling failure mode is named as running people at capacity and calling it dedication (WP p.13). The content engine is sized to avoid burning the founders out (WP p.30). But there is no instrument that reads the founders' own bandwidth, and the record's claim that founders are not exempt is implicit. Session 16 owns the founder's time, energy, and self-management. It designs the founder-level read, and decides who sets a founder's own learning tempo, which Session 1 could not resolve. The founders' hand-written version of this read is prompt H of their working-with-me documents.

## 5. The room-owning role is named differently in the two halves of the record

**Target session:** S8 (`86ajgmhxx`), Ch4, team structures

**Why S8 needs it.** The white paper's management layer names the co-lead who owns the how of the experience, the team, and the floor as the Maitre d, "the Hospitality Manager in full deployment," freed from the host stand (WP p.10). The canon's service model assigns floor authority to a "floor manager" who owns the room, and its uniform system lists "floor manager" as a role and "hosts" as another (canon 08). The canon's arrival choreography also places a Maître d' at the top of the porch steps. It is not clear from the record whether "floor manager" and "Maitre d" are one role under two names, or whether the canon's floor manager is a shift-level designation below the co-lead. The white paper lists role naming as an open item (WP p.15). S8, which owns team structures and already carries the chef-gated back-of-house question (`86ajgmmc3`), should resolve the naming before it draws the structure, so it does not draw two roles where the record holds one.
