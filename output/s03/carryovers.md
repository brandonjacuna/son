# Session 3: new carryover items

Each item names its target session or sessions, a priority, and why that session needs it. Routed against the session sequence: S4 cadence and exercises, S5 recruiting, S6 hiring, S7 onboarding, S8 team structures, S9 diagnosing and restructuring, S10 team environment, S11 complexities and inclusion and communication, S12 coaching and hard feedback, S13 formal review and compensation, S14 performance tiers, S15 managing managers and managing out, S16 the conclusion, S17 assembly.

---

**The cadence rows of the readiness test.** *Target: S4. Priority: urgent.* Session 3 wrote twenty yes-or-no rows defining "the operating system is running" (page section 12) and left the cadence rows to Session 4: the leads' review has run a stated number of consecutive weeks; the goal cycle has produced one page per unit on schedule; mid-period and end-period scoring have occurred; the house review has produced a written record; the mechanism reset is scheduled; the feedback loop window is set; the employee NPS rhythm is set; the whole-house gathering is placed if approved. Session 4 writes them in the same form and adds them to the table so the test is complete before dinner's gate.

**The mechanism set and the frequency mapping.** *Target: S4. Priority: high.* Session 3 placed the record's existing mechanisms in her frame and added three: the leads' review (weekly, cross-period, opening on the leading indicators), the house review (quarterly, one for the whole house, five failure modes as standing scorecard), and the phase-gate review (once per opening). It set the rule that each metric is read by the mechanism matched to how fast it can move, and named which metric goes where in principle (page sections 10.2 to 10.5). Session 4 sets the exact rhythms, the agenda templates, the loop-closure window, the employee NPS rhythm, the mechanism reset after dinner's first quarter and annually, and the pre-baseline goal cycle's calendar.

**The operating system runs on ratified daypart charters, and the period count is a parameter.** *Target: S4, S8. Priority: high.* The record and canon disagree on whether the building has three core service periods or four (page section 13.2). Session 3 resolved the operating system's dependency by making it run whatever set of periods holds a ratified daypart charter, with dinner alone as the first configuration. Session 4 places each charter's drafting and ruling ahead of each opening and must not assume four. Session 8, designing team structures, must design for a variable period count and must not build a lunch team before the three-partner decision is taken.

**"Direct lead" on the hiring sheet means domain lead, and the range travels with the offer.** *Target: S5, S6, S7. Priority: high.* The white paper commits to giving every candidate a single sheet with "the org chart, their direct lead, communication expectations... and the advancement path" (WP p.18). At Sŏn "direct lead" is the domain lead who signs off competencies and holds the development review, not a manager who assigns, reviews, and escalates in her sense (page section 3.2). The sheet's org chart is the web rendered on the team home. Sessions 5 and 6 state the position's range and triggers from the decision-rights register in recruiting and hiring materials. Session 7 delivers them in onboarding so that readiness row 4 (every server and floor manager can state their range unprompted) is an onboarding output.

**The floor manager and the Maitre d: one role or two.** *Target: S8. Priority: high.* The white paper, canon 1.0, and the deck 3.0 disagree on whether the floor manager is the Maitre d, a distinct role beneath the Maitre d, or a threshold role (page section 13.3). Session 3 filed the founder decision. Session 8 designs the floor's structure from the ruling and, until it lands, treats "the owner of the room" as an incomplete owner in every decision-rights entry it touches: the server's escalation destination, the transition trigger, and emergency authority.

**Interfaces are counted in service periods and handoffs, and the leadership-line list is needed for the variety test.** *Target: S8. Priority: high.* Session 3 replaced her interface-complexity variable (people) with service periods and handoffs, and held the record's leadership delta as a claim to be tested by requisite variety against eight named disturbance classes (page sections 6.3 and 6.5). Session 8 produces the actual leadership-line list with each line's domain, and runs the pre-opening pass of the test against it. Session 8 also designs each new period's interfaces through the daypart charter's "what it hands on" and "what it draws from" sections before the period opens.

**The five failure modes are the house review's standing scorecard, and the diagnosing chapter consumes them.** *Target: S9. Priority: high.* Session 3 replaced her QBR's cross-functional focus areas with the record's five failure modes (tension slack, node overload, sequence corruption, skill invisibility, cooling failure) and mapped readiness-test failures to them (page sections 10.4 and 12.3). Session 9, on diagnosing and rebuilding, should build Sŏn's diagnostic from the failure-mode reference rather than from her chapter, and should treat a lead who produces stability by hand as node overload to be corrected by restoring the system.

**A goal met at a bandwidth cost is a system failure.** *Target: S10, S14. Priority: high.* Her "Performance = results × behaviors" and the Pyrrhic victory survive at Sŏn as a rule rather than a review note: a goal's score is read against the leading indicators of the period in which it was pursued, and a goal met while employee NPS or the cultural labor score fell is scored as a cooling failure (page section 7.6). This depends on the founder decision on "the order is the decision." Session 10 uses bandwidth as the team environment's primary read. Session 14 must not build a performance tier that rewards results achieved at that cost.

**Internal communications: three channels, non-co-presence, and the transparency line.** *Target: S11. Priority: high.* Session 3 set the trigger as non-co-presence by hour, adopted her four tests with accessibility read across hour, strand, and language, and set the three-channel rule (spoken at the brief of every open period, written on the team home, carried by the lead or mentor). It recommended the crisis rule and filed the transparency line as a founder decision (page section 11). Session 11's communication material should build on these rather than her Dunbar trigger, and should design for a team that chooses its interview language, which the record permits.

**The shift close is the feedback system's first source, and the review never contains new information.** *Target: S12, S13. Priority: normal.* The shift close captures every incident with its range status and the qualitative side of the metric layer; the personal development page is system-surfaced (page sections 10.1, 14). Session 12's feedback system should draw from the close rather than from a manager's observation alone. Session 13's formal review should read the same metrics and the same development page the person can see, so that the record's "no surprises" rule holds structurally.

**No lagging indicator drives compensation, and the generosity range's value is set with Dominic's domain.** *Target: S13. Priority: high.* The record rejects lagging indicators as the primary read and Session 3 held them as owned, reviewed last, and never primary (page section 8.4). Session 13's compensation architecture must not make revenue, labor percentage, or food cost a driver of the point system beyond what the record already states. Two decision-rights values Session 3 could not carry, the generosity range in value and the financial recovery threshold, are set with Dominic's domain in Session 13 and never appear in this program.

**Zero targets and protocol breaches as performance events.** *Target: S14. Priority: normal.* Session 3 set zero targets for food-safety critical events, David protocol breaches, known customers unrecognized on arrival, ghosted candidates, and feedback items open past the loop window (page section 8.3). Session 14, on performance tiers, decides how a zero-target breach is read at the person level, remembering that the record's first question is "what allowed this to happen," not "who failed."

**Who reviews the leads, and lead-to-lead disagreement.** *Target: S15. Priority: high.* The two leads are parallel with no one above them. Session 3 set the Class C rule: a disagreement between them escalates to the founder whose domain holds the disputed outcome, or to both partners if both (page section 4.3). The leads' own development review is the founders' matter. Session 15, on managing managers and managing out, designs the founders' review of the leads from this, and runs the failure-mode reference before any managing-out decision.

**Brandon's seat owns the operating system; the absence rule and founder attention.** *Target: S16. Priority: normal.* Session 3 assigned the operating system one owner, Brandon's seat, and the stack one owner, Dominic's seat, and set readiness row 14: the founders can be absent for a full service period with no decision deferred to their return (page sections 9.3 and 12). It named founder attention as a scarce resource split across the floor, the build, and the content engine. Session 16's founder writing should address how Brandon holds the operating system without becoming its integration layer, and the cultural labor score definition, which is his to supply.

**The team home is the wiki's staff-facing face, and the vocabulary line is an audit rule.** *Target: S17. Priority: high.* Session 3 specified the team home as the human-readable face of the operating system, distinct from the OS surface, organized per unit on the workbook's table (page sections 11.7 and 14). Session 17 assembles it from the ratified pages. Session 17 also audits every assembled page for the vocabulary line ("operating system" for the human system, "the stack" or "the OS surface" for the software, never "the OS" alone), for any tool named as the data hub before the hub confirmation lands, and for the canon drift list Session 3 added to the reconciliation.
